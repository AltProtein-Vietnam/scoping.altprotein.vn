# 28. Moves that pay off in any 2050

**In brief.**
- A scenario set is useful only if it changes what people do today. We tested 21 candidate moves against the four 2050 worlds of [[ch23-scenarios-2050]]. The moves come from the policy options, the plays and the futures research.
- Seven are no-regret moves (moves that pay off whatever happens). Most are public goods, and most can start in 2026 to 2028 at modest cost.
- Six are options: cheap to hold now, and valuable in some worlds.
- Two are bets that should wait for named signposts, because they pay off in one world and lose money in others. One is a hedge that limits the damage of the bad worlds.
- The most urgent move is a new-food route in the Food Safety Law and its decree, in 2026 to 2027.
- Five feed moves were retired in version 0.8, so 16 of the 21 moves remain.

> **Method note.** We class a move as **no-regret** if it pays off in all four worlds; an **option** if it is cheap to hold and pays off strongly in at least one; a **bet** if it needs a large commitment and pays off in only one or two; and a **hedge** if its main value is limiting losses in the stressed worlds. This follows the EU Joint Research Centre's stress test, which found 8 of 29 policy challenges common to all four of its scenarios; we treat such items as no-regret [@VIS-35] {general|High}. Each classification is our judgement {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. The four worlds are A Regional workshop, B Comfortable price-taker, C Security build-out and D Squeezed importer. Full table: `robust_moves.csv`.

Lead actors use these short forms: MAE (Ministry of Agriculture and Environment), MOH (Ministry of Health), MOST (Ministry of Science and Technology), MOF (Ministry of Finance), MOET (Ministry of Education and Training), MOIT (Ministry of Industry and Trade), NSO (National Statistics Office) and DFIs (development finance institutions). Play and public-good codes (such as T5 or P1) refer to [[ch26-plays]].

## 28.1 Seven no-regret moves

| Move | What it involves | Lead actors | Start by | Why it pays off everywhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RM-01 Publish a national protein balance** | A yearly balance of the protein people eat, by food, source and origin, with a memo line for the imported feed behind domestic meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish | MAE; NSO; MOH (National Institute of Nutrition) | 2028 | The two largest levers in the balance model (soybean-meal inclusion and feed conversion, in the memo line) are unmeasured; every foreign target we tracked has a statistic [@VIS-15; @VIS-13] {general\|Medium} |
| **RM-03 New-food route with deadlines** | A new-food procedure with statutory timelines and reliance on reference regulators (accepting their approvals), including a safety lane for cultivated food | MOH; MAE | 2027 to 2028 | Needed to import approved ingredients as well as to make them; the Food Safety Law window is open now [@REG-12] {VN-direct\|Medium}. For cultivated food, the FAO and WHO 2023 hazard framework and Singapore's approvals give a ready reliance basis [@SLC-05; @SLC-06; @RGN-04] {general\|Medium} |
| **RM-04 Shared food-grade pilot capacity** | 10,000 to 30,000 L of open, food-grade pilot fermentation, with sensory and food-safety testing for the products made on it | MOST; Ho Chi Minh City; universities; DFIs | 2030 | Rivals site pilot capacity in designated zones: Thailand's Eastern Economic Corridor of Innovation has two 15,000 L fermenters. We found none open in Vietnam [@RGN-36; @FTB-42; @INF-02; @INF-05] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| **RM-05 Protein-quality laboratory and open price series** | Accredited testing for protein quality, authenticity and contaminants; a public price and cost series for food protein ingredients, including domestic soybean and mung bean farm-gate prices and imported food-bean prices by origin | MOST; MAE; a university | 2028 | Imports and domestic products need the same checks, and adulteration is a listed wildcard, a low-probability, high-impact event ([[app-f2-drivers-signals]]). None of the Vietnamese laboratory pages we read lists amino-acid digestibility tests [@INF-03; @INF-10; @INF-11; @INF-13; @INF-14] {VN-direct\|Medium}. The EU legume roadmap proposes a similar observatory of legume prices and volumes [@PPE-05] {general\|Medium} |
| **RM-06 Residue-carbon rules and a by-product atlas** | Mapped volumes of cassava pulp, straw, stillage (distillery residue), okara (soy pulp from tofu and soy milk) and brewery streams; collection contracts; food-safety rules for food-grade side streams | MAE; provinces; industry | 2028 | Straw must be fully collected on 1 million ha by 2030; EU feed law bans some waste streams [@NTS-32; @NGF-14] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| **RM-08 Climate-proof siting** | Raised sites and surface water in the Mekong Delta; process designs for cooling water at 40 °C | Provinces; MAE; investors | 2027 | Subsidence of about 1.1 cm a year; plants built after 2035 in coastal Ca Mau (former Ca Mau and Bac Lieu) or Vinh Long (former Vinh Long, Tra Vinh and Ben Tre) face flood risk in their lifetime (our inference, as is the 40 °C design value) [@CLM-13; @CLM-14; @CLM-16] {VN-direct\|Medium} {fx:projection} |
| **RM-09 Process-engineering skills** | Two tracks: senior process engineers through a talent master's under Decision 1002, and fermentation operators through MOIT food colleges and FIRI (MOIT's Food Industries Research Institute) with tolling hosts; plus a buyer-set product challenge lab; industry placements | MOET; MOIT; MOST; universities; industry | 2027 | Senior biotechnology talent is the named bottleneck; graduate targets already exist [@ECF-39; @ECF-41] {VN-direct\|Medium}. We found no alternative-protein course and no fermentation short course for industry [@VCO-21; @SLW-14] {VN-direct\|Medium} |

{{kn:kn-no-regret-moves}}

**The protein balance (RM-01) has a first version.** We built one from FAO food balance sheets: the protein supply by food, the share of each food that is imported, and a memo line for the imported feed behind domestic animal foods ([[ch01-why-vietnam]], section 1.1; open tables in `protein_supply_by_source.csv`, `self_sufficiency_by_food.csv` and `animal_protein_import_reliance.csv`). It shows that 19% to 23% of the protein supply was imported food in 2023 (our calculation) [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. With the feed memo line, 61% to 71% of animal protein rested on imports, directly or through feed (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low}. We found no food balance sheet published by a Vietnamese agency (OQ-462) {VN-direct|Medium}. A national version would replace FAO estimates where they look weak, such as eggs and milk ([[app-r2-disagreements]], DG-174 and DG-175).

**An official hook exists for its headline indicator.** The national food-security resolution to 2030 (Resolution 34/NQ-CP, 2021) sets production volumes but no import, feed or protein indicator [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High}. MAE's 2026 plan asks to "step by step reduce import dependence by developing domestic raw-material areas" [@SEC-02] {VN-direct|High}. A protein balance would let MAE measure that task. Its headline indicator should be the share of the protein people eat made from domestic inputs, defined by where the inputs come from, not where the processing happens. We found no announced successor to Resolution 34 after 2030; one would be a natural home for the indicator (our judgement; OQ-455) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.

**The protein balance method matches Europe's.** The leading EU non-governmental analysis of soy dependency builds its protein supply and use matrix from the same FAOSTAT food balance sheets as our first version [@PPE-03; @PPE-04] {general|High}. So neither RM-01 nor the statistics part of RM-13 needs a different method.

**Skills (RM-09) has two tracks and a product component.** The move needs an owner for operator training and an answer to a sequencing problem:
- **Senior engineers.** Decision 1002 (2025) sets up 100 talent engineer and master's programmes. MOET's Official Letter 5695 (17 September 2025) asks universities to propose postgraduate programmes that pair biotechnology with an application field, with requested state laboratory investment [@SLW-07; @SLW-08] {VN-direct|Medium}. A "biotechnology plus food process engineering" master's fits this annual cycle (our suggestion; OQ-505).
- **Operators.** The summary of Decision 1002 we read has no vocational or operator component [@SLW-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. MOIT runs the food colleges and FIRI, which has departments of fermentation technology and of enzyme and protein technology [@SLW-17; @INF-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. So MOIT joins the lead actors.
- **The constraint is equipment, not fees.** A 21-hour hands-on fermentation course in the United States costs USD 3,100, half of it at a pilot-scale bioreactor [@SLW-09] {general|High}. A first cohort of 20 Vietnamese engineers would cost about USD 62,000 in fees in 2027, travel excluded (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. But shared pilot capacity (RM-04) is planned only by 2030. Until then, hands-on modules must run on the industrial tanks of tolling hosts or abroad (OQ-504, OQ-508).
- **A teaching capstone.** Once the shared line exists, it can host a pilot-scale capstone for a process-engineering master's, co-taught by industry. Berkeley's Master of Bioprocess Engineering runs its capstone at a pilot unit with a 300 L bioreactor [@AML-32] {general|High}. Teaching would also give the shared pilot and tolling line (T5) a steady user (OQ-517).
- **A buyer-set product challenge lab.** One or two food-technology faculties run a one-semester, credit-bearing product-design course. Teams work on problems set and part-funded by Vietnamese buyers, for textured protein (T1), koji and fungal foods (T9) or plant drinks (T10). The University of California, Berkeley, ran such classes from 2017 to 2024, with about 32 students a semester (our calculation) and at least three startups [@AML-21; @AML-22; @AML-11; @AML-13; @AML-15] {general|Medium}. Vietnam already has a showcase: the national Food Innovation and Development (FID) student contest drew 144 projects from 58 institutions in 2025 [@AML-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. Drop this component if no firm sets and co-funds a problem in the first cycle, if fewer than 15 students enrol in each of the first two cycles, or if no firm trials a prototype within two cycles (our tests).

**The public goods fold in.** Four public goods remain among the plays in [[ch26-plays]]. P1 (a proportionate new-food procedure) becomes RM-03. P4 (a protein-quality laboratory) and P6 (an open cost model and price series) become RM-05. P5 (a by-product atlas) becomes RM-06. In [[ch23-scenarios-2050]], P4 and P6 already scored no-regret, and P1 and P5 held in every world. The related policy options are in [[ch27-policy-options]].

## 28.2 Six options: cheap to hold

| Move | What it involves | Pays off most in | Start by | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RM-10 Reserve hub sites** | Name food-fermentation hubs (clusters of plants on one site) in the MAE biotechnology-cluster plan (due December 2026) and in provincial hi-tech agriculture zones | A, C (D partly) | 2026 to 2027 | The bioeconomy map is still blank; provinces can now create hi-tech agriculture zones themselves [@HUB-09; @HUB-26; @NTS-37] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| **RM-11 Small permanent protein-innovation fund** | USD 5 to 15 M a year for food-protein innovation, reviewed each five-year plan | A, C; useful in all | 2027 | Denmark about USD 12 M, Germany about EUR 6 M (its protein-crop strategy) and Canada about USD 22 M (a plant-protein supercluster) a year; we annualised Denmark and Canada from multi-year totals [@VIS-01; @VIS-24; @VIS-06] {general\|Medium} |
| **RM-12 Concessional (below-market) finance window** | DFI co-finance and guarantees with an offtake (an agreement to buy the output) from a named food manufacturer, for first plants the size of a cassava-sugar fermentation hub (T4) or a shared pilot and tolling plant (T5); ready to open when a signpost trips | C, D | 2028 (ready) | Cutting the capital charge from 12% to 9% does as much as the cost learning expected by 2030 (our calculation) {VN-direct\|Low} {fx:estimate}. A domestic textured-protein line (T1, USD 0.28 to 1.38 M installed) can draw on existing instruments: SME credit guarantees and finance leases, probably SME Development Fund loans, and possibly Decree 57/2018 support for processing plants [@SLK-20; @SLK-01; @SLK-10; @SLK-21] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| **RM-13 Join regional approval and statistics networks** | Take part in any reliance work by the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) and in the EU-style balance-sheet method | A, C | 2027 | Reliance is how smaller regulators keep pace (our judgement) {general\|Low}; the EU is developing harmonised feed and food protein balance sheets [@VIS-15] {general\|Medium} |
| **RM-14 Seed research on cultivated seafood cells and gas fermentation** | Small programmes with foreign partners on Vietnamese species (Asian seabass and grouper proposed) and gas-fermentation strains; open flavour profiles of farmed fish | A, C | 2028 | Vietnam has 2 of 494 gas-fermentation papers (OpenAlex, September 2026) [@FTG-14] {VN-direct\|High}. Food-oriented muscle cell lines now exist abroad for grouper and Asian seabass [@SLS-14; @SLS-15] {general\|Low} |
| **RM-15 Clear rules for second-hand fermentation equipment** | Customs and tax treatment; a register of available used tanks, 2027 to 2030 | C (A and D partly) | 2027 | Meati's assets sold for USD 75,000 against an estimated USD 15 to 20 M original spend [@FTB-38] {general\|Medium}. The one ethanol-plant retrofit the Good Food Institute cites, by Superbrewed Food, was paused in 2024 and its first output moved to a contract manufacturer [@SLF-12; @SLF-14] {general\|Medium} |

**The innovation fund (RM-11) is large by Vietnamese standards.** USD 5 to 15 M a year is VND 130 to 390 billion at 26,000 VND per USD. That is about 47% to 140% of everything NAFOSTED, the national science foundation, approved for new projects in 2023 (VND 278.3 billion, all fields). It is 70% to 210% of NAFOSTED's 2023 disbursements of VND 185.5 billion (our calculation) [@SLW-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. A first step at NAFOSTED scale would be one directed call in 2027 of about VND 9 to 10 billion (USD 0.35 to 0.38 M), enough for 10 ordinary or 3 strong-group projects (our calculation) {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:estimate}. Its owner, size and co-funders are in [[ch06-knowledge-talent]], section 6.7 (OQ-507).

**The concessional window (RM-12) is for fermentation plants, not textured protein.** The Ho Chi Minh City SME credit guarantee fund can guarantee up to about USD 1.3 M per customer for investment loans (our calculation) [@SLK-20] {VN-direct|High}. That covers almost all of a T1 line's installed cost, but not a fermentation first plant of tens of millions of USD. So the window matters at T4 and T5 scale ([[app-s11-capital]]).

**Seed research (RM-14) gains species and a flavour component.**
- **Species.** Asian seabass and grouper are farmed in Vietnam, and food-oriented muscle cell lines now exist abroad. Pukyong National University (Korea) published a seven-band grouper line in 2024, and Thiruvalluvar University (India) used Asian seabass lines to make small amounts of lab-grown fish meat in 2025 [@SLS-14; @SLS-15; @SLS-16] {general|Low}. We read these papers as metadata only. James Cook University published a barramundi (Asian seabass) muscle-line study in 2026 [@SLS-13] {general|Low}. The striped catfish lines we found were built for virology or toxicology, so catfish would serve as a low-cost model species ([[app-r2-disagreements]], DG-384) [@SLS-13; @SCI-10] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **Flavour.** An open dataset of the odour-active and taste compounds of cooked tra catfish, tilapia, carp and pond loach, with their off-flavours. It would use gas chromatography with mass spectrometry and with olfactometry (GC-MS and GC-O) and a trained panel, and serve makers of plant-based, hybrid and cultivated seafood. Coefficient Giving's 2026 taste call names carp, tilapia, milkfish, catfish and pond loach, and asks for open, pre-competitive flavour data; no awards were public on 15 October 2026 [@CGG-03; @CAP-57] {general|Medium}. Vietnamese groups already study cooked pangasius flavour and off-flavour in pond tilapia [@CGG-15; @CGG-16] {VN-direct|Low}. Whether a Vietnamese laboratory can run GC-O is open (OQ-520), and so is whether the 2026 awards cover these fish (OQ-519).

## 28.3 Two bets, each with a trigger

Each bet pays off in one world and loses money in others, so each waits for a trigger. One is contract precision fermentation (microbes engineered to make one protein). The other is sugar from rice straw.

| Bet | Pays off in | Trigger before scaling | Why wait |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RM-18 Contract precision-fermentation plant for export** | A (C partly) | A new-food route, an ASEAN reliance mechanism or regional capacity outside China, and an anchor customer | Works today only for high-value proteins [@FTB-41] {general\|Medium} |
| **RM-19 Cellulosic sugar from rice straw** | C (A partly) | An Asian straw plant above 80% of design capacity for a full year (our threshold) | Every US and European commercial plant has shut (Brazil's Raizen plant still runs); India's reached 62% [@NGF-11; @NGF-33] {general\|Medium} |

Triggers map to the signposts in `signposts_2050.csv`. A bet placed before its trigger trips is a speculative investment, not a policy.

## 28.4 One hedge

**RM-21 Import monitoring and equal standards for protein imports.** Track the volumes and unit values of plant, microbial and fermented protein-ingredient imports for food. Apply the same food-safety and quality rules to imports and to domestic products. No baseline series exists today {VN-direct|Low}. This limits the damage of a dumping episode (imports sold below cost) in worlds B and D without closing the market.

Soybeans belong on the list, because they carry the soy foods people eat. Vietnam's soybean imports came 89% from Brazil and the United States in 2025 (our calculation from customs shares of 45.8% and 43.6%) [@MAC-09] {VN-direct|High}. There is a precedent for an origin shock. The United States embargoed soybean exports in June 1973 and reinstated contracts by 1 October. Japan, then 97% import-dependent, responded by financing a new origin in Brazil rather than by growing more at home [@PPE-12] {general|Medium}. So RM-21 should track soybean imports and their origin concentration; no new move is needed.

**Five feed moves retired.** We retired five moves in version 0.8, because feed is now context only. They were RM-02, a feed-efficiency and formulation programme; RM-07, an omega-3 sourcing plan for aquafeed; RM-16, bulk microbial feed-protein capacity; RM-17, a power-to-protein demonstration on the Mekong shrimp coast; and RM-20, diversified feed-protein sourcing and stocks. They stay in `robust_moves.csv` as the record. In the balance model, better feed conversion and formulation would cut 2050 soybean-meal need by about 3.4 Mt. That is about twice what the study's large alternative-protein scenario (S-ALT) would cut (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. That lever sits outside the scope of this study ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]).

## 28.5 Sequencing: the first three years

> **For policy makers.** Six of the seven no-regret moves, and one cheap option, can start between September 2026 and the end of 2028. Existing institutions and modest budgets are enough. In order of urgency:
> 1. a new-food route in the Food Safety Law and its decree (RM-03, 2026 to 2027);
> 2. named sites in the MAE biotechnology-cluster plan (RM-10, an option, December 2026);
> 3. climate-proof siting rules for new plants (RM-08, 2027);
> 4. two skills tracks (RM-09, 2027): a talent master's for senior engineers under Decision 1002, and operator training through MOIT colleges and FIRI, with hands-on work on tolling hosts' tanks until shared pilot capacity exists;
> 5. a first national protein balance (RM-01, 2028);
> 6. a by-product atlas and residue rules (RM-06, 2028);
> 7. a protein-quality laboratory and open price series (RM-05, 2028).
>
> Shared food-grade pilot capacity (RM-04) follows by 2030, and can then host the RM-09 teaching capstone.

## 28.7 What we could not resolve

Several open questions decide which world is unfolding and how large the moves should be. The most important are:
- whether any Vietnamese agency compiles a food or protein balance sheet, the base for RM-01 (OQ-462);
- Vietnamese time series of feed conversion and soybean-meal inclusion, which the feed memo line of the protein balance needs;
- the purity and volume of carbon dioxide (CO2) at named sites;
- whether full-scale fermentation plants qualify for the incentives of hi-tech agriculture zones;
- whether Vietnam's next climate plan under the Paris Agreement (NDC 3.0) will add agriculture or livestock caps for 2035;
- which plants train their own fermentation operators, and whether any would take outside trainees before shared pilot capacity exists (OQ-504).

All are logged in [[app-r1-open-questions]], most with the futures prefix, and disagreements between sources in [[app-r2-disagreements]].
