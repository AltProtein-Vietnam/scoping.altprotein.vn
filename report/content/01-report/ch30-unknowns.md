# 30. What we do not know, and who can tell us

**In brief.**

- We built this report from public sources over eleven research rounds. By the end, each new search added little.
- The remaining gaps sit in ministry files, company plants and customs records, not on the open web. Most can be closed cheaply: a written question to a ministry, a call to a starch factory or food maker, or a customs data request.
- We list the eighteen unknowns that matter most for protein for people. They are ranked by how much each would change a decision in this report, with the play or move it unblocks ([[ch26-plays]], [[ch28-robust-moves]]) and who to ask.
- Four come from the food-security research of 2026 and decide how far the security case reaches: where the inputs of alternative-protein foods made in Vietnam come from, the share of Vietnamese soybeans in tofu and soy milk, whether any Vietnamese agency compiles a food balance, and whether an African swine fever vaccine protects against the new recombinant strains.
- Six unknowns mattered only for feed plays. Those plays were retired in version 0.8, so the six appear as context below the list.
- The full register holds 473 open questions (`open_questions.csv`) and 373 disagreements between sources, each with the position we took (`disagreements.csv`).

> **Demand questions.** Part III, the demand chapters, adds 114 demand-side open questions (OQ-156 to OQ-269, [[app-r1-open-questions]]). The ones that most change the demand picture ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]) are:
>
> - how many people keep *chay* (vegetarian) days;
> - how much meat is eaten as processed products;
> - protein per canteen meal;
> - the food and feed split of plant-protein imports;
> - any tested Vietnamese evidence on willingness to pay and names.

## 30.1 The eighteen unknowns that matter most

Eighteen unknowns now decide most. Each row names the play or move it unblocks, the cheapest way to close it and its number in the register.

The "Unblocks" column uses the codes for plays (T) and public goods (P) in [[ch26-plays]], and for moves (RM) in [[ch28-robust-moves]]:

- T1: domestic textured plant protein
- T4: a cassava-starch-to-fermentation-sugar hub (a cluster of plants on one site)
- T5: shared pilot and tolling fermentation (renting time on someone else's fermenter)
- T7: high-value, low-dose precision-fermentation ingredients
- T10: mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks
- P1: a proportionate new-food procedure; P4: a protein-quality laboratory
- RM-01: a national protein balance, published yearly
- Security case: the first argument for protein for people in [[ch01-why-vietnam]] (section 1.6)

**Reading.**

- **Items 1 and 2 are about rules and are time-sensitive.** Each could be settled by a single written reply. Together they decide whether most food plays have a clear route.
- **Items 3 to 7 decide the economics of T1, T4 and T5**, the three plays that lead under almost every weighting. They need industry calls, not more desk research.
- **Items 8 to 11 decide how far the security case reaches.** Chapter 1 finds that only protein made from domestic inputs reduces import dependence. Items 8 and 9 measure that share for new and existing plant protein. Item 10 is the cheapest step towards a national protein balance, and item 11 decides whether swine fever stays a lasting exposure. Each needs a call or a letter, not more desk research.
- **Items 12 to 18 cover testing, cooling costs, regional pilot plants, and routes to Singapore and halal markets** for the other plays.

**Context: feed.** Six questions mattered only for the feed plays and feed public goods. These were retired in version 0.8 because feed is now context only. They stay in the register ([[app-r1-open-questions]]) and still bear on the feed behind the meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish people eat ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]):

- whether a raw material on the livestock feed list (yeast, duckweed or insect meal) is legal in aquafeed without a trial; the aquafeed list has no raw-material section (OQ-043);
- the fishmeal share in Vietnamese shrimp and pangasius feeds (OQ-002);
- how much Chinese microbial protein enters Vietnamese feed, and at what price (OQ-034);
- official aquafeed output by species, with industry figures of 3.9 to 4.8 Mt against 6.5 Mt from the US Department of Agriculture (USDA) (OQ-003);
- whether the 2030 target of 30 to 32 Mt of industrial feed covers aquafeed: answered at Medium confidence, livestock feed only (OQ-127);
- whether a new feed ingredient such as single-cell protein counts as "animal feed" and so is not subject to VAT (OQ-089).

**Other food-security questions.** The food-security research of 2026 added open questions from OQ-447 onwards. Beyond items 8 to 11, these bear most on the protein people eat:

- what share of the fish eaten in Vietnam is wild-caught (OQ-454). Policy plans to cut the wild catch from about 3.8 Mt in 2025 to 2.8 Mt in 2030 [@NTS-08; @MAC-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:projection};
- whether tofu, soy milk and *chay* products sold more during the swine fever waves of 2019 and 2025 (OQ-450);
- whether a successor to the national food-security resolution after 2030 will carry an import or protein indicator (OQ-455);
- national swine fever outbreaks, culls and vaccine use by farm type in 2026 (OQ-447);
- the national stunting rate for children under five in 2025 (OQ-464).

## 30.2 Where sources disagree

We recorded 372 disagreements between sources and stated the position we took for each ([[app-r2-disagreements]]). These are the ones that most affect this report's conclusions on protein for people:

| Topic | The disagreement | Position taken |
|---|---|---|
| Protein-ingredient import tonnage | Vietnam's own import data against China's export data for the same trade (mirror data) | China's mirror data for tonnage; Vietnam's weights are estimated ([[ch03-regional]]) |
| Precision-fermentation titer benchmark (titer is the concentration of product in the fermenter) | "Above 50 g/L" presented as a calculated threshold | An expert assertion, not a modelled figure ([[ch10-technology-fit]]) |
| Fermentation cost structure in the regional study by the Good Food Institute Asia Pacific and Hawkwood Biotech | Earlier drafts read model feedstock as 10 to 18% of cost against 30 to 65% in cited studies | No conflict: model feedstock is 37 to 56%, within the cited ranges, and 10 to 18% is depreciation; labour and power are a minor share ([[ch03-regional]]) |
| Start date for E10 petrol (10% ethanol), a new buyer of cassava | 1 January 2026 against 1 June 2026 | 1 June 2026; January was the circular's effective date ([[ch19-outlook-2035]]) |
| Entobel financing (an insect-feed deal, cited in chapter 8 for its deal structure) | "About USD 36 million" against USD 32.5 million documented | USD 32.5 million ([[ch08-capital]]) |
| Whether alternative protein reduces disease risk | Advocacy and company authors say replacing meat lowers pandemic and resistance risk; peer-reviewed work ties risk to farming, trade and antibiotic use, and no study measures an effect | The study makes no such claim; at the benchmark scale it would not move herd-level risk (DG-356; [[ch01-why-vietnam]]) |
| Maize eaten as food, which sets the imported share of protein supply | FAO: 1,617 kt in 2023; OECD-FAO: 639 kt | FAO, because its items sum to the protein total: 19% to 23% of protein imported, or 18% to 22% on OECD-FAO (DG-363; [[ch01-why-vietnam]]) |
| Stunting among children under five, latest year | 18% in 2023 against 14.8% for 2025 in one press report | 18% until the National Institute of Nutrition publishes (DG-365; [[ch11-protein-diet]]) |

Three disagreements now matter only as feed context. The first is aquafeed volume: industry 3.9 to 4.8 Mt in 2025 against USDA 6.5 Mt, unresolved, with both shown and never averaged in [[ch01-why-vietnam]]. The others are the pool of fishmeal that new ingredients could replace, and the fishmeal price. Appendix R2 keeps all three with the positions taken.

## 30.3 Limits of this report

- **Tools.** The general web-search budget ran out part-way through the first wave. After that, we used direct reads of known pages, legal databases, scholarly indexes (Scite and OpenAIRE) and trade data from the World Bank's World Integrated Trade Solution. We could not reach some sources; each is logged ([[app-m1-method]]).
- **Language.** We read Vietnamese-language legal texts and press directly. Vietnamese-language journals are only partly indexed, so we undercount Vietnamese research output ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **Field data.** The retail audit is a convenience sample of 186 products in 11 stores in two cities in September 2026 ([[app-s2-retail-audit]]).
- **Cost estimates.** Our cost stacks (breakdowns of production cost) are indicative, not feasibility studies ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **No interviews.** This draft relies on documents. The unknowns above are the agenda for a round of interviews.

## 30.4 How to help close the gaps

Ministries, starch factories, food makers, fermentation plants, laboratories and investors may hold these answers. If you hold one, you can help close the gap. AltProtein Vietnam will record corrections in `open_questions.csv` and `disagreements.csv` and date each change, so the website can show what has been closed since publication.
