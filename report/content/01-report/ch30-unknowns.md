# 30. What we do not know, and who can tell us

**In brief.**

- We built this report from public sources over thirteen research rounds. By the end, each new search added little.
- The remaining gaps sit in ministry files, company plants and customs records, not on the open web. Most can be closed cheaply: a written question to a ministry, a call to a factory or food maker, or a customs data request.
- We list the eighteen unknowns that matter most, ranked by how much each would change a decision in this report, with who to ask.
- Four decide how far the security case reaches: where the inputs of Vietnamese alternative-protein foods come from, how much of the soy in tofu is Vietnamese, whether any agency compiles a food balance, and whether a swine fever vaccine protects against the new strains.

> **Demand questions.** The questions that most change the demand picture are how many people keep *chay* (vegetarian) days, how much meat is eaten processed, the protein in a canteen meal, how much of the plant-protein imports go to food rather than feed, and any tested evidence on willingness to pay ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

## 30.1 The eighteen unknowns that matter most

The "Unblocks" column uses the play codes of [[ch26-plays]] and the move codes of [[ch28-robust-moves]]: T1 textured plant protein; T4 cassava sugar hub; T5 shared pilot and tolling fermentation; T7 high-value precision fermentation; T10 mung bean and rice protein for drinks; P1 new-food procedure; P4 protein-quality laboratory; RM-01 national protein balance. "Security case" is the first argument in [[ch01-why-vietnam]], section 1.6.

| # | Unknown | Unblocks | Who to ask, or the cheapest way to close it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the draft Food Safety Law define "new food", or delegate it to the Government? | P1 and all food plays | The National Assembly's draft portal; the Vietnam Food Administration |
| 2 | Do purified precision-fermented proteins need a GMO food certificate? What are the timeline and fee for making them in Vietnam? | T7 | A written question to the agriculture ministry and the Vietnam Food Administration |
| 3 | Will Vedan, Ajinomoto or Daesang rent tank time to others, and how much is idle? | T5 | A direct question to their planning teams |
| 4 | Does anyone extrude textured soy in Vietnam? | T1 | Ask chay makers and ingredient distributors |
| 5 | Import volumes of textured protein, soy isolate and gluten at the finest tariff level | T1 | Customs statistics or a trade-data service |
| 6 | The factory-gate price and use of cassava pulp | T4 | Calls to three to five starch factories in Tay Ninh; the cassava association |
| 7 | Current prices of cassava starch, glucose syrup and molasses | T4 | Cassava and sugar associations; starch factories |
| 8 | Where does the protein in Vietnamese alternative-protein foods come from: domestic or imported inputs? | T1; security case | Customs data by importer; calls to three textured-soy and chay makers |
| 9 | What share of the soybeans in tofu and soy milk is grown in Vietnam? | Security case; RM-01 | Vinasoy and a large tofu maker or bean trader |
| 10 | Does any Vietnamese agency compile a national food or protein balance? | RM-01 | One call to the statistics office and the agriculture ministry |
| 11 | Is a swine fever vaccine that protects against the new strains in trials or licensing? | Security case (disease) | Vietnamese vaccine makers; the ministry's licensing lists |
| 12 | Which Vietnamese laboratories are accredited for amino-acid and digestibility tests? | P4, T1, T10 | The main food laboratories; the accreditation body |
| 13 | How much cooling energy does a Vietnamese fermentation plant use? | All fermentation plays | Engineers at Vedan, Ajinomoto or AB Mauri |
| 14 | Can a product made in Vietnam be sold in Singapore under a Singapore approval? | T7; the 2035 regional hub ([[ch19-outlook-2035]]) | The Singapore Food Agency's import rules |
| 15 | ScaleUp Bio's capacity, prices and willingness to take Vietnamese clients | T5, T7 | ScaleUp Bio (Singapore) |
| 16 | Is Thailand's public pilot plant food-grade, and on what terms for foreign clients? | T5, T7 | The plant's contacts |
| 17 | Where do Vietnam's high-protein plant drinks source their pea protein? | T10 | Vinamilk; customs consignee data |
| 18 | Do Malaysian and Indonesian certifiers accept Vietnamese fermented ingredients as halal? | Export | Vietnam's national halal centre; a Malaysian certifier |

Our ranking {VN-direct\|Medium}.

**Reading.**

- **Items 1 and 2 are about rules, and time-sensitive.** Each could be settled by one written reply. Together they decide whether most food plays have a clear route.
- **Items 3 to 7 decide the economics of T1, T4 and T5,** the three leading plays. They need industry calls, not more desk research.
- **Items 8 to 11 decide how far the security case reaches.** Only protein made from domestic inputs reduces import dependence. Items 8 and 9 measure that share. Item 10 is the cheapest step towards a protein balance. Item 11 decides whether swine fever stays a lasting risk.
- **Items 12 to 18 cover testing, cooling costs, regional pilot plants, and routes to Singapore and halal markets.**

**Other food-security questions** that bear on the protein people eat:

- what share of the fish eaten in Vietnam is wild-caught, as policy cuts the wild catch about 27% by 2030 [@NTS-08; @MAC-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:projection};
- whether tofu, soy milk and chay sold more during the swine fever waves of 2019 and 2025;
- whether a food-security policy after 2030 will carry an import or protein indicator;
- swine fever outbreaks and culls by farm type in 2026;
- the national stunting rate for children under five in 2025.

## 30.2 Where sources disagree

Where sources disagree, we give both and state the position we took. These disagreements most affect the report's conclusions:

| Topic | The disagreement | Position taken |
|---|---|---|
| Protein-ingredient import tonnage | Vietnam's import data against China's export data for the same trade | China's data for tonnage, because Vietnam's weights are estimated ([[ch03-regional]]) |
| The yield needed for bulk precision fermentation | "Above 50 g/L" presented as a calculated threshold | An expert view, not a modelled figure ([[ch10-technology-fit]]) |
| Fermentation cost structure in the regional study | Whether feedstock or capital is the largest cost line | Feedstock is 37% to 56%; labour and power are minor ([[ch03-regional]]) |
| Start date of petrol with 10% ethanol | January or June 2026 | 1 June 2026 ([[ch19-outlook-2035]]) |
| Whether alternative protein reduces disease risk | Advocacy and company authors say yes; peer-reviewed work ties risk to farming, trade and antibiotic use, and no study measures an effect | We make no such claim ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]) |
| Maize eaten as food | FAO and OECD-FAO differ by more than half | FAO, because its items add up: 19% to 23% of protein imported, or 18% to 22% on OECD-FAO ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]) |
| Child stunting, latest year | 18% in 2023, against 14.8% for 2025 in one press report | 18% until the national nutrition institute publishes ([[ch11-protein-diet]]) |

## 30.3 Limits of this report

- **Tools.** The general web-search budget ran out early in the first round. After that, we used direct reads of known pages, legal databases, research indexes and trade data. Some sources were out of reach.
- **Language.** We read Vietnamese laws and press directly. Vietnamese journals are only partly indexed, so we undercount Vietnamese research ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **Field data.** The retail audit is a convenience sample of 186 products in 11 stores in two cities.
- **Cost estimates.** Our cost breakdowns are indicative, not feasibility studies ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **No interviews.** This report relies on documents. The unknowns above are the agenda for a round of interviews.

## 30.4 How to help close the gaps

Ministries, starch factories, food makers, fermentation plants, laboratories and investors may hold these answers. If you hold one, please write to AltProtein Vietnam. We will record each correction and date it, so readers can see what has been closed since publication.
