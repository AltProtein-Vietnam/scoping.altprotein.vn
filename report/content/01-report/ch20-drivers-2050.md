# 20. Drivers to 2050: what is near-certain, what is not

**In brief.** This chapter and the four after it look past the 2035 horizon of chapter 19.
- **Some forces are close to certain,** because the people, rules or physics are already in place. The working-age population peaks between 2035 and 2040; smallholder pig farms keep disappearing; African swine fever stays endemic; agriculture must cut its emissions to 56 MtCO2e (million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent) by 2050; the Mekong Delta keeps sinking faster than the sea rises; and Vietnam keeps importing most of its soy and maize.
- **Together they make the meat, eggs and farmed fish people eat more industrial and more reliant on imported feed.**
- **Two drivers hit the protein people eat directly.** African swine fever now comes mostly from strains the licensed vaccines do not cover, so pork supply and prices will keep swinging. Wild fish is shrinking: the marine fish stock in 2016 to 2020 was 22.1% below 2000 to 2005, and policy cuts the catch about 27% by 2030.
- **Two external questions decide most for protein made in Vietnam for people.** Does imported protein, mostly the feed behind domestic meat, come under chronic stress, or stay cheap and ample, as the published baseline to 2035 expects? And does Asia's fermentation industry become an open regional market, or one where Chinese scale sets every price?
- **These two questions are the axes** of the 2050 scenarios in [[ch23-scenarios-2050]]. Vietnam's own choices are not axes: they are the moves tested in [[ch28-robust-moves]].

> **How to read the 2050 chapters.** Forward-looking claims carry a second badge after the evidence tag. *Trend* means an observed change we expect to continue; *projection* a published model result or an official target (the text says which); *estimate* our own calculation; *signal* an early development that may or may not scale; *wildcard* a low-probability, high-impact event; *vision* a normative goal, used only in [[ch24-vision-2050]]. Every such sentence names its horizon year. Method: [[app-m2-futures-method]].

These forces are already in motion. Policy can speed them up or soften them, but not reverse them before 2050.

**Readings.**
- **Demography and disease make the meat people eat more industrial and more reliant on imported feed.** Fewer, larger farms buy compound feed, made mostly from imported soy and maize. So the animal protein people eat rests more on imports, not less {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Disease is a shock to pork, not to protein as a whole.** When African swine fever hit, people ate more chicken, in 2019 and again in 2025 (section 20.3). The licensed vaccines give no protection against the recombinant strains, so ASF stays a recurring risk to pork supply and prices to 2035 [@PAN-33; @GEO-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:trend}. Its steady cost to health comes from food and from handling pigs: *Streptococcus suis* (a bacterium carried by pigs) and *Salmonella* in pork ([[app-f2-drivers-signals]], F2.2.2).
- **Alternative protein does not reduce disease risk at the scale demand supports.** The benchmark demand path spares about 142,000 pigs a year in 2035, against about 1.2 million culled for ASF in 2025 (our calculation) [@MAC-01] {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. We found no study that measures lower zoonotic, pandemic or antimicrobial-resistance risk from replacing animal-source food. The claims that it does come from reviews and opinion, several by advocacy or company authors [@PAN-30; @PAN-31] {general|Medium}. Costed plans to prevent pandemics target wildlife trade, deforestation and surveillance, not diet change [@PAN-28] {general|Medium}. The value of alternative protein here is narrower: a source of protein that does not fail when animals do.
- **Wild fish is under more pressure from fishing and policy than from climate.** Fish and seafood supplied 25.7% of the animal protein supply in 2023 (our calculation) [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|High}. Climate change cuts catch potential in Vietnam's waters by 2050 by 2.8% to 6.8% on a low-emission path (RCP2.6) and by 4.3% to 11.7% on a high-emission path (RCP8.5). These are two FAO models, which we do not average (disagreement DG-357) [@SEC-07] {general|High} {fx:projection}. Both falls are smaller than the fall in the stock since the early 2000s and the policy cut to 2030. Alternative protein for food does not touch this exposure: the demand routes replace meat and *chay* (traditional vegetarian food) protein, not fish.
- **The climate case for alternative protein in Vietnam rests on imported feed, manure and land, not on national methane totals.** Crop cultivation, mainly rice, holds two-thirds of the agricultural methane cap (30.7 of 45.9 MtCO2e in 2030; 45.9 is our sum of the crop and livestock caps) [@CLM-02] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection}. This confirms [[ch09-economics]].

## 20.2 Critical uncertainties

These drivers matter a great deal and could go either way by 2050. We found no basis for assigning probabilities to most of them.

**How Vietnam frames food security.** The food-security resolution to 2030 (Resolution 34/NQ-CP, 2021) counts production volumes. By 2030 it aims for more paddy, meat, milk, eggs and aquatic products, for example 6.0 to 6.5 Mt of carcass meat and 2.6 Mt of fresh milk [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection} (official target). The full list is in [[app-f2-drivers-signals]], F2.1.9. It asks people to eat less rice and more meat, milk, eggs and fish. It sets no indicator for imports, feed or protein, and treats imports only as a "flexible" management tool [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High}. MAE's plan for 2026 adds one task for livestock: to "step by step reduce import dependence" by developing domestic raw-material areas [@SEC-02] {VN-direct|High}.

That task is the nearest official hook for a measure of how much of the protein people eat comes from domestic inputs ([[ch28-robust-moves]]). Peers that import feed, such as China and the EU, treat new protein mainly as a way to replace imported feed protein with domestic resources. We read none of them as counting alternative protein made from imported inputs as security [@SEC-04; @SEC-03; @VIS-15] {general|Medium}.

### Why these two uncertainties are the axes

Scenario practice needs two uncertainties that are high in impact, not controlled by the reader, and largely independent of each other {general|Low}.
- **Import stress decides how much a domestic second source of protein is worth.** The meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish people eat rest on imported feed. In 2023 about 61% to 71% of the animal protein people ate rested on imports, directly or through feed (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low}. Chronic stress on those imports raises the value of protein made at home for people. But only protein made from domestic inputs reduces the exposure; protein made from imported soy or Chinese ingredients moves it ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]]). On today's prices, protein made in Vietnam by fermentation costs more than the imported plant proteins it would compete with ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]). Only a sustained supply shock or a security premium changes that.
- **The structure of Asia's fermentation market decides whether a Vietnamese plant can sell at all, and to whom.** Chinese capital costs and capacity set the landed price that any Vietnamese product has to beat.
- **The two are largely independent.** Anchoveta, maize yields in the Americas and shipping lanes do not depend on Chinese industrial policy, and the reverse.

Vietnam's own choices (the food law, public money, a national protein balance) are deliberately **not** axes. They are the moves we test against the scenarios in [[ch28-robust-moves]]. Growth, trade politics, carbon pricing and disease enter as sensitivities within each scenario and as wildcards.

## 20.3 Shocks already on the record, and wildcards

Vietnam's protein supply has already absorbed large shocks:
- ASF killed or forced the culling of nearly 6 million pigs in 2019, over a fifth of the herd [@GEO-14; @GEO-15] {VN-direct|Medium}. **In protein terms it was a pork shock, not a national shortfall.** Live-weight pig output fell 13.8%, but poultry output rose 16.5% the same year, and in early 2020 the government ordered pork imports [@PAN-03] {VN-direct|High}. Household tofu use did not change between 2018 and 2020 [@DIE-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **Consumers felt disease more than imported feed.** Pork prices rose 57.23% in 2020, after the epidemic. In 2022, the year world feed prices peaked, pork prices fell 10.68% [@SEC-08; @SEC-09] {VN-direct|High}.
- ASF returned in 2025. About 1.2 million pigs were culled, about 3.8% of the herd (our calculation). Culling sales lowered live pig prices to about VND 50,000 per kg in November 2025, and people switched to chicken [@MAC-01; @MAC-12] {VN-direct|High}.
- The 2026 Middle East conflict raised world urea prices 54% in one month and Vietnamese feed prices 10 to 14% (January to April 2026, poultry association estimate) [@COST-30; @COST-33; @MAC-24] {VN-direct|Medium}.

The shock register (`shock_register.csv`, 20 events), the disease events (`disease_shocks.csv`) and 26 wildcards (`wildcards.csv`) are in [[app-f2-drivers-signals]].

> **Speculative.** Six wildcards would change the picture most:
> - a multi-year anchoveta collapse that holds fishmeal above USD 2,500 per t (from 2027, recurring to 2050; partly under way in 2026) {fx:wildcard};
> - simultaneous maize losses of more than 10% in the four largest exporters, a 7% chance in a given year at 2 °C of warming and 86% at 4 °C [@CLM-24] {general|High} {fx:projection};
> - a closure of the South China Sea or Malacca Strait lasting months (2030 to 2050); over a quarter of world soybean exports transited the Malacca Strait in a 2017 analysis [@GEO-40] {general|Medium} {fx:wildcard};
> - Chinese export dumping of microbial protein from spare capacity (2028 to 2035) {fx:wildcard};
> - mutual recognition of novel-food approvals across the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) (2030 to 2035) {fx:wildcard};
> - firm renewable power at or below USD 20 per MWh in southern Vietnam (2040 to 2050) {fx:wildcard}.
>

## 20.5 What this means

- **Investors:** the two uncertainties to price are import stress and Chinese costs. Everything else is second-order before 2035.
- **Policy makers:** the near-certain trends all point to more imported protein behind the meat people eat, and more compound feed. None of Vietnam's 2050 plans (power, hydrogen, climate caps) has a protein or feed number to go with it ([[app-f5-targets-hubs]]). The food-security resolution to 2030 counts volumes only. MAE's 2026 task to reduce import dependence is the nearest hook for a protein-security measure; count the origin of the inputs, not the place of processing.
- **Startups:** for a food start-up, the trends that matter most are residue-based carbon (straw rules), clean power and heat-tolerant process design. The feed-additive market opened by the antibiotic ban, and aquafeed for marine fish, are outside this study's scope.
- **Manufacturers:** plan for recurring shocks rather than a trend. Disease shocks reach consumer prices; feed-price shocks mostly squeeze farm margins. Multi-supplier protein strategies and formulation flexibility are the proven defences.
- **Research and international bodies:** Vietnam has no published protein or feed balance and no official number beyond 2030 for meat, feed or aquaculture. A national protein balance of what people eat, with the imported feed behind it as a memo line, is the most useful public good ([[ch28-robust-moves]]). Two cheap gaps to close are the 2026 ASF series (OQ-447) and the share of the fish people eat that comes from capture (OQ-454).
