# 20. Drivers to 2050: what is near-certain, what is not

**In one paragraph.** This chapter and the four after it look past the 2035 horizon of chapter 19. We start with the forces that will shape Vietnam's protein supply whatever anyone decides. Some are close to certain because the people, rules or physics are already in place: the working-age population peaks between 2035 and 2040; smallholder pig farms keep disappearing; African swine fever stays endemic; agriculture must cut its emissions to 56 MtCO2e by 2050; the Mekong Delta keeps sinking faster than the sea rises; and Vietnam keeps importing most of its soy and maize. Others are open. Of these, two external questions decide most for alternative protein: whether imported feed protein comes under chronic stress (fishmeal collapses, simultaneous crop failures in exporting countries, blocked shipping) or stays cheap and ample, as the published baseline to 2035 expects; and whether Asia's fermentation industry becomes an open regional market or one where Chinese scale sets every price. These two questions are the axes of the 2050 scenarios in [[ch23-scenarios-2050]].

> **How to read the 2050 chapters.** Forward-looking claims carry a second badge after the evidence tag. *Trend* means an observed change we expect to continue; *projection* a published model result or an official target (the text says which); *estimate* our own calculation; *signal* an early development that may or may not scale; *wildcard* a low-probability, high-impact event; *vision* a normative goal, used only in [[ch24-vision-2050]]. Every such sentence names its horizon year. Method: [[app-m2-futures-method]].

## 20.1 Near-certain trends

These forces are already in motion. Policy can speed them up or soften them, but not reverse them before 2050.

| Driver | What we expect | Horizon | Channel to protein supply | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ageing | Working-age population peaks at about 72 million around 2035 to 2040; people aged 65 and over rise from 9.5% (2025) to 20.0% (2050) of the population {fx:projection} | 2035 to 2050 | Fewer farm workers; automation favours integrators and factories | [@GEO-31] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Farm labour and smallholder exit | 16.5 million rural farm, forestry and fishery workers in 2025, down 2.84 million since 2016; about 13.8 million (2035) and 10.6 million (2050) if the 2016 to 2025 decline of 1.75% a year continues {fx:estimate} | 2035, 2050 | Feed use moves to compound feed; feedstock collection needs contracts, not casual labour | [@GEO-36] {VN-direct\|Low} |
| Industrial livestock | Small household farms supply 35 to 40% of pig output, and household farming is falling 5 to 7% a year; African swine fever has shifted output to large-scale and professional farms {fx:trend} | 2030s | More compound feed and more buyers able to specify novel ingredients | [@GEO-14; @GEO-15; @GEO-47] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Endemic pig disease | 952 to 3,029 African swine fever outbreaks a year in 2020 to 2024, after the 2019 epidemic; recombinant strains that current vaccines do not fully cover rose from 14.1% (2023) to 42.2% (2024) of positive samples in the north and centre {fx:trend} | to the 2030s | Recurring swings in feed demand; stronger case for biosecure, closed supply chains | [@GEO-15; @GEO-17; @GEO-18; @GEO-19] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| No preventive antibiotics | A ban on prophylactic antibiotics in livestock, set for 1 January 2026 by a 2020 decree {fx:trend} | 2026 onwards | Near-term market for fermentation-made additives: probiotics, yeast products, enzymes | [@GEO-24] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Agricultural emission caps | Agriculture capped at 64 MtCO2e in 2030 and 56 MtCO2e in 2050, against a 2020 base of 104.5 MtCO2e (the NDC's business-as-usual level) {fx:projection} (official target) | 2030, 2050 | Pressure on livestock methane and manure; alternative protein is not among the official levers | [@CLM-01; @CLM-08; @NTS-24] {VN-direct\|High} |
| A sinking, saltier delta | Groundwater pumping lowers the delta about 1.1 cm a year, about three times the current rate of global sea-level rise; subsidence and riverbed incision could enlarge the salinity-affected area by 10 to 27% by about 2050 {fx:projection} | 2050 | Coastal land moves from rice to shrimp: more aquafeed protein, fewer rice by-products | [@CLM-12; @CLM-13; @CLM-15; @CLM-21; @CLM-28] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Humid heat | The delta gains up to 60 extra days a year above a wet-bulb globe temperature of 32 °C before 2040 {fx:projection} | 2040 | Closed, cooled animal houses; higher power demand; heat-tolerant process design | [@CLM-16; @CLM-22] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Shrinking capture fisheries | Capture to fall from about 3.8 Mt (2025) to 2.8 Mt (2030); the trawl fleet that lands trash fish to shrink to about 8,360 boats by 2030 {fx:projection} (official target) | 2030 | More formulated aquafeed, especially for marine fish and lobster | [@NTS-08; @AQF-15; @AQF-06; @MAC-15] {VN-direct\|Medium} |

**Two readings.**
- **Demography and disease push towards industrial feed, which helps new ingredients.** A novel protein is sold to feed mills and integrators, not to 8 million farm households. Every step towards fewer, larger buyers makes it easier to prove, specify and sell a new ingredient {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **The climate case for alternative protein in Vietnam rests on imported feed, manure and land, not on national methane totals.** Crop cultivation, mainly rice, holds two-thirds of the agricultural methane cap (30.7 of 45.9 MtCO2e in 2030; 45.9 is our sum of the crop and livestock caps) [@CLM-02] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection}. This confirms [[ch09-economics]].

## 20.2 Critical uncertainties

These drivers matter a great deal and could go either way by 2050. We found no basis for assigning probabilities to most of them.

| Uncertainty | One end | Other end | What we know now | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Feed-protein import stress** | Benign: ample soy and maize, fishmeal recovers | Chronic: repeated fishmeal collapses, joint crop failures in exporting countries, shipping disruption | The published baseline to 2035 is ample supply: China's soybean imports fall 12% and Argentina's protein-meal exports rise 17% {fx:projection} (OECD-FAO baseline). But the 2026 Peruvian anchoveta season landed only about 25% of a quota that was already the lowest in a decade {fx:signal}, and NOAA gives over 90% odds of a very strong El Niño in winter 2026 to 2027 {fx:projection} (published probability) | [@GEO-37; @GEO-38; @HSC-28; @HSC-29; @HSC-30] {general\|Medium} |
| **Asia's fermentation market** | Open: several countries build capacity, trade freely and share approvals | Chinese price dominance: Chinese scale and costs set every regional price | A plant that costs USD 100 million to build in the US costs about USD 15 million in China (interviewees' rule of thumb); Angel Yeast opened an 11,000 t a year yeast-protein plant in 2025 {fx:signal}. China (15th Five-Year Plan, 2026 to 2030) and Korea (a 2025 synthetic biology law) fund bio-manufacturing programmes {fx:projection} | [@HSC-11; @HSC-13; @HSC-14; @ECF-29; @ECF-33] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| Trade politics | Stable rules | Tariffs and purchase deals set sourcing | US tariff policy on Vietnam went through four regimes (three changes) in 16 months (2025 to 2026, our count), with three different rates applied; Vietnamese firms signed memorandums to buy over USD 2.9 billion of US farm goods {fx:trend} | [@GEO-02; @GEO-03; @GEO-04; @GEO-48] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Growth path | About 5% a year | 10% a year to 2030, then 7.0 to 7.5% (official) | Official aim: GDP per person about USD 38,000 by 2050 (current prices) {fx:projection} (official target). On slower paths, real GDP per person in 2035 is about USD 7,400 to 8,300 {fx:estimate} | [@NTS-13; @NTS-14; @GT-11; @GEO-33; @GEO-35] {VN-direct\|Low} |
| Clean-power delivery | Plans met; firm renewable power cheap | LNG and coal stay long | The first green hydrogen plant had only perimeter works in May 2026, three years after ground-breaking {fx:signal} | [@FTG-27; @GEO-26] {VN-direct\|Medium} |

### Why two axes, and why these two

Scenario practice needs two uncertainties that are high in impact, not controlled by the reader, and largely independent of each other {general|Low}.
- **Import stress** decides whether domestic protein is worth its cost premium. On today's prices, microbial feed protein made in Vietnam costs several times soybean-meal protein ([[ch09-economics]]); only a sustained fishmeal shortage or a security premium changes that.
- **The structure of Asia's fermentation market** decides whether a Vietnamese plant can sell at all, and to whom. Chinese capital costs and capacity set the landed price that any Vietnamese product must beat.
- **The two are largely independent.** Anchoveta, maize yields in the Americas and shipping lanes do not depend on Chinese industrial policy, and the reverse.

Vietnam's own choices (the food law, feed lists, public money, a protein statistic) are deliberately **not** axes. They are the moves we test against the scenarios in [[ch28-robust-moves]]. Growth, trade politics, carbon pricing and disease enter as sensitivities within each scenario and as wildcards.

## 20.3 Shocks already on the record, and wildcards

Vietnam's protein supply has already absorbed large shocks: ASF killed or forced the culling of nearly 6 million pigs in 2019, over a fifth of the herd [@GEO-14; @GEO-15] {VN-direct|Medium}; the 2026 Middle East conflict raised world urea prices 54% in one month and Vietnamese feed prices 10 to 14% (January to April 2026, poultry association estimate) [@COST-30; @COST-33; @MAC-24] {VN-direct|Medium}. The shock register (`shock_register.csv`, 17 events) and 26 wildcards (`wildcards.csv`) are in [[app-f2-drivers-signals]].

> **Speculative.** Six wildcards would change the picture most. A multi-year anchoveta collapse that holds fishmeal above USD 2,500 per t (from 2027, recurring to 2050; partly under way in 2026) {fx:wildcard}; simultaneous maize losses of more than 10% in the four largest exporters, a 7% chance in a given year at 2 °C of warming and 86% at 4 °C [@CLM-24] {general|High} {fx:projection}; a closure of the South China Sea or Malacca Strait lasting months (2030 to 2050); over a quarter of world soybean exports transited the Malacca Strait in a 2017 analysis [@GEO-40] {general|Medium} {fx:wildcard}; Chinese export dumping of microbial protein from spare capacity (2028 to 2035) {fx:wildcard}; ASEAN mutual recognition of novel-food approvals (2030 to 2035) {fx:wildcard}; and firm renewable power at or below USD 20 per MWh in southern Vietnam (2040 to 2050) {fx:wildcard}. Sources and signposts for each: [[app-f2-drivers-signals]].

## 20.5 What this means

- **Investors:** demography and disease are consolidating the buyer base. The two uncertainties to price are import stress and Chinese costs; everything else is second-order before 2035.
- **Policy makers:** the near-certain trends all point to more imported protein and more compound feed. None of Vietnam's 2050 plans (power, hydrogen, climate caps) has a protein or feed number to go with it ([[app-f5-targets-hubs]]).
- **Startups:** the first markets created by these trends are functional feed additives (antibiotic ban), aquafeed for marine fish (trash-fish replacement) and residue-based carbon (straw rules).
- **Manufacturers:** plan for recurring shocks rather than a trend. Multi-supplier protein strategies and formulation flexibility are the proven defences.
- **Research and international bodies:** Vietnam has no published protein or feed balance and no official number beyond 2030 for meat, feed or aquaculture. A shared baseline is the most useful public good ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
