# 20. Drivers to 2050: what is near-certain, what is not

**In brief.** This chapter and the four after it look past 2035.
- **Some forces are close to certain:** an ageing population, fewer small pig farms, endemic African swine fever, a cap on farm emissions, a sinking Mekong Delta, and continued imports of soy and maize.
- **Together they make the meat, eggs and farmed fish people eat more industrial and more reliant on imported feed.**
- **Two drivers hit the protein people eat directly.** Swine fever now comes mostly from strains the vaccines do not cover, so pork prices will keep swinging. And wild fish is shrinking.
- **Two outside questions decide most.** Will imported protein come under chronic stress? And will Asia's fermentation industry stay open, or will Chinese scale set every price? These are the axes of the 2050 scenarios in [[ch23-scenarios-2050]].

> **How to read the 2050 chapters.** Claims about the future carry a second badge: *trend* (an observed change we expect to continue), *projection* (a published model result or official target), *estimate* (our calculation), *signal* (an early development that may or may not scale), *wildcard* (unlikely but high-impact), or *vision* (a goal, used only in [[ch24-vision-2050]]). Every such sentence names its year.

## 20.1 Near-certain trends

These forces are already in motion. Policy can speed or soften them, but not reverse them before 2050.

| Driver | What we expect | How it reaches the protein people eat | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ageing | The working-age population peaks around 2035 to 2040; over-65s rise from 9.5% to 20% of people by 2050 {fx:projection} | Fewer farm workers; automation favours large firms | [@GEO-31] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Fewer farm workers | About 13.8 million in 2035 and 10.6 million in 2050, from 16.5 million in 2025, if the trend continues {fx:estimate} | More meat comes from farms on factory-made feed | [@GEO-36] {VN-direct\|Low} |
| Industrial livestock | Small farms still supply 35% to 40% of pork, but fall 5% to 7% a year {fx:trend} | More pork from large farms on imported soy and maize | [@GEO-14; @GEO-15; @GEO-47] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Endemic pig disease | Swine fever is now mostly new mixed strains, 60% to 79% of samples in 2025, which licensed vaccines do not cover {fx:trend} | Recurring swings in pork supply and prices; people switch to chicken | [@GEO-15; @GEO-17; @GEO-18; @GEO-19; @PAN-33; @PAN-03; @MAC-01] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| No preventive antibiotics | Ban on routine antibiotics in livestock since 2026 {fx:trend} | Less antibiotic use per animal | [@GEO-24; @PAN-21; @PAN-23] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Farm emission caps | Agriculture capped at 56 MtCO2e (million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent) in 2050, about half the business-as-usual level {fx:projection} (official target) | Pressure on livestock methane and manure; alternative protein is not an official lever | [@CLM-01; @CLM-08; @NTS-24] {VN-direct\|High} |

| Energy transition | Non-hydro renewables planned at 74% to 75% of power by 2050 {fx:projection} (official target) | Clean power becomes possible for fermentation, but delivery lags ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]) | [@GT-11; @FTG-15; @FTG-16; @GEO-26] {VN-direct\|High} |

**Readings.**
- **Demography and disease make the meat people eat more reliant on imported feed.** Fewer, larger farms buy factory feed made mostly from imported soy and maize {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Disease is a shock to pork, not to protein as a whole.** People switched to chicken in 2019 and 2025. Swine fever stays a recurring risk to pork to 2035 [@PAN-33; @GEO-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:trend}.
- **Alternative protein does not reduce disease risk at the scale demand supports.** The benchmark demand path spares about 142,000 pigs a year in 2035, against about 1.2 million culled in 2025 (our calculation) [@MAC-01] {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. We found no study that measures lower pandemic or drug-resistance risk from replacing meat [@PAN-30; @PAN-31] {general|Medium}. Pandemic prevention plans target wildlife trade and surveillance, not diet [@PAN-28] {general|Medium}. The value here is narrower: protein that does not fail when animals fall sick.
- **Wild fish is under more pressure from fishing than from climate.** Fish supplied about a quarter of animal protein in 2023 (our calculation) [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|High}. Climate cuts catch potential by 3% to 12% by 2050, depending on emissions and model [@SEC-07] {general|High} {fx:projection}. That is smaller than the fall in fish stocks so far. Alternative protein replaces meat, not fish. The one route that touches fish is candidate play T11: fish cakes with 10% to 30% of the fish protein replaced by plant protein ([[ch26-plays]], section 26.8).
- **The catch cut also squeezes surimi, a food export.** Vietnam exported over USD 344 million of surimi and fish cakes in 2025, and exporters already report raw-material shortages [@SLS-01; @SLS-03; @SLS-04; @SLS-22] {VN-direct|Medium}. The planned cut will tighten supply (our reading) {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:estimate}.
- **The climate case rests on imported feed, manure and land, not national methane.** Rice, not livestock, holds two-thirds of the farm methane cap [@CLM-02] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection}.

## 20.2 Critical uncertainties

These drivers matter a great deal and could go either way by 2050.

| Uncertainty | One end | Other end | What we know now | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stress on imported protein** | Ample soy and maize; fishmeal recovers | Repeated fishmeal collapses, crop failures, shipping disruption | The published baseline to 2035 expects ample supply {fx:projection}. But the 2026 Peruvian anchovy season landed only about a quarter of a record-low quota {fx:signal}, and a very strong El Niño is likely in 2026 to 2027 {fx:projection} | [@GEO-37; @GEO-38; @HSC-28; @HSC-29; @HSC-30] {general\|Medium} |
| **Asia's fermentation market** | Open: several countries build, trade and share approvals | Chinese scale sets every price | A plant that costs USD 100 million in the US costs about USD 15 million in China (industry rule of thumb) {fx:signal}; China and Korea fund biomanufacturing {fx:projection} | [@HSC-11; @HSC-13; @HSC-14; @ECF-29; @ECF-33] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| Trade politics | Stable rules | Tariffs and purchase deals set sourcing | US tariffs on Vietnam changed three times in 16 months {fx:trend} | [@GEO-02; @GEO-03; @GEO-04; @GEO-48] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Growth | About 5% a year | 7% to 10% a year (official) | Official aim: about USD 38,000 GDP per person by 2050 {fx:projection} (official target) | [@NTS-13; @NTS-14; @GT-11; @GEO-33; @GEO-35] {VN-direct\|Low} |
| Clean power | Plans met; cheap firm renewable power | Gas and coal stay long | The first green hydrogen plant had only perimeter works in 2026 {fx:signal} | [@FTG-27; @GEO-26] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Carbon pricing | Stays on power, steel and cement | Reaches farming and food | The pilot excludes agriculture; Denmark will tax livestock emissions from 2030 {fx:projection} | [@ECF-12; @ECF-13; @CLM-18; @VIS-04] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Disease | A fully protective swine fever vaccine | A 2019-scale die-off; bird flu adapting to mammals | No vaccine covers the new strains {fx:signal}; the 2030 plan aims for 30% fewer outbreaks {fx:projection} (official target) | [@HSC-31; @PAN-33; @PAN-10; @GEO-46; @PAN-01] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Political framing | Security and industry | Backlash or indifference | Security framing is rising in the EU, Japan and Korea {fx:signal}; China promotes microbial protein, mainly for feed {fx:signal}; some US states ban cultivated meat | [@VIS-15; @VIS-13; @SEC-04; @HSC-35; @HSC-36] {general\|Medium} |

**How Vietnam frames food security.** The food-security resolution to 2030 counts production volumes, such as 6.0 to 6.5 Mt of meat [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection} (official target). It asks people to eat less rice and more meat, milk, eggs and fish. It has no indicator for imports, feed or protein [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High}. The agriculture ministry's 2026 plan adds one task: to "step by step reduce import dependence" for livestock inputs [@SEC-02] {VN-direct|High}. That is the nearest hook for a protein-security measure ([[ch28-robust-moves]]). China and the EU treat new protein as a way to replace imported feed with domestic resources [@SEC-04; @SEC-03; @VIS-15] {general|Medium}.

### Why these two uncertainties are the axes

Scenario practice needs two uncertainties that matter a lot, that Vietnam does not control, and that are largely independent {general|Low}.
- **Import stress decides how much a domestic second source of protein is worth.** In 2023, 61% to 71% of the animal protein people ate rested on imports (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low}. Chronic stress raises the value of protein made at home. But only protein made from domestic inputs reduces the exposure ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]]). On today's prices, fermentation protein made in Vietnam costs more than imported plant protein ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]).
- **The shape of Asia's fermentation market decides whether a Vietnamese plant can sell, and to whom.** Chinese costs set the price to beat.
- **The two are largely independent.** Anchovy stocks and American harvests do not depend on Chinese industrial policy.

Vietnam's own choices, such as the food law and public money, are deliberately not axes. They are the moves tested in [[ch28-robust-moves]].

## 20.3 Shocks already on the record, and wildcards

Vietnam's protein supply has already absorbed large shocks:
- **2019 swine fever: a pork shock, not a national shortfall.** Pig output fell 14%, but poultry rose 17%, and home tofu use did not change [@GEO-14; @GEO-15; @PAN-03; @DIE-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **Consumers felt disease more than feed prices.** Pork prices rose 57% in 2020, but fell in 2022, the year world feed prices peaked [@SEC-08; @SEC-09] {VN-direct|High}.
- **2025 swine fever.** About 1.2 million pigs were culled, and people switched to chicken [@MAC-01; @MAC-12] {VN-direct|High}.
- **2026 Middle East conflict.** World urea prices rose 54% in a month, and feed prices 10% to 14% [@COST-30; @COST-33; @MAC-24] {VN-direct|Medium}.

> **Speculative.** Six wildcards would change the picture most:
> - a multi-year anchovy collapse that holds fishmeal above USD 2,500 per t (from 2027) {fx:wildcard};
> - simultaneous maize losses of over 10% in the four largest exporters: a 7% chance a year at 2 °C of warming, and 86% at 4 °C [@CLM-24] {general|High} {fx:projection};
> - a months-long closure of the South China Sea or Malacca Strait (2030 to 2050) [@GEO-40] {general|Medium} {fx:wildcard};
> - Chinese dumping of microbial protein from spare capacity (2028 to 2035) {fx:wildcard};
> - mutual recognition of novel-food approvals across Southeast Asia (2030 to 2035) {fx:wildcard};
> - firm renewable power at or below USD 20 per MWh in southern Vietnam (2040 to 2050) {fx:wildcard}.

## 20.5 What this means

- **Investors:** price two uncertainties: import stress and Chinese costs. Everything else is second-order before 2035.
- **Policy makers:** the near-certain trends all point to more imported protein behind the meat people eat. None of Vietnam's 2050 plans has a protein or feed number. Use the ministry's 2026 task to reduce import dependence as the hook for a protein-security measure, and count the origin of the inputs, not the place of processing.
- **Startups:** the trends that matter most for food are carbon from crop residues, clean power and heat-tolerant process design.
- **Manufacturers:** plan for recurring shocks, not a trend. Buying from several suppliers and flexible recipes are the proven defences.
- **Researchers and international bodies:** Vietnam has no published protein balance and no official number beyond 2030 for meat, feed or fish farming. A national protein balance is the most useful public good ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
