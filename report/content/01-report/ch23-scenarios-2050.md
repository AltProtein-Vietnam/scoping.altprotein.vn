# 23. Four worlds in 2050: scenarios for Vietnam's protein supply

**In brief.**
- Chapter 20 found two outside uncertainties that decide most for protein made in Vietnam: whether imported protein comes under chronic stress, and whether Asia's fermentation market stays open or falls under Chinese price dominance. Crossing them gives four worlds for 2050. The first starts high: in 2023 about 61% to 71% of the animal protein people ate rested on imports, directly or through feed.
- **Regional workshop** (cheap imports, open market): Vietnam specialises in contract fermentation and food ingredients.
- **Comfortable price-taker** (cheap imports, Chinese price dominance): cheap soy and cheap Chinese products leave little room for production at home.
- **Security build-out** (dear imports, open market): Vietnam's own carbohydrate and side streams become the base for more protein made at home.
- **Squeezed importer** (dear imports, Chinese price dominance): feed efficiency and local niches carry the load.
- Two public goods pay off in every world: a protein-quality laboratory and an open price series. Vietnam's own choices decide where it lands inside each world: the same outside conditions can produce a regional centre or a price-taker.

> **Scenarios, not forecasts.** The four worlds are internally consistent pictures. We built them with the two-axis, four-scenario design of the EU Joint Research Centre's food 2050 exercise, with signposts in the spirit of the "triggers" of the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) [@VIS-35; @VIS-36; @VIS-32] {general|High}. We assign no probabilities. Each scenario is {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. The soybean-meal ranges are indicative mappings to the model scenarios of [[ch22-protein-balance-2050]], not new model runs. Method: [[app-m2-futures-method]].

## 23.1 The two axes

**The first axis is stress on imported protein.** It covers the imported feed behind the meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish produced in Vietnam, and imported protein foods. Today's signposts track mainly feed inputs: soy, maize and fishmeal. The second axis is whether Asia's fermentation market stays open or falls under Chinese price dominance.

**Where the first axis starts.** In 2023 about 19% to 23% of Vietnam's protein supply was imported food, up from 11% to 13% in 2010 [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Counting the imported feed behind domestic meat, eggs and farmed fish, about 61% to 71% of the animal protein people ate rested on imports, directly or through feed [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low}. Both are our calculations from FAO food balance sheets, which measure supply, not intake ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).

So far, stress on imported feed has landed mainly on farm margins, not on consumer prices. In 2022, the year world feed prices peaked, pork prices fell 10.68%; after African swine fever (ASF) in 2020 they rose 57.23% [@SEC-09; @SEC-08] {VN-direct|High}. Chronic import stress in the 2040s could change that, but the evidence so far does not show it (our reading).

| Axis | Benign or open end | Stressed or dominated end | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stress on imported protein** | Soy, maize and fishmeal stay ample; price spikes are short. This is the published baseline to 2035 {fx:projection} | Fishmeal shortages recur; joint crop losses in exporting countries become more frequent in the 2040s; shipping lanes are disrupted | [@GEO-37; @HSC-28; @CLM-24; @CLM-27; @GEO-40] {general\|Medium} |
| **Asia's fermentation market** | Several countries build capacity, trade openly and rely on each other's approvals | Chinese scale and costs set the regional price of every fermentation product; others shrink to niches | [@HSC-11; @HSC-13; @HSC-14; @FTB-42; @ECF-29] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |

## 23.2 The four worlds

Codes such as T4 refer to the plays (business opportunities) of [[ch26-plays]]; [[front-how-to-read]] lists every code. The soybean-meal lines show the imported feed behind meat in each world.

### A. Regional workshop (benign imports, open market)

- **Picture in 2050.** Imported soy and maize stay cheap on average. Several Asian countries run fermentation plants and trade under shared or mutually recognised approvals. Vietnam has a working new-food route and open pilot plants. It hosts contract fermentation for regional brands and makes textured and fungal food ingredients. High-value precision-fermentation ingredients (from microbes engineered to make one specific protein) arrive after first-generation patents lapse in the mid-2030s [@FTB-21] {general|Low}.
- **Imported feed behind meat.** Soybean-meal need of about 7.0 to 10.4 Mt in 2050, depending almost entirely on feed efficiency {fx:estimate}.
- **Active hubs** (clusters of plants on one site). Ho Chi Minh City with Dong Nai; Tay Ninh (former Tay Ninh and Long An) sugar hub; the Mekong river belt ([[app-f5-targets-hubs]]).
- **Grows from:** 2035 Scenario 3, regulated regional hub ([[ch19-outlook-2035]]).
- **Signposts.** A reliance or mutual-recognition mechanism for novel foods in ASEAN (the Association of Southeast Asian Nations); Thai, Indian or Korean plants exporting across the region; fishmeal below USD 1,800 per t for a year; China's soybean imports below 100 Mt a year.

### B. Comfortable price-taker (benign imports, Chinese price dominance)

- **Picture in 2050.** Imported protein stays cheap, and Chinese scale sets the price of every fermentation product. Vietnam imports soybean meal and Chinese yeast, bacterial and textured proteins. Domestic alternative protein stays at *chay* (traditional vegetarian) food processing, tofu and a few feed-additive lines. Feed mills gain from cheap inputs, but the country stays exposed to any later shock.
- **Imported feed behind meat.** Soybean-meal need of about 10.4 to 11.7 Mt in 2050 (trend to high-income demand) {fx:estimate}.
- **Active hubs.** Ho Chi Minh City with Dong Nai, at small scale.
- **Grows from:** 2035 Scenario 1, import price-taker.
- **What alternative protein does to import dependence.** Protein made here from imported Chinese textured protein, isolates or gluten moves the dependence from American soy to China; it does not reduce it. China already supplied about 84% of the tonnage of these ingredients reaching Vietnam in 2025 (our calculation) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Only routes on domestic inputs reduce dependence, such as fermentation on cassava or sugar, and rice or peanut protein [@SEC-05; @SEC-14; @FS-24; @FS-01] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Signposts.** Imports of yeasts and protein preparations from China growing above 20% a year for three years; three or more Chinese single-cell or mycoprotein (fungal protein) plants above 20 kt a year by 2030; non-Chinese Asian pilot plants underused or closing.

### C. Security build-out (chronic stress, open market)

- **Picture in 2050.** Repeated fishmeal shortages, more frequent crop losses in exporting countries and shipping scares make imported protein dear and unreliable. Asian governments co-operate on approvals and invest in domestic protein. Vietnam makes more protein for people at home from its own carbohydrate and side streams. Fermentation sugar from cassava (T4), koji and fungal foods (T9), and mung bean and rice protein (T10) do well, using shared pilot capacity (T5). Koji is a food mould, as used for soy sauce.
- **Imported feed behind meat.** Soybean-meal need of about 7.0 to 8.8 Mt in 2050, if efficiency and alternative protein add up ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]) {fx:estimate}.
- **What adds security.** Routes on domestic inputs, such as fermentation on cassava or sugar and rice protein. Protein made from imported soy or Chinese ingredients shares the stress (our reading) [@SEC-05; @SEC-14] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Active hubs.** All six candidate hubs.
- **Grows from:** 2035 Scenario 2, feed-first domestic build-out, then Scenario 3.
- **Signposts.** Fishmeal above USD 2,500 per t for 12 months; two extreme El Niño events within a decade; joint maize or soybean losses above 10% in two major exporters in one year; war-risk insurance suspended on a main shipping lane.

### D. Squeezed importer (chronic stress, Chinese price dominance)

- **Picture in 2050.** Imported protein is dear and unreliable. Chinese producers, with scale and state backing, capture most of the regional market for microbial and fermented protein. Domestic production survives where local feedstock or freshness give an edge, or where the state treats capacity as strategic.
- **Imported feed behind meat.** Soybean-meal need of about 7.0 to 10.4 Mt in 2050, with feed efficiency carrying most of the reduction {fx:estimate}.
- **Active hubs.** The Mekong river belt; Tay Ninh selling sugar; Ho Chi Minh City with Dong Nai at small scale.
- **Grows from:** 2035 Scenario 1 under stress, or Scenario 2 with state support.
- **What alternative protein does to import dependence.** As in World B, protein made from Chinese ingredients adds to the dependence on China. Only domestic-input routes add security here, which is why local feedstock is the edge that survives (our reading) [@BUY-02; @SEC-04] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Signposts.** The chronic-stress signposts of Scenario C together with the Chinese-dominance signposts of Scenario B; an export-dumping episode for microbial protein.

Full descriptions: `scenarios_2050.csv`, which also keeps an indicative microbial feed-protein share for each world as context. All 16 signposts, with thresholds, where to watch and their September 2026 status: `signposts_2050.csv` and [[app-f2-drivers-signals]].

## 23.3 Where the signposts point in September 2026

The evidence points both ways on both axes, which is why we assign no probabilities.
- **Stress on imported protein: short-term stress, long-term baseline benign.** The 2026 season for Peruvian anchoveta, a small fish caught for fishmeal, landed about a quarter of a quota that was already the lowest in a decade. World fishmeal output fell 26% year on year in January to April 2026 [@HSC-29; @HSC-30] {general|Medium} {fx:signal}. The US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) gives over 90% odds of a very strong El Niño in winter 2026 to 2027 [@HSC-28] {general|High} {fx:projection} (published probability). The fishmeal-to-soy price ratio reached 6.2 in August 2026 [@GT-15] {general|High}. Yet the baseline to 2035 of the OECD-FAO Agricultural Outlook (by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development and FAO) expects ample soy [@GEO-37] {general|Medium} {fx:projection}.

{{kn:kn-anchoveta-2026}}

- **Fermentation market: Chinese scale growing, but not alone.** China added 11,000 t of yeast-protein capacity in 2025, and a 20,000 t mycoprotein plant is under construction [@HSC-13; @HSC-14] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {fx:signal}. China's flagship gas-fermentation plant halted [@FTG-06] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. Thailand, India and Korea now fund public pilot plants and food-tech plans [@FTB-42; @FTB-43; @FTB-17; @VIS-14] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {fx:signal}. US clearances went to Chinese and non-Chinese precision-fermentation firms alike in 2025 [@FTB-18; @FTB-19] {general|Medium}. China's 2024 food-security guidance promotes microbial protein, mainly for feed, with sugar from non-grain biomass, and aims for a diversified food supply "fully built" by 2035 [@SEC-04] {general|High} {fx:signal}. That adds weight to the Chinese-dominance end.
- **Disease: a signpost has tripped, but disease is not an axis.** In 2025 recombinant ASF strains made up 79% of typed samples in the north, 76% in the centre and 60% in the south. The Ministry of Agriculture and Environment (MAE) says the three licensed vaccines give no protection against them [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}. That crosses the signpost of a recombinant share above 50% ([[ch20-drivers-2050]], section 20.3). Yet the 2025 wave culled about 3.8% of the herd, against about 23% in 2019 (our calculation) [@MAC-01; @MAC-12; @PAN-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. So a second die-off on the 2019 scale (wildcard WC-03) stays possible in 2027 to 2035 {fx:wildcard}. It would move pork supply within a world rather than choose the world.

## 23.4 Wildcards that jump between worlds

A wildcard is a low-probability, high-impact event. Some would move Vietnam from one world to another within a year ([[app-f2-drivers-signals]], `wildcards.csv`).

| Wildcard | Moves towards | Horizon |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-year anchoveta collapse holding fishmeal above USD 2,500 per t {fx:wildcard} | C or D | 2027, recurring to 2050 |
| A closure of the South China Sea or Malacca Strait lasting months {fx:wildcard} | C or D | 2030 to 2050 |
| Chinese export dumping of microbial protein {fx:wildcard} | B or D | 2028 to 2035 |
| A sustained soy glut as China cuts soybean-meal use {fx:wildcard} | A or B | 2030 to 2040 |
| ASEAN mutual recognition of novel-food approvals {fx:wildcard} | A or C | 2030 to 2035 |
| A serious novel-food safety scandal in Asia {fx:wildcard} | B (food); feed less affected | any year |

## 23.5 Stress-testing the seven plays and four public goods

We scored each active play and public good from [[ch26-plays]] in each 2050 world: 2 where it thrives, 1 where it holds with conditions, 0 where it struggles. The scores are our judgement from the evidence in chapters 20 to 22 {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Plays are business opportunities (T codes); public goods are shared assets such as a laboratory or open data (P codes). Three feed plays (T2, T3 and T6) and two feed public goods (P2 and P3) were retired in version 0.8, because feed is now context only. Their scores stay in `play_robustness.csv` as a record.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Domestic textured plant protein | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | Holds everywhere |
| T4 Cassava starch to fermentation sugar hub | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | Holds everywhere |
| T5 Shared pilot and tolling fermentation | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | Holds everywhere |
| T7 High-value precision-fermentation ingredients | 2 | 0 | 1 | 0 | Bet |
| T8 Cultivated seafood research | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | Option |
| T9 Koji and fungal foods on side streams | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | Holds everywhere |
| T10 Mung bean and rice protein for drinks | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | Holds everywhere |
| P1 Proportionate new-food procedure | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | Holds everywhere |
| P4 Protein-quality laboratory | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | No-regret |
| P5 By-product atlas | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | Holds everywhere |
| P6 Open cost model and price series | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | No-regret |

Classes: no-regret (strong in all four worlds); holds everywhere (at least viable in all four; "robust" in the data file); bet (weak in at least one world but strong in another, so it needs signposts before scaling); option (weak in at least one world and strong in none, so cheap to hold as research). Tolling means renting time on someone else's fermenter. Reasons for each score: `play_robustness.csv`.

**Readings.**
- **Two public goods pay off in every world.** A protein-quality laboratory (P4) and an open cost model and price series (P6) score 2 in all four worlds. They serve imported and domestic products alike.
- **Most business plays hold everywhere, but none thrives everywhere.** Five of the seven plays (T1, T4, T5, T9 and T10) hold in every world with conditions. So do two public goods: the new-food procedure (P1) and the by-product atlas (P5). No play thrives in the Comfortable price-taker world.
- **One play is a bet and one is an option.** Both score 0 in the two worlds of Chinese price dominance (B and D). High-value precision fermentation (T7) needs an open regional market, so it should scale only when its signposts trip ([[ch28-robust-moves]]). Cultivated seafood research (T8) is cheap to hold as an option.
- **The fermentation market decides more than import stress.** Every active play at least holds in the two open-market worlds (A and C). The only weak scores come in the two worlds of Chinese price dominance (B and D). So readiness matters more than prediction: plants, sites and finance prepared in the late 2020s can scale quickly if an open-market world arrives, and cost little if it does not.

## 23.6 What this means

- **Investors:** underwrite plays that hold in all four worlds and thrive in two (T4, T5 and T9). Hold T7 as an option with named triggers: a new-food route, an ASEAN reliance mechanism or regional capacity outside China, and an anchor customer ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Policy makers:** the two no-regret items are both public goods, and both are cheap. Inside the same outside world, a new-food route and open pilot capacity decide whether Vietnam gets World A's outcomes or ends up a price-taker, as in World B. In the worlds of Chinese price dominance, protein made from imported Chinese ingredients adds no security; only routes on domestic inputs do. A measure of protein security should count where the inputs come from, not where the protein is processed.
- **Startups:** local feedstocks and freshness are the edges that survive Chinese price dominance.
- **Manufacturers:** in worlds C and D, imported protein is dear and unreliable. Food makers that can switch between imported and domestic protein ingredients, and buy from more than one origin, are better hedged. Feed mills face the same stress; formulation flexibility and multi-origin sourcing are their hedges.
- **Research and international bodies:** tracking the 16 signposts yearly, with the disease, wild-fish and policy signposts of [[ch20-drivers-2050]], and publishing them, is a low-cost service to every actor.
