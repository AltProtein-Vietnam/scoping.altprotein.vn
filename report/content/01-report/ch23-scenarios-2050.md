# 23. Four worlds in 2050: scenarios for Vietnam's protein supply

**In brief.**
- Two outside questions decide most for protein made in Vietnam: will imported protein come under chronic stress, and will Asia's fermentation market stay open or fall under Chinese price dominance? Crossing them gives four worlds for 2050.
- **Regional workshop** (cheap imports, open market): Vietnam specialises in contract fermentation and food ingredients.
- **Comfortable price-taker** (cheap imports, Chinese dominance): cheap soy and cheap Chinese products leave little room for production at home.
- **Security build-out** (dear imports, open market): Vietnam's own starch and side streams become the base for more protein made at home.
- **Squeezed importer** (dear imports, Chinese dominance): feed efficiency and local niches carry the load.
- Two public goods pay off in every world: a protein-quality laboratory and an open price series. Vietnam's own choices decide where it lands inside each world.

> **Scenarios, not forecasts.** The four worlds are consistent pictures, built with a standard two-axis design [@VIS-35; @VIS-36; @VIS-32] {general|High}. We assign no probabilities. Each scenario is {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. The soybean meal ranges map to the model scenarios of [[ch22-protein-balance-2050]].

## 23.1 The two axes

**The first axis is stress on imported protein:** mainly the imported feed behind the meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish produced in Vietnam, plus imported protein foods. It starts high. In 2023 about 61% to 71% of the animal protein people ate rested on imports, directly or through feed (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).

So far, feed stress has landed on farm margins, not consumer prices. Pork prices fell in 2022, when world feed prices peaked, but rose 57% in 2020 after swine fever [@SEC-09; @SEC-08] {VN-direct|High}. Chronic stress in the 2040s could change that.

**The second axis is whether Asia's fermentation market stays open** or falls under Chinese price dominance.

| Axis | Benign or open end | Stressed or dominated end | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stress on imported protein** | Soy, maize and fishmeal stay ample; spikes are short. This is the published baseline to 2035 {fx:projection} | Recurring fishmeal shortages, more frequent crop failures, disrupted shipping | [@GEO-37; @HSC-28; @CLM-24; @CLM-27; @GEO-40] {general\|Medium} |
| **Asia's fermentation market** | Several countries build, trade openly and accept each other's approvals | Chinese scale sets the price of every fermentation product | [@HSC-11; @HSC-13; @HSC-14; @FTB-42; @ECF-29] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |

## 23.2 The four worlds

Codes such as T4 refer to the plays in [[ch26-plays]].

### A. Regional workshop (cheap imports, open market)

- **Picture in 2050.** Imported soy and maize stay cheap. Several Asian countries run fermentation plants and accept each other's approvals. Vietnam has a new-food route and open pilot plants. It hosts contract fermentation for regional brands, and makes textured and fungal food ingredients. High-value fermented ingredients follow once early patents lapse in the mid-2030s [@FTB-21] {general|Low}.
- **Imported soybean meal behind meat:** about 7.0 to 10.4 Mt in 2050, depending mostly on feed efficiency {fx:estimate}.
- **Grows from:** the 2035 regulated regional hub ([[ch19-outlook-2035]]).
- **Signposts:** a Southeast Asian system to accept novel-food approvals; regional plants exporting across Asia; cheap fishmeal.

### B. Comfortable price-taker (cheap imports, Chinese dominance)

- **Picture in 2050.** Imported protein stays cheap, and Chinese scale sets every fermentation price. Vietnam imports soybean meal and Chinese microbial and textured proteins. Domestic alternative protein stays at chay (vegetarian) food and tofu. The country stays exposed to any later shock.
- **Imported soybean meal behind meat:** about 10.4 to 11.7 Mt in 2050 {fx:estimate}.
- **Grows from:** the 2035 import price-taker.
- **What alternative protein does to import dependence.** Protein made from Chinese ingredients moves the dependence from American soy to China; it does not reduce it. China already supplied about 84% of these ingredients in 2025 (our calculation) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Only domestic-input routes, such as fermentation on cassava or sugar and rice protein, reduce dependence [@SEC-05; @SEC-14; @FS-24; @FS-01] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Signposts:** fast-rising protein imports from China; several large Chinese microbial protein plants by 2030; regional pilot plants closing.

### C. Security build-out (dear imports, open market)

- **Picture in 2050.** Repeated fishmeal shortages, crop failures and shipping scares make imported protein dear and unreliable. Asian governments co-operate on approvals and invest in domestic protein. Vietnam makes more protein from its own starch and side streams: fermentation sugar from cassava (T4), koji and fungal foods (T9), and mung bean and rice protein (T10), using shared pilot plants (T5).
- **Imported soybean meal behind meat:** about 7.0 to 8.8 Mt in 2050 ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]) {fx:estimate}.
- **What adds security:** only domestic-input routes. Protein made from imported soy shares the stress (our reading) [@SEC-05; @SEC-14] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Grows from:** the 2035 feed-first build-out, then the regional hub.
- **Signposts:** fishmeal above USD 2,500 per t for a year; two extreme El Niño events in a decade; joint crop failures in major exporters; war-risk insurance suspended on a main shipping lane.

### D. Squeezed importer (dear imports, Chinese dominance)

- **Picture in 2050.** Imported protein is dear and unreliable, and Chinese producers capture most of the regional market for fermented protein. Domestic production survives where local raw material or freshness give an edge, or where the state treats it as strategic.
- **Imported soybean meal behind meat:** about 7.0 to 10.4 Mt in 2050, with feed efficiency doing most of the work {fx:estimate}.
- **Grows from:** the 2035 import price-taker under stress, or the feed-first build-out with state support.
- **What adds security:** as in World B, only domestic inputs (our reading) [@BUY-02; @SEC-04] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Signposts:** the stress signposts of World C plus the dominance signposts of World B; Chinese dumping of microbial protein.

## 23.3 Where the signposts point in September 2026

The evidence points both ways on both axes, which is why we assign no probabilities.
- **Imported protein: short-term stress, long-term calm.** The 2026 Peruvian anchovy season, the main source of fishmeal, landed about a quarter of a record-low quota. World fishmeal output fell 26% early in 2026 [@HSC-29; @HSC-30] {general|Medium} {fx:signal}. A very strong El Niño is likely in 2026 to 2027 [@HSC-28] {general|High} {fx:projection}. Yet the published outlook to 2035 expects ample soy [@GEO-37; @GT-15] {general|Medium} {fx:projection}.

{{kn:kn-anchoveta-2026}}

- **Fermentation market: Chinese scale is growing, but not alone.** China added large yeast and fungal protein plants [@HSC-13; @HSC-14] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {fx:signal}, though its flagship gas-fermentation plant halted [@FTG-06] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. Thailand, India and Korea fund public pilot plants [@FTB-42; @FTB-43; @FTB-17; @VIS-14] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {fx:signal}. US approvals in 2025 went to Chinese and non-Chinese firms alike [@FTB-18; @FTB-19] {general|Medium}. China's 2024 food-security guidance promotes microbial protein [@SEC-04] {general|High} {fx:signal}.
- **Disease: a signpost has tripped, but disease is not an axis.** New swine fever strains, which vaccines do not cover, were 60% to 79% of samples in 2025 [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}. A 2019-scale die-off stays possible in 2027 to 2035 [@MAC-01; @MAC-12; @PAN-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:wildcard}. It would move pork supply within a world rather than choose the world.

## 23.4 Wildcards that jump between worlds

| Wildcard | Moves towards | Horizon |
|---|---|---|

| A months-long closure of the South China Sea or Malacca Strait {fx:wildcard} | C or D | 2030 to 2050 |
| Chinese dumping of microbial protein {fx:wildcard} | B or D | 2028 to 2035 |
| A lasting soy glut as China uses less soybean meal {fx:wildcard} | A or B | 2030 to 2040 |

## 23.5 Stress-testing the seven plays and four public goods

We scored each play (a business opportunity) and public good (a shared asset) from [[ch26-plays]] in each world: 2 where it thrives, 1 where it holds with conditions, 0 where it struggles. The scores are our judgement {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.

| T4 Cassava starch to fermentation sugar hub | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | Holds everywhere |

Classes: **no-regret** means strong in all four worlds; **holds everywhere** means at least viable in all four; a **bet** is weak in one world but strong in another, so it needs signposts before scaling; an **option** is weak somewhere and strong nowhere, so it is cheap to hold as research. Tolling means renting time on someone else's fermenter.

**Readings.**
- **Two public goods pay off in every world:** a protein-quality laboratory (P4) and an open cost model and price series (P6). They serve imported and domestic products alike.
- **Most business plays hold everywhere, but none thrives everywhere.** No play thrives in the Comfortable price-taker world.
- **High-value precision fermentation (T7) is a bet,** needing an open regional market, so scale it only when its signposts trip ([[ch28-robust-moves]]). **Cultivated seafood (T8) is a cheap option.**
- **The fermentation market matters more than import stress.** Every play at least holds in the two open-market worlds. So readiness matters more than prediction: plants, sites and finance prepared in the late 2020s can scale fast if an open world arrives, and cost little if it does not.

## 23.6 What this means

- **Investors:** back plays that hold in all four worlds and thrive in two (T4, T5 and T9). Hold T7 as an option with named triggers: a new-food route, a regional approval system or capacity outside China, and an anchor customer ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Policy makers:** the two no-regret items are cheap public goods. Within the same outside world, a new-food route and open pilot plants decide whether Vietnam becomes a regional workshop or a price-taker. Measure protein security by where inputs come from, not where protein is processed.
- **Startups:** local raw materials and freshness are the edges that survive Chinese dominance.
- **Manufacturers:** in the dear-import worlds, food makers that can switch between imported and domestic ingredients, and buy from several origins, are better hedged.
- **Researchers and international bodies:** tracking and publishing the signposts each year is a cheap service to every actor ([[ch20-drivers-2050]]).
