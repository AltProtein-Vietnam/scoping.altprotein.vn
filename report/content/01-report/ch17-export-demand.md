# 17. Export demand: who abroad would buy Vietnamese alternative protein

**In brief.**
- Vietnam exports almost no protein ingredients: USD 0.52 million of textured protein in 2023, against China's USD 380 million.
- It does export wrapped foods at scale: USD 128 million of dumplings and similar foods in 2023. In 2025 it out-sold China and Thailand in the UK, the US and Korea. A plant-protein export would join this base, through food exporters.
- Demand abroad for finished plant-based meat is falling in the US and flat in Europe.
- Vietnam's clearest advantage is tariffs: zero duty in the EU, the UK and Japan on lines where Thai and Chinese goods pay 6% to 13%.
- The edge has conditions: rules on imported flour in wrappers, no meat fillings to the EU, a new US tariff, EU limits on meat names, and approvals abroad for any novel ingredient.

Five points stand out {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

- **Textured protein is untested as an export, and China sets its price.** China exported USD 380 million in 2024, at about USD 2.13 per kg [@EXP-40] {general|High} {dx:revealed}. The EU imported 3 t of textured protein from Vietnam in 2025, against USD 42.6 million from China [@XBA-28] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **Vietnam already sells wrapped foods at scale.** In 2025 the US imported USD 48 million of stuffed pasta from Vietnam, the EU USD 32 million and Japan USD 15.5 million [@XBA-28] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Most are filled with meat or seafood. The few Vietnam-made plant items on Western shelves are vegetable-led and low in protein [@XBA-26; @ORG-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.
- **Surimi is sold mostly to other countries' fish-cake makers,** cheaply [@SLS-01; @SLS-05] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The largest buyer may resist blends: a big Korean fish-cake maker is focusing on products with 80% or more fish [@SLS-11] {general|Medium} {dx:revealed}. So a fish-cake blend fits the home market better than the surimi trade (our reading).
- **Seafood chains may already sell protein ingredients to Europe.** Vinh Hoan, a pangasius exporter, sells collagen and gelatin [@EXP-39; @EXP-49; @EXP-32] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Certified seafood chains are the nearest model for selling a novel protein ingredient abroad (our inference) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **The firms that hold foreign buyers are food exporters, not plant-based brands.** Wrapped-food, cashew and seafood exporters already have the buyers, certificates and logistics [@EXP-19; @EXP-49; @XBA-20; @AIS-39] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

We found no Western plant-based brand making products in Vietnam.

## 17.3 Access: a tariff edge with conditions, and four barriers

**The tariff edge.** Vietnamese textured protein, food preparations, plant drinks and stuffed pasta enter the EU and the UK duty-free. Thai and Chinese goods pay 7% to 13% [@EXP-23; @EXP-24] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Japan admits Vietnamese textured protein free, against 6.6% for Chinese goods [@EXP-41] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Thailand and Indonesia have no EU agreement in force [@EXP-47; @EXP-48] {general|High}. {{kn:kn-eu-tariff-edge}}

**The edge is largest on wrapped foods.** In Japan, Vietnamese dumplings pay 3.9% against 21.3% for China and Thailand, a 17.4-point edge [@ORG-02; @ORG-05; @ORG-07; @XBA-28] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The UK and Canada charge Vietnam nothing [@ORG-03] {VN-direct|High}.

{{chart:chart-tariff-edge}}

**Barrier 1: the United States.** Since July 2026 the US has charged an extra 12.5% on Vietnamese goods, including prepared plant-based foods [@EXP-22] {VN-direct|High}. The US is the largest buyer of Vietnamese dumplings, so that growth is now exposed [@XBA-28] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. With US plant-based meat sales falling, do not send plant-based exports there first. {{kn:kn-us-301-tariff}}

**Barrier 2: labels.** Since August 2026 the EU restricts 32 meat words on plant-based foods, such as "chicken", "beef" and "steak" [@EXP-25] {general|Medium}. "Milk" and "cheese" are reserved for dairy [@EXP-26] {general|Medium}. Export packs need descriptive names, such as "soy protein strips".

**Barrier 3: approvals abroad.** Products made from soy, cashew, rice or mushrooms need no approval in the EU or Singapore. A new fungal strain, a precision-fermented protein or cultivated meat does [@EXP-46; @EXP-34; @EXP-45] {general|High}. Vietnam has no new-food route, so an exporter of a novel protein would apply abroad first ([[ch07-rules]]).

**Barrier 4: rules of origin.** The zero rate depends on the recipe [@ORG-01; @XBA-30] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

- **Wrapped foods to the EU.** Imported flour or starch may be at most 20% of the product's weight. Flour milled in Vietnam from imported wheat counts as imported; Vietnamese rice flour and cassava starch count as local. Imported textured soy is not limited [@ORG-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **Chay foods to the EU.** Imported textured soy may be up to 10% of the product. Soy isolate and gluten are not limited [@ORG-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **The UK, Japan and Canada.** The recipe does not bind under the Pacific trade agreement [@XBA-30; @ORG-06] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Meat.** Vietnam cannot ship meat products to the EU. So EU-bound dumplings are seafood or plant-filled, and a plant-protein filling is the practical substitute for pork [@ORG-10; @ORG-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

So origin rules give a domestic textured-soy plant no export customer (our reading) {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. The EU's deforestation rule probably does not reach plant foods made from imported soy protein; this should be confirmed [@EXP-27; @EXP-28] {general|Low} {dx:inferred}.

## 17.4 The diaspora channel: real, small, *chay*

About 2.3 million people of Vietnamese origin live in the US, and over 300,000 Vietnam-born people in Australia [@EXP-35; @EXP-36] {general|High}. US Asian grocers sell Vietnam-made chay spring rolls, vegan jerky and soups, none marketed on protein [@EXP-37] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Dry high-protein chay pieces under Vietnamese brands reach Asian grocers in Europe and beyond [@XBA-26; @XBA-27; @EXP-39] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

This channel could take higher-protein chay at similar prices, with frozen logistics in place. The gap is protein in frozen formats. It will not justify a novel-protein plant on its own {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

## 17.5 Feed rules for seafood: context, not a target

Seafood buyers set rules for the feed behind Vietnamese shrimp and pangasius. They are context here.

- **Standards.** A major seafood certification body's feed standard became mandatory in 2026, and from 2028 certified farms must use certified feed mills. Vietnam has the most certified mills of any country [@FBA-01; @EXP-30; @FBA-02; @FBA-03; @FBA-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **No reward for microbial protein.** No buyer rule we found requires or pays more for fish raised on yeast, fungal or bacterial protein [@FBA-01; @FBA-05] {general|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Exports are also shifting to China [@EXP-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **Soy and deforestation.** UK supermarkets have committed to deforestation-free soy [@EXP-33] {general|High} {dx:revealed}. The EU rule does not cover fish, so it reaches Vietnamese seafood only through retailer and certification rules (our reading) [@EXP-27] {general|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **Inactive yeast is traded both ways,** for food and feed together [@FBA-17; @FBA-19] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

## 17.6 Competitors and what would make Vietnam a preferred supplier

China dominates textured protein exports. Thailand has store-brand plant-based exporters but no EU agreement. Malaysia has a halal system and a lower US tariff [@EXP-40; @EXP-12; @EXP-13; @EXP-11; @EXP-52; @EXP-22; @EXP-47] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Japan's leading soy protein maker supplies from home [@XBA-18] {general|High} {dx:revealed}. CJ, which makes dumplings in Vietnam, plans to supply Europe from Hungary instead [@XBA-20; @XBA-23] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}.

What would make Vietnam a preferred supplier (our synthesis) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}:

- **For EU and UK buyers, through importers and brand owners:** zero tariffs, origin-compliant wrappers, frozen logistics and seafood-grade food-safety certificates. The product should be a plant-protein version of a wrapped food these buyers already take from Vietnam [@EXP-23; @EXP-24; @XBA-04; @XBA-11; @XBA-07; @XBA-20; @XBA-25] {general|Medium} {dx:inferred}.
- **For Japanese buyers of wrapped foods:** the 17.4-point edge on dumplings. We do not know who in Japan buys them, or whether they would take a plant-protein version.
- **For Japanese and Korean ingredient buyers (weak):** sales are tiny and local makers are expanding [@XBA-28; @XBA-18; @XBA-17; @EXP-41] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Pursue only with a named buyer for a specified fungal or fermented protein.
- **Against:** the US tariff, no domestic protein-ingredient base, no approvals abroad, reliance on imported soy and gluten, and CJ's move to Hungary.

## 17.7 What this means for the frontier

**For each reader.**
- **Startups:** the best first export is a plant-protein version of a dumpling, bun or roll a buyer already takes from Vietnam. Sell it through European importers of Asian foods, and avoid restricted meat and dairy words. Higher-protein chay for the diaspora comes second. Plant-based seafood is the weakest bet.
- **Investors:** export demand justifies certified co-packing capacity more than a new brand. Price in the US tariff, and budget for foreign approvals for any novel ingredient. Export rules give a domestic textured-soy plant (play T1, [[ch26-plays]]) no customer.
- **Manufacturers:** a European food-safety certificate, such as IFS Food or BRCGS, is the entry ticket [@XBA-04; @XBA-11] {general|High} {dx:revealed}. For EU wrapped foods, check the flour share of the wrapper and use Vietnamese rice flour or cassava starch where needed.
- **Researchers:** cost a compliant rice-flour or cassava-starch wrapper against wheat.
- **Policy makers:** protect the EU, UK and Japanese preferences. Publish a guide for wrapped-food exporters on the EU flour rule. Build a new-food route that mirrors EU and Singapore evidence rules, so one application serves several markets.
- **International organisations and funders:** instead of a buyer survey, ask three EU importers and two brand owners to quote for one vegetable and one plant-protein wrapped product. Fund a first EU or Singapore application for a Vietnamese fungal or microbial protein. Trade fairs where the industry ministry pays for a national stand are a cheap venue for such a quote test [@SLK-16] {VN-direct|High}.
