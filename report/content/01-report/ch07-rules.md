# 7. Rules of the game: routes to market, incentives and reforms

**In one paragraph.** For food, Vietnam has an open door but no gate: there is no novel-food category, so a new protein food is sold through the same self-declaration as tofu, yet the Food Safety Law also treats "new-technology food not yet permitted for circulation" as recallable, and no procedure exists to obtain that permission. The result is legal uncertainty, not prohibition. Precision-fermented proteins now probably count as "products of a GMO" under a January 2026 decree. For feed, the picture is clearer and more open: insects, duckweed, algae and several yeasts are already permitted livestock feed materials, and a trial route exists for new ones, though the aquafeed list has a drafting gap and using unlisted feed materials now draws fines. Labels are mostly settled already: a new goods-label decree (Decree 37/2026) keeps the old rules, voluntary standards set protein and vegetarian claims, and no rule reserves meat or milk words. Incentives favour biotechnology in general and recombinant proteins in particular, but the largest cash support is out of reach, and tariffs give no shelter: Chinese and ASEAN protein ingredients enter duty-free. The new Food Safety Law, due for first comments at the October 2026 National Assembly session and for a vote at the May 2027 session, is the one near-term window to write a proportionate new-food procedure; a clause needs to be in the draft revised between the two sessions, in practice by early 2027. This chapter is a summary; the full register is in [[app-s9-regulation]]. It is not legal advice.

## 7.1 Who owns what after the 2025 restructuring

On 1 March 2025 Vietnam cut its ministries to 14. The Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development and the Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment merged into the **Ministry of Agriculture and Environment (MAE)**; the Ministry of Science and Technology absorbed the Ministry of Information and Communications; the Ministry of Finance absorbed the Ministry of Planning and Investment [@GT-04; @GT-05; @GT-06] {VN-direct|High}. From 1 July 2025 the country also moved from 63 to 34 provinces and abolished the district tier ([[app-s10-admin-map]]) [@GT-01; @GT-02] {VN-direct|High}.

| Topic | Who decides now | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Processed food declarations, additives, health supplements, the Food Safety Law revision | Ministry of Health (Vietnam Food Administration) | [@REG-08] |
| Livestock feed (lists, trials) | MAE, Department of Livestock Production and Animal Health; many procedures now with provinces | [@REG-42; @REG-35; @REG-41] |
| Aquafeed (lists, trials) | MAE, Department of Fisheries and Fisheries Surveillance; trial recognition with provinces | [@REG-42; @REG-41] |
| GMO biosafety and genetic resources | MAE | [@REG-18; @REG-60] |
| High-tech lists, standards, sandbox law, IP | Ministry of Science and Technology (MOST) | [@GT-06; @REG-47; @REG-48] |
| Tax incentives | Ministry of Finance | [@REG-45] |

After the mergers, one ministry (MAE) holds feed, aquafeed, GMO biosafety, genetic resources and the carbon market, and one (MOST) holds standards, the sandbox, high-tech status and IP. That makes a coordinated approach to new proteins administratively simpler than before 2025.

## 7.2 Food: an open door with no gate

**The law.** The Law on Food Safety (55/2010, amended 2018) prohibits "using raw materials not of the type used for food to process food" (Art 5(1)) and lists "food that is a new-technology product not yet permitted for circulation" among foods subject to recall (Art 55(1)(c)). No implementing text defines a new-technology product or says how permission is obtained [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}.

**The procedure in force.** Market entry still runs on Decree 15/2018 [@REG-02; @REG-06] {VN-direct|High}:
- *Self-declaration* (Art 4 to 5) for processed pre-packaged food, additives and processing aids: the firm files its own product specification with a lab test report and may sell immediately. Ingredients made or imported only for a firm's own production are exempt.
- *Registration* (Art 6 to 8) for health supplements (21 working days at MOH), medical and special-diet foods, products for children under 36 months, and additives with new uses or not on the permitted list (7 working days).
- **No provision covers foods with no history of use or new ingredients.**

**The suspended replacement.** Decree 46/2026 (January 2026) and Resolution 66.13/2026, designed to replace much of Decree 15/2018 and tighten declarations and import inspection, caused port congestion and were suspended in February and April 2026 until a new Food Safety Law takes effect [@REG-05; @REG-06; @REG-08] {VN-direct|High}.

**The new law.** The Ministry of Health's draft revised Food Safety Law, as notified to the WTO as G/SPS/N/VNM/187 on 3 August 2026, had 71 articles in 8 chapters, with a stated approval target of October 2026 and entry into force on 1 January 2027 [@REG-12; @REG2-04] {VN-direct|Medium}. Under the 2026 legislative programme, the National Assembly will review it at the second 2026 session, as a press report of 22 September 2026 noted [@REG-14] {VN-direct|Medium}. On 24 September 2026 the National Assembly Standing Committee settled the timetable: first comments at the October 2026 session and consideration for passage at the May 2027 session, on a draft that now has 76 articles in 9 chapters [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. The law will therefore pass in two readings, and the 1 January 2027 start date in the WTO notice cannot hold (our reading). The window to add a new-food clause runs from the October session into early 2027, while the draft is revised between readings. The new draft adds risk-based classification, traceability for high-risk foods, duties for collective meal providers and conditions for online food delivery; the press report does not mention new foods [@APR-08] {VN-direct|Medium}.

{{kn:kn-food-law-window}}

Government press describes two product groups: foods that must register a declaration or declare an applied standard before sale, and foods needing no declaration, both under post-market surveillance. The drafters rejected making all pre-packaged foods register [@REG2-05; @REG2-06] {VN-direct|Medium}. **None of the summaries, submissions or stakeholder comments we read mentions novel food, new ingredients, history of use or a sandbox.** We could not read the full draft text [@REG2-04; @REG2-05; @REG2-06; @APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**What this means.** Ordinary plant-based and fermented foods from known ingredients face no pre-approval, now or (as described) under the new law. A genuinely new ingredient, such as a fungal biomass from a strain with no food history, can be self-declared, but an inspector could treat it as "not a food-type raw material" or a "new-technology product not yet permitted", and there is no way to get it cleared in advance. Investors should price this as enforcement-discretion risk. Founders should talk to the Vietnam Food Administration in writing before launch.

## 7.3 Precision fermentation and the GMO rules

Decree 43/2026 (effective 26 January 2026) overhauled the GMO rules [@REG-18; @REG-21] {VN-direct|High}:
- one MAE procedure now certifies a GMO for both food and feed use, with 67 working days of expert review, or 17 working days if at least five developed OECD or G20 countries already allow it [@REG-18; @REG-23] {VN-direct|Medium};
- gene-edited organisms without foreign DNA are self-assessed and notified;
- a new **certificate for GMOs used in closed production** (Art 37a), with conditions on donor and recipient safety, containment and waste treatment, filed on Forms 32 and 33 with MAE; the review time, fee and validity were not published in the texts we read [@REG2-11; @REG2-12] {VN-direct|High};
- "product of a GMO" now means a product made from a GMO that contains GM genetic material **"or protein from the modified gene"** [@REG2-11] {VN-direct|High}.

A purified recombinant protein is, literally, protein from a modified gene. **A precision-fermented protein is therefore probably a "product of a GMO" in Vietnam, even if no DNA remains.** GM labelling applies only when a GM ingredient exceeds 5% of total ingredients, and not where neither the modified gene nor its product can be detected (Decree 15/2018 Art 10; Joint Circular 45/2015 Art 1) [@REG-02; @LBL-10; @REG-18] {VN-direct|High}. A low-dose use such as lactoferrin at about 0.08% of an infant formula is far below the trigger (our calculation) [@LBL-25] {VN-direct|Medium}, so the label question bites on high-inclusion uses and on whether "non-GMO" claims stay lawful. Making it in Vietnam needs the closed-production certificate. No text says whether the purified protein also needs a GMO food-use certificate, which is written for organisms rather than products. This is the single most important open legal question for precision fermentation in Vietnam, and a joint MAE and Ministry of Health clarification would settle it ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

## 7.4 Labelling, claims, fortification and halal

- **The label decree changed; the rules did not.** Decree 37/2026 (23 January 2026) replaced the goods-label Decree 43/2017 and its amendment, Decree 111/2021 [@LBL-07; @LBL-08] {VN-direct|Medium}. Its text keeps the two rules that matter most for new proteins: a product name must not mislead about the nature or composition of the goods, and any ingredient used in the name must be quantified on the label (Art 11 and 13(4) of Decree 43/2017, carried over) [@LBL-06; @LBL-09] {VN-direct|Medium}. We could not read the new label chapter, so its article numbers are unknown.
- **Protein is on every label.** Since 1 January 2026 every non-exempt pre-packaged food must declare energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat and sodium. Circular 30/2026/TT-BYT replaced Circular 29/2023/TT-BYT on 10 July 2026 with the same core rules; foods made by small food businesses are exempt [@LBL-01; @LBL-02] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Claims are set by voluntary standards.** TCVN 14429:2025 (from Codex) allows "source of protein" at 5 g and "high protein" (*cao đạm*) at 10 g per 100 g, or 2.5 g and 5 g per 100 kcal (our calculation from the 50 g reference value in TCVN 7088:2025) [@LBL-03; @LBL-04] {VN-direct|High}. A content claim needs no registration and no advertising approval [@REG-02; @LBL-19] {VN-direct|High}. TCVN 13625:2023 defines vegetarian and vegan claims, but its "vegetarian" allows egg, milk and honey and it says nothing on alliums, so it is broader than Buddhist *chay* (vegetarian food) [@LBL-15] {VN-direct|High}.
- **No reserved names.** No rule reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese) for animal products; a name must only not mislead, and makers already qualify meat words, as in *Heo hầm chay* (chay stewed pork) [@FORM-01]. The new dairy regulation QCVN 28:2026/BCT covers dairy only, and TCVN 12443:2018 sets a floor of 2.0 g of protein per 100 ml for the name *sữa đậu nành* (soy milk) [@LBL-16; @LBL-17; @FTR-06; @BUY-05] {VN-direct|Medium}. No standard sets a minimum meat content for processed meats (TCVN 7049, 7050 and 7048:2020), so a product with a plant extender keeps its meat name, with the named meat's share printed [@LBL-11; @LBL-12; @LBL-13] {VN-direct|High}.
- **No allergen list.** Vietnam has no mandatory allergen list; labels need only generic "warning information" [@REG2-24; @REG2-25] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Fortification reaches no protein food.** Decree 09/2016 makes iodised salt, iron and zinc in wheat flour used in processing, and vitamin A in cooking oil, mandatory; tofu, textured soy and soy milk are not vehicles [@NQR-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. A 2025 draft amendment concerned iodised salt in fish sauce, and we found no amending decree [@NQR-03] {VN-direct|Low}. Processed pre-packaged food self-declares (Decree 15/2018 Art 4) unless it falls under Art 6 (health supplements, medical foods, special-diet foods and products for children up to 36 months), which must register; that a fortified food follows the same rule is our inference [@NQR-04] {VN-direct|High}.
- A voluntary national halal framework has been in force since 1 June 2026 (Decree 127/2026); it is silent on fermentation-derived or GM-derived ingredients [@REG-27] {VN-direct|High}.

## 7.5 Feed: more open, with two traps

**Livestock and poultry feed.** The permitted raw-material list (Circular 21/2019 Appendix VI as amended by Circular 31/2022) already includes **insects**, aquatic plants including **duckweed (*bèo*) and algae**, and since February 2023 **inactive yeasts** (Torula or Candida yeast, yeast grown on molasses, and by-products of amino-acid production) [@REG-32; @REG-33] {VN-direct|High}. These need no feeding trial, only a certified factory, a conformity declaration against the feed contaminant standard QCVN 01-190:2020 and a label [@REG-36; @REG-29; @REG-32] {VN-direct|High}. We found no amendment to the list since the yeast entries were added in February 2023, and it has no entry for bacterial single-cell protein or filamentous fungal biomass [@REG-33; @REG2-07; @REG2-09] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**A new material needs a trial.** Feed containing a "new substance" must be trialled under the Law on Animal Husbandry (Art 37) and Circular 94/2025. The livestock department approves the trial protocol (15 plus 5 working days) and recognises the result (15 plus 5 working days); the recognised result is the basis for adding the material to the list, which is updated annually [@REG-29; @REG-34; @REG-32] {VN-direct|High}. Agency time is about 40 working days; the trial itself and the wait for the list update come on top. **Our estimate of the total is 6 to 18 months; that is an estimate, not a documented timeline.** Results of recognised state research projects are exempt from a separate trial (Art 37(2)) [@REG-29] {VN-direct|High}.

> **Correction.** Earlier drafts attributed feed trials to Article 11 of Decree 13/2020 and quoted a 12 to 18 month timeline. Article 11 covers factory inspections; trials come from the Law on Animal Husbandry and Circular 94/2025, and no text gives a total duration [@REG-30; @REG-34].

**Trap 1: the aquafeed list.** Aquafeed has its own permitted list (Circular 16/2026 Appendix II) and its own trial rule: under the Law on Fisheries (Art 35(1)), aquafeed containing any raw material "not on the list" must be trialled [@REG2-01; @REG2-02] {VN-direct|High}. **The appendix has no raw-material section at all.** It lists chemicals, bioactives, 30 microorganisms (including *Candida utilis*, *Pichia farinosa*, *Saccharomyces* and *Aspergillus oryzae*), vitamins and enzymes, but not insect meal, single-cell protein, microalgae, duckweed or fermented soybean meal [@REG2-01] {VN-direct|High}. Read literally, the law catches every raw material, including fishmeal; practice evidently differs. A startup cannot tell from the texts whether its ingredient needs an aquafeed trial. A written opinion from the Department of Fisheries is the cheapest fix.

**Trap 2: fines.** Since 5 August 2026, using a feed raw material not on the permitted list costs VND 10 to 20 million per material, plus a one-to-three-month suspension of feed production and forced repurposing of the material, or destruction if that is impossible (Decree 211/2026) [@REG2-10] {VN-direct|High}. Running the trial first is now clearly worth its cost.

**Provinces decide more.** Since July 2025 and January 2026, provincial chairs handle feed-factory certificates, supplement-feed reviews (15 working days), import licences for unlisted feed (10 working days), import inspection, and recognition of aquafeed trial results [@REG-35; @REG-41] {VN-direct|High}. The choice of province now affects time to market.

## 7.7 Incentives

- **Corporate income tax.** The Law on Corporate Income Tax 67/2025 (effective 1 October 2025) gives 10% for 15 years plus four years' exemption and nine years at 50% to high-tech enterprises and new projects applying listed high technology; agricultural and aquatic processing gets preferential rates [@REG-45] {VN-direct|Medium}. The guiding Decree 320/2025 adds two tests that matter here: agricultural processing exemptions need agricultural or aquatic raw materials of at least 30% of production cost (a cassava-based fermentation plant probably passes; one on imported refined sugar may not, our interpretation), and products made with technology applied for the first time in Vietnam get three years of exemption once the novelty is confirmed [@REG2-18] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **The high-technology list.** Decision 23/2026 (effective 1 July 2026) lists synthetic biology, new-generation microbial technology, tissue and cell technology in agriculture, and **"technology for producing recombinant proteins, enzymes and monoclonal antibodies"** as priority high technologies, and recombinant proteins and microbial products among encouraged products. There is no item for alternative protein, food or feed as such [@REG-47] {VN-direct|High}.
- **The Investment Support Fund is out of reach.** Decree 182/2024 requires at least VND 12,000 billion of capital or VND 20,000 billion of annual revenue for high-tech product projects, and VND 3,000 billion for R&D centres [@REG-46] {VN-direct|High}. No alternative-protein project in Vietnam is near these sizes.
- **VAT works against new feed proteins.** Feed is "not subject to VAT" since 1 July 2025, so a feed-protein producer cannot reclaim VAT paid on equipment, power and inputs; a food-ingredient producer can [@COST-24] {VN-direct|High}. We estimate this adds about 5% to 10% to taxed input costs (see [[ch09-economics]]).
- **Tariffs give no shelter.** Soybean meal and feed maize have been duty-free since March 2025; fishmeal and soybeans were already at 0% [@REG2-21; @REG2-23] {VN-direct|Medium}. Food protein ingredients carry small most-favoured-nation (MFN) duties: wheat gluten 10%, full-fat soy flour 8%, protein concentrates and textured protein 5%, protein substances 3%, active yeast 10%, inactive yeast and dead single-cell organisms 7% [@REG2-20; @REG2-23; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}. These rates apply only to origins without a trade agreement, chiefly the United States. Chinese and ASEAN product pays 0% on all of these lines (HS 1109, 1208.10, 2106.10, 3504, 2102.10, 2102.20 and 2304) under ACFTA and RCEP in 2025 to 2027 and at 0% under ATIGA, and Korean product pays 0% on the protein lines under AKFTA, with a certificate of origin [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. So a domestic textured-protein, isolate or single-cell-protein maker has no tariff edge over Chinese product. {{kn:kn-fta-duty-china}}

## 7.8 Sandboxes

The Law on Science, Technology and Innovation (93/2025, effective 1 October 2025) allows controlled testing of technologies and products "not yet regulated or differing from current law" for up to three years, extendable once, with liability exemptions for regulators and implementers who follow the rules [@REG-48; @REG2-13] {VN-direct|High}. **No national implementing procedure exists yet**; the four decrees guiding the law do not set one up [@REG2-14; @REG2-15] {VN-direct|High}. Hanoi does have one: Resolution 29/2025 sets the procedure under the Capital Law, and Decision 6150 (December 2025) lists biotechnology and high-tech agriculture as priority fields, approved by the Hanoi People's Committee [@REG2-16; @REG2-17] {VN-direct|High}. Hai Phong (Decision 248/2025) and Da Nang (Resolution 20/2026) have issued controlled-testing rules too [@REG2-16] {VN-direct|Low}. The Hanoi sandbox is the most concrete route we found for a supervised cultivated-meat or precision-fermentation pilot, though whether a city sandbox can set aside national food law has not been tested.

## 7.9 Strategies: biotechnology yes, alternative protein by name no

- Resolution 36-NQ/TW (2023) targets a biotechnology industry worth 7% of GDP by 2030 and replacement of at least 50% of imported biotech products [@REG-51] {VN-direct|High}.
- Resolution 57-NQ/TW (December 2024) targets R&D spending of 2% of GDP by 2030 and pilot mechanisms for firms to test new technology under state supervision [@REG-50] {VN-direct|High}.
- The livestock strategy (Decision 1520/2020) targets 30 to 32 Mt of industrial feed by 2030 and encourages biotech feed materials and by-products such as brewery waste and cassava residue to replace imports [@REG-54] {VN-direct|High}.
- The food-systems transformation plan (Decision 300/2023) targets a 10% cut in food-system greenhouse gas emissions against 2020 [@REG-55] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**None of these names alternative, novel or microbial protein** [@REG-51; @REG-54; @REG-55] {VN-direct|High}. The policy language supports the category without committing to it.

## 7.10 Genetic resources and strains

Commercial use of Vietnamese genetic resources, including microorganisms isolated from Vietnamese fermented foods or cassava waste, needs an access licence (a council review of up to 90 days, then a decision within 15 days) and benefit sharing of at least 1% of annual product revenue or 2% of the value of an IP transfer (Decree 59/2017) [@REG-60] {VN-direct|High}. Any "indigenous strain" business model must budget for this.

## 7.11 How neighbours built their routes

- **Singapore:** pre-market approval for foods without 20 years of use as food; supplying an unapproved novel food has been an offence since 28 November 2025 [@REG-63] {VN-adjacent|High}. Singapore approval gives no legal status in Vietnam.
- **Thailand:** a novel-food notification (2016) with safety assessments done by FDA-recognised assessment units, separate forms for cultivated meat and "non-novel" foods, and a published approved list [@REG2-26] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **South Korea:** amended an existing instrument (temporary standards for new food ingredients) in about nine months (May 2023 to February 2024) to open applications for cell-cultured ingredients in February 2024 [@RGN-19; @RGN-21; @RGN-34] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **Vietnam's own model:** the 2026 GMO decree already contains a reliance lane (17 working days if five developed countries approve). A similar lane for new foods approved by reference regulators would not be a foreign import.

See [[ch03-regional]] and [[app-s3-regional]].

## 7.12 What this means

- **The binding constraint depends on the pathway.** Food from known ingredients: none. New food ingredients: legal uncertainty, with no positive clearance. Precision fermentation: GMO product status. Cultivated: no route. Feed: relatively open for listed materials, with the aquafeed ambiguity and new fines for unlisted ones.
- **The window is now.** A one-line delegation clause in the new Food Safety Law ("the Government shall regulate new foods and new food ingredients"), with the detail in the implementing decree, is cheap and only possible while the draft is before the National Assembly: from first comments in October 2026 to the vote considered in May 2027, so in practice until early 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Without it, the next chance may be years away ([[ch27-policy-options]]).
- **Investors:** ask for written regulator opinions (Vietnam Food Administration; Department of Fisheries; MAE on GMO status) as diligence items.
- **Startups:** choose products whose inputs are already listed (feed) or have food history (food); choose your province deliberately; budget for benefit sharing if you commercialise a Vietnamese strain.
- **International bodies:** technical help for a Thai-style assessment-unit model and a reliance lane fits the 2026 to 2027 drafting window.
