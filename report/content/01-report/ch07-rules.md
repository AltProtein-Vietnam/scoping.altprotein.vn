# 7. Rules of the game: routes to market, incentives and reforms

**In brief.**
- Vietnam has an open door for new protein foods but no gate. There is no novel-food category, so a new protein food is sold through the same self-declaration as tofu. But the law also treats unpermitted "new-technology food" as recallable, and no procedure exists to get permission. The result is legal uncertainty, not a ban.
- Proteins made by engineered microbes probably count as products of a genetically modified organism (GMO) under a January 2026 decree.
- Labels are mostly settled. No rule reserves meat or milk words.
- Incentives favour biotechnology, but tariffs give no shelter: Chinese and ASEAN protein ingredients enter duty-free.
- The new Food Safety Law is the one near-term window for a new-food procedure. A clause has to be in the draft by early 2027, before the vote considered for May 2027.

This chapter is not legal advice.

## 7.1 Who owns what after the 2025 restructuring

In March 2025 Vietnam merged its ministries [@GT-04; @GT-05; @GT-06] {VN-direct|High}. In July 2025 it cut its provinces from 63 to 34 [@GT-01; @GT-02] {VN-direct|High}.

| Topic | Who decides now | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Processed food, additives, supplements, the Food Safety Law | Ministry of Health (MOH), through the Vietnam Food Administration | [@REG-08] |
| Animal and fish feed | Ministry of Agriculture and Environment (MAE), with provinces | [@REG-42; @REG-35; @REG-41] |
| GMO safety and genetic resources | MAE; provinces issue genetic resource licences | [@REG-18; @REG-60; @SLF-02] |
| High-tech lists, standards, sandboxes, intellectual property | Ministry of Science and Technology (MOST) | [@GT-06; @REG-47; @REG-48] |
| Tax incentives | Ministry of Finance (MOF) | [@REG-45] |

After the mergers, fewer ministries hold the relevant powers. That makes a joined-up approach to new proteins simpler than before.

## 7.2 Food: an open door with no gate

**The law.** The Food Safety Law of 2010 bans processing food from "raw materials not of the type used for food". It also lists "new-technology food not yet permitted for circulation" among foods to recall. No rule says what a new-technology product is or how to get permission [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}.

**The procedure in force.** Market entry runs on Decree 15/2018 [@REG-02; @REG-06] {VN-direct|High}:
- *Self-declaration* for processed packaged food and additives: the firm files its own specification with a lab test and may sell at once.
- *Registration* for health supplements, medical foods and products for young children.
- **Nothing covers foods with no history of use, or new ingredients.**

A stricter replacement decree caused port congestion in early 2026 and was suspended until the new law takes effect [@REG-05; @REG-06; @REG-08] {VN-direct|High}.

**The new law: a window to early 2027.** The National Assembly gave the draft Food Safety Law its first reading in October 2026 and will consider passing it in May 2027 [@REG-12; @REG2-04; @REG-14; @APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. The draft adds risk-based classification and traceability. **None of the summaries or comments we read mentions novel food, new ingredients or a sandbox.** We could not read the full draft [@APR-08; @REG2-04; @REG2-05; @REG2-06] {VN-direct|Medium}.

{{kn:kn-food-law-window}}

**What this means.** Plant-based and fermented foods from known ingredients need no approval, now or under the new law as described. A genuinely new ingredient, such as fungal protein from a strain with no food history, can be self-declared. But an inspector could treat it as "not a food-type raw material", and there is no way to get it cleared in advance. Investors should price this as enforcement risk. Founders should ask the Vietnam Food Administration for a written view before launch.

## 7.3 Precision fermentation and the GMO rules

Precision fermentation uses engineered microbes to make one specific protein. A January 2026 decree overhauled the GMO rules [@REG-18; @REG-21] {VN-direct|High}:
- one MAE procedure now certifies a GMO for food and feed, in 67 working days, or 17 if five developed countries already allow it [@REG-18; @REG-23] {VN-direct|Medium};
- gene-edited organisms without foreign DNA are self-assessed;
- a new certificate covers GMOs used in closed production. Its review time and fee are not published [@REG2-11; @REG2-12] {VN-direct|High};
- "product of a GMO" now includes **"protein from the modified gene"** [@REG2-11] {VN-direct|High}.

**So a precision-fermented protein is probably a GMO product in Vietnam, even if no DNA remains.**

GM labels apply only above 5% of the ingredients [@REG-02; @LBL-10; @REG-18] {VN-direct|High}. A low-dose use, such as lactoferrin in infant formula at about 0.08%, falls far below that (our calculation) [@LBL-25] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Making such a protein in Vietnam needs the closed-production certificate. No text says whether the purified protein also needs a GMO food certificate. This is the most important open legal question for precision fermentation in Vietnam. A joint note from MAE and the Ministry of Health would settle it ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

## 7.4 Labelling, claims, fortification and halal

- **The label rules did not change.** A 2026 decree replaced the label decree but kept the two rules that matter: a name must not mislead, and any ingredient named must be quantified [@LBL-07; @LBL-08; @LBL-06; @LBL-09] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Protein is on every label.** Since January 2026 packaged foods must declare protein, energy, carbohydrate, fat and sodium [@LBL-01; @LBL-02] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Claims follow voluntary standards.** "Source of protein" needs 5 g and "high protein" (*cao đạm*) 10 g per 100 g [@LBL-03; @LBL-04] {VN-direct|High}. Such a claim needs no registration [@REG-02; @LBL-19] {VN-direct|High}. The national "vegetarian" standard allows egg, milk and honey, so it is broader than Buddhist *chay* [@LBL-15] {VN-direct|High}.
- **No reserved names.** No rule reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese) for animal products. A name must only not mislead, as in *Heo hầm chay* (chay stewed pork) [@FORM-01; @LBL-16; @LBL-17; @FTR-06; @BUY-05] {VN-direct|Medium}. No standard sets a minimum meat content for processed meats, so a product with a plant extender keeps its meat name [@LBL-11; @LBL-12; @LBL-13] {VN-direct|High}.
- **No allergen list.** Labels need only general warnings [@REG2-24; @REG2-25] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Fortification reaches no protein food.** Mandatory fortification covers salt, wheat flour and cooking oil, not tofu or soy milk [@NQR-02; @NQR-03; @NQR-04] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Halal.** A voluntary national halal framework started in June 2026. It says nothing on fermented or GM ingredients [@REG-27] {VN-direct|High}.

## 7.5 Feed rules: context only

Feed rules matter here only as context.

- **Livestock feed is more open than food.** Duckweed, algae and inactive yeasts are already permitted, with no feeding trial [@REG-32; @REG-33; @REG-36; @REG-29] {VN-direct|High}.
- **A new feed material needs a trial**, plus about 40 working days of agency time [@REG-29; @REG-34; @REG-32] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Fish feed rules have a gap.** Any unlisted raw material needs a trial, and the list has no section for microbial protein, algae or duckweed [@REG2-01; @REG2-02] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Unlisted feed materials are fined** VND 10 to 20 million each since August 2026 [@REG2-10] {VN-direct|High}.

## 7.7 Incentives: biotechnology favoured, but no tariff shelter

- **Corporate income tax.** High-tech firms and projects pay 10% for 15 years, with four years' exemption and nine at half rate [@REG-45] {VN-direct|Medium}. Farm-processing exemptions need local farm inputs worth at least 30% of production cost. A cassava-based plant probably passes; one on imported sugar may not (our interpretation) [@REG2-18] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **The high-tech list.** The 2026 list names "technology for producing recombinant proteins", synthetic biology and microbial technology. It has no item for alternative protein as such [@REG-47] {VN-direct|High}.
- **The national investment support fund is out of reach.** It needs projects of at least VND 12,000 billion (about USD 460 million) [@REG-46] {VN-direct|High}. No alternative-protein project is near that.
- **Value added tax (VAT) favours food makers.** A food-ingredient maker can reclaim VAT on inputs; a feed maker cannot [@COST-24] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Tariffs give no shelter.** Duties on protein ingredients are small (3% to 10%) and apply mainly to US product. Chinese, Southeast Asian and Korean product enters duty-free under regional trade agreements [@REG2-20; @REG2-23; @FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. So a domestic maker of textured protein or isolate has no tariff edge over Chinese product.

## 7.8 Sandboxes: a law, but only city procedures

A 2025 science law allows controlled testing of products "not yet regulated", for up to three years, extendable once [@REG-48; @REG2-13] {VN-direct|High}. **No national procedure exists yet** [@REG2-14; @REG2-15] {VN-direct|High}.

Hanoi has one, with biotechnology and high-tech farming as priorities. Hai Phong and Da Nang have issued rules too [@REG2-16; @REG2-17] {VN-direct|Medium}. The Hanoi sandbox is the most concrete route for a supervised pilot of cultivated meat or precision fermentation. Whether a city sandbox can set aside national food law has not been tested.

## 7.9 Strategies: biotechnology yes, alternative protein by name no

- The 2023 biotechnology resolution targets an industry worth 7% of GDP by 2030 [@REG-51] {VN-direct|High}.
- The 2024 science resolution targets research spending of 2% of GDP by 2030 [@REG-50] {VN-direct|High}.
- The livestock strategy encourages feed from by-products to replace imports [@REG-54] {VN-direct|High}.
- The food-systems plan targets a 10% cut in food-system emissions [@REG-55] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**None names alternative, novel or microbial protein** [@REG-51; @REG-54; @REG-55] {VN-direct|High}. Policy supports the field without committing to it.

## 7.10 Genetic resources and strains: budget for benefit sharing

Commercial use of Vietnamese microbes, such as strains from fermented foods, needs an access licence. Since January 2026 the province issues it, in about 19 working days (our calculation) [@SLF-02; @REG-60] {VN-direct|High}. Benefit sharing is unchanged: at least 1% of yearly product revenue [@REG-60; @SLF-02] {VN-direct|High}. Any business built on a Vietnamese strain has to budget for it.

## 7.11 How neighbours built their routes

- **Singapore** requires approval for foods without 20 years of use [@REG-63] {VN-adjacent|High}. A Singapore approval gives no legal status in Vietnam.
- **Thailand** uses recognised assessors and publishes an approved list [@REG2-26] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **South Korea** amended an existing rule in about nine months to accept cell-cultured ingredients [@RGN-19; @RGN-21; @RGN-34] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **Vietnam's own model:** the 2026 GMO decree already relies on approvals in five developed countries. A similar route for new foods would build on Vietnamese practice ([[ch03-regional]]).

## 7.12 What this means

- **The barrier depends on the product.** Food from known ingredients: none. New food ingredients: legal uncertainty. Precision fermentation: GMO status. Cultivated meat: no route.
- **The window is now.** A one-line clause in the new Food Safety Law is cheap: "the Government shall regulate new foods and new food ingredients", with the detail in the decree. It is only possible while the draft is before the National Assembly, in practice until early 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Without it, the next chance may be years away ([[ch27-policy-options]]).
- **Investors:** ask for written regulator opinions as part of due diligence.
- **Startups:** choose ingredients with a history of food use, choose your city if you need a sandbox, and budget for benefit sharing if you use a Vietnamese strain.
- **International bodies:** help with an assessor model and a reliance route fits the 2026 to 2027 drafting window.
