# 7. Rules of the game: routes to market, incentives and reforms

**In brief.**
- Vietnam has an open door for new protein foods but no gate. There is no novel-food category, so a new protein food is sold through the same self-declaration as tofu. But the Food Safety Law also treats "new-technology food not yet permitted for circulation" as recallable, and no procedure exists to get that permission. The result is legal uncertainty, not prohibition.
- Precision-fermented proteins now probably count as "products of a GMO" (genetically modified organism) under a January 2026 decree.
- Labels are mostly settled already. A new goods-label decree (Decree 37/2026) keeps the old rules, voluntary standards set protein and vegetarian claims, and no rule reserves meat or milk words.
- Incentives favour biotechnology in general and recombinant proteins in particular, but the largest cash support is out of reach. Tariffs give no shelter: Chinese and ASEAN protein ingredients enter duty-free.
- The new Food Safety Law is the one near-term window to write a proportionate new-food procedure. It is due for first comments at the October 2026 National Assembly session and for a vote at the May 2027 session. A clause needs to be in the draft revised between the two sessions, in practice by early 2027.

This chapter is a summary; the full register is in [[app-s9-regulation]]. Feed rules appear only as context (section 7.5). It is not legal advice.

## 7.1 Who owns what after the 2025 restructuring

On 1 March 2025 Vietnam cut its ministries to 14 [@GT-04; @GT-05; @GT-06] {VN-direct|High}:
- the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development and the Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment merged into the **Ministry of Agriculture and Environment (MAE)**;
- the Ministry of Science and Technology (MOST) absorbed the Ministry of Information and Communications;
- the Ministry of Finance (MOF) absorbed the Ministry of Planning and Investment.

From 1 July 2025 the country also moved from 63 to 34 provinces and abolished the district tier ([[app-s10-admin-map]]) [@GT-01; @GT-02] {VN-direct|High}.

| Topic | Who decides now | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Processed food declarations, additives, health supplements, the Food Safety Law revision | Ministry of Health (MOH), through the Vietnam Food Administration | [@REG-08] |
| Livestock feed (lists, trials) | MAE, Department of Livestock Production and Animal Health; many procedures now with provinces | [@REG-42; @REG-35; @REG-41] |
| Aquafeed (lists, trials) | MAE, Department of Fisheries and Fisheries Surveillance; trial recognition with provinces | [@REG-42; @REG-41] |
| GMO biosafety and genetic resources | MAE | [@REG-18; @REG-60] |
| High-tech lists, standards, sandbox law, intellectual property (IP) | MOST | [@GT-06; @REG-47; @REG-48] |
| Tax incentives | MOF | [@REG-45] |

After the mergers, one ministry (MAE) holds feed, aquafeed, GMO biosafety, genetic resources and the carbon market. Another (MOST) holds standards, the sandbox, high-tech status and IP. That makes a coordinated approach to new proteins administratively simpler than before 2025.

## 7.2 Food: an open door with no gate

**The law.** The Law on Food Safety (55/2010, amended 2018) prohibits "using raw materials not of the type used for food to process food" (Art 5(1)). It also lists "food that is a new-technology product not yet permitted for circulation" among foods subject to recall (Art 55(1)(c)). No implementing text defines a new-technology product or says how permission is obtained [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}.

**The procedure in force.** Market entry still runs on Decree 15/2018 [@REG-02; @REG-06] {VN-direct|High}:
- *Self-declaration* (Art 4 to 5) for processed pre-packaged food, additives and processing aids: the firm files its own product specification with a lab test report and may sell immediately. Ingredients made or imported only for a firm's own production are exempt.
- *Registration* (Art 6 to 8) for health supplements (21 working days at MOH), medical and special-diet foods, products for children under 36 months, and additives with new uses or not on the permitted list (7 working days).
- **No provision covers foods with no history of use or new ingredients.**

**The suspended replacement.** Decree 46/2026 (January 2026) and Resolution 66.13/2026 were designed to replace much of Decree 15/2018 and tighten declarations and import inspection. They caused port congestion and were suspended in February and April 2026 until a new Food Safety Law takes effect [@REG-05; @REG-06; @REG-08] {VN-direct|High}.

**The new law: a window from October 2026 into early 2027.** The window to add a new-food clause runs from the October session into early 2027, while the draft is revised between readings.
- The Ministry of Health notified its draft revised Food Safety Law to the World Trade Organization (WTO) as G/SPS/N/VNM/187 on 3 August 2026. The draft then had 71 articles in 8 chapters, with a stated approval target of October 2026 and entry into force on 1 January 2027 [@REG-12; @REG2-04] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- Under the 2026 legislative programme, the National Assembly will review it at the second 2026 session, as a press report of 22 September 2026 noted [@REG-14] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- On 24 September 2026 the National Assembly Standing Committee settled the timetable: first comments at the October 2026 session and consideration for passage at the May 2027 session. The draft now has 76 articles in 9 chapters [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.

So the law will pass in two readings, and the 1 January 2027 start date in the WTO notice cannot hold (our reading). The new draft adds risk-based classification, traceability for high-risk foods, duties for collective meal providers and conditions for online food delivery. The press report does not mention new foods [@APR-08] {VN-direct|Medium}.

{{kn:kn-food-law-window}}

Government press describes two product groups, both under post-market surveillance: foods that must register a declaration or declare an applied standard before sale, and foods needing no declaration. The drafters rejected making all pre-packaged foods register [@REG2-05; @REG2-06] {VN-direct|Medium}. **None of the summaries, submissions or stakeholder comments we read mentions novel food, new ingredients, history of use or a sandbox.** We could not read the full draft text [@REG2-04; @REG2-05; @REG2-06; @APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**What this means.** Ordinary plant-based and fermented foods from known ingredients face no pre-approval, now or (as described) under the new law. A genuinely new ingredient, such as a fungal biomass from a strain with no food history, can be self-declared. But an inspector could treat it as "not a food-type raw material" or a "new-technology product not yet permitted", and there is no way to get it cleared in advance. Investors should price this as enforcement-discretion risk. Founders should talk to the Vietnam Food Administration in writing before launch.

## 7.3 Precision fermentation and the GMO rules

Precision fermentation uses engineered microorganisms to make a specific protein or ingredient ([[app-r3-glossary]]). Decree 43/2026 (effective 26 January 2026) overhauled the GMO rules [@REG-18; @REG-21] {VN-direct|High}:
- one MAE procedure now certifies a GMO for both food and feed use, with 67 working days of expert review. It takes 17 working days if at least five developed countries of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) or the G20 already allow it [@REG-18; @REG-23] {VN-direct|Medium};
- gene-edited organisms without foreign DNA are self-assessed and notified;
- a new **certificate for GMOs used in closed production** (Art 37a) sets conditions on donor and recipient safety, containment and waste treatment. Firms file it on Forms 32 and 33 with MAE; the review time, fee and validity were not published in the texts we read [@REG2-11; @REG2-12] {VN-direct|High};
- "product of a GMO" now means a product made from a GMO that contains GM genetic material **"or protein from the modified gene"** [@REG2-11] {VN-direct|High}.

A purified recombinant protein is, literally, protein from a modified gene. **So a precision-fermented protein is probably a "product of a GMO" in Vietnam, even if no DNA remains.**

GM labelling applies only when a GM ingredient exceeds 5% of total ingredients. It does not apply where neither the modified gene nor its product can be detected (Decree 15/2018 Art 10; Joint Circular 45/2015 Art 1) [@REG-02; @LBL-10; @REG-18] {VN-direct|High}. A low-dose use such as lactoferrin at about 0.08% of an infant formula is far below the trigger (our calculation) [@LBL-25] {VN-direct|Medium}. So the label question bites on high-inclusion uses and on whether "non-GMO" claims stay lawful.

Making a precision-fermented protein in Vietnam needs the closed-production certificate. No text says whether the purified protein also needs a GMO food-use certificate, which is written for organisms rather than products. This is the single most important open legal question for precision fermentation in Vietnam. A joint MAE and Ministry of Health clarification would settle it ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

## 7.4 Labelling, claims, fortification and halal

- **The label decree changed; the rules did not.** Decree 37/2026 (23 January 2026) replaced the goods-label Decree 43/2017 and its amendment, Decree 111/2021 [@LBL-07; @LBL-08] {VN-direct|Medium}. Its text keeps the two rules that matter most for new proteins. First, a product name must not mislead about the nature or composition of the goods. Second, any ingredient used in the name must be quantified on the label (Art 11 and 13(4) of Decree 43/2017, carried over) [@LBL-06; @LBL-09] {VN-direct|Medium}. We could not read the new label chapter, so its article numbers are unknown.
- **Protein is on every label.** Since 1 January 2026 every non-exempt pre-packaged food must declare energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat and sodium. Circular 30/2026/TT-BYT replaced Circular 29/2023/TT-BYT on 10 July 2026 with the same core rules; foods made by small food businesses are exempt [@LBL-01; @LBL-02] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Claims are set by voluntary standards.** For protein, the Vietnamese national standard (TCVN) 14429:2025, taken from the international Codex food standards, allows "source of protein" at 5 g and "high protein" (*cao đạm*) at 10 g per 100 g. Per 100 kcal, the thresholds are 2.5 g and 5 g (our calculation from the 50 g reference value in TCVN 7088:2025) [@LBL-03; @LBL-04] {VN-direct|High}. A content claim needs no registration and no advertising approval [@REG-02; @LBL-19] {VN-direct|High}. TCVN 13625:2023 defines vegetarian and vegan claims. But its "vegetarian" allows egg, milk and honey and it says nothing on alliums, so it is broader than Buddhist *chay* (vegetarian food) [@LBL-15] {VN-direct|High}.
- **No reserved names.** No rule reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese) for animal products. A name must only not mislead, and makers already qualify meat words, as in *Heo hầm chay* (chay stewed pork) [@FORM-01]. The new dairy technical regulation QCVN 28:2026/BCT covers dairy only. TCVN 12443:2018 sets a floor of 2.0 g of protein per 100 ml for the name *sữa đậu nành* (soy milk) [@LBL-16; @LBL-17; @FTR-06; @BUY-05] {VN-direct|Medium}. No standard sets a minimum meat content for processed meats (TCVN 7049, 7050 and 7048:2020). So a product with a plant extender keeps its meat name, with the named meat's share printed [@LBL-11; @LBL-12; @LBL-13] {VN-direct|High}.
- **No allergen list.** Vietnam has no mandatory allergen list; labels need only generic "warning information" [@REG2-24; @REG2-25] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Fortification reaches no protein food.** Decree 09/2016 makes three fortifications mandatory: iodised salt, iron and zinc in wheat flour used in processing, and vitamin A in cooking oil. Tofu, textured soy and soy milk are not vehicles [@NQR-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. A 2025 draft amendment concerned iodised salt in fish sauce, and we found no amending decree [@NQR-03] {VN-direct|Low}. Processed pre-packaged food self-declares (Decree 15/2018 Art 4) unless it falls under Art 6. That article covers health supplements, medical foods, special-diet foods and products for children up to 36 months, which must register. That a fortified food follows the same rule is our inference [@NQR-04] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Halal.** A voluntary national halal framework has been in force since 1 June 2026 (Decree 127/2026). It is silent on fermentation-derived or GM-derived ingredients [@REG-27] {VN-direct|High}.

## 7.5 Feed rules: context only

Feed rules matter to this study only as context. They govern the feed behind the meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish that people eat. The full analysis is in [[app-s9-regulation]]; the main facts are these.

- **Livestock feed is more open than food.** The permitted raw-material list already includes **insects**, aquatic plants including **duckweed (*bèo*) and algae**, and since February 2023 **inactive yeasts**. The list is Circular 21/2019 Appendix VI as amended by Circular 31/2022. The yeasts are Torula or Candida yeast, yeast grown on molasses, and by-products of amino-acid production [@REG-32; @REG-33] {VN-direct|High}. These need no feeding trial, only a certified factory, a conformity declaration against the feed contaminant standard QCVN 01-190:2020 and a label [@REG-36; @REG-29; @REG-32] {VN-direct|High}.
- **A new feed material needs a trial.** Feed containing a "new substance" must be trialled under the Law on Animal Husbandry (Art 37) and Circular 94/2025. Agency time is about 40 working days; the trial itself and the wait for the annual list update come on top [@REG-29; @REG-34; @REG-32] {VN-direct|High}.
- **The aquafeed list has no raw-material section.** Under the Law on Fisheries (Art 35(1)), aquafeed containing any raw material "not on the list" must be trialled [@REG2-01; @REG2-02] {VN-direct|High}. But the list (Circular 16/2026 Appendix II) covers chemicals, bioactives, 30 microorganisms, vitamins and enzymes, not insect meal, single-cell protein, microalgae, duckweed or fermented soybean meal [@REG2-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Unlisted feed materials are fined.** Since 5 August 2026, using a feed raw material not on the permitted list costs VND 10 to 20 million per material (Decree 211/2026). The penalty also brings a one-to-three-month suspension of feed production and forced repurposing of the material, or destruction if that is impossible [@REG2-10] {VN-direct|High}.

> **Correction.** Earlier drafts attributed feed trials to Article 11 of Decree 13/2020 and quoted a 12 to 18 month timeline. Article 11 covers factory inspections; trials come from the Law on Animal Husbandry and Circular 94/2025, and no text gives a total duration [@REG-30; @REG-34].

## 7.7 Incentives: biotechnology favoured, but no tariff shelter

- **Corporate income tax.** The Law on Corporate Income Tax 67/2025 took effect on 1 October 2025. It gives high-tech enterprises and new projects applying listed high technology 10% for 15 years, plus four years' exemption and nine years at 50%. Agricultural and aquatic processing gets preferential rates [@REG-45] {VN-direct|Medium}. The guiding Decree 320/2025 adds two tests that matter here [@REG2-18] {VN-direct|Medium}:
  - agricultural processing exemptions need agricultural or aquatic raw materials of at least 30% of production cost. A cassava-based fermentation plant probably passes; one on imported refined sugar may not (our interpretation);
  - products made with technology applied for the first time in Vietnam get three years of exemption once the novelty is confirmed.
- **The high-technology list.** Decision 23/2026 (effective 1 July 2026) lists synthetic biology, new-generation microbial technology, tissue and cell technology in agriculture, and **"technology for producing recombinant proteins, enzymes and monoclonal antibodies"** as priority high technologies. It lists recombinant proteins and microbial products among encouraged products. There is no item for alternative protein, food or feed as such [@REG-47] {VN-direct|High}.
- **The Investment Support Fund is out of reach.** Under Decree 182/2024, high-tech product projects need at least VND 12,000 billion of capital or VND 20,000 billion of annual revenue. Research and development centres need VND 3,000 billion [@REG-46] {VN-direct|High}. No alternative-protein project in Vietnam is near these sizes.
- **VAT favours food-ingredient makers.** A food-ingredient producer can reclaim VAT paid on equipment, power and inputs. Feed has been "not subject to VAT" since 1 July 2025, so a feed-protein producer cannot [@COST-24] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Tariffs give no shelter.** A domestic textured-protein, isolate or single-cell-protein maker has no tariff edge over Chinese product.
  - Food protein ingredients carry small most-favoured-nation (MFN) duties: wheat gluten 10%, full-fat soy flour 8%, protein concentrates and textured protein 5%, protein substances 3%, active yeast 10%, inactive yeast and dead single-cell organisms 7% [@REG2-20; @REG2-23; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}. These rates apply only to origins without a trade agreement, chiefly the United States.
  - Chinese and ASEAN product pays 0% on all of these lines. That applies under the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area (ACFTA) and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) in 2025 to 2027, and at 0% under the ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement (ATIGA). Korean product pays 0% on the protein lines under the ASEAN-Korea Free Trade Area (AKFTA), with a certificate of origin. The lines are harmonised system (HS) codes 1109, 1208.10, 2106.10, 3504, 2102.10, 2102.20 and 2304 [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}.
  - Soybean meal and feed maize have been duty-free since March 2025; fishmeal and soybeans were already at 0% [@REG2-21; @REG2-23] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 7.8 Sandboxes: a law, but only city procedures

The Law on Science, Technology and Innovation (93/2025, effective 1 October 2025) allows controlled testing of technologies and products "not yet regulated or differing from current law". Tests may run for up to three years, extendable once, with liability exemptions for regulators and implementers who follow the rules [@REG-48; @REG2-13] {VN-direct|High}. **No national implementing procedure exists yet**; the four decrees guiding the law do not set one up [@REG2-14; @REG2-15] {VN-direct|High}.

Hanoi does have one. Resolution 29/2025 sets the procedure under the Capital Law. Decision 6150 (December 2025), approved by the Hanoi People's Committee, lists biotechnology and high-tech agriculture as priority fields [@REG2-16; @REG2-17] {VN-direct|High}. Hai Phong (Decision 248/2025) and Da Nang (Resolution 20/2026) have issued controlled-testing rules too [@REG2-16] {VN-direct|Low}. The Hanoi sandbox is the most concrete route we found for a supervised cultivated-meat or precision-fermentation pilot. But whether a city sandbox can set aside national food law has not been tested.

## 7.9 Strategies: biotechnology yes, alternative protein by name no

- Resolution 36-NQ/TW (2023) targets a biotechnology industry worth 7% of GDP by 2030 and replacement of at least 50% of imported biotech products [@REG-51] {VN-direct|High}.
- Resolution 57-NQ/TW (December 2024) targets R&D spending of 2% of GDP by 2030 and pilot mechanisms for firms to test new technology under state supervision [@REG-50] {VN-direct|High}.
- The livestock strategy (Decision 1520/2020) targets 30 to 32 Mt of industrial feed by 2030. It encourages biotech feed materials and by-products such as brewery waste and cassava residue to replace imports [@REG-54] {VN-direct|High}.
- The food-systems transformation plan (Decision 300/2023) targets a 10% cut in food-system greenhouse gas emissions against 2020 [@REG-55] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**None of these names alternative, novel or microbial protein** [@REG-51; @REG-54; @REG-55] {VN-direct|High}. The policy language supports the category without committing to it.

## 7.10 Genetic resources and strains: budget for benefit sharing

Commercial use of Vietnamese genetic resources needs an access licence. That includes microorganisms isolated from Vietnamese fermented foods or cassava waste. The licence takes a council review of up to 90 days, then a decision within 15 days. Benefit sharing is at least 1% of annual product revenue or 2% of the value of an IP transfer (Decree 59/2017) [@REG-60] {VN-direct|High}. Any "indigenous strain" business model has to budget for this.

## 7.11 How neighbours built their routes

- **Singapore:** pre-market approval for foods without 20 years of use as food; supplying an unapproved novel food has been an offence since 28 November 2025 [@REG-63] {VN-adjacent|High}. Singapore approval gives no legal status in Vietnam.
- **Thailand:** a novel-food notification (2016), with safety assessments done by assessment units that Thailand's Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recognises. It has separate forms for cultivated meat and "non-novel" foods, and a published approved list [@REG2-26] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **South Korea:** amended an existing instrument (temporary standards for new food ingredients) in about nine months (May 2023 to February 2024). This opened applications for cell-cultured ingredients in February 2024 [@RGN-19; @RGN-21; @RGN-34] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **Vietnam's own model:** the 2026 GMO decree already contains a reliance lane (17 working days if five developed countries approve). A similar lane for new foods approved by reference regulators would not be a foreign import.

See [[ch03-regional]] and [[app-s3-regional]].

## 7.12 What this means

- **The binding constraint depends on the product.** Food from known ingredients: none. New food ingredients: legal uncertainty, with no positive clearance. Precision fermentation: GMO product status. Cultivated meat and seafood: no route. Feed rules are context only (section 7.5).
- **The window is now.** A one-line delegation clause in the new Food Safety Law is cheap: "the Government shall regulate new foods and new food ingredients", with the detail in the implementing decree. It is only possible while the draft is before the National Assembly: from first comments in October 2026 to the vote considered in May 2027, so in practice until early 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Without it, the next chance may be years away ([[ch27-policy-options]]).
- **Investors:** ask for written regulator opinions (Vietnam Food Administration; MAE on GMO status) as diligence items.
- **Startups:** choose products whose ingredients have a history of use as food; choose your city deliberately if you need a sandbox; budget for benefit sharing if you commercialise a Vietnamese strain.
- **International bodies:** technical help for a Thai-style assessment-unit model and a reliance lane fits the 2026 to 2027 drafting window.
