# 11. What Vietnam eats and pays for protein

**In brief.**

- **Meat intake is high and still rising.** People ate 136.4 g of meat a day in 2020, against a recommended 50 to 80 g. Animal foods supply 42% of protein, twice the 1990 share.
- **There is no protein gap to fill.** Adults eat enough protein, and school lunches already carry enough. Child stunting is set before age two, through poor diet diversity.
- **Soy foods are everyday food, made from imported beans.** In 2021, 40% of adults had eaten tofu, soy milk or beans the day before.
- **Protein is cheap.** Eggs and chicken cost VND 34,000 to 45,000 per 100 g of protein. Imported plant-based meat costs 12 to 18 times as much.
- **Protein is a topping, filling or side dish.** When pork prices jumped after African swine fever, households switched to chicken, not tofu.

> **How to read Part III.** Part III treats demand as an input to supply choices, not a marketing plan. Each demand claim says how the evidence was gathered: stated (what people say), revealed (what people and firms do), tested (tastings and trials) or inferred (our inference). Stated evidence usually overstates behaviour.

## 11.1 A high-meat diet that is still rising

**Intake.** Vietnamese people ate 136.4 g of meat per person per day in 2020, up from 84 g in 2010. City dwellers ate 155.3 g. The national recommendation is 50 to 80 g [@DIE-01; @DIE-02; @DIE-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. We found no newer national survey [@APR-42] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-meat-intake-2020}}

**Supply.** In 2023 total protein supply was 102.2 g per person per day, and 42% came from animal foods. That share was 21% in 1990 [@DIE-07; @DIE-08] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Rice still supplies 27.0 g of protein a day, and soy foods 4.9 g [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-animal-protein-share}}

**Origin.** The soy foods people eat rest almost wholly on imported beans: 97.5% of the soybean supply was imported in 2023. Vietnam's own soybean crop could cover at most 9% of the beans used as food (our calculation) [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. We found no figure for the share of Vietnamese beans in tofu and soy milk ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]). {{kn:kn-soybean-import-dependence}}

**Daily reach.** In a 2021 national survey, 40% of adults had eaten beans, tofu or soy milk the day before, in town and country alike. In the same survey, 92% had eaten meat, poultry or fish [@APR-36; @BRD-01; @BRD-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. So soy foods are an everyday side dish for two adults in five, eaten beside meat rather than instead of it (our inference) {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

**Direction.** Meat eating per person is projected to rise 25% from 2025 to 2035, led by poultry [@DIE-11] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:projection}. That adds about 1.57 Mt (million tonnes) of meat a year by 2035, almost all from more meat per person (our calculation) [@DIE-11; @DIE-36] {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. The livestock strategy's 2030 target for meat per person was already reached in 2023, even as health guidance asks for red meat in moderation (section 11.6) [@APR-10; @DIE-07] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection}. {{kn:kn-meat-growth-2035}}

**Who eats more.** At home, urban and rural households now use the same amount of meat. Urban growth has moved to eating out. The poorest fifth of households nearly doubled its meat use from 2010 to 2024, so lower-income households are catching up [@DIE-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

## 11.2 Enough protein for adults, and for school-age children

**Adults eat enough protein**, about 73 to 80 g a day [@DIE-32; @DIE-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The 2026 recommendation is about 0.93 g per kg of body weight, from "diverse animal and plant sources" [@DIE-04] {VN-direct|High}. So the average adult eats enough protein, and more meat than advised.

**Children's nutrition problems are real, but school-age children have no protein gap** (our inference from the evidence below). Stunting, being too short for one's age, fell to 18% of children under five in 2023, but is 32% among ethnic minority children [@APR-27; @DIE-01; @DIE-03] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Stunting is set before age two.** So it runs through the foods babies eat alongside breast milk, not school meals [@APR-44; @NQR-28; @NQR-29] {general|Medium} {dx:inferred}. In 2020 to 2021, only 55% of children aged 6 to 23 months ate a diverse enough diet: 37% in the poorest households and under 25% in some ethnic minority groups [@FBS-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The constraint is access to diverse and fortified foods for poor infants, not national protein supply (our inference) {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

**What school meals contain:**

- **Urban lunches.** One Hanoi primary school's lunches carry about 32 g of protein, close to the whole day's need [@PMR-26; @NQR-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **Upland boarding schools.** We coded 353 meals from 13 ethnic-minority boarding schools in 2026. Almost every lunch and dinner had meat, fish or egg, and none had textured soy or mock meat [@UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-04; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **Weighed diets.** Boarding diets met protein and zinc needs, but only about 40% of calcium and vitamin A needs. Breakfast is the thin meal [@UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Two consequences for new protein:**

- **Replacing all the meat needs added zinc; a blend does not.** National guidance doubles a child's zinc need when a diet has little animal protein [@APR-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. A minced dish with 20% to 30% of its protein from textured soy keeps good protein quality (our calculation) [@NQR-05; @NQR-07] {general|Medium} {dx:inferred}.
- **Products for children under two face a high bar.** They must register as foods for young children, meet a stricter protein-quality test, and beat milk and fortified cereals [@NQR-04; @PRA-02; @NQR-31; @NQR-28; @NQR-29; @NQR-30] {general|Medium} {dx:tested}. School routes are in [[ch15-channels]].

**Overweight is rising too.** Among 5 to 19 year olds it rose from 8.5% to 19.0% between 2010 and 2020, and adult diabetes doubled [@DIE-01; @DIE-28] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Vietnam's protein problems are too much meat in cities and poor infant feeding in poor communities. Neither is a gap a cheap school protein dish would fill (our inference) {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-stunting}}

Three benchmarks follow {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}:

- **The plant floor** is tofu and dry beans, at about VND 15,000 to 40,000 per 100 g of protein.
- **The mass animal floor** is eggs and chicken, at VND 34,000 to 45,000. A mass-market protein food competes here. {{kn:kn-protein-price-floor}}
- **The mainstream meat reference** is lean pork, at about VND 71,000. Beef and shrimp are premium. Shrimp is the one mass protein where a seafood analogue has room on price.

Imported plant-based meat costs 12 to 18 times as much as eggs and chicken per gram of protein [@FORM-01; @COST-39; @COST-40; @COST-41; @DIE-12] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. In the US and Europe, demand stalled at premiums of 35% to 77% ([[ch13-consumers]]; [@GLB-03; @GLB-09; @GLB-10]) {general|High} {dx:revealed}. Vietnam's gap is ten times larger. {{kn:kn-analogue-price-gap}}

## 11.4 Protein is a topping, filling or side dish

Meat took 27% of household food spending in 2024, and tofu under 1%. Meals eaten out took 22.5%, rising to 30% in cities [@DIE-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

- **The noodle and bread bases carry little protein** [@DIE-12] {VN-direct|High}. In noodle dishes, the protein is almost all topping.
- **Protein comes as a topping, side dish or filling:** sliced meat or meatballs on noodles, braised pork or tofu beside rice, pork roll or egg in *bánh mì* (filled baguette) (our description) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **Chay makers already copy these parts:** vegetarian pork roll, meatballs, fish cake and beef slices [@FORM-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

So the formats that fit are toppings, fillings, rolls, balls and mince, not steaks or burgers. We found no measured portion sizes for protein in common dishes.

## 11.5 When pork prices jump, people switch to other animal protein, not tofu

After African swine fever reached Vietnam in 2019, the pig producer price rose 70%. The chicken price stayed flat [@DIE-25] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Consumer pork prices rose 57% in 2020 [@SEC-08] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**People switched to chicken and imports, not tofu.** Pork eating fell 13% in 2019, while chicken eating rose 26% and chicken imports doubled [@DIE-10] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Home tofu use did not move [@DIE-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The government ordered pork imports and encouraged "pork substitutes and frozen pork" [@PAN-03] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. A month of pork avoidance in Hanoi in March 2019 was a short food-safety panic [@PAN-34] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:stated}.

**The 2025 wave repeated the pattern.** Consumers "increasingly substituted chicken for pork" [@MAC-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. We found no data showing that tofu, soy milk or *chay* sold more in either wave.

**Two lessons for makers of new protein.** First, a pork shock does not send buyers to plant protein. Second, the reason processed-meat makers use soy is steady cost saving, not a hedge. Replacing 20% of the lean meat in *chả lụa* (pork roll) with soy saves about VND 9,100 to 16,600 per kg, at every pork price since 2019 (our calculation) [@DIE-25; @HXE-01] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. So a new extender has to beat hydrated soy, not pork ([[ch14-frontier-demand]], [[ch16-business-buyers]]).

## 11.6 Health and food safety: strong worries, smaller changes in behaviour

**Food-safety worry is near universal.** Over 90% of shoppers in one pork study worried about contamination [@DIE-30; @DIE-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}.

**People pay less for safety than they say.** A hypothetical choice survey found premiums of 70% or more for safe pork. A real-money auction found 20% [@DIE-29; @DIE-30] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:tested}. Plan on the lower figure.

**Health guidance already says eat less red meat.** The Ministry of Health's 2024 dietary tips ask people to eat fish, poultry, nuts and seeds daily and red meat in moderation. A 2026 decision tells people with diet-related disease to favour fish, lean meat, eggs, milk and legumes [@APR-17; @APR-18; @APR-16; @APR-15] {VN-direct|High} {dx:stated}. Neither names a plant-based product.

**Behaviour does not yet follow.** Home meat use rose in every income group from 2010 to 2024 [@DIE-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

## 11.7 Vietnam eats more meat than its income predicts

At its income level, Vietnam's meat supply of 60.5 kg per person already exceeds Japan's long-run plateau. It is more than twice Thailand's, and close to China's at the same income [@DIE-07; @DIE-08; @DIE-09] {VN-adjacent|High} {dx:revealed}.

{{chart:chart-animal-protein-ladder}}

Three readings follow {VN-adjacent|Medium} {dx:inferred}:

1. **No single income level marks a meat plateau.** Food culture and fish supply set plateaus more than income. No neighbour has shown a lasting fall in meat per person as income grew.
2. **The animal share of protein levels off at 55% to 60%** in Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Malaysia. At 42%, Vietnam has room to rise.
3. **The market a new protein could take a share of is growing.** Each 1% of meat protein is about 9,200 t of protein a year, worth roughly USD 140 to 250 million at today's prices (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @COST-39; @COST-40; @DIE-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

## 11.8 What this means for new protein

- **Price.** Mass-market protein foods compete with eggs and chicken. A meat analogue in mainstream shops needs to aim at lean pork or below. We found no evidence that supports a mass product priced above pork.
- **Protein.** Do not sell "more protein" to adults. Match meat's protein density in foods that replace meat: 12 to 20 g per 100 g, against a 4.7 g median for today's *chay* foods ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]]).
- **Format.** Build for the dish: toppings, fillings, rolls, balls and mince.
- **Growth.** Poultry carries most meat growth to 2035, so chicken-style formats deserve as much attention as pork. Urban growth is in eating out, which points to selling through foodservice ([[ch15-channels]]).
- **Nutrition.** Do not pitch new protein as the answer to child undernutrition. School meals meet protein needs; the gaps are breakfast, calcium and vitamin A {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

**For each reader.**

- **Startups:** set price targets per 100 g of protein against eggs, chicken and lean pork, and design formats for named dishes.
- **Investors:** demand is growing, but the money is in cheap everyday protein. The evidence does not support business models that need consumer premiums above about 20%.
- **Manufacturers:** a pork extender saves money at every pork price. Value it on that saving, and test any new extender against your own soy recipe.
- **Researchers:** measure protein per serving in common dishes, and protein quality, zinc and iron in tofu, textured soy and blended dishes.
- **Policy makers:** school guidance sets no animal share, but the menu software still prints an animal-protein standard of at least 48% ([[ch15-channels]]). Show zinc, iron and sodium per lunch instead [@APR-04; @NQR-02; @APR-16; @APR-17] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **International organisations:** treat urban overeating of meat and child undernutrition as separate problems. For children under two, the issue is standards for weaning foods, where plant products face a high bar.
