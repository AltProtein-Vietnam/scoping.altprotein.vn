# 25. What demand asks of the frontier: product targets, plays and public goods

**In one paragraph.** Part III's answer to "what demand exists for new protein in Vietnam?" is clear, even where the evidence is thin. Demand pulls new protein as an ingredient inside foods Vietnamese already eat, sold to the firms and kitchens that make them: chay makers replacing imported textured soy, meat processors extending pork, plant-milk makers adding protein, canteens feeding workers on about VND 25,000 to 31,000 a meal, and exporters of dumplings and other wrapped foods. It does not pull branded replacements for meat. We tested this against 80 named actors and 16 further lines of research ([[ch29-actor-check]]). The core answer holds, but the route to each buyer is narrower and more concrete than our first reading suggested. The rules on school menus, protein claims, blend labels, tariffs and sugary drinks are already written; soy, not meat, is the price an extender must beat; and domestic textured soy is an import-substitution plant best owned by a chay maker, co-packer or distributor and financed with debt, not a venture bet. This chapter turns that into eleven target product profiles, a demand check of the ten plays of [[ch26-plays]] and twenty-one cheap moves, each with the funder or budget that would pay for it.

> **Method note.** How the actor check was run, which questions Part III answered and what each new line of research changed are in [[ch29-actor-check]] and [[app-m4-actor-check-waves]]. This chapter carries the results into the product profiles, the play check and the moves.

## 25.1 Demand findings that change what to make

**Ten findings from Part III.**

1. **The price to beat is eggs and chicken,** at VND 34,000 to 45,000 per 100 g of protein; lean pork (about VND 71,000) is the ceiling for a mass meat analogue. Imported analogues sit at 12 to 18 times eggs and chicken ([[ch11-protein-diet]]) {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-protein-price-floor}}
2. **Adults do not need more protein; dishes need denser protein.** Protein sits in toppings, fillings and rolls; chay foods have a median of 4.7 g per 100 g against 19 g for lean pork ([[ch11-protein-diet]]). For school-age children protein is not the gap either: published Hanoi primary-school lunches carry about 31.8 g (our calculation), and upland boarding lunches and dinners carried meat, fish or egg in 240 of 242 meals; the documented shortfalls are breakfast, calcium and vitamin A [@PMR-26; @UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Stunting (18% of children under five in 2023, 32% among ethnic minority children) is a problem of the youngest children, where plant products face the young-child protein-quality pattern and registration as foods for children under 36 months [@APR-27; @NQR-01; @REG-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
3. **Few want to eat less meat** (15% of urban online respondents, [[ch13-consumers]]), and meat per person is projected to rise 25% by 2035 ([[ch11-protein-diet]]) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:stated}.
4. **What people ask for is variety, cooking fit, fewer additives and safety,** not a closer copy of meat ([[ch13-consumers]]) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:stated}.
5. **The modern plant category that sells is plant milk,** with soy milk priced at or below dairy, and nut milk at premium-dairy prices, by local incumbents. The best plant-milk SKUs sell 60 to 79% of the litres of the best dairy SKUs on Lazada and Tiki, and Vinamilk's high-protein nut milk sells as a premium niche of about 70,300 L at its Lazada store [@MKT-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
6. **Chay is the ready channel and the wrong impact count:** a shelf, a cold chain, a calendar and makers already buying imported protein, but chay days are already in the meat baseline ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]) [@CHY-23]. Fake chay is a latent rumour, not a live scandal: we found no confirmed case of meat in a Vietnamese chay product from 2015 to 2026 [@TRU-22], and a laboratory PCR test for animal DNA in chay food has been developed [@TRU-24]; chay's live trust issues are borax, microbes and unlabelled goods [@TRU-14; @TRU-16; @TRU-18; @CHY-23] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
7. **Blends are the entry route, and soy already holds it.** One large processor lists soy protein in 31% of its meat formulations [@BUY-05], and seven commercial Vietnamese sausages in one study contained soy; in that test, sausages made with soy or peanut protein were liked equally [@HXE-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
8. **Words carry risk:** *nhân tạo* (artificial) and *giả* (fake) are loaded, and *độn* (padding, filler) means hidden cost-cutting; *lên men* (fermented) helps. A declared soy ingredient in branded sausages draws no observed penalty [@NOV-69; @NOV-81; @TRU-02; @TRU-03; @NOV-63; @TRU-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
9. **Kitchens and factories come before shelves, and they already buy cheap plant protein.** Only an ingredient fits a canteen's protein budget of VND 7,500 to 14,000 a meal, and domestic dry soy-gluten pieces already reach kitchens at about VND 4,000 to 5,100 per 20 g of protein, against VND 7,200 to 9,500 for firm branded tofu and VND 8,800 for eggs (our calculation from [@CPC-12; @CHN-41; @CHN-39]) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-canteen-protein-budget}}
10. **The volumes that exist are business-to-business.** On the benchmark path the routes create demand for about 19,000 t of protein a year by 2035, of which about 11,000 to 17,000 t made in Vietnam for the home market, mostly as import substitution, and displace about 0.2% of meat protein ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-demand-bench-2035}}

**Six findings from the actor check that change the picture.**

- **Schools buy tofu beside meat, and the rule that mattered has expired.** Decision 1340's animal-protein target ended in 2020 [@CHN-33]. The current guidance, Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT (December 2025), sets no animal share and asks for plant protein (legumes, tofu) at least twice a week. Published menus in our audit (`school_menu_weeks.csv`) meet that in only 9 of 48 school-weeks, plant protein appears on 15.1% of 239 lunch days, almost always as tofu beside a meat dish, and the menu software that caterers use prints a standard of "animal protein at least 48%". A plant ingredient in the plant slot competes with tofu and replaces tofu, not meat; the only school route that removes meat is a labelled blend in the minced-meat and fish-cake dishes served on 28.5% of days [@AIB-39; @PMR-10; @PMR-26] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The rule window is Decision 973/QĐ-TTg: a MOET *bán trú* (school lunch and midday care) circular due in 2026 and digital menu tools due in 2027 to 2028 [@PMR-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **A sugary-drink excise will shape plant milk.** From 1 January 2027 drinks above 5 g of total sugar per 100 ml pay 8% (10% from 2028); dairy is exempt and whether sweetened soy, nut and cereal milks are in scope is unresolved. Core Fami soy milks carry 6.5 to 9.3 g, and the reference high-protein nut milk is also above 5 g [@FTR-01; @FTR-02; @FTR-05; @FTR-17; @FTR-18] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **There is no tariff shelter.** Textured protein, soy isolate, pea protein and gluten enter from China, ASEAN and Korea at 0% under ACFTA and RCEP; the 5%, 3% and 10% duties apply only to other origins, and Chinese, French and Canadian pea protein also enter duty-free [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **Retailer private label is a working route for chay.** All five Co.op Select frozen chay dishes are made by one certified co-packer, GN Foods, and Lotte Mart sells its own chay label; the products are starch and vegetable led and carry no protein claim [@CPC-15; @AIB-28; @ECO-35; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **Most label rules are already in force.** A high-protein claim needs 10 g per 100 g (TCVN 14429:2025, voluntary), and 20 of 43 audited foods already meet the source-of-protein condition per 100 g without claiming it; TCVN 13625:2023 already defines vegetarian and vegan claims; any ingredient named in a product name must be quantified; no standard sets a minimum meat content; and the GMO label applies only above 5% of ingredients [@LBL-03; @LBL-15; @LBL-06; @LBL-11; @LBL-12; @LBL-13; @LBL-10] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **Soy is the extender benchmark, not meat.** Replacing 20% of the lean in *chả lụa* (steamed pork roll) with hydrated soy saves VND 9,100 to 16,600 per kg at every annual hog price from 2019 to 2024 (our calculation), so a new extender must beat hydrated soy at about USD 2.6 to 2.8 per kg of protein, and win on sensory tolerance or the label, not on price against pork [@HXE-01; @DIE-25; @HXE-10; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

## 25.3 The ten plays, checked against demand

The market-pull scores of [[ch26-plays]] were set from supply-side evidence of buyers. Part III's evidence suggests two changes and no reductions, and the actor check keeps both while replacing the evidence behind T1 and T2 {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}:

| Play | Market pull, supply view | Demand view | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Domestic textured plant protein | 4 | **5** | Several named buyers already buy the imported equivalent: HS 2106.10 imports from China, India and Serbia were 12,832 t in 2025; export origin rules add no customer [@BUY-02; @TIC-12; @ORG-01] |
| T2 Functional microbial feed ingredients | 4 | 4 | Vietnam already imports about 9,200 t of inactive yeast a year for food and feed, 95% of it at about USD 1.1 per kg; export standards do not require microbial ingredients, and BAP counts algae and other aquatic microorganism ingredients toward its marine-ingredient target [@FBA-17; @FBA-01; @FBA-05] |

| T4 Cassava sugar hub | 2 | 2 | Export demand is for starch; no new protein demand [@EXP-39] |
| T5 Shared pilot and tolling | 2 | 2 | An enabler; adds a need for application and sensory trials |
| T6 Duckweed feed | 2 | 2 | Not examined by the demand streams |
| T7 High-value precision-fermentation ingredients | 2 | 2, conditional | Buyers of the target ingredient exist and sell non-GMO; the GMO label bites only above 5% of ingredients [@NOV-59; @NOV-62; @NOV-63; @LBL-10] |
| T8 Cultivated seafood research | 1 | 1 | Revealed Asian sales are tiny; the regional cultivated-seafood firms have turned to pet food and skincare for revenue, with no food product on sale [@NOV-10; @AIS-34; @AIS-35] |

**T1: the market pull stands, the thesis changes.** T1 keeps a market pull of 5 because named chay makers already buy the imported product. But the pool a soy extrusion line can serve is at most about 12,800 t of HS 2106.10 imports from three low-cost origins, not the whole estimated food-use pool of 40,000 t of imported plant-protein ingredients: the near tripling of plant-protein imports is mostly HS 3504 protein at about USD 1 per kg, which needs other plants [@BUY-02; @TIC-12; @TIC-14] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Capital is not the barrier. Shandong makers list complete lines of 0.2 to 1.5 t an hour, with dryer, at USD 11,000 to 100,000 (list prices) [@TIC-02; @TIC-03; @TIC-07]; by our calculation a whole plant installs for about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million, and working capital of USD 0.6 to 3.5 million is the larger need {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-tvp-line-capex}} There is no tariff shelter: Chinese product lands at USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg, and India-origin textured soy lands lower still. The delivered price of food-grade defatted soy flour, 63 to 85% of cost and imported from the same countries that export the finished product, sets the sign of the margin (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-t1-flour-breakeven}} The benchmark path supports about one 1 t/h line, or two 0.5 t/h lines, by 2035. T1 is therefore an import-substitution plant best owned by a large chay maker, a group of chay makers, a co-packer or an ingredient distributor and financed with debt and trade finance, not venture equity: its return is a commodity spread that Chinese exporters can compress, it holds no intellectual property and its exit is a trade sale {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**T2: the same score on revealed evidence.** Our first reading took T2's pull from export feed standards. Those standards are largely neutral: ASC v1.2, BAP and GLOBALG.A.P. do not require a microbial ingredient, although BAP counts algae and other aquatic microorganism ingredients toward its marine-ingredient target; from 1 May 2028 ASC farm audits will need feed from an ASC-certified mill, and 15 Vietnamese mills are certified [@FBA-01; @FBA-02; @FBA-03; @FBA-04; @FBA-05; @FBA-06] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The revealed pull is that Vietnam already imports about 9,200 t of inactive yeast a year (HS 2102.20, food and feed together), 95% of it at about USD 1.1 per kg, and Vietnam already exports a product in the same HS code [@FBA-17; @FBA-19] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Nutreco, a global leader in animal nutrition and aquafeed, reached 0.6% novel ingredients group-wide in 2025 (1.5% in its aquafeed arm, Skretting) against a 5 to 10% target [@FBA-07] {VN-adjacent|High} {dx:revealed}.

**What changes in the ranking.** With the two market-pull changes and the published weights, domestic textured plant protein (T1) leads the balanced preset at 3.95, against 3.9 for the cassava sugar hub (T4), and plant-drink proteins (T10) move up to third place for startups, joint third for manufacturers and fourth for investors. The lead is 0.05: a one-point fall in T1's market pull or defensibility returns first place to T4. T1 leads under the balanced, investor, startup and manufacturer presets; T4 stays first or second under every preset and leads under the policy, research and international presets, where the shared pilot and tolling play (T5) stays second. The frontier bets (T7, T8) stay where they were (our calculation from `plays.csv`, `play_weight_presets.csv` and `play_demand_check.csv`) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**Proposed supply-score changes, not applied.** We have not changed `plays.csv`. The actor-check evidence suggests three supply-score changes, recorded in the `note_v0_4` column of `play_demand_check.csv` for readers to apply with the interactive ranking in [[ch26-plays]]: T1 capital efficiency from 4 to 5 and Vietnam advantage from 3 to 2, which nets to zero on the balanced score (T1 stays first at 3.95 and first under the investor, startup and manufacturer presets); and T2 defensibility from 3 to 2, on weak evidence, which would leave T5 sole third under the balanced preset (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**Demand-side kill tests** to add to the plays (`play_demand_check.csv`):

- **T1** fails if delivered food-grade defatted soy flour costs more than about USD 700 to 940 per t for a 1 t/h line at 70% utilisation (USD 590 to 900 for a 0.5 t/h line), if fewer than three buyers sign conditional orders totalling at least 2,000 t a year, or if India-origin textured soy (USD 0.98 per kg FOB) becomes the reference price (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **T2** fails if two head-to-head trials at 1 to 2% inclusion against the commodity yeast mills already buy show no gain worth the price gap (about 1.2 to 1.8% of shrimp-feed cost), or if the delivered price stays above about USD 1.1 per kg without such a gain [@FBA-17] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **T7** is confined to export and business-to-business uses if purified precision-fermented proteins must carry a GMO declaration at the doses buyers use.
- **T9** fails if, at the replacement level that gives the same saving as soy, a fungal blend in industrial *xúc xích* or a coarse filling scores below the processor's own soy-extended formula in a blind test, or if no processor will pay above hydrated-soy parity (about USD 1.2 per kg dry, the 2025 unit value of HS 2106.10 imports from China, at an assumed 45% protein) [@TIC-12; @HXE-10] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **T10** fails if an isolate cannot reach the band between Chinese and Western pea protein with UHT stability, or if host drinks cannot be made at or below 5 g of total sugar per 100 ml once plant milks are ruled inside the sugary-drink excise [@FTR-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

## 25.4 Twenty-one cheap moves, and who would pay

Most of the demand gaps in Part III can be closed for tens of thousands of USD. The full list, with who, when and who would pay, is in `demand_moves.csv`; the underlying questions are in [[app-r1-open-questions]]. Every move now names a payer, or says why none was found. Only four of the original nineteen moves have a funder whose stated scope fits and that accepts Vietnam: two consumer-acceptance studies fit the Bezos Centre for Sustainable Protein at NUS, and two naming and GMO rules fit Nova Vista's market-shaping programme. Diet-change and culture-change work is excluded by Nova Vista and now by ACE Movement Grants, and ReRoot Asia has no Vietnam track [@AFN-05; @AFN-15; @AFN-29; @FUF-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. {{kn:kn-funder-fit}} The rest belong to industry, to trade bodies or to national budgets.

**Measure.**

- **Chay days (DMV-01).** First ask the Global Diet Quality Project or Gallup to tabulate the 2021 flesh-free share (7.9% of adults) by the lunar date of the recall day, then add a chay-day question to a probability survey [@APR-36; @BRD-03]. *Who would pay:* no philanthropic payer; national survey budgets (NSO, NIN) or GAIN's Diet Quality Questionnaire.
- **Customs declarations (DMV-02)** for HS 3504, 2106.10, 1109 and 2102.20.10 (yeast for animal feeding), to split food from feed and name the buyers of T1 and T2 products. *Who would pay:* partly Nova Vista or World Bank and ADB technical assistance; otherwise a would-be T1 or T2 owner.
- **Sensory and choice tests (DMV-03) and a randomised label and name test (DMV-04).** *Who would pay:* the Bezos Centre at NUS, with a Vietnamese institute.
- **One canteen and school trial with three arms (DMV-05):** an extender or canteen ingredient in minced dishes; a chay-day menu; and a school arm in which one caterer that writes one menu for several schools serves a textured-soy or blended dish in the plant slot and in one minced dish, with daily published menus as controls. Log the dish species replaced, because animals spared per meal differ about 34-fold between a pork and a chicken dish (our calculation) and chicken leads only 13% of canteen protein dishes [@UPL-27; @UPL-28; @UPL-29; @UPL-30; @UPL-32; @FUF-03]. *Who would pay:* the extender arm partly fits Nova Vista's procurement lever and the Bezos Centre; Humane World Viet Nam fits the chay-day arm as implementer, but no current funder both covers Vietnam and funds diet change; the school arm has no external payer.
- **A minimart repeat test (DMV-06), the share of meat eaten as processed products (DMV-09) and a Nha Trang hotel and menu audit (DMV-10).** *Who would pay:* industry and AltProtein Vietnam; Humane World fits the hotel audit as implementer.
- **Quote requests to wrapped-food buyers (DMV-11):** one vegetable and one plant-protein SKU from three EU importers of Asian frozen foods and two brand owners already sourcing from Vietnam [@XBA-20], with their certification and origin requirements. Retailer protein targets need no survey; they are published [@XBA-02; @XBA-14]. *Who would pay:* industry and trade bodies.
- **A soy-flour price sheet (DMV-21):** five delivered quotes for food-grade defatted soy flour and three for Chinese plain textured soy, with the T1 breakeven. It costs tens of hours and decides the T1 kill test [@TIC-12; @TIC-13]. *Who would pay:* the would-be owner or its lender.

**Build public goods.**

- **A chay safety and composition survey (DMV-07)** that tests borax, preservatives, microbes and protein first, with animal DNA as a PCR screen (the QUATEST 3 method awaits accreditation); publish with VFA, publish methods and do not name small makers [@TRU-24]. *Who would pay:* partly the Bezos Centre, UNICEF Viet Nam or ACIAR; otherwise food-safety budgets.
- **A standing panel of research and procurement buyers (DMV-08)** to publish specifications, minimum orders and qualification times. *Who would pay:* an industry association.
- **A feed carbon-footprint benchmark (DMV-12)** that helps ASC-certified mills meet GHG reporting; it will not by itself create demand for microbial ingredients [@FBA-01]. *Who would pay:* partly ADB Ventures, IFC or ACIAR; otherwise the mills.

**Set rules (policy makers).** These add a demand-side reason to the policy options of [[ch27-policy-options]]. The Food Safety Law takes first comments at the National Assembly's October 2026 session and is considered for passage at the May 2027 session, so a new-food clause needs to be in the draft revised between the two sessions, in practice by early 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Two of the four label moves are largely law already and need enforcement and testing, not new rules; one should defend a permissive regime; one shrinks to a single question [@LBL-06; @LBL-10; @LBL-15] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Decree 37/2026 has replaced Decree 43/2017 for goods labels, and Circular 30/2026/TT-BYT has replaced Circular 29/2023 for nutrition labels.

- **Chay claims (DMV-13).** TCVN 13625:2023 already defines vegetarian and vegan claims, but its vegetarian class allows egg, milk and honey and it says nothing on alliums. Ask VFA to state that an unqualified *chay* or *thuần chay* (strict vegetarian) claim means the vegan criteria, with egg or milk named in a qualifier, and enforce the existing rules alongside DMV-07.
- **Blend labels (DMV-14).** Any meat named in a product name must already be quantified. Narrow the move to a voluntary industry code for generic names such as *giò lụa* (steamed pork roll) and *xúc xích* that states the meat share when a plant or fungal protein replaces 10% or more of the meat; its value is defensive, as proof against a *độn* accusation [@LBL-06; @TRU-03]. *Who would pay:* the processors.
- **Names (DMV-15).** We found no rule that reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese); names must only not mislead, and practice already qualifies meat words. Defend this permissive regime in the new Food Safety Law decree and aim vocabulary guidance at state and media use.
- **GMO status (DMV-16).** The GMO label applies only above 5% of ingredients, so lactoferrin-type uses (about 0.08%) are not caught. Ask MAE and VFA one question: is a recombinant protein identical to the native protein a detectable product of the modified gene, and may a product containing one below 5% still claim non-GMO? *Who would pay for DMV-15 and DMV-16:* Nova Vista, or for a small Vietnamese organisation ACE Movement Grants (policy work only, deadline 18 October 2026).
- **School menus (DMV-17).** National guidance already has no animal share. Ask MOET, NIN and Ajinomoto to replace the menu software's 48% animal-protein default with protein-quality, zinc, iron and sodium targets per lunch, and to add textured soy and legume products to the software's food list, inside the MOET circular due in 2026 and the digital tools due in 2027 to 2028 [@PMR-26; @PMR-01]. A second version is a procurement standard for innovative protein in public and state-enterprise canteens. *Who would pay:* UNICEF Viet Nam's 2027 to 2031 programme for the school version; Nova Vista's procurement lever for the canteen version.
- **Customs subheadings (DMV-18)** for soy isolate, pea protein and textured protein. *Who would pay:* MOF's own budget, partly World Bank or ADB.
- **An origin note for exporters (DMV-19)** on the EU cap of 20% non-originating flour and starch in wrapped foods and the 10% tolerance for imported textured soy in 2106 foods, and on claiming CPTPP for the UK; imported soy protein is not the main constraint [@ORG-01]. *Who would pay:* industry and trade bodies.
- **A written sugary-drink ruling (DMV-20):** the Ministry of Finance to state, before 1 January 2027, whether soy milk (TCVN 12443:2018), nut and cereal milks above 5 g of total sugar per 100 ml are *nước giải khát* (soft drinks) [@FTR-01; @FTR-13]. *Who would pay:* the plant-milk makers.

## 25.5 What not to do

- Do not size a business on stated survey intent or on the published Vietnamese market sizes; neither has a revealed basis.
- Do not launch premium imported-style analogues into gourmet channels expecting mass uptake, and do not enter foodservice with an imported branded analogue through business-to-business distributors: CP Meat Zero minced was listed on Kamereo at 1.5 to 2.1 times CP's own minced pork per kg, and none of its eight SKUs was orderable in September 2026 [@AIS-25; @CPC-12] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- Do not price a mass product above pork, a chay product above today's chay shelf, or an extender above hydrated soy without proof that it allows more undetected replacement.
- Do not lead with "meat replacement", "the future of meat", "lab" or environmental claims, and do not use *độn* or "x% meat replaced" wording; lead with taste in familiar dishes, protein, safety and origin, and name the plant part as an ingredient.
- Do not name products *thịt nhân tạo* (artificial meat) or market cultivated or precision-fermented products to chay buyers.
- Do not start in schools with a branded or meat-replacement product. The school plant slot is small (about 1,000 t of protein a year, our calculation from an estimated 0.8 billion school lunches a year in `demand_assumptions.csv`), filled by tofu and under-used; enter later as a caterer ingredient that beats tofu on cost, or as a labelled blend in minced dishes [@PMR-10; @AIB-39; @CHN-29; @CHN-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Do not start in wet markets or tourist hotels either.
- Do not pitch a fortified soy dish to upland boarding or border-commune kitchens as filling a meatless lunch: their published lunches carry meat almost every day, and a Decree 339 lunch spent 79 to 98% of its non-rice money on pork (91 to 98% on pork and tofu together). Pitch cost per gram of protein against pork, which these kitchens already stretch with tofu [@UPL-01; @UPL-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- Do not present import substitution, or protein upgrades on existing chay days, to welfare or climate funders as meat displaced, animals spared or CO2e avoided: they deliver none (our reasoning), and ACE Movement Grants will not fund diet-change work in any region [@FUF-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.
- Do not count sales into existing chay days as meat displaced.

## 25.6 What would change our view

- A tested Vietnamese study (choice experiment or in-store trial) showing repeat purchase of a modern analogue or blend at 20% or more above the meat it replaces.
- Retail or e-commerce panel data showing sustained growth of modern plant-based meat since 2024.
- A probability survey, or a lunar-date tabulation of the 2021 diet survey, showing that chay keeping is rising among young urban people, which would make added chay days a real displacement route.
- A large processor launching a declared blend with a novel protein, or a formula maker sourcing a precision-fermented protein.
- A new Food Safety Law, now expected to be considered for passage in May 2027, with a working new-food route and a clear treatment of purified fermentation proteins.
- Delivered soy-flour quotes below about USD 700 per t, which would make a domestic textured-soy line pay even at the high-cost end.
- A food-safety shock in meat followed by a measurable switch to plant proteins, which the 2019 to 2020 pork shock did not produce.

**For each reader.**

- **Startups:** sell a familiar food with little capital, and aim first for ingredient, private-label or co-packing supply to incumbents. A restaurant menu listing for your own analogue brand is not a business customer: in the region such firms mostly went quiet or pivoted. Of 24 regional and Vietnamese firms since 2019 in our firm register (`firm_outcomes.csv`, counted in `firm_base_rates.csv`, our calculation), 7 are active, 4 pivoted, 2 exited and 11 are dormant; we found no sign of activity after 2022 at any of the 4 Vietnamese meat-analogue startups, while all 3 makers of traditional formats are trading. TiNDLE (pivoted to private label), Emmay (trading) and Karana (dormant in our register) are examples [@AIS-28; @AIS-39; @BRD-08]. No founder has yet tested the ingredient route, so treat it as the hypothesis to test, not a proven path {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.
- **Investors:** T1 is a debt-financed import-substitution plant for a chay maker, co-packer or distributor, not a venture thesis; underwrite the delivered soy-flour price, conditional orders and cost per gram of protein. Investors now say they fund offtake, contracted revenue and unit economics, which T1 and T10 can show and consumer analogues cannot [@AIS-01; @AIS-15] {general|Medium} {dx:stated}.
- **Manufacturers:** a domestic textured soy, a non-soy isolate for drinks at or below 5 g of sugar per 100 ml, and a fungal ingredient that beats your own soy-extended formula are the three new ingredients your products can use. Test blends first in industrial *xúc xích* and coarse fillings, not *giò lụa*, blind and at equal saving against your soy recipe; keep 10 to 20% replacement in fine emulsions and 30% in coarse mince; and consider private-label chay through an existing co-packer [@HXE-01; @HXE-04; @HXE-09; @CPC-15] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **Research bodies:** the tested-evidence gap (sensory, choice and label tests in Vietnamese formats) is the most useful thing you can close for the whole sector, and the Bezos Centre at NUS is the one funder whose scope fits it.
- **Policy makers:** most label rules already exist; enforce them, defend permissive naming, answer the single GMO question, rule on plant milks and the sugary-drink excise before 2027, and put protein quality, zinc and cost into the school menu software.
- **International organisations and funders:** match the route to your unit and your rules. Welfare funders: a canteen trial with Humane World Viet Nam, focused on chicken dishes, which lead only one protein dish in eight in our coding of caterers' menus [@UPL-27; @UPL-28; @UPL-29; @UPL-30; @UPL-32]. Market-shaping funders: canteen procurement and the naming and GMO rules; Nova Vista's programme funds procurement, advance market commitments and retailer ratio targets, excludes whole-food, culture-change and diet-change work, and wants more policy staff especially in Japan and Korea. R&D funders: isolate and fungal functionality and Vietnamese blind tests. Nutrition agencies: protein quality and zinc in school food standards. On the benchmark path the displacing routes spare about 2.4 to 2.7 million animals a year by 2035, 93 to 94% of them chickens and ducks, and avoid about 0.14 Mt CO2e (our calculation, [[ch18-demand-sizing]]), so climate is a co-benefit, not the case; corporate pledges are a weak lever, since Accor's dish target is the only one we found that binds Vietnamese units [@AFN-05; @AFN-23; @FUF-03; @FUF-04; @AFN-40] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The chay-day survey item belongs in national surveys.
