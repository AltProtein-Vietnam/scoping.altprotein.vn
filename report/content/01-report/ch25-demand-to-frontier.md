# 25. What demand asks of the frontier: product targets, plays and public goods

**In brief.**

- Demand pulls new protein as an ingredient inside foods Vietnamese already eat, sold to the firms and kitchens that make them. It does not pull branded meat replacements.
- The buyers are *chay* (vegetarian food) makers replacing imported textured soy, meat processors extending pork, plant-milk makers adding protein, canteens, and exporters of dumplings and other wrapped foods.
- We tested this against 80 named actors ([[ch29-actor-check]]). The answer holds, but the route to each buyer is narrower and more concrete.
- Soy, not meat, is the price an extender has to beat. An extender is a plant or fungal protein that replaces part of the meat in a product.
- Domestic textured soy is an import-replacement plant, best owned by a chay maker, co-packer or distributor and financed with debt. It is not a venture bet.
- This chapter sets out ten product profiles, a demand check of the seven plays in [[ch26-plays]], and twenty cheap moves, each with who would pay.

---

## 25.1 Demand findings that change what to make

**Ten findings from Part III.**

1. **The price to beat is eggs and chicken,** at VND 34,000 to 45,000 per 100 g of protein. Lean pork is the ceiling for a mass meat copy ([[ch11-protein-diet]]) {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-protein-price-floor}}
2. **Adults do not need more protein; dishes need denser protein.** Chay foods average 4.7 g of protein per 100 g, against 19 g for lean pork. School lunches already carry enough protein; the gaps are breakfast, calcium and vitamin A [@PMR-26; @UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Child stunting is set before age two, where plant products face a high bar [@APR-27; @NQR-01; @REG-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
3. **Few want to eat less meat** (15% of urban online respondents), and meat eating is projected to rise 25% by 2035 ([[ch13-consumers]]) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:stated}.
4. **People ask for variety, cooking fit, fewer additives and safety,** not a closer copy of meat {VN-direct|Low} {dx:stated}.
5. **The modern plant category that sells is plant milk.** Online, the best plant milks sell 60% to 79% of the litres of the best dairy milks [@MKT-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
6. **Chay is a ready channel but the wrong place to count less meat.** Chay days are already in the meat baseline ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]) [@CHY-23]. Its trust problems are borax, microbes and unlabelled goods, not hidden meat [@TRU-14; @TRU-16; @TRU-18; @TRU-22; @TRU-24] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
7. **Blends are the entry route, and soy already holds it.** One large processor lists soy in a third of its meat recipes [@BUY-05; @HXE-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
8. **Words carry risk.** *Nhân tạo* (artificial), *giả* (fake) and *độn* (filler) are loaded. *Lên men* (fermented) helps. Declared soy draws no penalty [@NOV-69; @NOV-81; @TRU-02; @TRU-03; @NOV-63; @TRU-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
9. **Kitchens already buy cheap plant protein.** Dry soy pieces reach kitchens at about VND 4,000 to 5,100 per 20 g of protein, cheaper than tofu or eggs (our calculation from [@CPC-12; @CHN-41; @CHN-39]) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-canteen-protein-budget}}
10. **The volumes that exist are business-to-business.** On the benchmark path, demand reaches about 19,000 t of protein a year by 2035, mostly replacing imports. It displaces about 0.2% of meat protein ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-demand-bench-2035}}

**Six findings from the actor check.**

- **Schools serve tofu beside meat.** The 2025 school guidance sets no animal share and asks for plant protein twice a week. Menus meet that in 9 of 48 weeks, almost always with tofu beside meat. The menu software still prints "animal protein at least 48%". So a plant ingredient in the plant slot replaces tofu, not meat. Only a labelled blend in minced dishes removes meat [@CHN-33; @AIB-39; @PMR-10; @PMR-26] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The rule window is a 2026 school-meal rule and 2027 to 2028 digital menu tools [@PMR-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **A sugar tax will shape plant milk.** From 2027, sweet drinks above 5 g of sugar per 100 ml are taxed; dairy is exempt, and sweet plant milks may be caught [@FTR-01; @FTR-02; @FTR-05; @FTR-17; @FTR-18] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **There is no tariff shelter.** Textured soy, isolates, pea protein and gluten enter duty-free from China, Southeast Asia and Korea [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **Store brands are a working route for chay.** One co-packer makes all of Co.op's frozen chay dishes, none with a protein claim [@CPC-15; @AIB-28; @ECO-35; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **Most label rules are already in force:** the high-protein claim, vegetarian definitions, quantity rules for named ingredients, and a GMO label only above 5% [@LBL-03; @LBL-15; @LBL-06; @LBL-11; @LBL-12; @LBL-13; @LBL-10] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- **Soy is the extender benchmark.** Replacing 20% of the lean in pork roll with soy saves money at every pork price since 2019. A new extender must beat hydrated soy, at about USD 2.6 to 2.8 per kg of protein, and win on texture or label (our calculation) [@HXE-01; @DIE-25; @HXE-10; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

## 25.2 Target product profiles

A target product profile states what a product has to be for the demand that exists {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. There are ten active profiles, TPP-01 to TPP-10, and one candidate, TPP-12. In the table, HoReCa means hotels, restaurants and catering suppliers.

| Profile | First buyers | Price target | Protein target | Has to have | First test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TPP-01 Domestic textured soy | Chay makers; co-packers; ingredient distributors | At or below Chinese landed price (USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg); soy flour below about USD 700 to 940 per t | 50% to 70% | Several shapes; non-GMO where asked; debt-financed owner | Five delivered soy-flour quotes; conditional orders of 2,000 t a year from three buyers |
| TPP-02 Higher-protein chay foods | Chay brands; store brands through their co-packers | Today's chay shelf price | 10 to 12 g per 100 g | "10 g đạm/100 g" on the front; short label; stock for lunar peaks | A 10 g version of a store-brand product |
| TPP-03 Extender for processed meat | Vissan, Masan, C.P., CJ, SG Food | At or below hydrated soy | Finished product 10 g or more per 100 g | 10% to 20% in smooth products, up to 30% in mince; plant part named | Blind test against the processor's soy recipe, in sausage |
| TPP-04 Canteen protein ingredient | Caterers, through HoReCa suppliers | Below dry soy pieces per 20 g of protein | About 20 g per adult dish | Holds up in bulk cooking; supplier papers | Caterer trial |
| TPP-05 Rice or mung bean isolate | Vinamilk, Vinasoy, Nutifood, TH; processors and bakeries to replace egg white | Between Chinese and Western pea protein (USD 2 to 7 per kg); below egg white powder | 80% to 88% isolate | Stable in long-life drinks; neutral; soy-free; a paid starch co-product | Samples to two dairy research teams and two processors |
| TPP-06 Fungal binder | Bakery, seasoning makers, processors | Against egg white, dairy and yeast protein | About 45% | Clean label; binding and savoury taste | Trials against yeast protein and egg white |
| TPP-07 Precision-fermented specialty protein | Formula and medical nutrition makers; export | Against conventional lactoferrin and whey | Infant and medical grade | GMO question settled | A legal opinion |
| TPP-08 Export plant-based wrapped foods | EU and UK importers and brand owners | Near European store-brand prices | Claims where true | Food-safety certificate; EU flour rule met | Quotes from three importers and two brand owners |
| TPP-09 Higher-protein chay for the diaspora | Asian grocers abroad | USD 3.50 to 10 a pack | 10 g or more per 100 g | Frozen logistics | Listing test with one importer |
| TPP-10 Cultivated meat or seafood | None at home before the mid-2030s | n/a | n/a | Blend format; not sold as chay | Research partnerships only |
| TPP-12 Fish-cake blends (candidate) | Fish-cake makers; caterers | Plant protein far below fish protein per kg | 10% to 30% of fish protein from plants | Fish share on the label | A maker trial and taste panel |

**TPP-05 has a second, better-paid job: replacing egg white.** Vietnam imported 2,536 t of egg white and egg powder in 2025, at about USD 5.90 per kg [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Mung bean isolate is already accepted as safe in the US for many foods [@SLP-11] {general|High}. Mung beans alone cost USD 3.0 to 4.9 per kg of isolate, above Chinese pea protein but below egg white (our calculation) [@SLP-14; @DIE-12; @BUY-09] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The market is small, about 250 t of isolate a year for a tenth of egg imports. Potato protein comes closest to egg white in tests, so test one function first, such as gelling in sausage [@CGG-23] {general|Medium} {dx:inferred}. A cheap first test is a buyer-sponsored student project at a university [@AML-05] {general|Medium}.

**TPP-12 is a candidate, and its demand is untested.** Fish-cake makers would replace 10% to 30% of the fish protein with plant protein, with the fish share printed [@LBL-06; @LBL-08] {VN-direct|Medium}. Plant protein costs far less than surimi per kg of protein (our calculation) [@FORM-01; @ECO-40; @ECR-06; @BUY-07; @SLS-05; @SLS-20] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. But no buyer asks for a blend: Korea's leading maker is moving to products with 80% or more fish, and taste panels abroad rejected high plant shares [@SLS-01; @SLS-11; @SLS-07; @SLS-06] {general|Medium} {dx:tested}.

## 25.3 The seven active plays, checked against demand

Demand evidence raises two market-pull scores and lowers none {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

| Play | Market pull, supply view | Demand view | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Domestic textured plant protein | 4 | **5** | Named buyers already buy 12,832 t a year of the imported equivalent [@BUY-02; @TIC-12; @ORG-01] |
| T4 Cassava sugar hub | 2 | 2 | Export demand is for starch [@EXP-39] |
| T5 Shared pilot and tolling | 2 | 2 | An enabler |
| T7 High-value precision-fermentation ingredients | 2 | 2, conditional | Buyers exist and sell non-GMO; low doses escape the GMO label [@NOV-59; @NOV-62; @NOV-63; @LBL-10] |
| T8 Cultivated seafood research | 1 | 1 | Asian sales are tiny [@NOV-10; @AIS-34; @AIS-35] |
| T9 Koji and fungal foods | 2 | 2 | Soy already fills the extender role [@NOV-63; @NOV-56; @NOV-57] |
| T10 Mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks | 3 | **4** | Vinamilk buys pea protein for a high-protein milk; the sugar tax and duty-free pea narrow the room [@BUY-16; @MKT-03; @FTR-02; @FTR-32] |

**T1: the market pull stands, but the thesis changes.** A textured-soy line can serve at most about 12,800 t a year of imports, not the whole 40,000 t plant-protein pool, which is mostly gluten and isolates [@BUY-02; @TIC-12; @TIC-14] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Capital is not the barrier.** Chinese lines list at USD 11,000 to 100,000, and a whole plant installs for about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million. Working capital, USD 0.6 to 3.5 million, is the larger need (our calculation) [@TIC-02; @TIC-03; @TIC-07] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-tvp-line-capex}}

**Flour decides the margin.** Chinese product lands duty-free at USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg. Food-grade soy flour is 63% to 85% of cost, so its delivered price decides whether a line pays (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-t1-flour-breakeven}}

The benchmark path supports about one 1 t-an-hour line by 2035. So T1 is an import-replacement plant, best owned by a chay maker, co-packer or distributor and financed with debt. Its return is a commodity margin that Chinese exporters can squeeze {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**What changes in the ranking.** With the two changes, T1 leads the balanced weighting at 3.95, just ahead of the cassava sugar hub (T4) at 3.9. T4 stays first or second under every weighting. Plant-drink proteins (T10) move up to third for investors, startups and manufacturers (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. T11, fish-cake blends, stays a candidate with untested demand {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**Demand-side kill tests:**

- **T1** fails if food-grade soy flour costs more than about USD 700 to 940 per t delivered; if fewer than three buyers sign conditional orders for 2,000 t a year; or if cheaper Indian textured soy becomes the reference price (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. It also fails if no anchor buyer's bank will finance the supplier's invoices [@SLK-05; @SLK-18; @SLK-19] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.
- **T7** is confined to export and business uses if these proteins must carry a GMO label at the doses buyers use.
- **T9** fails if a fungal blend scores below the processor's soy recipe in a blind test at equal saving, or if no processor pays more than hydrated soy [@TIC-12; @HXE-10] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **T10** fails if an isolate cannot match pea protein on price and stability, or if host drinks cannot be made with 5 g of sugar or less per 100 ml [@FTR-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. The mung bean arm needs a paid buyer for its starch; the rice arm needs a rice syrup or starch maker to host it, and we found none in Vietnam (our calculation) [@SLP-14; @DIE-12; @BUY-09; @FS-26; @SLP-01; @SLP-08] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **T11** fails if a taste panel or first buyer rejects it at 15% plant protein, or if export buyers demand more fish [@SLS-11] {general|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

## 25.4 Twenty cheap moves, and who would pay

Most demand gaps can be closed for tens of thousands of USD. Every move names a payer, or says why none was found.

**Only four of the original nineteen moves have a funder whose scope fits and that accepts Vietnam:** two consumer studies for the Bezos Centre for Sustainable Protein in Singapore, and two naming and GMO rules for Nova Vista's market-shaping programme. Other diet-change funders exclude Vietnam or this kind of work [@AFN-05; @AFN-15; @AFN-29; @FUF-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. {{kn:kn-funder-fit}} The rest belong to industry, trade bodies or national budgets.

One research funder's interests fit Vietnamese fish science: Coefficient Giving's 2026 call named flavour work on catfish, tilapia and other farmed fish [@CGG-03; @CAP-57; @MAC-15] {general|Medium} {dx:stated}. None of its 61 earlier alternative-protein grants went to Vietnam or Southeast Asia [@CGG-02; @CGG-14; @CGG-19] {general|High}.

**Measure.**

- **Chay days (DMV-01).** Ask the 2021 diet survey's owners to tabulate meat-free days by lunar date; then add a chay-day question to a national random-sample survey [@APR-36; @BRD-03]. *Who would pay:* national survey budgets.
- **Customs declarations (DMV-02)** to split food from feed and name the buyers of textured soy. *Who would pay:* partly Nova Vista or development banks; otherwise a would-be T1 owner.
- **Taste and choice tests (DMV-03) and a label test (DMV-04).** *Who would pay:* the Bezos Centre, with a Vietnamese institute.
- **A canteen and school trial (DMV-05)** with three arms: an extender in minced dishes, a chay-day menu, and one caterer serving textured soy or a blend in several schools. Log the animal each dish replaces, because animals spared per meal differ about 34-fold between pork and chicken dishes (our calculation) [@UPL-27; @UPL-28; @UPL-29; @UPL-30; @UPL-32; @FUF-03; @CGG-11] {general|Medium} {dx:tested}. *Who would pay:* Nova Vista or the Bezos Centre for the extender arm; no current funder covers the other arms.
- **A minimart repeat test (DMV-06), the share of meat eaten processed (DMV-09) and a Nha Trang hotel audit (DMV-10).** *Who would pay:* industry.
- **Quote requests to wrapped-food buyers (DMV-11)** from three EU importers and two brand owners [@XBA-20; @XBA-02; @XBA-14]. Trade-fair stands paid for by the industry ministry are a cheap venue [@SLK-16] {VN-direct|High}. *Who would pay:* industry and trade bodies.
- **A soy flour price sheet (DMV-21):** five delivered quotes for food-grade soy flour, and three for Chinese textured soy. It decides the T1 kill test [@TIC-12; @TIC-13]. *Who would pay:* the would-be owner or its lender.

**Build public goods.**

- **A chay safety and composition survey (DMV-07):** test for borax, preservatives, microbes and protein, then animal DNA. Publish with the food safety authority, without naming small makers [@TRU-24]. *Who would pay:* partly the Bezos Centre, UNICEF or Australia's agricultural research centre.
- **A standing panel of food-industry buyers (DMV-08)** to publish specifications, minimum orders and approval times. *Who would pay:* an industry association.

**Set rules.** These moves support the policy options in [[ch27-policy-options]]. The Food Safety Law is due for a vote in May 2027, so a new-food clause must be drafted by early 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Most label rules already exist and need enforcement, not new rules [@LBL-06; @LBL-10; @LBL-15] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

- **Chay claims (DMV-13).** Ask the food safety authority to state that an unqualified *chay* or *thuần chay* (vegan) claim means vegan, with egg or milk named in a qualifier.
- **Blend labels (DMV-14).** A voluntary industry code to state the meat share under generic names such as *giò lụa* (pork roll), as a defence against the filler charge [@LBL-06; @TRU-03]. *Who would pay:* the processors.
- **Names (DMV-15).** Keep the permissive naming rules in the new law's decree.
- **GMO status (DMV-16).** Ask one question: is a protein made by microbes, identical to the natural one, a detectable GMO product, and may a product with it below 5% still claim non-GMO? European groups asked the EU the same in 2026 [@PPE-06] {general|Medium}. *Who would pay for DMV-15 and DMV-16:* Nova Vista.
- **School menus (DMV-17).** Ask the menu software's makers to replace the 48% animal-protein default with targets for protein quality, zinc, iron and sodium, and to add textured soy and legumes to the food list [@PMR-26; @PMR-01]. *Who would pay:* UNICEF for schools; Nova Vista for public canteen buying standards.
- **Customs codes (DMV-18)** for soy isolate, pea protein and textured protein. *Who would pay:* the finance ministry.
- **An origin guide for exporters (DMV-19)** on the EU rules for wrapped foods [@ORG-01]. *Who would pay:* trade bodies.
- **A sugar tax ruling (DMV-20)** before 2027 on whether sweet plant milks are soft drinks [@FTR-01; @FTR-13]. *Who would pay:* the plant-milk makers.

## 25.5 What not to do

- Do not size a business on survey intent or published Vietnamese market sizes.
- Do not launch premium imported-style meat copies into gourmet shops or restaurant supply. An imported plant-based pork range on a supply platform was dearer than real pork, and could not be ordered by 2026 [@AIS-25; @CPC-12] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- Do not price a mass product above pork, a chay product above today's chay shelf, or an extender above hydrated soy.
- Do not lead with "meat replacement", "lab" or environmental claims, or use *độn* (filler). Lead with taste in familiar dishes, protein, safety and origin.
- Do not call products *thịt nhân tạo* (artificial meat), or market cultivated or precision-fermented products to chay buyers.
- Do not start in schools with a branded product. The school plant slot is small and filled by tofu [@PMR-10; @AIB-39; @CHN-29; @CHN-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Do not start in wet markets or tourist hotels either.
- Do not pitch a fortified soy dish to upland school kitchens as replacing meatless lunches. Their lunches already carry meat; pitch cost per gram of protein against pork [@UPL-01; @UPL-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.
- Do not present import replacement, or upgrades on existing chay days, to welfare or climate funders as meat displaced. They displace none [@FUF-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

## 25.6 What would change our view

- A tested Vietnamese study showing repeat buying of a meat copy or blend at 20% or more above the meat it replaces.
- Shop data showing sustained growth of modern plant-based meat since 2024.
- Survey evidence that chay keeping is rising among young urban people.
- A large processor launching a declared blend with a new protein, or a formula maker buying a precision-fermented protein.
- A new Food Safety Law with a working new-food route.
- Delivered soy flour quotes below about USD 700 per t.
- A meat safety scare followed by a measurable switch to plant protein, which the 2019 to 2020 pork shock did not produce.

**For each reader.**

- **Startups:** sell a familiar food with little capital, as an ingredient, store brand or co-packed product. Of 24 regional firms we tracked since 2019, only 7 are active, and the Vietnamese survivors make familiar foods [@AIS-28; @AIS-39; @BRD-08]. No founder has yet tested the ingredient route, so treat it as the hypothesis to test {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.
- **Investors:** T1 is a debt-financed import-replacement plant, not a venture thesis. Underwrite the soy flour price, conditional orders and cost per gram of protein. Investors now fund contracted revenue and unit economics, which T1 and T10 can show [@AIS-01; @AIS-15] {general|Medium} {dx:stated}.
- **Manufacturers:** three new ingredients can go into your products: domestic textured soy, a non-soy isolate for low-sugar drinks, and a fungal ingredient that beats your soy recipe. Test blends first in factory sausage and coarse fillings, blind and at equal saving [@HXE-01; @HXE-04; @HXE-09; @CPC-15] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **Researchers:** the gap in tested evidence, taste, choice and label tests in Vietnamese foods, is the most useful thing you can close.
- **Policy makers:** enforce the label rules that exist, keep naming permissive, answer the GMO question, rule on plant milks before 2027, and put protein quality and zinc into the school menu software.
- **International organisations and funders:** match the route to your unit and rules. Welfare funders: a canteen trial aimed at chicken dishes. Research funders: isolate and fungal function, and Vietnamese blind tests. Market-shaping funders: canteen procurement and the naming and GMO rules. Climate is a co-benefit, not the case [@AFN-05; @AFN-23; @FUF-03; @FUF-04; @AFN-40] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
