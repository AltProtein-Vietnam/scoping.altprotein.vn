# 19. Outlook to 2035

**In brief.**
- **The calendar is front-loaded.** The rule changes that decide the next decade fall in 2025 to 2027. Most of what decides the path to 2035 will be observable by 2028.
- **The Food Safety Law is the window that closes.** The National Assembly takes first comments in October 2026 and considers passage in May 2027, so the law cannot take effect on 1 January 2027 as first planned.
- **Demand growth will come from income and diet, not headcount.** Population will grow only about 5% between 2025 and 2035.
- **Feed is context.** By 2030 the state targets 30 to 32 Mt (million tonnes) of industrial feed and 6.0 to 6.5 Mt of meat. That feed is the hidden import behind the meat people eat.
- **Three plausible states for 2035:** import price-taker, feed-first domestic build-out and regulated regional hub. The third is the one in which Vietnam makes new protein foods for people at home.
- **Four things decide the path:** a workable route for new foods in the law; whether Vietnamese makers can approach Chinese costs; whether the state backs its biotechnology strategy with money and approvals; and, for the feed-first path only, the gap between fishmeal and soybean-meal prices.

> **Scenarios, not forecasts.** The three states below are internally consistent pictures built on the milestones in this chapter. We do not assign probabilities. Each comes with signposts: observable events that would tell a reader which path is unfolding.

**Reading.**
- **Demand growth comes from diet, not headcount.** Population grows about 5% from 2025 to 2035 and peaks around 2049 [@GT-12] {VN-direct|Medium}. So most growth in demand for protein will come from higher incomes and changing diets.
- **The meat people eat will rest on more imported feed.** Reaching 30 to 32 Mt of feed by 2030 from 21.5 Mt in 2024 means 5.7 to 6.9% growth a year. Feed output would be 8.5 to 10.5 Mt higher in 2030 than in 2024 (our calculation from [@MAC-28; @REG-54]) {VN-direct|Medium}. Every extra tonne needs protein, which today is almost entirely imported ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]). The feed scheme (Decision 1625/QD-TTg) indicates that the target covers livestock feed only [@NTS-23] {VN-direct|Medium}. That makes it look out of reach; see [[ch22-protein-balance-2050]].
- **Feed prices are context.** The ratio of fishmeal to soybean-meal prices widened from 3.8 in 2024 to 6.2 in August 2026 [@GT-15] {general|High}. It matters for the feed-first path (Scenario 2), not for food.
- **No official long-run feed price paths exist.** World Bank forecasts stop at 2027 [@GT-14] {general|High}. Any 2030 or 2035 price is a scenario assumption.

Chapters 20 to 24 extend this outlook to 2050 ([[ch20-drivers-2050]] to [[ch24-vision-2050]]), and [[ch28-robust-moves]] turns it into moves.

## 19.3 Three plausible states in 2035

Each state has a picture, its drivers, what it would mean for the protein people eat, and signposts.

### Scenario 1: Import price-taker

- **Picture.** Vietnam's alternative protein is mostly imported: Chinese single-cell protein (protein from microbes), yeast protein and textured soy, and food ingredients approved in Singapore or Thailand. Domestic activity is *chay* (vegetarian food) processing, tofu and a few insect-meal plants. Feed protein imports grow with the feed industry.
- **For people.** New protein foods reach people mostly as imported ingredients, which Vietnamese *chay* and tofu makers turn into food. The meat, eggs and farmed fish people eat rest on more imported feed. Vietnam adds no new domestic source of protein for people.
- **Drivers.** The Food Safety Law passes without a workable new-food route. Chinese scale keeps landed prices below Vietnamese cost. Fishmeal falls back from its 2026 peak. Agritech venture capital stays near zero.
- **Signposts.**
  - No defined new-food procedure in the law or decree by the end of 2027.
  - Rising imports of yeasts and protein preparations from China.
  - No domestic extruder or fermentation plant above pilot scale announced by 2028.
  - Fishmeal below USD 1,800 per tonne for a full year.

### Scenario 2: Feed-first domestic build-out

- **Picture.** Several Vietnamese plants turn side streams into feed protein: cassava pulp in Tay Ninh (former Tay Ninh), brewer's spent grain near Ho Chi Minh City and Hai Phong (former Hai Phong, Hai Duong), seafood by-products in the Mekong Delta. Products are yeast or bacterial protein, duckweed and insect meal. Food applications stay small.
- **For people.** Little changes in what people eat by 2035. Domestic feed protein adds an input behind farmed fish and meat, which this study treats as context. The plants, skills and investors could later turn to food, but a food product would still need a new-food route and food-grade production.
- **Drivers.** Fishmeal stays above about USD 2,000 per tonne. MAE keeps adding new ingredients to the feed lists. High-tech incentives reach fermentation plants. Development finance repeats the structure of the Entobel insect-feed deal: private equity, a development finance institution and an offtake, that is, a buyer's agreement to take the output ([[ch08-capital]]). Demand for E10 petrol makes cassava roots costly, pushing processors towards residues.
- **Signposts.**
  - A second insect or microbial-protein plant above 5,000 t a year reaches financial close by 2028.
  - A feed-list update names microbial protein for aquafeed.
  - A top-five aquafeed producer signs a multi-year offtake.
  - Fishmeal averages above USD 2,000 per tonne in 2027.
  - Feed output is on track for 30 Mt in 2030.

### Scenario 3: Regulated regional hub

- **Picture.** Vietnam has a working new-food route that relies on (accepts the assessments of) Singapore, Korea and other reference regulators. It hosts contract fermentation for brands from the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN). Ho Chi Minh City and Dong Nai (former Dong Nai, Binh Phuoc) are the manufacturing base. "Hub" here means a cluster of food-fermentation plants that serves the region. Domestic brands sell hybrid and fermented products.
- **For people.** This is the path in which Vietnam makes new protein foods for people at home: hybrid meat-and-plant products, fermented foods and contract-made ingredients. It adds a second, domestic source of protein for people. Demand still limits its size: on the benchmark path of our demand model, new routes displace about 0.2% of meat protein by 2035 ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.
- **Drivers.** The new law defines new food and allows reliance. A decree makes the science-law sandbox usable for food. High-tech certification works for precision fermentation. Resolution 57 research money funds a national protein platform. Renewables make low-carbon power available.
- **Signposts.**
  - A "new food" (*thực phẩm mới*) definition and procedure in the law or decree.
  - The first sandbox approval for a food product.
  - A foreign precision-fermentation or contract-manufacturing firm registers an investment in Vietnam.
  - Vietnam aligns with an ASEAN novel-food reliance mechanism.
  - A negative signpost: Thailand's planned public precision-fermentation pilot plant is built first and draws the same investors ([[ch03-regional]]).

Source for all three scenarios: AltProtein Vietnam synthesis of [@GT-10; @GT-11; @GT-15; @REG-12; @REG-47; @REG-54; @RGN-35; @CAP-01; @CAP-05; @ECO-05] and chapters 2 to 10, 26 and 27 {VN-direct\|Low}.

**The scenarios are not exclusive.** Scenario 2 can happen without Scenario 3, since feed rules are already more open than food rules. Scenario 3 needs the same fermentation skills, pilot capacity and investors that Scenario 2 would build. For food, this study's route to them is shared food-grade pilot and tolling fermentation (play T5; tolling means renting time on someone else's fermenter), not feed plants first ([[ch26-plays]]).

## 19.4 Four uncertainties to track

The first three uncertainties matter for protein for people. The fourth matters only for the feed-first path.

| Uncertainty | What to watch | Where | Threshold that matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does the food law open a workable route? | Text of the new law and its decree | National Assembly records; MOH drafts | A "new food" definition with a procedure and timelines |
| Can Vietnam approach Chinese cost? | Landed prices of Chinese microbial protein and textured soy (duty-free); Vietnamese pilot costs; delivered soy-flour prices | Customs data; company disclosures | Vietnamese cost within 20% of landed imports |
| Will the state fund and approve, not only plan? | Sandbox approvals, fund investments, pilot-line funding; feed-list updates as context | MAE, MOST, city fund announcements | At least one of each by 2028 |
| How wide is the fishmeal-soy gap? (feed context; decides Scenario 2) | Monthly fishmeal and soybean meal prices | World Bank Pink Sheet (monthly commodity prices) | Fishmeal above USD 2,000 per tonne; ratio above 5 |

**There is no tariff cushion in the cost test.** Chinese and ASEAN textured protein, isolates, gluten, yeast and single-cell protein enter at 0% in 2025 to 2027 [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. Three trade agreements set that rate: the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area (ACFTA), the ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement (ATIGA) and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP). In 2025 China shipped textured protein and concentrates (harmonised system code HS 2106.10) to Vietnam at USD 1.24 per kg free on board (FOB, before freight and insurance). With freight and port costs, that lands at about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Two regional events also matter:
- whether Thailand builds its public precision-fermentation pilot plant and contract-manufacturing network [@RGN-35] {VN-adjacent|Medium};
- what lies behind the September 2026 termination notice for a Thai biotechnology plant [@RGN-38] {VN-adjacent|Low}.

## 19.5 What this means

- **Investors:** the regulatory events that matter most fall within 18 months. For food, track the new law's text and the landed price of Chinese textured soy. The fishmeal-soy ratio is a leading indicator only for feed-protein deals, which are outside this study's scope.
- **Policy makers:** the Food Safety Law window is the one that closes. A new-food clause needs to be in the draft revised after the October 2026 first reading, by early 2027, before the vote considered in May 2027. The 2030 feed target implies a large new protein requirement, met mostly by imports, behind the meat people eat. A national protein balance would show that exposure ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Startups:** build on residues and side streams rather than cassava roots, which now compete with fuel ethanol. Use the new province names in licences and documents.
- **Manufacturers:** multi-supplier protein strategies reduce exposure to volatile prices. A food maker planning a domestic textured-protein line needs a delivered quote for food-grade soy flour first ([[ch09-economics]]). The feed target implies feed output 8.5 to 10.5 Mt higher in 2030 than in 2024; for this study that is context.
- **Research bodies and international bodies:** long-run projections for Vietnamese meat, aquaculture and feed are missing from public sources we could reach. A published baseline would help everyone plan.
