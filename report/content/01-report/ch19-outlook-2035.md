# 19. Outlook to 2035

**In brief.**
- **The calendar is front-loaded.** The rule changes that decide the next decade fall in 2025 to 2027. Most of what decides the path to 2035 will be visible by 2028.
- **The Food Safety Law is the window that closes.** The National Assembly considers passing it in May 2027.
- **Demand growth will come from income and diet.** Population grows only about 5% from 2025 to 2035.
- **Three plausible states for 2035:** import price-taker, feed-first domestic build-out, and regulated regional hub. Only the third has Vietnam making new protein foods at home.

> **Scenarios, not forecasts.** The three states below are consistent pictures, not predictions. We assign no probabilities. Each comes with signposts: events that would show which path is unfolding.

**Reading.**
- **Demand growth comes from diet, not headcount.** Population grows about 5% to 2035 and peaks around 2049 [@GT-12] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **The meat people eat will rest on more imported feed.** The 2030 feed target means 6% to 7% growth a year, and every extra tonne needs protein that is almost all imported (our calculation from [@MAC-28; @REG-54]) {VN-direct|Medium}. The target now looks out of reach ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]) [@NTS-23] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Feed prices are context.** Fishmeal rose to over six times the price of soybean meal by August 2026 [@GT-15] {general|High}. That matters for the feed-first path, not for food. No official price forecast goes beyond 2027 [@GT-14] {general|High}.

## 19.3 Three plausible states in 2035

### Scenario 1: Import price-taker

- **Picture.** Vietnam's alternative protein is mostly imported: Chinese microbial protein and textured soy, and ingredients approved in Singapore or Thailand. Domestic activity is *chay* (vegetarian food) processing and tofu.
- **For people.** Vietnam adds no new domestic source of protein. The meat people eat rests on more imported feed.
- **Drivers.** The Food Safety Law passes without a workable new-food route. Chinese scale keeps import prices below Vietnamese cost. Venture capital stays near zero.
- **Signposts.** No new-food procedure by the end of 2027. Rising protein imports from China. No domestic extruder or fermentation plant beyond pilot scale by 2028.

### Scenario 2: Feed-first domestic build-out

- **Picture.** Several Vietnamese plants turn side streams into feed protein: cassava pulp, brewer's spent grain and seafood by-products. Food uses stay small.
- **For people.** Little changes in what people eat by 2035. The plants, skills and investors could later turn to food, but would need a new-food route.
- **Drivers.** High fishmeal prices, new ingredients added to the feed lists, incentives for fermentation plants and development finance for first plants ([[ch08-capital]]).
- **Signposts.** A microbial-protein plant over 5,000 t a year is financed by 2028. A big fish-feed maker signs a multi-year purchase deal. Fishmeal stays above USD 2,000 per tonne.

### Scenario 3: Regulated regional hub

- **Picture.** Vietnam has a working new-food route that accepts approvals from Singapore, Korea and other trusted regulators. It hosts contract fermentation for Southeast Asian brands, around Ho Chi Minh City and Dong Nai. Local brands sell blended and fermented products.
- **For people.** This is the path in which Vietnam makes new protein foods at home. It adds a second, domestic source of protein. Demand still limits its size: on the benchmark path, new routes displace about 0.2% of meat protein by 2035 ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.
- **Drivers.** The new law defines new food and allows reliance on trusted regulators. A sandbox works for food. Research money funds a national protein platform.
- **Signposts.** A "new food" (*thực phẩm mới*) definition and procedure in law. The first sandbox approval for a food. A foreign contract fermenter invests in Vietnam. A negative signpost: Thailand builds its public pilot plant first and draws the same investors ([[ch03-regional]]).

Source for all three: our synthesis of [@GT-10; @GT-11; @GT-15; @REG-12; @REG-47; @REG-54; @RGN-35] and chapters 2 to 10 {VN-direct\|Low}.

**The scenarios are not exclusive.** Scenario 3 needs the same fermentation skills, pilot capacity and investors that Scenario 2 would build. For food, this study's route to them is shared food-grade pilot and tolling fermentation (play T5, [[ch26-plays]]), not feed plants first.

## 19.4 Four uncertainties to track

| Uncertainty | What to watch | Threshold that matters |
|---|---|---|
| Does the food law open a workable route? | Text of the new law and its decree | A "new food" definition with a procedure and timelines |
| Can Vietnam approach Chinese cost? | Landed prices of Chinese textured soy and microbial protein; delivered soy-flour prices | Vietnamese cost within 20% of imports |
| Will the state fund and approve, not only plan? | Sandbox approvals, fund investments, pilot-line funding | At least one of each by 2028 |
| How wide is the fishmeal-soy gap? (feed context only) | Monthly fishmeal and soybean meal prices | Fishmeal above USD 2,000 per tonne |

**There is no tariff cushion.** Chinese and Southeast Asian protein ingredients enter duty-free [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. Chinese textured protein lands at about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Two regional events also matter: whether Thailand builds its public pilot plant [@RGN-35] {VN-adjacent|Medium}, and why a Thai biotech plant gave notice of closure in 2026 [@RGN-38] {VN-adjacent|Low}.

## 19.5 What this means

- **Investors:** the rule changes that matter most fall within 18 months. For food, track the new law's text and the landed price of Chinese textured soy.
- **Policy makers:** the Food Safety Law window is the one that closes. A new-food clause needs to be in the draft by early 2027. A national protein balance would show how much the 2030 feed target rests on imports ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Startups:** build on residues and side streams rather than cassava roots, which now compete with fuel ethanol. Use the new province names.
- **Manufacturers:** buy protein from several suppliers to reduce price risk. Before planning a textured-protein line, get a delivered quote for food-grade soy flour ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **Researchers and international bodies:** public long-run projections for Vietnamese meat, aquaculture and feed are missing. A published baseline would help everyone plan.
