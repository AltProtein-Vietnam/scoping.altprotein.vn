# 19. Outlook to 2035

**In one paragraph.** The calendar is front-loaded. Vietnam's new science and corporate tax laws took effect in October 2025, E10 petrol became mandatory in June 2026, new high-technology lists applied from July 2026, and the National Assembly takes first comments on a new Food Safety Law in October 2026 and considers passage in May 2027, so the law cannot take effect on 1 January 2027 as first planned. By 2030 the state targets 30 to 32 Mt of industrial feed and 6.0 to 6.5 Mt of meat; population will grow only about 5% between 2025 and 2035, so demand growth will come from income and diet. Fishmeal reached its highest price this decade in August 2026, six times the price of soybean meal. Which of three plausible paths Vietnam takes by 2035 depends on four things: whether the food law opens a workable route for new ingredients, whether the fishmeal-soy price gap stays wide, whether Vietnamese producers can approach Chinese costs, and whether the state backs its biotechnology strategy with money and approvals. Most of these will be observable by 2028.

> **Scenarios, not forecasts.** The three states below are internally consistent pictures built on the milestones in this chapter. We do not assign probabilities. Each comes with signposts: observable events that would tell a reader which path is unfolding.

**Reading.**
- **Feed is the growth engine.** Reaching 30 to 32 Mt by 2030 from 21.5 Mt in 2024 means 5.7 to 6.9% growth a year, with feed output 8.5 to 10.5 Mt higher in 2030 than in 2024 (our calculation from [@MAC-28; @REG-54]) {VN-direct|Medium}. Every extra tonne needs protein, which today is almost entirely imported ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]). The feed scheme (Decision 1625/QD-TTg) indicates that the target covers livestock feed only [@NTS-23] {VN-direct|Medium}, which makes it look out of reach; see [[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]. Chapters 20 to 24 extend this outlook to 2050 ([[ch20-drivers-2050]] to [[ch24-vision-2050]]), and [[ch28-robust-moves]] turns it into moves.
- **Demand growth comes from diet, not headcount.** Population grows about 5% from 2025 to 2035 and peaks around 2049 [@GT-12] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **The fishmeal-soy gap is at a high.** The ratio of fishmeal to soybean meal prices widened from 3.8 in 2024 to 6.2 in August 2026 [@GT-15] {general|High}. Global fishmeal output was about 4.5 Mt in 2016 and wild catch is static, while aquafeed demand is projected to grow [@FM-06; @FM-35] {general|Medium}, which supports high prices, but monthly prices can fall back with Peruvian fishing seasons.
- **No official long-run feed price paths exist.** World Bank forecasts stop at 2027 [@GT-14] {general|High}. Any 2030 or 2035 price is a scenario assumption.

## 19.3 Three plausible states in 2035

### Scenario 1: Import price-taker

- **Picture.** Vietnam's alternative protein is mostly imported: Chinese single-cell protein, yeast protein and textured soy, and food ingredients approved in Singapore or Thailand. Domestic activity is *chay* processing, tofu and a few insect-meal plants. Feed protein imports grow with the feed industry.
- **Drivers.** The Food Safety Law passes without a workable new-food route. Chinese scale keeps landed prices below Vietnamese cost. Fishmeal falls back from its 2026 peak. Agritech venture capital stays near zero.
- **Signposts.** No defined new-food procedure in the law or decree by the end of 2027; rising imports of yeasts and protein preparations from China; no domestic extruder or fermentation plant above pilot scale announced by 2028; fishmeal below USD 1,800 per tonne for a full year.

### Scenario 2: Feed-first domestic build-out

- **Picture.** Several Vietnamese plants turn side streams into feed protein: cassava pulp in Tay Ninh, brewer's spent grain near Ho Chi Minh City and Hai Phong, seafood by-products in the Mekong Delta. Products are yeast or bacterial protein, duckweed and insect meal. Food applications stay small.
- **Drivers.** Fishmeal stays above about USD 2,000 per tonne. MAE keeps adding new ingredients to the feed lists. High-tech incentives reach fermentation plants. Development finance repeats the Entobel structure (private equity, a DFI and an offtake). E10 demand makes cassava roots costly, pushing processors towards residues.
- **Signposts.** A second insect or microbial-protein plant above 5,000 t a year reaches financial close by 2028; a feed-list update names microbial protein for aquafeed; a top-five aquafeed producer signs a multi-year offtake; fishmeal averages above USD 2,000 per tonne in 2027; feed output on track for 30 Mt in 2030.

### Scenario 3: Regulated regional hub

- **Picture.** Vietnam has a working new-food route that relies on Singapore, Korea and other reference regulators. It hosts contract fermentation for ASEAN brands, with Ho Chi Minh City and Dong Nai as the manufacturing base. Domestic brands sell hybrid and fermented products.
- **Drivers.** The new law defines new food and allows reliance. A decree makes the science-law sandbox usable for food. High-tech certification works for precision fermentation. Resolution 57 research money funds a national protein platform. Renewables make low-carbon power available.
- **Signposts.** A "new food" (*thực phẩm mới*) definition and procedure in the law or decree; the first sandbox approval for a food product; a foreign precision-fermentation or contract-manufacturing firm registers an investment in Vietnam; Vietnam aligns with an ASEAN novel-food reliance mechanism. A negative signpost: Thailand's planned public precision-fermentation pilot plant is built first and draws the same investors ([[ch03-regional]]).

Source for all three scenarios: AltProtein Vietnam synthesis of [@GT-10; @GT-11; @GT-15; @REG-12; @REG-47; @REG-54; @RGN-35; @CAP-01; @CAP-05; @ECO-05] and chapters 2 to 10, 26 and 27 {VN-direct\|Low}.

**The scenarios are not exclusive.** Scenario 2 can happen without Scenario 3, since feed rules are already more open than food rules. Scenario 3 is unlikely without at least part of Scenario 2, because the same fermentation skills, pilot capacity and investors are needed.

## 19.4 Four uncertainties to track

| Uncertainty | What to watch | Where | Threshold that matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does the food law open a workable route? | Text of the new law and its decree | National Assembly records; MOH drafts | A "new food" definition with a procedure and timelines |
| How wide is the fishmeal-soy gap? | Monthly fishmeal and soybean meal prices | World Bank Pink Sheet | Fishmeal above USD 2,000 per tonne; ratio above 5 |
| Can Vietnam approach Chinese cost? | Landed prices of Chinese microbial protein and textured soy (duty-free); Vietnamese pilot costs; delivered soy-flour prices | Customs data; company disclosures | Vietnamese cost within 20% of landed imports |
| Will the state fund and approve, not only plan? | Feed-list updates, sandbox approvals, fund investments, pilot-line funding | MAE, MOST, city fund announcements | At least one of each by 2028 |

There is no tariff cushion in the cost test: Chinese and ASEAN textured protein, isolates, gluten, yeast and single-cell protein enter at 0% under ACFTA, ATIGA and RCEP in 2025 to 2027 [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}, and China shipped textured protein and concentrates (HS 2106.10) to Vietnam at USD 1.24 per kg FOB in 2025, landing at about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Two regional events also matter: whether Thailand builds its public precision-fermentation pilot plant and contract-manufacturing network [@RGN-35] {VN-adjacent|Medium}, and what lies behind the September 2026 termination notice for a Thai biotechnology plant [@RGN-38] {VN-adjacent|Low}.

## 19.5 What this means

- **Investors:** the key regulatory events fall within 18 months. The fishmeal-soy ratio is a leading indicator for feed-protein deals; track it monthly.
- **Policy makers:** the Food Safety Law window is the one that closes: a new-food clause must be in the draft revised after the October 2026 first reading, by early 2027, before the vote considered in May 2027. The 2030 feed target implies a large new protein requirement that will be met by imports unless domestic options are ready.
- **Startups:** build on residues rather than cassava roots, which now compete with fuel ethanol; use the new province names in licences and documents.
- **Manufacturers:** the feed target implies feed output 8.5 to 10.5 Mt higher in 2030 than in 2024, with a volatile fishmeal price; multi-supplier protein strategies reduce exposure.
- **Research bodies and international bodies:** long-run projections for Vietnamese feed, meat and aquaculture are missing from public sources we could reach; a published baseline would help everyone plan.
