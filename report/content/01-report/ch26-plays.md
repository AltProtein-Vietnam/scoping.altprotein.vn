# 26. Seven plays and four public goods

**In brief.**

- A "play" is a specific business or programme that someone could start in Vietnam, with a named product, buyer and partner. We describe seven, all about protein for people. They range from a textured-protein line that could sell within two years to cultivated-seafood research that will not reach a plate this decade.
- Each play states what has to be true for it to work and the tests that would kill it, so readers can check it rather than take it on trust. We score the plays on seven criteria and publish the weights, so each audience can re-rank them.
- Three plays lead: **domestic textured plant protein (T1)**, a **cassava-starch-to-fermentation-sugar hub (T4)** and **shared pilot and tolling fermentation (T5)**. The hub is a cluster of plants on one site that turns part of the cassava starch Vietnam exports into sugar for food fermentation. T5 is a shared food-grade pilot line plus rented time on existing fermenters (tolling), which most fermentation plays need first.
- T1 and T4 are in the top three under every preset weighting; T5 is in the top three under five of the seven presets (our calculation).
- The textured-protein line is cheap to equip but earns its margin only if food-grade soy flour can be bought below about USD 700 to 940 per t. So it suits an incumbent with buyers and bank credit better than a venture-backed startup.
- Four public goods, mainly rules, a laboratory and data, would lower the cost of all the plays. We no longer recommend plays that make feed ingredients (section 26.6).
- In version 0.10 we screened 170 published solutions, including the Good Food Institute's 112-entry database. None displaces the leading plays. Three candidates are listed but not yet ranked: hybrid fish cakes and fish balls (T11), domestic food-grade soybean and mung bean (T12) and a characterised set of Vietnamese food strains (P7). Each must first pass one test: buyer demand, farm economics or a willing host (section 26.8).

> **Read with care.** [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]] checks these plays against demand evidence. The demand evidence suggests raising market pull for domestic textured plant protein (T1, from 4 to 5) and mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks (T10, from 3 to 4). It leaves the other plays unchanged. With those two scores T1 leads the balanced ranking. The scores on this page remain the supply-side scores; `play_demand_check.csv` holds the demand view and the demand-side kill tests.
>
> **New evidence on T1.** New cost and buyer evidence changes the T1 thesis. A domestic textured-protein line is an import-substitution plant, best owned by a *chay* (traditional vegetarian food) maker, a co-packer (a firm that makes products under another firm's brand) or an ingredient distributor. It is best financed with debt and trade finance, not venture equity. Chinese product enters duty-free, and the delivered price of food-grade soy flour decides the margin.
>
> Proposed score changes for T1 are recorded as text in [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]] and `play_demand_check.csv`: capital efficiency 4 to 5 and Vietnam advantage 3 to 2. They make no change to its balanced score, and T1 still leads the demand-view balanced ranking at 3.95. The scores in section 26.2 are unchanged.

> **How to use this chapter.** The scores are our judgement, based on the evidence in chapters 2 to 10. They are a starting point for discussion, not an investment rating. The data file `plays.csv` holds every field shown here, and `play_weight_presets.csv` holds the weights, so the website can let readers change them. The codes T1 to T10 and P1 to P6 are also listed in [[front-how-to-read]].

## 26.1 How the plays are scored

We score each play from 1 to 5 on seven criteria. The weighted score is each criterion's score times its weight, added up and divided by 100.

| Criterion | What a 5 means | What a 1 means |
|---|---|---|
| Market pull | Several named buyers already buy the imported or incumbent equivalent | No identifiable buyer or need |
| Vietnam advantage | A clear, hard-to-copy Vietnamese edge | Any neighbour does it as well or better |
| Technical readiness | Commercial elsewhere, skills present in Vietnam | Laboratory stage with major unknowns |
| Route to market | Ordinary food or listed feed rules apply | No legal route exists |
| Capital efficiency | First revenue for under a few million USD, or via tolling | Tens to hundreds of millions of USD before revenue |
| Defensibility | Function, logistics or intellectual property that imports cannot easily match | Commodity with cheaper imports |
| Public value | Large contribution to protein security or national capability | Little wider benefit |

## 26.3 The plays

### T1. Domestic textured plant protein

- **What.** Dry textured soy protein and soy-gluten granules and slices, made in Vietnam for *chay* brands, frozen-food makers and co-packers. High-moisture whole cuts come later.
- **Why Vietnam.** Every audited Vietnamese product with 10 g of protein or more per 100 g runs on imported textured soy, soy protein or gluten, much of it from China. We found no commercial extrusion in Vietnam, and ordinary food rules apply [@FORM-01; @FORM-02; @FORM-05; @FORM-11] {VN-direct|Medium}. There is no tariff shelter: Chinese, ASEAN and Korean textured protein enters at 0% under ASEAN's free trade agreements with China and Korea, the ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}. The pool a soy line can serve is up to about 12,800 t a year of imports under harmonised system (HS) code 2106.10 (2025; the code also covers concentrates and isolates, so the textured share is unknown) [@BUY-02; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **What has to be true.** Ex-factory cost at or below landed Chinese textured protein: USD 1.24 per kg free on board (FOB, the price at the exporting port) in 2025, and USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg landed (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. A supply of food-grade, low-temperature defatted soy flour below the breakeven price: about USD 700 to 940 per t delivered for a line making 1 tonne an hour (1 t/h) at 70% utilisation (our calculation). Buyers who value freshness, customisation and local supply.
- **Kill tests.** T1 fails if any of these is true:
  - delivered flour costs more than about USD 700 to 940 per t;
  - fewer than three buyers sign conditional orders totalling at least 2,000 t a year;
  - the Chinese landed price stays below Vietnamese full cost at 2,800 t a year (0.5 t/h at 70%) or 5,600 t (1 t/h at 70%), on 8,000 operating hours a year;
  - Indian textured soy (USD 0.98 per kg FOB in 2025) becomes the reference price;
  - no bank agrees to finance the supplier's receivables against an anchor buyer. Without an anchor, the working capital of USD 0.6 to 3.5 million has no named source [@SLK-18; @SLK-19] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **First customers.** An Nhien, Au Lac, Xuan Hong, LC Foods, Cholimex, CJ Foods Vietnam, Vissan, Thoai An, GN Foods (maker of at least one Co.op Select chay product) and other co-packers [@FORM-01; @CPC-15] {VN-direct|Medium}. The long tail of small chay makers can be reached through ingredient distributors such as Brenntag, VMC and TDGIMEX [@TIC-18; @BUY-36; @BUY-37] {VN-direct|Medium}. A 1 t/h line at 90% needs 26 to 131 buyers the size of Vissan's soy-protein use (our calculation).
- **Partners.** Extrusion-line suppliers; a soy crusher for a food-grade flake stream [@FORM-10]; the Food Industries Research Institute (FIRI) for pilot trials; a bank or trade-finance provider for flour and receivables. Three routes already exist under Vietnamese law (we describe instruments, not providers):
  - receivables finance or factoring against an anchor buyer (Circular 20/2024/TT-NHNN). Banks such as BIDV run supply-chain finance for small firms referred by an anchor company, advancing up to 85% of order value [@SLK-05; @SLK-19] {VN-direct|Medium};
  - a finance lease, or a sale and leaseback, of the line from a licensed leasing company (Circular 26/2024/TT-NHNN) [@SLK-01; @SLK-24] {VN-direct|High};
  - for a small or medium-sized owner, a guaranteed loan. The Ho Chi Minh City SME Credit Guarantee Fund caps guarantees at about VND 34 billion (about USD 1.3 million at 26,000 VND per USD) per customer, which covers most of a T1 line [@SLK-20] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Horizon and capital.** 0 to 2 years. Chinese lines for textured vegetable protein of 0.2 to 1.5 t/h with dryer are listed at USD 11,000 to 100,000 [@TIC-02; @TIC-03; @TIC-07] {general|Low}. By our calculation, a line installed in Vietnam costs USD 0.28 to 1.38 million, and working capital of USD 0.6 to 3.5 million is the larger need ([[ch09-economics]], [[app-s12-costs]]). The cost of a European-grade line is unverified (USD 2.0 to 5.6 million installed on the earlier equipment assumption).

> **New evidence on T1.** The T1 thesis changes. T1 is an import-substitution ingredient plant. It is best owned by a large chay maker, a group of chay makers or co-packers, an ingredient distributor or a soy processor. It is best financed with bank debt and trade finance rather than venture equity. It has no intellectual property. Its margin is the spread between the price of soy flour and the price of the textured product, and Chinese exporters can narrow it. It displaces almost no meat.
>
> On the benchmark demand path it supports about one 1 t/h line, or two 0.5 t/h lines, by 2035, not three or four. Market pull stays at 5 in the demand view because named buyers already buy the imported equivalent. See [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]] for the demand check and the proposed score changes; the scores above are unchanged.

### T4. Cassava starch to fermentation sugar hub

- **What.** Turn part of the cassava starch Vietnam exports into fermentation-grade glucose next to starch factories, and put food fermenters on the same sites. A hub here means a cluster of plants on one site.
- **Why Vietnam.** Vietnam shipped 2.15 Mt of cassava starch in 2023, 93.5% to China, while China imported only USD 0.16 million of glucose syrup from Vietnam in 2024 [@RGN-53; @RGN-55] {VN-direct|High}. Starch factories cluster in Tay Ninh and the Central Highlands ([[ch04-asset-map]]).
- **What has to be true.** Cassava prices settle after the 2025 to 2026 spike and the E10 fuel-ethanol roadmap (petrol with 10% ethanol) [@FS-08; @FS-11; @FS-12] {VN-direct|Medium}. At least one fermenter agrees to locate on the site. Wastewater and biogas infrastructure can serve fermentation.
- **Kill tests.** No fermenter commits within two years; swings in cassava prices rule out long-term sugar contracts.
- **First customers.** Domestic food fermenters (yeast, enzymes, koji and fungal foods, amino acids for food); foreign biomass or precision-fermentation food firms looking for a lower-cost ASEAN site ([[ch03-regional]]).
- **Horizon and capital.** 1 to 3 years. We have not estimated the capital: it is standard starch-industry equipment, and Vedan already built about 140,000 t a year of glucose capacity [@VCO-03] {VN-direct|Medium}.

### T5. Shared pilot and tolling fermentation

- **What.** A food-grade pilot line (hundreds of litres to a few cubic metres), plus negotiated tolling on existing industrial tanks, open to startups and researchers. Tolling means renting time on someone else's fermenter.
- **Why Vietnam.** Aerobic tanks (fermenters supplied with air) exist inside Vedan, Ajinomoto and Daesang [@VCO-05; @IND-08; @IND-11]. But we found no published tolling offer and no open pilot fermenter ([[ch05-industrial-base]]) [@INF-02; @INF-05] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **What has to be true.** An anchor host and an operator; enough users; public or development co-funding.
- **Kill tests.** No host accepts tolling terms; use stays below about a third of capacity in the first two years.
- **First customers.** Food startups working on T7 and T9; research groups; foreign firms testing Vietnamese feedstocks for food ingredients. Likely domestic users include YERA, which makes yeast protein for food in Nghe An, and Emmay, which makes mushroom and mycelium foods [@RDL-01; @AIS-39] {VN-direct|Low}. University teaching could also fill line time: Berkeley runs its bioprocess master's capstone on a pilot line with a 300 L bioreactor [@AML-32] {general|Medium}. ScaleUp Bio (Singapore) and EECi (Thailand) already compete for the same users [@RGN-10; @RGN-36] {general|Medium}.
- **Partners.** Saigon Hi-Tech Park [@INF-06], FIRI, the amino-acid producers, and an access arrangement with the pilot plant at Thailand's Eastern Economic Corridor of Innovation (EECi) [@RGN-36].
- **Horizon and capital.** 2 to 4 years; pilot-scale capital, not estimated for Vietnam (OQ-485). One benchmark: Metrosert (Estonia) is building an open, food-grade pilot plant with 50 L, 500 L and 5,000 L fermenters and drying, on a construction contract close to EUR 2 million (scope unclear) [@SLF-11] {general|Low}.

### T7. High-value, low-dose precision-fermentation ingredients

- **What.** Enzymes, heme or sweet proteins and specialty functional proteins, developed by Vietnamese teams and made under contract abroad until a domestic option exists. Low-dose flavour fats fit the same model: one Western brand has its fat made under contract by a Chinese microbial-oil producer [@SLF-09] {general|Medium}. Bulk fats do not (section 26.6).
- **Why Vietnam.** Vietnamese groups already express proteins in yeast ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]). Low-dose products escape the bulk-protein benchmark for titer, the concentration of product in the fermenter (our inference) [@SCI-01; @SCI-05] {general|High}.
- **What has to be true.** A product that is economic at 1 to 10 grams per litre (our assumption); freedom to operate [@SCI-08]; a foreign approval, or an ingredient use outside novel-food rules; a clear route on the status of genetically modified organisms ([[ch07-rules]]).
- **Kill tests.** No customer letter of intent after a sample programme; contract-manufacturing cost above the target price at pilot scale.
- **Partners.** ScaleUp Bio, EECi or Chinese contract development and manufacturing organisations [@RGN-10; @RGN-36; @RGN-24] {general|Medium}.
- **Horizon and capital.** 2 to 5 years; few fixed assets at first.

### T8. Cultivated seafood research

- **What.** Adapting fish cells to grow without serum, expressing growth factors, and preparing for regulation, with foreign partners. Two research tasks lead:
  - **Cheap media.** Low-cost hydrolysate media from Vietnamese soy, rice bran or fish by-products. In the published cost model, amino acids cost USD 19 per kg of cells at scale and growth factors USD 3, so amino acids are the larger cost line. A hydrolysate medium is the only modelled case that brings fed-batch cost down to USD 22 per kg, and its author says suitable hydrolysates "do not exist today" [@COST-47] {general|High}.
  - **Species.** Food-oriented muscle cell lines were published abroad for seven-band grouper (2024) and Asian seabass (2025), both farmed in Vietnam. We read these papers at metadata level only, so this is a candidate choice [@SLS-14; @SLS-15; @SLS-16] {general|Low}. Striped catfish stays a low-cost model species: its known lines are virology tools [@SCI-10; @SCI-12] {general|High}. Shrimp waits until a true continuous line exists.
- **Why Vietnam.** Vietnam exports about USD 2.2 billion of pangasius a year ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]) [@MAC-01]. Vietnamese groups express growth factors and culture stem cells ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]). A tissue-engineering laboratory at the Ho Chi Minh City University of Science cultured beef cells on a 3D-printed scaffold in 2022 (press report; no peer-reviewed output found) [@SLC-01] {VN-direct|Low}. Vinh Hoan has invested in regional cultivated-seafood firms [@CAP-16] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **What has to be true.** A food-suitable cell line from a Vietnamese species (none known for catfish or shrimp [@SCI-10; @SCI-12] {general|High}). The published cost model also assumes an engineered line with efficient metabolism [@COST-47] {general|High}. Five or more years of research funding; a scale-up partner.
- **Kill tests.** No serum-free, suspension-capable line after three years; no hydrolysate-based medium supports fish-cell growth over several passages within three years (OQ-521).
- **Partners.** Holders of the grouper and seabass lines (Pukyong National University, Thiruvalluvar University, James Cook University Singapore), with Nha Trang University or the Research Institute for Aquaculture No. 3 (RIA3) as hosts (OQ-496) [@SLS-13] {general|Low}.
- **Horizon and capital.** 5 to 10 years or more; research grants.

### T9. Koji and fungal foods on food-grade side streams

- **What.** Solid-state fermented foods and ingredients made with characterised *Aspergillus oryzae* or other food moulds. They grow on okara (the pulp left from soy milk and tofu), broken rice or brewer's spent grain.
- **Why Vietnam.** A *tương* (fermented soybean paste) tradition; local gene tools for *A. oryzae* [@RD-33; @RD-34] {VN-direct|Medium}. Koji on food substrates goes through ordinary self-declaration ([[ch07-rules]]). The Vietnam Type Culture Collection (VTCC) holds more than 15,000 strains, 3,869 of them documented [@SLF-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. A Vietnamese screen found yeasts from fermented foods with 62 to 67% protein in flasks, but without safety data [@SLF-04] {VN-direct|Low}. Abroad, koji mycelium deli meats are sold commercially in the United States; their maker raised USD 50 million by 2023 [@AML-29] {general|Medium}.
- **Partners.** VTCC; Phenikaa University; tofu and soy-milk makers and breweries for side streams. Characterised strains are candidate public good P7 (section 26.8).
- **What has to be true.** A characterised strain without risk of the mould toxins cyclopiazonic acid or 3-nitropropionic acid [@SCI-19] {general|High}; a food-grade substrate supply; acceptance in *chay* and mainstream dishes.
- **Kill tests.** Strain safety data cannot be obtained; sensory trials fail with target buyers.
- **Horizon and capital.** 1 to 3 years; low.

### T10. Mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks

- **What.** Concentrates or isolates from mung bean and rice, replacing imported pea protein in high-protein plant drinks and yoghurts.
- **Why Vietnam.** A large plant-milk industry. Vinamilk's high-protein line uses pea protein, for which no Vietnamese producer was found [@FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low}. Ordinary food rules apply.
- **What has to be true.** Functional and sensory parity; cost near imported pea protein; a buyer specification; reliable mung bean supply (65% of imports from Myanmar [@FORM-08]); honest protein-quality claims (human mung bean digestibility is about 20% below pig values [@SCI-42] {general|High}).
- **Raw material must fit the price.** At 2023 to 2025 import prices, mung beans alone cost USD 3.0 to 4.9 per kg of isolate, assuming 60 to 80% protein recovery. That is above Chinese pea protein at USD 2 to 3 per kg (our calculation) [@SLP-14; @BUY-09] {VN-direct|Medium}. So a mung bean line needs a paid starch co-product, or a higher-priced job such as replacing egg white powder ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]], TPP-05). Rice protein from broken rice costs USD 5.1 to 6.6 per kg of product in raw material alone, so it works only as a co-product of a rice syrup or starch line. We found no such line in Vietnam (our calculation) [@SLP-08; @SLP-01] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Kill tests.** The buyer rejects on taste or stability; cost exceeds imported pea protein by more than buyers will pay for local sourcing; no buyer for the mung bean starch co-product at a price that brings raw-material cost inside the TPP-05 band (OQ-479); for the rice arm, no rice syrup or starch host within two years (OQ-478).
- **First customers.** Vinamilk, TH and other plant-milk makers. A cheap first test is a buyer-sponsored university project that characterises rice or mung bean protein against the buyer's pea-protein specification. A Brazilian egg producer used a Berkeley student team this way in 2020 [@AML-05] {general|Medium}.
- **Horizon and capital.** 2 to 4 years; moderate, not estimated.

## 26.4 Four public goods

These are not businesses. They lower the cost and risk of every play above, and most are cheap. [[ch27-policy-options]] sets out who would do each. DIAAS is a measure of protein quality.

| ID | Public good | What it unlocks | Policy options |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Proportionate new-food procedure | T7, T9 and food uses of fungal biomass | PO-015, PO-019 |
| P4 | Protein-quality and function laboratory (amino acids, DIAAS, functional properties) | T1, T9, T10, and any novel-food dossier | ([[ch27-policy-options]]) |
| P5 | Yearly by-product atlas (volume, location, price, composition) | T4, T9 | ([[ch27-policy-options]]) |
| P6 | Open cost model and public price series for food protein ingredients | All fermentation plays; investor screening | ([[ch27-policy-options]]) |

Version 0.10 adds three details from the solutions screen ([[app-s14-solutions-screen]]):

- **P1.** For cultivated food, the 2023 FAO and WHO framework for the safety of cell-based food gives a ready basis for a reliance lane, alongside Singapore's approvals [@SLC-05; @SLC-06; @RGN-04] {general|Medium}.
- **P4 now covers function as well as quality.** Buyers specify function: solubility, water and oil holding, emulsifying, foaming and gelling. We found no fee-for-service functional testing in Vietnam [@SLP-17] {VN-direct|Low}. A standard panel can follow Embrapa's open method guide of 2022 [@SLP-10] {general|High}, plus buyer tests such as gel strength in a sausage model and stability in ultra-high-temperature (UHT) drinks. It would publish an open dataset for mung bean, broken rice, rice bran, peanut, sesame and okara, linked to P5 (OQ-480).
- **P6 can start from a public dataset.** The GFI and Hawkwood techno-economic dataset of 2025 is open, so new effort can go to Vietnamese inputs and the domestic price series [@SLF-10] {general|Medium}. That series should include farm-gate prices of soybean and mung bean, and import unit values of food beans by origin [@AQF-20; @SEC-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 26.5 Sequencing: a portfolio view

- **Now to 2028.** T1 as a business, in a debt-financed plant owned by an incumbent or a small or medium-sized firm; T4 if a fermenter commits; T9 as a small pilot; P1 through the Food Safety Law; T5 designed and funded.
- **2028 to 2031.** T5 in operation; T10 if a drinks buyer writes a specification; T7 through contract manufacturing.
- **2031 onward.** T8 if a food-suitable cell line exists and a regional approval route is working.

## 26.6 What we do not recommend, and why

### Feed-ingredient plays are retired

The study now concentrates on protein for people, so we no longer treat feed ingredients as something to make. We retired three feed-ingredient plays: functional microbial feed ingredients for shrimp and pangasius (T2), bulk microbial protein for fishmeal replacement (T3) and duckweed on aquaculture effluent for feed (T6). We also retired the two public goods that served them: aquafeed raw-material clarity (P2) and public feed-trial capacity (P3). Their earlier record, with scores, stays in `plays.csv` and [[app-m5-changelog]]. We kept the codes of the other plays and public goods, so the numbering has gaps.

We also do not recommend replacing soybean meal in pig and poultry feed with fermentation protein. The price gap is 4.6 to 5.1 times at the low end of our cost range ([[ch09-economics]]).

### Other routes we do not recommend

- **Premium imported-style consumer analogues as a first business.** They sell at an order of magnitude above chicken and egg protein and appear only in a few premium stores ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]], [[ch09-economics]]).
- **Commodity precision-fermented dairy or egg proteins made in Vietnam.** Titers, rules and missing infrastructure all point the other way ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **Cultivated-meat production.** No cell line and no route. Factory cost alone is 7 to 15 times the shop price of lean pork or chicken breast. On the cost model's own doubling from factory to shelf, the gap is about 15 to 29 times (our calculation) [@COST-47; @COST-39; @COST-40] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **Bulk fats made by fermentation.** Even an ideal microbial-oil plant would sell at USD 1.20 to 1.81 per kg, against palm oil at USD 1.12 and coconut oil at USD 1.85 per kg in August 2026 [@SLF-08; @FM-01] {general|Medium}. Only low-dose flavour fats make sense, bought in or made under contract as in T7 [@SLF-09] {general|Medium}.
- **Cultivated or fermentation-made surimi.** Vietnamese frozen surimi sold for USD 1.67 to 2.24 per kg in 2025 [@SLS-05] {VN-direct|Medium}. That is below GFI's best-case cultivated cost of USD 6.43 per kg, and far below modelled cultivated costs [@GSD-01; @COST-47] {general|Medium}. A plant-protein blend is the only surimi route we propose (T11, section 26.8).

## 26.7 What this means

- **Investors:** T1 is near-term, but as bank debt and trade finance for a plant owned by an incumbent or a small or medium-sized firm, not a venture thesis. In Vietnam, trade finance means receivables finance against a large anchor buyer, finance leases for the equipment and SME guarantees (section 26.3). Leasing covers equipment only; the larger working-capital need depends on an anchor buyer [@SLK-01; @SLK-05; @SLK-19] {VN-direct|Medium}. T4 needs a strategic partner and development capital. Use the kill tests as diligence questions, starting with a delivered flour quote for T1.
- **Startups:** T1 and T9 can reach revenue on today's rules. Build the buyer specification (T1, T10) before the plant. For T1, bring in a chay maker or distributor as co-owner or anchor buyer, and start with flour quotes and conditional orders.
- **Manufacturers:** you are the natural owner of T1 and the first customer for T10. The cheapest way to start one of them is pooled offtake from two or three chay makers behind one line, a letter of intent or a joint trial. Pool the offtake through parallel supply contracts between each buyer and the line owner, or through a cooperative of at least five members (Law on Cooperatives 2023). Competing chay makers should not agree among themselves on the price they pay: the Law on Competition 2018 bans price-fixing agreements between firms in the same market (our reading, not legal advice) [@SLK-08; @SLK-09] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Policy makers:** P1 costs little and unlocks several plays. T4 and T5 are where provincial and national investment promotion can act.
- **Research bodies:** T8, T9 and the public goods P4 to P6 are research-led.
- **International bodies:** T4 and T5 fit development finance and technical assistance.

## 26.8 Three candidates from the solutions screen

In version 0.10 we screened 170 published solutions for Vietnam: the 112 entries of the Good Food Institute's Solutions Database, and 58 publications, research outputs and grants from three other sources ([[app-s14-solutions-screen]]). Most were already covered by the plays above, or were frontier work that Vietnam would buy rather than make. None displaces T1, T4 or T5.

Three candidates came out of the screen. We list them here, with scores for comparison, but do not rank them yet. Each still fails a test the active plays have passed: buyer demand, farm economics or a willing host. Their full cards are in `plays.csv`.

**T11. Hybrid fish cakes and fish balls.**
- **The idea.** Makers of fish cakes, fish balls and fish tofu (*chả cá*, *cá viên*, *đậu hũ cá*) replace 10 to 30% of the fish protein with plant protein, and print the fish share on the label.
- **Why Vietnam.** Surimi and fish-cake exports passed USD 344 million in 2025, through more than 100 exporters [@SLS-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. The wild fish behind them is being squeezed. The wild catch is planned to fall from 3.83 Mt in 2025 to 2.8 Mt by 2030 [@MAC-15; @NTS-08] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection}, and exporters already report raw-material shortages [@SLS-04; @SLS-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. Plant protein costs 3.6 to 7.5 times less than surimi per kg of protein in 2025 (our calculation, on an assumed surimi dry matter) [@SLS-05] {VN-direct|Low}. It needs no new plant.
- **Why it is not ranked.** Its score comes from ease of entry, not demand. Taste panels abroad accepted blends below 30 to 40% replacement but rejected them above it [@SLS-07] {general|Medium}. A leading Korean maker, Dongwon F&B, is moving to 80% fish or more [@SLS-11] {general|Medium}.
- **Effect on the ranking if scored.** It would place third or fourth under every preset and push T5 out of the top three under four presets (our calculation).

**T12. Domestic food-grade soybean and mung bean.**
- **The idea.** Contract-grown, non-GM, food-grade beans for soy milk, tofu and plant drinks.
- **Why Vietnam.** Soy foods carry about 181,000 t of protein a year (2023) on beans that are 97.5% imported [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. At its 2010 planted area, domestic soybean would supply 76% to 95% of 2025 food soybean use (our calculation) [@AQF-20] {VN-direct|Low}. Vinasoy already breeds food varieties [@AIB-06] {VN-direct|Medium}. It is the one in-scope route we found that raises the domestic share of protein people already eat at that scale.
- **The constraint.** Domestic beans cost about 1.4 to 2.3 times imports [@AQF-20; @SEC-05] {VN-direct|Low}. The method comes from The Protein Project's legume roadmap for Europe, which pairs a buyer-led taskforce with a farm-economics model [@PPE-05] {general|Medium}.

**P7. Characterised Vietnamese food-strain set.**
- **The idea.** Sequence and screen 20 to 50 moulds and yeasts already used in Vietnamese ferments, then supply them on one standard licence.
- **Why it matters.** It removes T9's first kill test, strain safety data.
- **Why now.** Decree 43/2026 cut the access licence for genetic resources to about 19 working days at provincial level (our calculation). Benefit sharing of at least 1% of product revenue still applies [@SLF-02] {VN-direct|High}. We found 41 studies of microbes in Vietnamese ferments, but none on the safety of food strains [@SLF-03] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Two more proposals were folded into existing moves rather than listed. A buyer-set product challenge lab at Vietnamese food-technology faculties joins RM-09. Open flavour profiles of Vietnam's farmed fish join RM-14 ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
