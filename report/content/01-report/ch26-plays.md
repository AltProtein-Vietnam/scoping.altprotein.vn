# 26. Seven plays and four public goods

**In brief.**

- A "play" is a specific business or programme someone could start in Vietnam, with a named product, buyer and partner. We describe seven, all about protein for people.
- Each play states what has to be true and the tests that would kill it, so readers can check it. We publish the scores and weights, so each reader can re-rank them.
- Three plays lead: **domestic textured plant protein (T1)**, a **hub that turns cassava starch into fermentation sugar (T4)**, and **shared pilot and tolling fermentation (T5)**, where firms rent time on someone else's fermenter.
- The textured-protein line is cheap to equip but pays only if food-grade soy flour costs below about USD 700 to 940 per t. So it suits an established firm with buyers and bank credit better than a startup.
- Four public goods, mainly rules, a laboratory and data, would lower the cost of every play.
- We screened 170 published solutions; none displaces the leading plays. Three candidates are listed but not yet ranked (section 26.8).

> **How to use this chapter.** The scores are our judgement from chapters 2 to 10, a starting point for discussion, not an investment rating. Demand evidence in [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]] would raise T1's market pull from 4 to 5 and T10's from 3 to 4; with those, T1 leads the balanced ranking. The scores below are the supply-side view.

## 26.1 How the plays are scored

We score each play from 1 to 5 on seven criteria. The weighted score is each score times its weight, added up and divided by 100.

| Criterion | What a 5 means | What a 1 means |
|---|---|---|
| Market pull | Several named buyers already buy the imported or existing equivalent | No identifiable buyer |
| Vietnam advantage | A clear, hard-to-copy Vietnamese edge | Any neighbour does it as well |
| Technical readiness | Commercial elsewhere, skills in Vietnam | Laboratory stage |
| Route to market | Ordinary food rules apply | No legal route |
| Capital efficiency | First revenue for under a few million USD, or by renting tanks | Tens to hundreds of millions before revenue |
| Defensibility | Function, logistics or patents that imports cannot match | A commodity with cheaper imports |
| Public value | Large contribution to protein security or national capability | Little wider benefit |

## 26.3 The plays

### T1. Domestic textured plant protein

- **What.** Dry textured soy and soy-gluten pieces, made in Vietnam for *chay* (vegetarian food) brands, frozen-food makers and co-packers. Whole-cut products come later.
- **Why Vietnam.** Every audited Vietnamese product with 10 g of protein or more per 100 g runs on imported soy or gluten, much of it from China. Nobody in Vietnam makes textured soy, and ordinary food rules apply [@FORM-01; @FORM-02; @FORM-05; @FORM-11] {VN-direct|Medium}. There is no tariff shelter: Chinese and Southeast Asian product enters duty-free [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}. The import pool a line can serve is up to about 12,800 t a year [@BUY-02; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **What has to be true.** Factory cost at or below the Chinese landed price, about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Food-grade soy flour delivered below about USD 700 to 940 per t. Buyers who value freshness, custom products and local supply.
- **Kill tests.** T1 fails if flour costs more than that; if fewer than three buyers sign conditional orders for 2,000 t a year; if Chinese or cheaper Indian product stays below Vietnamese cost; or if no bank will finance the supplier against an anchor buyer [@SLK-18; @SLK-19] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **First customers.** Chay makers and frozen-food makers such as An Nhien, Au Lac, Xuan Hong, LC Foods, Cholimex, CJ Foods Vietnam, Vissan and Thoai An, and co-packers such as GN Foods [@FORM-01; @CPC-15] {VN-direct|Medium}. Smaller chay makers can be reached through ingredient distributors [@TIC-18; @BUY-36; @BUY-37] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Partners and finance.** Extrusion-line suppliers; a soy crusher for food-grade flour [@FORM-10]; the national food research institute for trials; and a bank. Vietnam already offers invoice finance against an anchor buyer, equipment leases, and loan guarantees for small firms of up to about USD 1.3 million [@SLK-05; @SLK-19; @SLK-01; @SLK-24; @SLK-20] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Horizon and capital.** 0 to 2 years. Chinese lines list at USD 11,000 to 100,000 [@TIC-02; @TIC-03; @TIC-07] {general|Low}. Installed in Vietnam, a line costs USD 0.28 to 1.38 million, and working capital of USD 0.6 to 3.5 million is the larger need (our calculation; [[ch09-economics]]).

> **The T1 thesis.** T1 is an import-replacement ingredient plant, best owned by a chay maker, a group of chay makers, a co-packer, a distributor or a soy processor, and financed with bank debt rather than venture equity. It has no patents. Its margin is the gap between the price of soy flour and the price of the textured product, which Chinese exporters can squeeze. It displaces almost no meat. On the benchmark demand path, it supports about one 1 t-an-hour line by 2035 ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

### T4. Cassava starch to fermentation sugar hub

- **What.** Turn part of the cassava starch Vietnam exports into fermentation-grade glucose next to the starch factories, and put food fermenters on the same site.
- **Why Vietnam.** Vietnam shipped 2.15 Mt of cassava starch in 2023, mostly to China, but almost no glucose [@RGN-53; @RGN-55] {VN-direct|High}. Starch factories cluster in Tay Ninh and the Central Highlands ([[ch04-asset-map]]).
- **What has to be true.** Cassava prices settle after the 2025 to 2026 spike and the fuel-ethanol mandate [@FS-08; @FS-11; @FS-12] {VN-direct|Medium}. At least one fermenter agrees to locate on the site.
- **Kill tests.** No fermenter commits within two years, or cassava price swings rule out long-term sugar contracts.
- **First customers.** Domestic food fermenters (yeast, enzymes, koji, amino acids), and foreign fermentation firms seeking a cheaper Southeast Asian site ([[ch03-regional]]).
- **Horizon and capital.** 1 to 3 years. Standard starch-industry equipment; Vedan already built large glucose capacity [@VCO-03] {VN-direct|Medium}.

### T5. Shared pilot and tolling fermentation

- **What.** A food-grade pilot line, from hundreds of litres to a few cubic metres, plus rented time on existing industrial tanks, open to startups and researchers.
- **Why Vietnam.** Large air-fed tanks exist inside Vedan, Ajinomoto and Daesang, but we found no rental offer and no open pilot fermenter ([[ch05-industrial-base]]) [@VCO-05; @IND-08; @IND-11; @INF-02; @INF-05] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **What has to be true.** A host and an operator; enough users; public or development co-funding.
- **Kill tests.** No host accepts rental terms, or use stays below a third of capacity in the first two years.
- **First customers.** Food startups such as YERA (yeast protein) and Emmay (mushroom foods); research groups; foreign firms testing Vietnamese raw materials [@RDL-01; @AIS-39] {VN-direct|Low}. University teaching can also fill line time [@AML-32] {general|Medium}. Singapore's and Thailand's pilot plants compete for the same users [@RGN-10; @RGN-36] {general|Medium}.
- **Partners.** Saigon Hi-Tech Park [@INF-06], the national food research institute, the amino-acid makers, and an access deal with Thailand's public pilot plant [@RGN-36].
- **Horizon and capital.** 2 to 4 years; not estimated for Vietnam. An Estonian open food-grade pilot plant with tanks up to 5,000 L has a building contract near EUR 2 million [@SLF-11] {general|Low}.

### T7. High-value, low-dose precision-fermentation ingredients

- **What.** Enzymes, heme or sweet proteins and specialty proteins developed by Vietnamese teams and made under contract abroad until a domestic option exists. Flavour fats used in small doses fit the same model [@SLF-09] {general|Medium}.
- **Why Vietnam.** Vietnamese groups already make proteins in yeast ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]). Low-dose products escape the yield problem of bulk protein (our inference) [@SCI-01; @SCI-05] {general|High}.
- **What has to be true.** A product that pays at low yields; freedom to operate [@SCI-08]; a foreign approval or a use outside novel-food rules; a clear GMO route ([[ch07-rules]]).
- **Kill tests.** No customer letter of intent after samples, or contract-making cost above the target price.
- **Partners.** Contract manufacturers in Singapore, Thailand or China [@RGN-10; @RGN-36; @RGN-24] {general|Medium}.
- **Horizon and capital.** 2 to 5 years; few fixed assets at first.

### T8. Cultivated seafood research

- **What.** Research with foreign partners on growing fish cells without serum, on growth factors and on regulation. Two tasks lead:
  - **Cheap growth media** from Vietnamese soy, rice bran or fish by-products. Amino acids are the largest medium cost, and the author of the main cost model says suitable cheap media "do not exist today" [@COST-47] {general|High}.
  - **Species.** Food-oriented cell lines exist abroad for grouper and Asian seabass, both farmed in Vietnam. We read only their titles and authors, so this is a candidate choice [@SLS-14; @SLS-15; @SLS-16] {general|Low}. Catfish is a cheap model species; shrimp waits for a true cell line [@SCI-10; @SCI-12] {general|High}.
- **Why Vietnam.** Vietnam exports about USD 2.2 billion of pangasius a year, and has groups working on growth factors and stem cells ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]) [@MAC-01; @SLC-01; @CAP-16] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Kill tests.** No serum-free line that grows in suspension after three years, or no cheap medium that supports fish cells.
- **Partners.** Holders of the grouper and seabass lines, with Nha Trang University or a national aquaculture institute as hosts [@SLS-13] {general|Low}.
- **Horizon and capital.** 5 to 10 years or more; research grants.

### T9. Koji and fungal foods on food-grade side streams

- **What.** Fermented foods and ingredients made with characterised koji mould or other food moulds, grown on okara (soy pulp), broken rice or brewer's spent grain.
- **Why Vietnam.** A *tương* (fermented soybean paste) tradition, and local genetic tools for koji mould [@RD-33; @RD-34] {VN-direct|Medium}. Koji on food ingredients goes through ordinary self-declaration ([[ch07-rules]]). A national culture collection holds over 15,000 strains [@SLF-01; @SLF-04] {VN-direct|Medium}. Koji-based deli meats sell in the US [@AML-29] {general|Medium}.
- **What has to be true.** A strain proven free of mould toxins [@SCI-19] {general|High}; steady food-grade side streams; acceptance in chay and everyday dishes.
- **Kill tests.** Strain safety data cannot be obtained, or taste trials fail with target buyers.
- **Partners.** The national culture collection; Phenikaa University; tofu makers and breweries. Characterised strains are candidate public good P7 (section 26.8).
- **Horizon and capital.** 1 to 3 years; low.

### T10. Mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks

- **What.** Concentrates or isolates from mung bean and rice, replacing imported pea protein in high-protein plant drinks and yoghurts.
- **Why Vietnam.** A large plant-milk industry. Vinamilk's high-protein line uses pea protein, which nobody makes in Vietnam [@FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **What has to be true.** Taste and function as good as pea; cost near imported pea protein; a buyer specification; reliable mung bean supply, mostly imported from Myanmar [@FORM-08]; honest protein-quality claims [@SCI-42] {general|High}.
- **Raw material must fit the price.** Mung beans alone cost USD 3.0 to 4.9 per kg of isolate, above Chinese pea protein at USD 2 to 3 (our calculation) [@SLP-14; @BUY-09] {VN-direct|Medium}. So a mung bean line needs a paid buyer for its starch, or a better-paid job such as replacing egg white ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]). Rice protein pays only as a by-product of a rice syrup or starch line, and we found none in Vietnam [@SLP-08; @SLP-01] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Kill tests.** The buyer rejects it on taste or stability; it costs far more than imported pea; no buyer for the starch; no rice host within two years.
- **First customers.** Vinamilk, TH and other plant-milk makers. A cheap first test is a buyer-sponsored university project [@AML-05] {general|Medium}.
- **Horizon and capital.** 2 to 4 years; moderate.

## 26.4 Four public goods

These are not businesses. They lower the cost and risk of every play, and most are cheap ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

| ID | Public good | What it unlocks | Policy options |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Proportionate new-food procedure | T7, T9 and food uses of fungal protein | PO-015, PO-019 |
| P4 | Protein-quality and function laboratory | T1, T9, T10, and any novel-food application | ([[ch27-policy-options]]) |
| P5 | Yearly by-product atlas (volume, location, price, composition) | T4, T9 | ([[ch27-policy-options]]) |
| P6 | Open cost model and public price series for protein ingredients | All fermentation plays; investor screening | ([[ch27-policy-options]]) |

Three details from the solutions screen:
- **P1.** For cultivated food, a 2023 international safety framework from FAO and the World Health Organization, alongside Singapore's approvals, gives a ready basis for relying on trusted regulators [@SLC-05; @SLC-06; @RGN-04] {general|Medium}.
- **P4 should test function as well as quality:** solubility, binding, foaming and gelling, which buyers specify. We found no such testing service in Vietnam [@SLP-17] {VN-direct|Low}. An open method guide already exists [@SLP-10] {general|High}.
- **P6 can start from an open global cost dataset,** so effort can go to Vietnamese inputs and a domestic price series, including farm-gate bean prices [@SLF-10; @AQF-20; @SEC-05] {general|Medium}.

## 26.5 Sequencing: a portfolio view

- **Now to 2028.** T1 as a debt-financed plant owned by an established firm; T4 if a fermenter commits; T9 as a small pilot; P1 through the Food Safety Law; T5 designed and funded.
- **2028 to 2031.** T5 running; T10 if a drinks buyer writes a specification; T7 through contract manufacturing.
- **2031 onward.** T8 if a food cell line exists and a regional approval route works.

## 26.6 What we do not recommend, and why

### Feed-ingredient plays are retired

The study concentrates on protein for people, so we no longer treat feed ingredients as something to make. That is why play and public-good numbers have gaps. We also do not recommend replacing soybean meal in pig and poultry feed with fermentation protein: the price gap is about five times ([[ch09-economics]]).

### Other routes we do not recommend

- **Premium imported-style meat copies as a first business.** They cost ten times more than chicken and egg protein ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]], [[ch09-economics]]).
- **Commodity precision-fermented dairy or egg proteins made in Vietnam.** Yields, rules and missing infrastructure all point the other way ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **Cultivated-meat production.** No cell line, no legal route, and costs 15 to 29 times the shop price of pork or chicken (our calculation) [@COST-47; @COST-39; @COST-40] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Bulk fats made by fermentation.** Even an ideal plant would cost more than palm oil [@SLF-08; @FM-01; @SLF-09] {general|Medium}.
- **Cultivated or fermentation-made surimi.** Vietnamese surimi sells for about USD 2 per kg, far below any cultivated cost [@SLS-05; @GSD-01; @COST-47] {VN-direct|Medium}. A plant-protein blend is the only surimi route we propose (T11, section 26.8).

## 26.7 What this means

- **Investors:** T1 is near-term, but as bank debt and trade finance for a plant owned by an established firm, not a venture thesis. The anchor buyer matters most for working capital [@SLK-01; @SLK-05; @SLK-19] {VN-direct|Medium}. T4 needs a strategic partner and development capital. Use the kill tests as diligence questions, starting with a delivered flour quote.
- **Startups:** T1 and T9 can earn revenue under today's rules. Write the buyer specification before building the plant. For T1, bring in a chay maker or distributor as co-owner or anchor buyer.
- **Manufacturers:** you are the natural owner of T1 and the first customer for T10. The cheapest start is pooled orders from two or three chay makers behind one line, through separate supply contracts or a cooperative. Competing makers must not agree among themselves on the price they pay, which competition law bans (our reading, not legal advice) [@SLK-08; @SLK-09] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Policy makers:** P1 costs little and unlocks several plays. T4 and T5 are where investment promotion can act.
- **Researchers:** T8, T9 and the public goods P4 to P6 are research-led.
- **International bodies:** T4 and T5 fit development finance and technical help.

## 26.8 Three candidates from the solutions screen

We screened 170 published solutions for Vietnam, including all 112 entries of the Good Food Institute's solutions database. Most were already covered by the plays above, or were frontier work Vietnam would buy rather than make. None displaces T1, T4 or T5.

Three candidates came out of the screen. We show their scores for comparison but do not rank them yet. Each still has to pass a test the active plays have passed.

| ID | Candidate | Market | Advantage | Readiness | Route | Capital | Defensibility | Public value | Balanced score | Test before ranking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T11 | Hybrid fish cakes and fish balls | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 2 | 3 | 3.50 | A maker trial and a taste panel at 10% to 30% plant protein |
| T12 | Domestic food-grade soybean and mung bean | 3 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 2 | 4 | 3.50 | A farm-economics test and conditional buyer contracts |
| P7 | Characterised Vietnamese food-strain set | | | | | | | | | A host collection agrees standard licence terms, and three firms ask for strains |

Source: AltProtein Vietnam scoring {VN-direct\|Low}.

**T11. Hybrid fish cakes and fish balls.**
- **The idea.** Fish-cake makers replace 10% to 30% of the fish protein with plant protein, and print the fish share.
- **Why Vietnam.** Surimi and fish-cake exports passed USD 344 million in 2025 [@SLS-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. The wild catch is planned to fall from 3.83 Mt to 2.8 Mt by 2030, and exporters already report shortages [@MAC-15; @NTS-08; @SLS-04; @SLS-03] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection}. Plant protein costs 3.6 to 7.5 times less than surimi per kg of protein (our calculation) [@SLS-05] {VN-direct|Low}. It needs no new plant.
- **Why it is not ranked.** Its score comes from ease of entry, not demand. Taste panels abroad rejected blends above 30% to 40%, and a leading Korean maker is moving to 80% fish or more [@SLS-07; @SLS-11] {general|Medium}.

**T12. Domestic food-grade soybean and mung bean.**
- **The idea.** Contract-grown, non-GM, food-grade beans for soy milk, tofu and plant drinks.
- **Why Vietnam.** Soy foods carry about 181,000 t of protein a year on beans that are 97.5% imported [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Returning to the 2010 soybean area would cover most food soybean use (our calculation) [@AQF-20; @AIB-06] {VN-direct|Low}. It is the one route we found that raises the domestic share of protein people already eat at that scale.
- **The constraint.** Domestic beans cost about 1.4 to 2.3 times imports [@AQF-20; @SEC-05; @PPE-05] {VN-direct|Low}.

**P7. Characterised Vietnamese food-strain set.**
- **The idea.** Sequence and screen 20 to 50 moulds and yeasts already used in Vietnamese ferments, and supply them on one standard licence.
- **Why it matters.** It removes T9's first kill test, strain safety.
- **Why now.** Access licences now take about 19 working days, though benefit sharing still applies [@SLF-02] {VN-direct|High}. We found no study on the safety of Vietnamese food strains [@SLF-03] {VN-direct|Medium}.
