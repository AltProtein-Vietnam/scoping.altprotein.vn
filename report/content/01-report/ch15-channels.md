# 15. Where protein is bought and eaten: retail, foodservice and institutions

**In brief.**
- **Channels decide what form a new protein can take.** Traditional markets and small grocers still take 83% of Vietnam's USD 55.5 billion food retail. A chilled or frozen product cannot follow meat into these markets. It fits the frozen *chay* (vegetarian) aisle of modern stores, retailers' own brands made by existing contract makers, or kitchens. Two chains, Bach Hoa Xanh and WinCommerce, run about 8,750 stores between them.
- **Restaurants have not shown that meat-free products sell.** Foodservice is large (USD 26.6 billion) and fragmented. Chains have tried meat-free items for eight years without disclosing a success. Most regional startups that put their own meat analogue (a plant product made to imitate meat) on menus went quiet.
- **Factory canteens buy protein on price.** They serve up to about 1.2 billion shift meals a year, mostly at VND 20,000 to 35,000, and put tofu in 9% of protein dishes. Kitchens already buy tofu and dry soy-gluten pieces at VND 4,000 to 9,500 per 20 g of protein, so only an ingredient that beats those fits.
- **Schools are the wrong place to start with a branded meat replacement.** National guidance (Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT, 2025) asks for plant protein twice a week, but menus use it on 15% of days, mostly as tofu beside meat. A plant ingredient there replaces tofu, not meat. Only a labelled blend in minced-meat and fish-cake dishes, served on 28.5% of days, removes meat.
- **Some channels add little volume.** Upland boarding kitchens spend their new public meal money mostly on pork. Corporate commitments are not a volume lever, and Nha Trang's hotels are a showcase, not a volume channel.

Four things follow for a new protein {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}:

- **Two listings reach most modern grocery stores.** Listings at Bach Hoa Xanh and WinCommerce reach about 8,750 stores (3,612 plus 5,141, our sum) [@CHN-12; @CHN-15]. All foreign-owned hypermarkets and supermarkets together number fewer than 200 (our count). Central Retail runs 43 GO! hypermarkets, 9 Tops and 23 Lan Chi stores, Lotte Mart 17 stores, and AEON 9 malls, with 30 planned by fiscal 2030 [@AIB-31; @AIB-33; @AIB-34] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The two big chains grow mainly through minimarts and in rural areas, so modern trade is no longer only an urban premium channel [@CHN-12; @CHN-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Tax and invoicing rules from 2025 that require traceable transactions are expected to favour modern trade further [@CHN-05] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.
- **The frozen shelf already exists.** Large stores such as Lotte Mart Nha Trang have signposted frozen chay sections of two or more freezer door units. SG Food, Vissan, CJ Cau Tre, bibigo and private labels stock them [@FORM-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. We found no current figure for household freezer ownership. Formats that need no cold chain (dry textured protein, retort pouches, cans) are the only way into the 650,000 traditional outlets [@CHN-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.
- **Private label reaches that shelf without a consumer brand.** Private label means a retailer's own brand. Saigon Co.op sells more than 2,500 private-label products from more than 100 Vietnamese makers, at 15 to 30% below comparable brands. One certified co-packer (a firm that makes products under another firm's brand), GN Foods, makes all five Co.op Select frozen chay dishes. Lotte Mart sells its own Choice L chay [@AIB-28; @CPC-15; @FORM-01; @ORG-14] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Today's private-label chay is based on starch and vegetables, sells at VND 107,800 to 141,500 per kg and makes no protein claim. We found no chay house brand at WinCommerce (one store checked online) or AEON [@CPC-15; @CPC-19; @CPC-20; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. A higher-protein version of an existing product would be a reformulation for the co-packer and a listing decision for the retailer, not a new brand (our inference; untested).
- **The integrated meat groups own the chilled meat route in modern trade.** These are groups that farm, process and sell meat. Masan MEATLife sells chilled pork in WinCommerce stores, and C.P. Vietnam runs more than 1,000 shops [@CHN-17] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. They are the natural makers of hybrid (meat and plant) products. But their own diversification is into animal protein. Vissan is building a 32,000-head pig farm, Masan MEATLife is moving into marinated and processed branded meat, and Vinamilk's Vinabeef chilled-beef plant (10,000 t a year) is operating [@AIB-15; @AIB-12; @AIB-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Online sales grow fast. They suit established brands for milk, and small makers for dry chay.** Grocery and food on the four big marketplaces sold VND 19.55 trillion in the first half of 2026. That is about 2 to 3% of food retail value on the 2024 base, up from about 1% (our calculation, doubling first-half sales for a year) [@ECR-02; @CHN-01] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The data firm Metric does not publish its category definition.

Brands' official stores sell most milk online. Shopee Mall (official) stores took 85% of liquid-milk sales on Shopee in early 2026 (Vinamilk 54%). Official or mall stores sold 93 to 100% of the milk units we read on Lazada [@ECR-01; @MKT-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Households buy plant milk online at a scale close to dairy. The best-selling plant milk sells 60% of the litres of the best-selling dairy milk on Lazada and 79% on Tiki. In the kitchen channel the figure is about 3% (our calculation on the cumulative "sold" counters that listings show) [@MKT-03; @MKT-05; @ECR-06] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

Dry chay pieces are different. Nine listings of dry soy-and-gluten "ribs" show about 40,700 kg sold, 82% of it from one small shop without an official store. Frozen chay sells hundreds of kilograms per listing, and no imported analogue appears [@MKT-03; @MKT-04] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. For a new protein, e-commerce is a place to test a milk or processed product through an established brand's official store. It is also a real route for a small maker of dry chay ingredients, but not a cheap launch route for a new consumer brand {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

## 15.2 Foodservice: large, fragmented, and silent about meat-free trials

Foodservice was worth USD 26.6 billion in 2024, across about 323,000 outlets. Full-service restaurants took 69% of sales, cafés and bars 18%, street stalls and kiosks 10% from about 152,000 outlets, and fast food 3% from about 11,000 [@CHN-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. More than 50,000 operators closed in the first half of 2025 [@CHN-03] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. Fast-food chains are few (about 1,000 outlets), but they buy centrally and can list a product nationally overnight. Hotpot and grill chains serve a lot of protein and are supplied centrally, so they are an under-examined route for hybrid and *chay* protein [@CHN-06] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Chains tried cheap, seasonal meat-free items; imported analogues did not stay.** Mass chains priced meat-free items the same as core items (VND 39,000). They timed them to the lunar calendar and used vegetables or chay ingredients, not imported analogues. The imported premium analogues came through a craft bar, a café chain and a HoReCa distributor, and did not stay. No operator has disclosed volumes, repeat rates or why items ended {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. Until outcomes are known, read chain launches as signs of supply, not as evidence of demand.

**Startups that sold their own analogue through restaurants fared no better.** Our register (`firm_outcomes.csv`) covers 24 regional and Vietnamese alternative-protein firms that entered, tested or left the market from 2019 to 2026. Of these, 7 are active, 4 pivoted, 2 exited and 11 show no activity after their last dated news. Of the 12 that launched their own brand on restaurant or café menus, 7 are dormant [@AIS-28; @BRD-08; @BRD-10] {VN-adjacent|Low} {dx:revealed}. We found no sign of activity after 2022 at any of the four Vietnamese meat-analogue startups in our register [@ECO-15; @ECO-19] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. All three makers of familiar formats (mushroom floss, cashew cheese, tempeh) are trading [@AIS-39; @AIS-42; @AIS-43; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

Three of the four pivots in our register moved towards private label, contract manufacturing or non-food ingredients; the fourth relaunched a retail blend under its own brand (`firm_outcomes.csv`). TiNDLE co-founder Andre Menezes said there is "no real underlying consumer demand to shift away from meat" [@AIS-28; @AIS-29] {general|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The regional exception, Indonesia's Green Rebel, grew through foodservice partners with regional dishes and raised USD 10.5 to 12.5 million in early 2026 [@AIS-30; @AIS-31] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

The register has only 24 firms, and "dormant" means only that we found no news. So it shows no advantage for putting a branded analogue on menus; it does not prove a disadvantage. No startup in the register has yet tried to sell an ingredient to established Vietnamese firms (`firm_outcomes.csv`, `firm_base_rates.csv`) {VN-adjacent|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-firm-outcomes}}

**Corporate commitments are not a volume lever in Vietnam.** One published commitment binds business units here. Accor's Good Food Policy asks all its hotels for 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030 (Novotel 25% by 2026). Accor ran 43 hotels in Vietnam in October 2024 [@AFN-40; @AFN-41; @AFN-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. The target counts dishes, which tofu, vegetables and noodles can fill (our inference).

Marriott's 2026 to 2030 goals contain no food goal. IHG, Hilton and Minor have cage-free egg pledges (to buy only eggs from hens not kept in cages), but delivery in Asia lags far behind. IHG East Asia and Pacific had 4.1% of hotels aligned in 2024, against 70.3% globally; Hilton Asia Pacific managed hotels reached 42% in 2025; Minor Hotels Asia Pacific and Indian Ocean reached 13% of egg volume in 2025 [@CPC-01; @CPC-04; @CPC-05; @CPC-07] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Sodexo's Vietnamese operation is unconfirmed. Lever Foundation turns pledges into buying ratios in Asian hotels and caterers. In July 2026 it extended its plant-based work to India and Thailand, not Vietnam [@CPC-08; @CPC-10; @AFN-39] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {dx:revealed}. We found no Vietnamese protein buyer with a published diversification target ([[ch16-business-buyers]]; `corporate_commitments.csv`).

## 15.3 Institutions: a large protein channel where price decides

**Factory canteens: large and price-set.** About 4.15 million people worked in industrial parks and economic zones at the end of 2023 [@CHN-37] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The trade union sets a floor price per meal: VND 25,000 when a caterer is hired and VND 20,000 when the firm cooks itself. These floors apply in minimum-wage regions I and II; in regions III and IV they are VND 22,000 and 18,000. Each meal must include a protein food [@AIB-43] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Prices paid run from VND 17,000 to 40,000. Hai Phong's 2026 average is about VND 27,000 in the west and 31,000 in the east, with some firms at VND 17,000. Foreign-invested factories there pay VND 22,000 to 35,000, and northern garment firms VND 17,000 to 25,000 [@UPL-34; @CHN-39; @CHN-38] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

The contract catering market is about USD 2 billion a year, and food is 60 to 80% of the meal price. Suppliers report informal commissions of VND 500 to 1,000 per meal to clients' purchasing staff. Haseca, which calls itself a leading caterer, reports more than 200,000 meals a day from more than 120 kitchens. Its meals come in five price tiers of VND 18,000 to 45,000 (company claim) [@CHN-41; @AIB-36] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. At about 300 meal days a year, industrial-park canteens serve up to about 1.2 billion meals (our upper-bound calculation) [@CHN-37; @CHN-39] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-canteen-meals}}

**What canteens serve: mostly pork, fish and chicken.** Five caterers' published sample menus (172 protein dishes, 2023 to 2025) lead with pork (33%), fish (26%) and chicken (13%). Then come egg (10%), tofu (9%, only 3% plant-only) and beef (6%). Minced or processed meat makes up 17% of protein dishes [@UPL-27; @UPL-28; @UPL-29; @UPL-30; @UPL-32] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}.

Haseca rotates "2 days pork, 2 days chicken or fish, 1 day tofu or egg", with a main dish of 90 to 120 g. One caterer serves *chay* dishes on two lunar days a month, and none names textured soy or mock meat [@UPL-32; @UPL-33] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. These are marketing samples, and four of the five come from the south.

The Ministry of Health's shift-meal nutrition guidance (Decision 1982/QĐ-BYT, chapter V) sets no plant slot and no protein norm. An expert from an occupational-health institute, quoted in the trade union's newspaper, advises 30 to 50% of protein from animal sources [@APR-16; @UPL-36] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. So a canteen ingredient has two slots. In the tofu slot it competes with tofu; in the minced-meat slot a blend displaces pork. Chicken leads only 13% of protein dishes, so a canteen meal switched to plant protein spares few animals; [[ch18-demand-sizing]] carries that arithmetic {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-canteen-dish-mix}}

{{chart:chart-canteen-dish-mix}}

**What a protein ingredient has to cost in a canteen** (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Food takes 60 to 80% of a VND 25,000 to 35,000 meal, so the food budget is VND 15,000 to 28,000 [@CHN-41; @CHN-39]. If the protein dish takes about half, it has VND 7,500 to 14,000.

Twenty grams of protein from eggs or chicken breast costs VND 6,900 to 9,000 at retail ([[ch11-protein-diet]]). From a branded retail textured or gluten analogue (VND 112,000 per 100 g of protein, [@FORM-01]) it costs VND 22,400, more than the whole protein budget. The supply study's indicative ex-factory cost (the cost at the factory gate) of textured plant protein is VND 3,700 to 10,700 per 100 g of protein ([[ch09-economics]]). At that cost, 20 g of protein costs VND 740 to 2,140. {{kn:kn-canteen-protein-budget}}

**The price that binds is what kitchens already buy.** Kamereo is a platform that supplies more than 4,000 restaurants, cafés and hotels (company claim). On it, 20 g of protein costs about VND 4,000 to 5,100 as domestic dry soy-gluten pieces, using a protein range taken from similar products. It costs VND 7,200 to 9,500 as branded firm tofu, VND 8,800 as eggs, VND 9,600 as chicken breast and VND 13,900 as minced pork (our calculation) [@CPC-12; @AIS-26; @DIE-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

Loose tofu shows 78,058 units on the platform's sold counter. CP Meat Zero shows 386 units across eight products and could not be ordered on 25 September 2026. Its minced and roast products were listed at 1.5 to 2.1 times the price per kilogram of CP's own minced pork (about 3.3 times for the bologna) [@CPC-12; @AIS-25] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

So a new ingredient has to beat dry soy-gluten pieces on cost per gram of protein or on function. It has to reach caterers through the chay-piece makers and HoReCa distributors that already supply them. A branded analogue at retail prices does not fit {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**Schools: price.** Lunch costs VND 35,000 in Ho Chi Minh City (2025 to 2026) and VND 40,000 in Hanoi for 2026 to 2027. Of the Hanoi price, the city pays VND 28,000 and parents VND 12,000 (Resolution 84/2026/NQ-HĐND of the city People's Council) [@PMR-06; @PMR-32; @PMR-33] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

In 2025 to 2026 Hanoi subsidised lunch for about 768,000 primary pupils at VND 20,000, or VND 30,000 in mountain communes and on Red River sandbars. The budget was VND 3,063 billion. In Hanoi, 90% of schools provide *bán trú* (school lunch and midday care) [@CHN-29] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. That budget buys roughly 100 to 150 million subsidised meals a year (our calculation from the budget and meal price) [@CHN-29] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. We found no national count of pupils eating at school.

**Schools: buyers.** Caterers, not the state, write the menus. In Hanoi a ward evaluation council assigns caterers and food, milk and water suppliers to each school. The caterer writes the menu with grams and calories, and the principal co-signs. One caterer wrote near-identical menus for three schools [@PMR-05; @PMR-06; @PMR-22; @PMR-23; @PMR-24] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

A Hai Phong school tender fixes the price at the regulated norm and scores caterers on capability and on the extra portion value they add. It asks for a food-safety certificate, revenue of at least VND 3 billion, three school or industrial-zone contracts and certified food suppliers [@PMR-07] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Schools: trust.** In April 2026 about 300 tonnes of pork from pigs with African swine fever, relabelled as clean, reached 26 Hanoi public schools and private kindergartens through two caterers [@CHN-36] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The Prime Minister's Dispatch 64/CĐ-TTg of 13 September 2026 requires traceable suppliers and procurement "not based solely on lowest price" [@CHN-30] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The Prime Minister's Directive 33/CT-TTg (14 August 2026) adds supplier listing, inspection and capacity checks. It also requires daily public disclosure of the caterer, ingredients, invoices, menus and photos of meal trays, without restricting any food type [@PMR-02; @AIB-42] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. In September 2026 Hanoi parents took school-meal samples to the national food-safety testing institute to check the meat in them [@TRU-21] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**National guidance asks for plant protein; menus fill it with tofu.** The rule now in force is Ministry of Health (MOH) Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT of 25 December 2025. It sets no animal share. It asks for:
- protein at 13 to 20% of energy;
- animal protein on at least 4 to 5 days a week, "alternating with plant protein (legumes, tofu) at least twice a week";
- fish 2 to 3 times a week;
- limits on processed meat and sausages.

Its 139-item food table lists tofu, soybeans and soy milk, but no textured soy, gluten or analogue. It is guidance with no sanction, and it excludes preschools [@AIB-39; @APR-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

Our first reading took school meals to be guided by the Prime Minister's Decision 1340/QĐ-TTg (2016). That was a school-milk programme whose target of animal protein above 40% of total protein ran only to 2020 [@CHN-33] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

One Hanoi school, Nguyen Trai, publishes the sheets its menu software prints. The software comes from the Ministry of Education and Training (MOET), Ajinomoto Vietnam and the National Institute of Nutrition (NIN). The sheets still print a standard of "animal protein at least 48%" of each lunch's protein. Of the school's 25 published days, 9 fall below it [@PMR-26; @AFN-46] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-menu-software-floor}}

**What schools serve: tofu beside meat.** We read 48 published school-weeks (239 lunch days) from 22 primary schools in Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi and Hai Phong, from March to September 2026. On 36 days (15.1%) a protein dish had plant protein, almost always tofu. Only 9 of 48 weeks met the twice-weekly rule, and 1 day in 239 had no animal protein. None of 333 protein dishes used textured soy, *chay* mock meat or a branded analogue [@PMR-10; @PMR-24; @PMR-31] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

Tofu is served as a second dish beside a meat or fish main (27 of 36 plant days) or cooked into a meat dish (8 of 36). Minced or processed meat or fish dishes (meatballs, pork loaf, sausages, fish cakes) appear on 28.5% of days [@PMR-10; @PMR-31] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. At Nguyen Trai, lunches carry about 31.8 g of protein on the menu, 17.5 g of it animal (our calculation from 25 days of its software sheets) [@PMR-26] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. Day-level coding is in `school_menu_weeks.csv`. {{kn:kn-school-plant-days}}

**The school verdict: do not start in schools with a branded or meat-replacement product.** A plant ingredient sold into the required plant slot replaces tofu, not meat, and competes with it on cost. Tofu costs VND 4,900 to 7,500 per 25 g of protein, and a textured-soy ingredient VND 925 to 2,675 before oil, sauce and labour (our calculation; textured-soy cost from [[ch09-economics]]) [@DIE-05; @DIE-23; @PMR-24; @DIE-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The slot is small. At today's frequency it holds about 1,000 t of protein a year (660 to 1,580 t). If every school met the rule, it would hold about 2,800 t (our calculation from an assumed 0.5 to 1.2 billion school lunches a year) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

The only school route that removes meat is a named processor's labelled blend in minced-meat and fish-cake dishes. A 25% blend in every such dish would remove about 460 t of meat protein a year (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

Blends raise no nutrition objection. A 20 to 30% soy extender keeps the dish's protein quality at a DIAAS (a measure of protein quality) of about 97 or more (older-child pattern). It gives 12 to 19% less zinc per gram of protein than all-pork. A dish that removes all meat would need added zinc [@NQR-05; @NQR-07; @APR-04] {general|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

For school-age children, protein is not the gap. The documented shortfalls are breakfast, calcium and vitamin A (see upland meals below).

Korea shows what a rule change can do. All 17 education offices recommend plant-based school meals once or twice a month. In 2023 Seoul's plant-based menus carried 26.7 g of protein, against 29.9 g for regular ones [@AFN-42] {VN-adjacent|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Where the school levers are.** The levers for plant protein in schools are not a nutrition target. They are the menu software's default and food list, the 2026 circular, and the few caterers that each write one menu for many schools {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. The Prime Minister's Decision 973/QĐ-TTg (School Health Programme 2026 to 2035, 1 June 2026) orders a MOET circular (a ministry's implementing rule) on *bán trú* meal organisation in 2026. It also orders MOET digital tools for balanced menus in 2027 to 2028 [@PMR-01; @APR-12] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Press coverage describes a model with 400 standard menus and a daily carton of fresh milk, which the decision's text does not name [@AFN-45] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}.

**The milk precedent.** The dairy strategy (Decision 309/QĐ-TTg, 2026) says school milk programmes use "only fresh milk" [@FTR-31; @APR-11] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Hanoi's 2018 school milk tender went to Vinamilk, whose milk is made entirely in Vietnam, with the state, the supplier and parents sharing the cost [@CHN-34] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Plant milk reaches schools only as a gift. Vinasoy has donated 13.8 million boxes of soy milk to 906,848 pupils since 2016, and plans to donate 1 million boxes to 30 schools in 2026 to 2027 [@AIB-06; @AIB-08] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Upland boarding and border-commune meals: the money goes on pork.** Public money now buys meals for ethnic-minority boarders and lunch for border-commune pupils:
- VND 936,000 a month and 15 kg of rice per semi-boarder (Decree 66/2025);
- VND 1,170,000 and 15 kg per full boarder in the new boarding schools (Decree 188/2026);
- VND 450,000 and 8 kg for about 418,000 pupils in land-border communes (Decree 339/2025; more than VND 2,000 billion and 30,000 t of rice a year at draft stage) [@UPL-20; @UPL-21; @APR-09; @UPL-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

School kitchens, not caterers, cook, and they spend the money on pork. We read 22 school-weeks of menus (2026) from 13 boarding schools in five provinces. There, 240 of 242 lunches and dinners carried a meat, fish or egg dish, and pork appeared in 102 of 124 lunches (82%; our register `upland_school_weeks.csv`). At one border school, pork took 97% of the Decree 339 lunch money (rice aside) on pork days, and pork and tofu together took 91% on tofu days [@UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-04; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

Kitchens already stretch pork with tofu. On tofu days at Muong Nhe the lunch used 35% less pork. Its protein items cost 27% less (the whole lunch 25% less), for about 8% less protein (our calculation) [@UPL-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:inferred}. No textured soy or mock meat appears in 353 meals.

The thin meal is breakfast: 86% carry little or no animal food (our menu coding, `upland_school_weeks.csv`). The only kitchen-weighed boarding diets we found met protein and zinc needs, but supplied 36.5% of calcium needs and 41 to 45% of vitamin A needs [@UPL-01; @UPL-18] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Stunting (32% among ethnic minority children under five) [@APR-27] is a problem of children below school age, outside school kitchens {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

At one Quang Ngai school, suppliers need a business registration and a food-safety certificate. A former principal in Bac Ha, Lao Cai, was arrested in 2024 for diverting more than VND 765 million of boarding support, after inspectors had found meals cut. So the plan is not always the plate [@UPL-26; @UPL-24] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

The one plant product with an observed job here is a pork extender sold at the tofu price per gram of protein. We found no sign that anyone has asked these kitchens whether they would buy it {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-upland-meals-animal}}

**Hospitals and the military.** In 2025 a public hospital in Ho Chi Minh City issued a request for quotations for patient meals, about 81,800 rice and 20,500 porridge meals a year. It requires at least 1,800 kcal a day for the rice diet (1,200 kcal for porridge). Each meal needs at least 60 g of lean meat, fish, chicken or seafood, or at least 120 g of tofu as a full replacement. A tofu version is among three sample menus [@AIB-44] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

The tofu swap gives about 13.1 g of protein against 11.4 g (our calculation) [@AIB-39]. It is the one public-meal specification we found that lets a plant food replace meat, and it names tofu.

The army's basic food allowance is VND 72,000 per soldier a day under Circular 96/2025/TT-BQP of the Ministry of National Defence [@AIB-45] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. We found no ration composition or procurement rule. Workplace and hospital meal guidelines are not legally binding [@APR-26] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

> **Correction.** In v0.6 this said the hospital tender requires 60 g of protein a day; the source sets no daily protein figure, and 60 g is the minimum weight of meat or fish per meal.

## 15.4 Tourism: Nha Trang is a showcase, not a volume channel

Vietnam received 21.2 million international visitors in 2025 (up 20%) and 15.9 million in the first eight months of 2026. In 2026 visitors from China were flat and from Korea down, while those from Russia and India grew fast [@CHN-43; @CHN-45] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

Khanh Hoa (Nha Trang) received 5.5 million international visitors in 2025 and has 70,500 rooms, over 40% of them four and five star. Its visitors come mainly from Korea, Russia and China [@CHN-47; @CHN-48; @CHN-49] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. India is the one fast-growing market where vegetarian eating is widespread. But its direct flights land in Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang, not Nha Trang [@CHN-52] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

**Verdict.** The project's hypothesis was that Nha Trang's hotels and restaurants, driven by tourists, are a channel for plant-based products. The evidence does not support it as a volume channel. Our rough calculation gives 1 to 2 million vegetarian or vegan meal occasions a year from international visitors to Khanh Hoa. That compares with 100 to 150 million subsidised school lunches in Hanoi alone and up to 1.2 billion factory meals nationally [@CHN-47; @CHN-29; @CHN-37; @CHN-39] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

Nha Trang is plausible as a small showcase for premium analogues and higher-protein chay dishes in four and five star hotels. Hotels now have one named reason to meet a supplier. Accor's target of 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030 binds its 43 Vietnamese hotels, and it has signed a Mercure in Nha Trang [@AFN-40; @AFN-41] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. The target counts dishes, which vegetables and tofu can fill, and Accor's 2025 cage-free pledge was "not yet achieved in Asia" [@AFN-18] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {dx:revealed}. One Vietnamese tempeh maker names a Sheraton hotel among its customers [@AIS-43] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

The hypothesis is cheap to test. Interview 20 hotel and restaurant food-and-beverage managers, starting with Accor's, and audit 30 menus. Domestic tourists who keep chay days may matter more there than foreign vegetarians (untested).

## 15.5 Channel economics: the data are absent, not weak

We found no Vietnamese source we could retrieve on retailer listing fees, trade margins, payment terms, distributor margins or frozen distribution costs per kilogram. The numbers we have are these:
- **Catering:** food at 60 to 80% of meal value, and commissions of 2 to 4% of a VND 25,000 meal [@CHN-41] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.
- **The supply study:** dried soy analogues retail at 5 to 17 times the indicative ex-factory cost of their protein ([[app-s12-costs]]).
- **Saigon Co.op:** private label priced 15 to 30% below comparable brands. Its supplier portal answers a product pitch within 10 working days, but its public pages list no document requirements [@AIB-28; @CPC-17; @CPC-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **Kamereo:** the first public prices for products delivered to kitchens (section 15.3) [@CPC-12] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Anyone modelling a consumer product should get three buyer quotes before setting a price.

## 15.7 What this means for new protein products

- **Sell ingredients to the firms that supply kitchens and factories before building a shelf brand.** Large protein demand, set by price, sits in canteens and in manufacturers that already buy imported textured soy and gluten. Reach caterers through the chay-piece makers and HoReCa distributors that supply them. Beat tofu and dry soy-gluten pieces on price per gram of protein.
- **If you make a consumer chay product, design it for the frozen chay section** of the big chains: at or below the meat price, 10 g or more protein per 100 g, and familiar formats. Or take a protein upgrade of an existing private-label product to the retailer through its co-packer.
- **Do not start in schools with a branded or meat-replacement product.** Enter later as a caterer ingredient that beats tofu on cost, or as a named processor's labelled blend in minced dishes.
- **Price meat-free foodservice items the same as meat items,** and time launches to the lunar calendar.
- **Use e-commerce to test,** through an established brand's official store or, for dry chay pieces, through specialist shops. Do not build a business on tourists. Use hotels as a showcase only if you already supply the big cities.

**For each reader.**
- **Startups:** sell a familiar food with little capital. Aim first to supply ingredients, private-label products or co-packing to established firms: a chay maker, a caterer, a retailer's co-packer. A restaurant menu listing for your own analogue brand is not a business customer; in the region such firms mostly went quiet or pivoted. No founder has yet tested the ingredient route, so treat it as the hypothesis to test, not a proven path.
- **Investors:** access to channels is concentrated in two grocery chains, a few meat groups, a few large caterers and one private-label co-packer for chay. Back companies whose product a channel partner buys as an ingredient or under its own label. Price the churn of foodservice items as a risk. Corporate commitments add no volume here.
- **Manufacturers:** established chay makers hold the frozen sections and convenience-store contracts. A co-packer can take a protein upgrade to a retailer's private label without a consumer brand. HoReCa distributors already sell tofu and dry soy-gluten pieces to kitchens in volume. Integrated meat groups are best placed to launch hybrids.
- **Research bodies:** test how products behave in bulk cooking (holding, reheating, absorbing broth), and work on dry formats that travel without a cold chain. Run a two-week plate-waste trial with one caterer that writes one menu for several schools. Test a textured-soy dish in the plant slot and a 20 to 30% blend in one minced dish, with the published daily menus as a free control series.
- **Policy makers:** national guidance already sets no animal share. Ask MOET, NIN and Ajinomoto to replace the menu software's "animal protein at least 48%" default with targets for protein quality, zinc and iron. Ask them also to add textured soy and legume products to its food list. Write both into the MOET *bán trú* circular due in 2026 and the digital menu tools due in 2027 to 2028 (Decision 973/QĐ-TTg). Directive 33/CT-TTg now sets supplier documents, and transparent canteen buying helps new entrants more than subsidies.
- **International organisations and funders:** match the trial to the outcome you fund. A canteen trial should log which animal the dish replaces. Chicken leads only 13% of canteen protein dishes (our coding, `canteen_dishes.csv`), and pork dishes spare few animals. Import substitution and protein upgrades on existing chay days spare none ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]). Humane World for Animals, which trained chefs and nutritionists in Vietnam in 2025, is an implementer to partner with [@AFN-23] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Vietnam is not listed as a School Meals Coalition member [@APR-38] {VN-adjacent|Low} {dx:revealed}. Corporate commitments are a weak lever: fund measurement of how Accor's Vietnamese hotels meet their target, and a disclosure request to Vinamilk, not a new pledge campaign. Fund the missing channel data: margins, listing fees, frozen distribution costs and national *bán trú* coverage.
