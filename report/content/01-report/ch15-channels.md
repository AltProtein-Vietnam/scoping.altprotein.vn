# 15. Where protein is bought and eaten: retail, foodservice and institutions

**In brief.**
- **Channels decide what form a new protein can take.** Traditional markets and small grocers still take 83% of food retail. A chilled or frozen product cannot follow meat there. It fits the frozen *chay* (vegetarian) aisle of modern stores, store brands, or kitchens.
- **Restaurants have not shown that meat-free products sell.** Chains have tried meat-free items for eight years without disclosing a success.
- **Factory canteens buy protein on price.** They serve up to 1.2 billion meals a year, mostly at VND 20,000 to 35,000. Only an ingredient cheaper than tofu and dry soy pieces fits.
- **Schools are the wrong place to start.** Menus fill plant-protein days with tofu beside meat, so a plant ingredient there replaces tofu, not meat.
- **Some channels add little volume.** Upland boarding kitchens spend new meal money mostly on pork, corporate pledges add little, and Nha Trang's hotels are a showcase, not a volume channel.

Four things follow for a new protein {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}:

- **Two listings reach most modern grocery stores.** Bach Hoa Xanh and WinCommerce together run about 8,750 stores. All foreign-owned supermarkets together number under 200 [@CHN-12; @CHN-15; @AIB-31; @AIB-33; @AIB-34] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The two chains grow mainly through small stores and in rural areas, and new tax rules favour them [@CHN-05] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.
- **The frozen shelf already exists.** Large stores have signposted frozen chay sections stocked by several brands [@FORM-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Only products that need no cold chain, such as dry textured protein or cans, can reach the 650,000 traditional outlets [@CHN-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.
- **Store brands reach that shelf without a consumer brand.** Saigon Co.op sells over 2,500 store-brand products from over 100 Vietnamese makers, 15% to 30% below branded prices. One co-packer makes all its frozen chay dishes [@AIB-28; @CPC-15; @FORM-01; @ORG-14] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Today's store-brand chay is starch and vegetables, with no protein claim [@CPC-15; @CPC-19; @CPC-20] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. A higher-protein version would be a recipe change for the co-packer, not a new brand (our inference; untested).
- **Meat groups own the chilled meat route.** Masan and C.P. sell chilled meat through modern stores and their own shops [@CHN-17] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. They are natural makers of meat and plant blends, but are investing in more animal protein [@AIB-15; @AIB-12; @AIB-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Online sales grow fast.** Online grocery is now about 2% to 3% of food retail (our calculation) [@ECR-02; @CHN-01] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Brands' official stores sell most milk online [@ECR-01; @MKT-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Households buy plant milk online at a scale close to dairy [@MKT-03; @MKT-05; @ECR-06] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. Dry chay pieces sell well from small shops too [@MKT-03; @MKT-04] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. So online suits testing a milk through an established brand, or selling dry chay ingredients. It is not a cheap launch route for a new consumer brand {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

## 15.2 Foodservice: large, fragmented, and silent about meat-free trials

Foodservice was worth USD 26.6 billion in 2024, across about 323,000 outlets, mostly full-service restaurants [@CHN-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Fast-food chains are few, but they buy centrally and can list a product nationally overnight. Hotpot and grill chains serve a lot of protein and are an under-examined route [@CHN-06] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Chains tried cheap, seasonal meat-free items; imported meat copies did not stay.** Mass chains priced meat-free items like core items and timed them to the lunar calendar. No operator has disclosed volumes or why items ended {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. Read chain launches as signs of supply, not demand.

**Startups that sold their own meat copy through restaurants fared no better.** Of 24 regional alternative-protein firms we tracked from 2019 to 2026, 12 launched their own brand on menus, and 7 of those are dormant [@AIS-28; @BRD-08; @BRD-10] {VN-adjacent|Low} {dx:revealed}. None of the four Vietnamese meat-copy startups has been active since 2022. All three makers of familiar formats are trading [@ECO-15; @ECO-19; @AIS-39; @AIS-42; @AIS-43; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. Most pivots moved to store brands or making products for others [@AIS-28; @AIS-29] {general|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The one regional exception grew through foodservice partners with local dishes [@AIS-30; @AIS-31] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The sample is small, so it shows no advantage for menu launches, not proof of a disadvantage {VN-adjacent|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-firm-outcomes}}

**Corporate pledges are not a volume lever here.** Accor asks its 43 Vietnamese hotels for 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030, but tofu and vegetables can fill that [@AFN-40; @AFN-41; @AFN-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. Other hotel groups' food pledges lag in Asia [@CPC-01; @CPC-04; @CPC-05; @CPC-07] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The main organisation turning pledges into buying targets in Asia does not yet work in Vietnam [@CPC-08; @CPC-10; @AFN-39] {VN-adjacent|Medium} {dx:revealed}. We found no Vietnamese protein buyer with a published target ([[ch16-business-buyers]]).

## 15.3 Institutions: a large protein channel where price decides

**Factory canteens: large and price-set.** About 4.15 million people worked in industrial parks in 2023 [@CHN-37] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The trade union sets a floor of VND 18,000 to 25,000 a meal, and each meal must include protein [@AIB-43] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Prices paid run from VND 17,000 to 40,000 [@UPL-34; @CHN-39; @CHN-38] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Contract catering is worth about USD 2 billion a year, and food is 60% to 80% of the meal price. Suppliers report informal commissions to buyers' staff [@CHN-41; @AIB-36] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. Canteens serve up to about 1.2 billion meals a year (our upper-bound calculation) [@CHN-37; @CHN-39] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-canteen-meals}}

**What canteens serve: mostly pork, fish and chicken.** In five caterers' sample menus, protein dishes are pork (33%), fish (26%), chicken (13%), egg (10%), tofu (9%) and beef (6%). Minced or processed meat makes up 17% [@UPL-27; @UPL-28; @UPL-29; @UPL-30; @UPL-32; @UPL-33] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. No caterer names textured soy or mock meat.

National guidance sets no plant slot for canteens [@APR-16; @UPL-36] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. So a canteen ingredient has two slots. In the tofu slot it competes with tofu. In the minced-meat slot, a blend displaces pork ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-canteen-dish-mix}}

{{chart:chart-canteen-dish-mix}}

**What a protein ingredient has to cost** (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. In a VND 25,000 to 35,000 meal, the protein dish has about VND 7,500 to 14,000 to spend [@CHN-41; @CHN-39]. Twenty grams of protein costs VND 6,900 to 9,000 from eggs or chicken. From a branded meat copy it costs VND 22,400, more than the whole budget. From textured soy at factory cost it costs under VND 2,200 ([[ch09-economics]]). {{kn:kn-canteen-protein-budget}}

**The price that binds is what kitchens already buy.** On a large restaurant supply platform, 20 g of protein costs about VND 4,000 to 5,100 as dry soy pieces, VND 7,200 to 9,500 as tofu and VND 13,900 as minced pork (our calculation) [@CPC-12; @AIS-26; @DIE-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Loose tofu has sold 78,058 units there; an imported plant-based pork range, 386 [@CPC-12; @AIS-25] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. So a new ingredient has to beat dry soy pieces on cost or function, and reach caterers through the firms that already supply them {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**Schools: price and buyers.** Lunch costs VND 35,000 to 40,000 in the big cities [@PMR-06; @PMR-32; @PMR-33] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Hanoi subsidised lunch for about 768,000 primary pupils in 2025 to 2026, roughly 100 to 150 million meals (our calculation) [@CHN-29] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Caterers, not the state, write the menus, and one caterer often writes near-identical menus for several schools [@PMR-05; @PMR-06; @PMR-07; @PMR-22; @PMR-23; @PMR-24] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Schools: trust.** In April 2026 about 300 tonnes of diseased pork, relabelled as clean, reached 26 Hanoi schools [@CHN-36] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. New rules from the Prime Minister now require traceable suppliers, buying "not based solely on lowest price", and daily publication of menus and meal photos [@CHN-30; @PMR-02; @AIB-42; @TRU-21] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**National guidance asks for plant protein; menus fill it with tofu.** The 2025 school meal guidance sets no animal share. It asks for animal protein on 4 to 5 days a week, "alternating with plant protein (legumes, tofu) at least twice a week". Its food table has no textured soy [@AIB-39; @APR-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. But the menu software schools use still prints a standard of "animal protein at least 48%" [@PMR-26; @AFN-46] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-menu-software-floor}}

**What schools serve: tofu beside meat.** We read 239 lunch days from 22 primary schools in 2026. Only 15% had a plant-protein dish, almost always tofu, and only 9 of 48 weeks met the twice-weekly rule. None of 333 protein dishes used textured soy or mock meat [@PMR-10; @PMR-24; @PMR-31] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Tofu comes as a second dish beside meat or fish. Minced meat or fish dishes appear on 28.5% of days [@PMR-10; @PMR-31] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-school-plant-days}}

**The school verdict: do not start there with a branded or meat-replacement product.** A plant ingredient in the plant slot replaces tofu, not meat, and competes on cost (our calculation) [@DIE-05; @DIE-23; @PMR-24; @DIE-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The slot holds only about 1,000 t of protein a year, or about 2,800 t if every school met the rule {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The only school route that removes meat is a named processor's labelled blend in minced dishes. A 25% blend in every such dish would remove about 460 t of meat protein a year (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Blends raise no nutrition objection; removing all meat would need added zinc [@NQR-05; @NQR-07; @APR-04] {general|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

Korea shows what a rule change can do: all its education offices recommend plant-based school meals once or twice a month [@AFN-42] {VN-adjacent|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Where the school levers are.** The levers are the menu software's default and food list, a 2026 education ministry rule on school meals, digital menu tools due in 2027 to 2028, and the few caterers that write menus for many schools [@PMR-01; @APR-12; @AFN-45] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

**The milk precedent.** School milk programmes use "only fresh milk" [@FTR-31; @APR-11; @CHN-34] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Plant milk reaches schools only as a gift: Vinasoy has donated 13.8 million boxes since 2016 [@AIB-06; @AIB-08] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Upland boarding meals: the money goes on pork.** New public money buys meals for ethnic-minority boarders and border-commune pupils [@UPL-20; @UPL-21; @APR-09; @UPL-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. School kitchens spend it on pork: it appeared in 82% of lunches we coded [@UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-04; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Kitchens already stretch pork with tofu, saving about a quarter of the lunch cost [@UPL-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:inferred}. The thin meal is breakfast, and the gaps are calcium and vitamin A, not protein [@UPL-01; @UPL-18] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Funds do not always reach the plate [@UPL-26; @UPL-24] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The one plant product with a job here is a pork extender at the price of tofu, and nobody has asked these kitchens if they would buy it {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-upland-meals-animal}}

**Hospitals and the military.** One public hospital's meal tender lets 120 g of tofu fully replace 60 g of meat or fish [@AIB-44; @AIB-39] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. It is the only public meal rule we found that lets a plant food replace meat, and it names tofu. We found no army ration rules [@AIB-45; @APR-26] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

## 15.4 Tourism: Nha Trang is a showcase, not a volume channel

Vietnam received 21.2 million international visitors in 2025 [@CHN-43; @CHN-45] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Khanh Hoa (Nha Trang) received 5.5 million, mainly from Korea, Russia and China [@CHN-47; @CHN-48; @CHN-49] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. India, where vegetarian eating is common, has no direct flights to Nha Trang [@CHN-52] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

**Verdict.** The evidence does not support Nha Trang's hotels as a volume channel. Foreign visitors to Khanh Hoa make 1 to 2 million vegetarian meal occasions a year (our rough calculation). Hanoi's subsidised school lunches alone number 100 to 150 million [@CHN-47; @CHN-29; @CHN-37; @CHN-39] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

Nha Trang is plausible as a small showcase in four and five star hotels, especially Accor's [@AFN-40; @AFN-41; @AFN-18; @AIS-43] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. Testing this is cheap: interview 20 hotel food managers and audit 30 menus.

## 15.5 Channel economics: the data are absent, not weak

We found no Vietnamese source on listing fees, trade margins, payment terms or frozen distribution costs. What we have:
- **Catering:** food is 60% to 80% of meal value, and commissions 2% to 4% [@CHN-41] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.
- **Shelf prices:** dried soy products retail at 5 to 17 times the factory cost of their protein ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **Saigon Co.op:** store brands 15% to 30% below branded prices; its supplier portal answers a pitch within 10 working days [@AIB-28; @CPC-17; @CPC-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Anyone pricing a consumer product should get three buyer quotes first.

## 15.6 Which channel carries which product first

Our judgement from the evidence above {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

| Product | First route | Second route | Not first | What it has to be |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Textured plant protein ingredient | Chay and processed-meat makers that already import textured soy | Contract caterers, through the firms that supply them; later, school and hospital kitchens for the tofu slot | Own-brand retail; a "replaces meat" pitch to schools | Near the Chinese landed price; cheaper per gram of protein than dry soy pieces and tofu; dry, traceable, certified |
| Higher-protein chay product | Established chay brands in frozen sections; store brands through their co-packers | Chay restaurants, chain seasonal items, canteen chay days | Wet markets, schools | At or below the meat version's price; 10 g or more protein per 100 g |
| Meat and plant blend | Meat groups and their stores, starting with sausage and coarse fillings | Factory canteens | Schools, except later as a labelled blend in minced dishes | Cheaper or better than the processor's soy recipe; plant part named as an ingredient, never as *độn* (filler) |
| Premium meat copy | Premium restaurants and cafés in the big cities | Premium retail and online; luxury hotels as a showcase | Mass retail, canteens, schools | Chef-grade performance in named dishes |
| Cultivated or precision-fermented product | Ingredient in blends through large makers | Premium restaurant showcase | All mass channels until a new-food route exists | A legal route first ([[ch07-rules]]) |

## 15.7 What this means for new protein products

- **Sell ingredients to the firms that supply kitchens and factories before building a shelf brand.** Beat tofu and dry soy pieces on price per gram of protein.
- **If you make a consumer chay product, design it for the frozen chay section:** at or below the meat price, 10 g of protein or more per 100 g, in familiar formats. Or take a protein upgrade to a store brand through its co-packer.
- **Do not start in schools.** Enter later as a caterer ingredient that beats tofu on cost, or as a labelled blend in minced dishes.
- **Price meat-free menu items like meat items,** and time launches to the lunar calendar.
- **Use online to test,** through an established brand's store. Do not build a business on tourists.

**For each reader.**
- **Startups:** sell a familiar food with little capital, as an ingredient, store brand or co-packed product. A menu listing for your own brand is not a business customer. No founder has yet tested the ingredient route, so treat it as the hypothesis to test.
- **Investors:** access is concentrated in two grocery chains, a few meat groups, a few large caterers and one store-brand co-packer for chay. Back firms whose product a channel partner buys. Corporate pledges add no volume here.
- **Manufacturers:** established chay makers hold the frozen sections. A co-packer can take a protein upgrade to a store brand without a consumer brand. Meat groups are best placed to launch blends.
- **Researchers:** test how products hold up in bulk cooking. Run a two-week trial with one caterer that writes menus for several schools: textured soy in the plant slot, and a 20% to 30% blend in one minced dish.
- **Policy makers:** ask the menu software's makers to replace the "animal protein at least 48%" default with targets for protein quality, zinc and iron, and to add textured soy and legume products to its food list. Write both into the 2026 school meal rule and the 2027 to 2028 digital menu tools.
- **International organisations and funders:** match the trial to the outcome you fund, and log which animal a dish replaces ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]). Humane World for Animals, which trained chefs in Vietnam in 2025, is a partner [@AFN-23] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Fund the missing channel data: margins, listing fees and frozen distribution costs.
