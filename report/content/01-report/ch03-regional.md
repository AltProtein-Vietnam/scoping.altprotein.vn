# 3. Vietnam in the region

**In brief.**
- A 2025 regional study scores Vietnam 100 on labour cost but 0 on regulatory readiness.
- Cheap inputs alone will not bring plants. Labour and power are a small share of fermentation cost, and Thailand is nearly as cheap, with better rules and more sugar.
- Singapore approves novel foods; Thailand and Korea have routes but no cultivated approvals yet. China builds at scale and sets the price to beat.
- Vietnam's regional role today is feedstock supplier. It ships over 2 Mt of cassava starch a year, mostly to China, where it is turned into fermentation sugar.
- The region points to two moves: keep the starch-to-sugar step at home, and publish a novel-food procedure with a fixed timeline.

---

## 3.1 How the region scores Vietnam

The only published regional comparison is the "Where to Build" study (2025) by the Good Food Institute Asia Pacific and Hawkwood Biotech. It scored nine countries from 0 to 100 on seven factors that decide where fermentation plants are built (100 = best in the group) [@RGN-01; @RGN-02] {VN-direct|High}.

| Factor | Vietnam input | Vietnam score | Best in region |
|---|---|---|---|
| Labour | USD 342 a month | 100 | Vietnam and Indonesia (100) |
| Utilities | USD 0.08 per kWh | 97 | Indonesia (99) |
| Construction cost | USD 1,672 per m² | 93 | Indonesia (100) |
| Business environment | World Bank index, about 70 | 30 | Singapore (100) |
| Sugar | 1.35 Mt produced, none exported (2024) | 26 | Thailand (100) |
| Talent | 779 researchers per million people | 26 | Korea (68) |
| Regulatory readiness | No framework, no approvals | 0 | Singapore and Australia (100) |

Source: [@RGN-01] {VN-direct\|High}.

{{chart:chart-hawkwood-scores}}

Three points matter more than the ranks.

1. **Vietnam is not unique on cost.** Indonesia scores as well or better on labour, utilities and construction. Thailand is nearly as cheap and scores far higher on sugar, business and rules [@RGN-01] {VN-direct|High}.
2. **Cheap inputs act on a small share of cost.** In the study's model plants, labour and power together are only about 12% to 21% of cost. Feedstock is the largest line, at 37% to 56% [@RGN-01] {general|High}.
3. **The sugar score understates Vietnam.** It ignores cassava starch, Vietnam's main fermentable carbohydrate ([[ch04-asset-map]]). But most of that starch is already sold abroad (section 3.5).

The study's three recommendations for Vietnam are all about rules [@RGN-01] {VN-direct|High}:
- fund the 2023 biotechnology resolution through programmes with named agency mandates;
- make clear which sites qualify for incentives, and when;
- "publish regulatory procedures and statutory timelines for novel food approvals".

It also finds that a capital grant, cheaper feedstock or a loan guarantee does more for a project than a tax holiday [@RGN-01] {general|High}. The study is a useful map of relative position, not a site-selection model: it gives no cost per kilogram and uses an outdated business index [@RGN-01] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 3.2 Rules: who approves what

Singapore approves novel foods. Most neighbours have a route but few or no approvals.

| Country | Novel-food route | Cultivated meat route | Approvals (September 2026) | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Singapore | Approval before sale since 2019, now in law | Yes | 18 novel foods | [@RGN-04; @REG-63] {VN-adjacent\|High} |
| Thailand | Ministerial notification (2016), with recognised assessors | Applications accepted since 2024 | No cultivated approval | [@REG2-26; @RGN-32] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| South Korea | Temporary standards, extended to cell culture in 2024; review up to 270 working days | Yes | None found | [@RGN-19; @RGN-21; @RGN-34] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| Japan | Case by case; draft guidance May 2026 | Draft | None found | [@RGN-33] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| China | New food raw material approvals | Expected from late 2026 | Fungal and yeast protein reported | [@RGN-24; @RGN-25] {general\|Low} |
| Australia and New Zealand | Novel and cell-cultured food standards | Yes | Cultivated quail (2025) | [@RGN-18; @RGN-20] {general\|Medium} |
| Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines | General provision or none | No | None found | [@RGN-01; @RGN-19; @RGN-20] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| **Vietnam** | **No novel-food category** ([[ch07-rules]]) | **No** | **None** | [@RGN-01] {VN-direct\|Medium} |

**Singapore's list** includes six cultivated meats, six fungal or bacterial proteins and three precision-fermented ingredients [@RGN-04; @RGN-29] {VN-adjacent|High}. In the United States, cultivated salmon was cleared in 2025 [@RGN-03] {general|High}.

{{chart:chart-regional-approvals}}

**A route is not an approval.** Korea and Thailand opened applications in 2024, but neither had approved a cultivated product by September 2026 [@RGN-20; @RGN-21; @RGN-32; @RGN-34] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. Staff and published review times matter as much as the legal text.

**Halal.** In 2025 Malaysia ruled that cultivated meat can be halal [@RGN-20; @RGN-28; @RGN-30] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. This matters for any Vietnamese product aimed at Malaysian, Indonesian or Middle Eastern buyers.

## 3.3 Strategies and state money

Neighbours have strategies with public money behind them. We found no alternative-protein budget line in Vietnam.

| Country | Strategy | Money found | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singapore | 2025 food strategy; alternative protein left out of 2035 targets | S$144 M research programme (2019); S$42 M for future-food projects (2025) | [@RGN-08; @RGN-09; @RGN-22] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| Thailand | Biotechnology framework 2026 to 2035 names alternative protein; plans public pilot and contract-manufacturing plants | Proposed 500 M baht a year for synthetic biology | [@RGN-35] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| South Korea | Food tech promotion law; cultivated-meat research centre (2027) | About USD 10 M for the centre | [@RGN-19; @RGN-21] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| China | Plans for new feed protein and "new types of food" | About USD 1 bn of state investment in biotech plants (2025) | [@RGN-22; @RGN-24; @RGN-25; @RGN-43; @RGN-56] {general\|Medium} |
| Vietnam | 2023 biotechnology resolution; high-tech incentives ([[ch07-rules]]) | No alternative-protein line found | [@RGN-01] {VN-direct\|Medium} |

Two moves matter directly for Vietnam.

- **Thailand is responding to the same ranking.** It plans public pilot and contract-manufacturing plants [@RGN-35] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. A Vietnamese project would then compete with a neighbour that has cheaper sugar, a working approval route and public pilot plants.
- **Singapore has stepped back from production.** It now acts as regulator, research centre and financier [@RGN-08; @RGN-09] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. That leaves room for cheaper neighbours to make Singapore-approved products, if their plants meet Singapore's import rules ([[ch30-unknowns]]).

## 3.4 Pilot and contract capacity a Vietnamese company can use

A Vietnamese company that needs a pilot plant today has to go abroad. Several plants offer tolling: renting time on someone else's fermenter.

| Facility | Country | What | Food status | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ScaleUp Bio | Singapore | Up to 10,000 L of fermentation plus processing | Food licence (2024) | [@RGN-10; @RGN-11; @RGN-12; @RGN-13] {VN-adjacent\|High} |
| EECi Biorefinery Pilot Plant | Thailand | 12 fermenters, up to 15,000 L; contract manufacturing | Not stated | [@RGN-36] {VN-adjacent\|Medium} |
| Chinese contract manufacturers | China | Largest and cheapest; may keep process know-how | Varies | [@RGN-24] {general\|Medium} |

Vietnam has no open food-grade pilot plant ([[ch05-industrial-base]]). So a Vietnamese startup scales up in Singapore (licensed but dear), Thailand (larger tanks, food status to confirm) or China (cheapest, but exposes more know-how).

**China sets the price.** Angel Yeast makes about 11,000 t a year of yeast protein, and a 20,000 t a year fungal protein plant began runs in June 2026. One foreign client said scale-up in China took half the time and half the cost [@RGN-24] {general|Medium}. Any Vietnamese microbial protein will be priced against these plants.

## 3.5 Trade: Vietnam sells starch, not fermentation sugar

**Imports of food protein ingredients are small.** Vietnam reported about USD 56 million of imports of soy isolate, protein concentrates and textured protein in 2023, mostly from the United States and China [@RGN-45; @RGN-46] {VN-direct|Medium}. Chinese export data show larger tonnages at prices that suggest much is feed-grade [@RGN-47; @RGN-48; @RGN-49; @RGN-50] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**Carbohydrate exports are large.** Vietnam exported 2.15 Mt of cassava starch worth USD 1.06 billion in 2023, 94% of it to China [@RGN-53] {VN-direct|High}. In 2024 China bought 39% of its cassava starch imports from Vietnam, but only USD 0.16 million of glucose syrup [@RGN-51; @RGN-52; @RGN-54; @RGN-55] {VN-direct|High}.

{{chart:chart-cassava-starch-trade}}

**Reading.** For exported starch, the step to glucose, the feedstock of every fermentation plant, happens in China. Vietnam makes glucose too, but for the maker's own use or the home market [@VCO-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. Moving part of that step to Vietnam is the most direct way to plug into the regional build-out ([[ch26-plays]], play T4).

## 3.7 How neighbours built their rules

| | Singapore | Thailand | South Korea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Over 90% import dependence; new food agency | Green economy agenda; foreign applicants | Industrial policy; conglomerate research |
| Instrument | New approval before sale, later written into law | Ministerial notification plus recognised assessors | Amended an existing rule in about nine months |
| First cultivated approval | About 2 years (2020) | None by September 2026 | None by September 2026 |
| Worth copying | Public list with specifications; help before applying | Existing science bodies as assessors | Published fee and review time |

Sources: [@RGN-04; @RGN-19; @RGN-32; @RGN-34; @RGN-44; @REG2-26] {VN-adjacent\|Medium}.

**Lessons that transfer (our assessment).** {VN-adjacent|Medium}
1. **A new law is not needed.** Korea added a step to an existing rule in about nine months. Vietnam's equivalent is a clause in the Food Safety Law now being drafted, with a procedure in its decree ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch27-policy-options]]).
2. **Borrow assessors.** Thailand named existing bodies as assessors. Vietnam could use the National Institute for Food Control.
3. **Rely on others' work.** Vietnam already accepts five trusted countries' approvals for genetically modified (GM) food ([[ch07-rules]]). The same logic can apply to novel foods.
4. **Publish the timeline.** A published fee and review period give applicants something to plan around.

## 3.8 What this means

- **Investors:** Vietnam is a cost story without a food approval route. Until one exists, export under another country's approval is the clearer route. Price any microbial protein against Chinese plants.
- **Policy makers:** publish a novel-food procedure with a fixed timeline, and pair it with capital grants rather than tax holidays. Thailand is already acting.
- **Startups:** plan scale-up in Singapore, Thailand or China, and use a Singapore approval as the regional credential. Budget 9 to 12 months or more after a complete application [@RGN-17] {VN-adjacent|Low}.
- **Manufacturers:** the bigger lever is not protein imports (about USD 56 million) but the starch Vietnam already ships to China.
- **Researchers:** Chinese cassava-to-protein work and Thai pilot plants are the fastest partnerships for catching up.
- **International bodies:** help draft the procedure, and consider a Thailand-Vietnam pilot-plant access arrangement.
