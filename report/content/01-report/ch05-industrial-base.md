# 5. The industrial base: fermentation and food processing

**In brief.**
- Vietnam's industrial base is large where food-protein makers need buyers and co-packers, and thin where they need process equipment.
- Frozen-food processors already make vegetarian lines next to meat, and retail private labels show that contract manufacturing of vegetarian food works.
- The sterile, aerated fermentation tanks that biomass and precision fermentation need sit almost entirely inside three amino-acid makers: Vedan, Ajinomoto and Daesang. None publicly offers contract manufacturing.
- We found no food-grade contract fermentation, no open pilot fermenter, no protein-isolate or concentrate plant, and no Vietnamese maker of textured vegetable protein (TVP).
- Soybean crushing is growing fast, but only to make feed.
- Feed mills are large and run at about half their capacity. They are context for this study, not partners for its plays.

---

## 5.1 Fermentation: large in volume, captive in practice

**Aerobic fermentation** uses stirred, aerated, sterilisable tanks. It is the kind used for amino acids, yeast, biomass fermentation (growing microbes whose cells are the protein) and precision fermentation (engineered microbes that make one specific protein) ([[app-r3-glossary]]). In Vietnam it is concentrated in three groups:

| Group | Location | What it makes | Capacity signals | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vedan Vietnam (Taiwan) | Dong Nai (Long Thanh) | MSG and glutamic acid, lysine, starch, glucose syrup, feed and fertiliser co-products | Glutamic acid nameplate raised to 180,000 t/yr in 2004 (last published); 700 m3 per fermentation tank (tank count not disclosed); Vietnam revenue USD 154.3 M in 2025 (down 7%); capex for equipment replacement only | [@VCO-03; @IND-01; @VCO-01] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Ajinomoto Vietnam (Japan) | Dong Nai (Bien Hoa) | MSG by fermentation of cassava starch and cane molasses; liquid and solid feed ingredients as co-products | MSG 150,000 t/yr (2023 environmental assessment); liquid feed ingredients 30,000 m3/yr proposed to rise to 50,000 | [@IND-08; @IND-09] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Daesang Vietnam (Miwon, Korea) | Phu Tho (Viet Tri); starch in Tay Ninh (former Long An) | MSG (Miwon) and starch | Over 35,000 t/yr of Miwon seasoning (2020 Korean press, which places the plant in Bac Giang, now part of Bac Ninh) | [@IND-11; @IND-13] {VN-direct\|Low} |

MSG is monosodium glutamate; capex is capital spending.

> **Correction.** Earlier notes used Vedan's 1995 glutamic acid nameplate of 36,000 t/yr. The last published nameplate is 180,000 t/yr (2004) [@VCO-03]. Neither is confirmed as current.

We estimate installed aerobic, aseptic (sterile) fermentation volume in Vietnam at **about 11,000 to 34,000 m3**, almost all inside these three groups. Anaerobic volume adds fuel ethanol (about 38,000 to 58,000 m3) and brewing (about 170,000 to 410,000 m3). That gives roughly 220,000 to 500,000 m3 of fermentation vessels in total (modelled estimate from published capacities; see [[app-s5-facilities]] and `fermentation_volume_estimate.csv`) {VN-direct|Low}.

**Other fermentation assets:**
- **Baker's yeast:** AB Mauri makes yeast at La Nga, Dong Nai (6,000 t/yr in 2019, when it was suspended for a pollution incident; current status not re-checked). Saf-Viet (Lesaffre) is the other maker; its site was not confirmed by our sources and its capacity is undisclosed [@IND-15; @IND-16; @IND-17] {VN-direct|Medium}. Yeast is the closest existing process to yeast single-cell protein (protein-rich microbial biomass), and it is small.
- **Fuel ethanol:** six or seven plants with about 450,000 to 490,000 m3 a year of design capacity. Only three ran in mid-2026, and E10 demand (petrol with 10% ethanol) is now pulling idle plants back to fuel [@IND-18; @IND-19; @IND-20] {VN-direct|Medium}. Ethanol fermenters are not aerated, so converting one to protein would need a retrofit, and the conversion would compete with E10 economics.
- **Breweries:** Sabeco runs 26 breweries with 2.4 billion litres a year of design capacity (about 60% used in 2024). Heineken's Vung Tau plant has 1.1 billion litres approved to rise to 1.6 billion. Carlsberg planned to raise its Phu Bai plant in Hue from 360 to 600 million litres [@IND-23; @IND-26; @IND-27] {VN-direct|Medium}. Breweries matter for food-grade side streams (spent grain and spent yeast, for koji and fungal foods) and trained staff, not for aerobic capacity.
- **Biologics:** Vietnam has 12 veterinary vaccine plants certified to the World Health Organization's good manufacturing practice (GMP-WHO), and Vabiotech makes a recombinant hepatitis B vaccine [@IND-34; @IND-37] {VN-direct|Medium}. A 100-million-dose human vaccine plant (VNVC, technology from Sanofi) is due in Tay Ninh at the end of 2027 [@IND-31; @IND-32] {VN-direct|Medium}. These are sources of GMP skills and quality systems, not tank volume.

**No contract fermentation.** We found no Vietnamese company offering food-grade contract fermentation at pilot or commercial scale. We also found no public facility that publishes an open pilot-fermentation service [@IND-01; @IND-08; @INF-02; @INF-04; @INF-05] {VN-direct|Medium}. The Food Industries Research Institute's (FIRI) pilot centre in Hanoi lists soy-milk, tofu, juice and powder processes but no fermenters [@INF-02] {VN-direct|High}. This is an absence of evidence in published sources, and direct enquiry should test it. Vedan's falling Vietnamese MSG sales and replacement-only capex [@VCO-01] suggest that the incumbents could have both idle capacity and a motive to talk. That is a hypothesis, not a finding. This gap is why shared pilot and tolling fermentation (play T5; tolling means renting time on someone else's fermenter) is one of the three plays that lead ([[ch26-plays]]).

## 5.2 Feed mills: large and half used, but context only

Feed matters to this study as the hidden import behind the meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish that people eat, not as a product to make. In 2023 there were 269 industrial feed mills with 43.2 Mt a year of design capacity. They produced 20.8 Mt in 2022, about 48% utilisation [@IND-39] {VN-direct|Medium}. Feed mills are not partners for this study's plays. The earlier record of the feed and aquafeed market is in [[app-s6-feed-market]]. Plant-level data, including the 2025 to 2026 consolidation of feed makers, are in [[app-s5-facilities]].

## 5.3 Soy and protein-ingredient processing

- **Crushing.** VAL (Bunge and Wilmar) crushes soybeans at Phu My 1 Industrial Park, Ho Chi Minh City (former Ba Ria-Vung Tau). After a USD 100 million second line in December 2025, it can crush 2.6 Mt of soybeans a year. City leaders expect it to supply about 30% of domestic soybean meal [@VCO-16; @FORM-10] {VN-direct|Medium}. The US Department of Agriculture (USDA) forecasts national crush rising to 3.0 Mt in 2027 [@MAC-04] {VN-direct|Medium}. **No food-grade defatted flake or soy-protein line is on record.**
- **Protein isolates and concentrates.** We found no soy, pea or rice protein isolate or concentrate plant in Vietnam [@FORM-11] {VN-direct|Medium}. Distributors sell Chinese soy protein and Chinese wheat gluten in 20 to 25 kg bags [@FORM-11; @FORM-13] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Textured vegetable protein (TVP).** No Vietnamese TVP extruder was identified. Chinese extrusion lines of 100 to 2,000 kg/h are marketed in Vietnam, and at least one chay maker says it imports food from partners in the United States, Australia and Canada [@FORM-34; @FORM-35] {VN-direct|Medium}. The only extrusion we documented is feed-grade (for example, Haid's aquafeed plants, including Hai Dai Vinh Long) [@IND-43] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Soy milk and tofu.** Vinasoy (owned by Quang Ngai Sugar) has had 390 million litres a year of soy-milk capacity since its third plant opened in 2017. The figure was still given in 2022 [@IND-49; @IND-50]. It claims about 80% of the soy-milk market [@IND-48] {VN-direct|Medium}. Tofu is mostly artisanal. One industrial maker, ToFu House in Hung Yen, states a capacity of 150 to 500 t a month and is building model fields of domestic soybeans [@IND-51] {VN-direct|Medium}. Tofu skin (*tàu hũ ky*) comes from craft villages such as My Hoa in Vinh Long, mostly from imported beans [@FORM-14] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**What this means for food.** A food-grade soy flour or flake stream from the growing crush sector is the obvious input for domestic textured plant protein (play T1). Nobody makes it yet.

## 5.4 Food processors and co-packers

- **Meat and frozen-food processors with vegetarian lines:**
  - Vissan (Ho Chi Minh City; about two-thirds owned by the state trading group SATRA, about a quarter by Masan MEATLife) [@VCO-13; @BUY-04];
  - CJ Foods Vietnam (Cau Tre and bibigo brands, owned by CJ CheilJedang) [@VCO-12];
  - Cholimex and SG Food (both in Vinh Loc Industrial Park) [@FORM-27; @FORM-31] {VN-direct|Medium}.

  These firms make *đồ chay* (traditional vegetarian food) on the same lines as meat products. They are the most plausible co-packers for plant-based products.
- **Private labels:** Saigon Co.op's Coop Select vegetarian lines are made under contract (one line by GN Foods in Tay Ninh) [@FORM-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. Retail private labels show that chay contract manufacturing already works.
- **Dairy:** Vinamilk, TH and Nutifood all sell plant-based milks; several are blended with cow's milk [@ECO-43; @ECO-45; @AIB-10] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Starch and glucose:** Vedan built 140,000 t a year of glucose syrup capacity in 1995 and sells glucose to third parties. Daesang has run a starch plant in Tay Ninh since 2005 [@VCO-03; @VCO-05; @IND-11] {VN-direct|Medium}. Glucose syrup from cassava is the obvious local carbon source for food fermentation, and it already exists.
- **Spray drying:** available in instant-coffee and dairy plants; toll drying for third parties was not documented.

## 5.5 Shared infrastructure and services

- **Parks:** Saigon Hi-Tech Park covers 913 ha with 165 projects and an incubator, a research and development (R&D) centre and a biotech centre [@INF-06] {VN-direct|High}. Hoa Lac Hi-Tech Park in Hanoi has a 263 ha R&D zone [@INF-25] {VN-direct|Medium}. A 2026 decree lets provinces create high-tech agriculture zones [@INF-23] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Testing:** FIRI's National Center for Food Analysis and Assessment (NACEFA) is ISO/IEC 17025-accredited (VILAS 259, its number under the Vietnam Laboratory Accreditation Scheme) and designated to test imported feed ingredients. The National Institute for Food Control (NIFC) is the Ministry of Health's reference food laboratory. QUATEST 3 opened a transaction office in Dong Nai in September 2026 [@INF-03; @INF-10; @INF-13] {VN-direct|High}. No laboratory page we read lists a protein-digestibility assay, which a protein-quality claim or a novel-food dossier needs. NACEFA lists amino-acid analysis by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) only (scope and accreditation unconfirmed) ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **Feed trials (context):** the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment (MAE) reorganised its state centres that run feed and aquafeed trials in 2025 to 2026; both may sell services [@INF-18; @INF-28] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Engineering:** plants are typically designed by foreign firms (Rieckermann designed the VNVC vaccine plant) and fitted out by local contractors (HTG Engineering built utilities and cleanrooms for Ajinomoto) [@IND-31; @IND-64; @INF-24] {VN-direct|Medium}. We found no local maker of sanitary fermenters, extruders or spray dryers.

## 5.6 What a food-protein venture can and cannot borrow

| Accredited testing (proximate, contaminants) | Yes | NACEFA, NIFC, Eurofins, QUATEST 3 | Yes |
| Amino-acid profile and digestibility testing | Digestibility not found on laboratory pages; NACEFA lists amino-acid analysis by HPLC (scope and accreditation unconfirmed) | NACEFA (amino acids only) | Likely overseas for now |
| Feed pelleting and extrusion (context) | Yes, about 48% utilised | 269 feed mills | Not a partner for food plays |

UHT is ultra-high-temperature processing, used for long-life drinks.

**For each reader.**
- **Investors:** do not back a thesis that assumes rentable aerobic fermentation in Vietnam. Treat Vedan and Ajinomoto as possible strategic partners, not as vendors: they have tanks, glucose and falling or flat MSG sales.
- **Startups:** the shortest paths to scale are:
  - tolling or joint-venture talks with an amino-acid producer;
  - frozen-food co-packers that already make chay;
  - cassava glucose bought from Vedan or other sellers as fermentation carbon.

  Otherwise, bring your own extruder or fermenter.
- **Manufacturers:** a food-grade soy stream from the growing crush sector is the obvious feedstock for Vietnamese TVP; nobody makes it yet.
- **Policy makers and international bodies:** the clearest missing public good is a shared, food-grade pilot facility with open-access rules. It would combine fermentation of about 50 to 3,000 litres, extrusion and spray drying. Saigon Hi-Tech Park or FIRI are natural hosts ([[ch27-policy-options]]).
