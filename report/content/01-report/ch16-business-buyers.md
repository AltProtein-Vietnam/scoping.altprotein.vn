# 16. Business buyers: the market for protein ingredients

**In brief.**
- **Food firms are the clearest buyers of new protein.** Vietnam imported about USD 285 million of protein ingredients in 2023, including USD 87 million of plant proteins.
- **Plant-protein imports from China nearly tripled from 2019 to 2025,** to about 54,000 t. But much of the jump is a cheap flow that looks like feed, so the food pool is smaller.
- **Domestic makers have no tariff shelter.** Chinese, Korean and Southeast Asian protein ingredients enter duty-free.
- **Win food makers first.** *Chay* (vegetarian food) makers are the most import-dependent and the most willing to switch. Meat processors use soy as a binder but do not extend meat at scale. Vinamilk buys pea protein for one niche milk.
- **No buyer is trying novel protein.** We found no Vietnamese food company piloting fungal protein, precision-fermented dairy protein or a novel blend.
- **A new ingredient must choose its price rung.** Commodity plant proteins cost about USD 2 per kg of protein; egg and dairy proteins USD 7 to 11.

**Plant proteins grew fastest by tonnage.** Chinese shipments of plant proteins to Vietnam rose from 18,375 t in 2019 to 53,976 t in 2025 [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Wheat gluten doubled. Chinese textured protein grew 54%; it did not triple.

{{chart:chart-plant-protein-imports}}

**Food imports follow the *chay* calendar.** Chinese textured protein shipments to Vietnam rise by a third before the Vu Lan month, the peak of chay eating, while shipments elsewhere stay flat [@BRD-05; @BRD-06] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. For an ingredient supplier, the seasonal swing is modest.

**Much of the 2024 jump is probably not food.** It came from one cheap line at about USD 1 per kg, less than half the usual price, with no lunar timing. Vietnamese customs mostly did not record it under the same code [@BRD-05; @BRD-06; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Price and timing suggest feed, pet food or technical use. If so, the food pool is nearer 35,000 t than the 40,000 t the demand model uses ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]) (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**Trade data cannot split food from feed.** Treat plant-protein imports as an upper bound for food demand until someone analyses individual customs declarations. That is one of the cheapest public goods on the demand side ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

{{chart:chart-ingredient-price-ladder}}

**Two rungs.** Commodity plant proteins cost about USD 1.7 to 2.4 per kg of protein at the border. Egg, dairy and premium plant proteins cost USD 7 to 11 and more [@BUY-02; @BUY-07; @BUY-09] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

**A domestic line would meet the cheapest grade.** China's price to Vietnam is its lowest to any partner, and India and Serbia sell lower still [@TIC-14; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-border-protein-price}}

**No tariff shelter.** Chinese, Southeast Asian and Korean product enters duty-free under regional trade agreements. Pea protein from France and Canada does too [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Duties of 3% to 10% apply only to other origins, chiefly the US [@REG2-20; @REG2-23; @FTR-32; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Chinese textured protein lands at about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg, or USD 2.0 to 2.1 per kg of protein (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-fta-duty-china}}

**A new ingredient must choose its rung and its job** (our calculation) [@BUY-02; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}:
- **Commodity textured protein or isolate:** match the landed cost of Chinese product.
- **Meat extender**, replacing 10% to 30% of the lean: match hydrated soy, about USD 2.6 to 2.8 per kg of protein ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **Binder**, holding water and fat at 1% to 3% of a recipe: price against egg and milk proteins. Fungal protein that binds as well could reach about USD 3.3 to 4.6 per kg. Here it meets a Chinese yeast protein already marketed in Vietnam (section 16.5).

**Shop and kitchen prices are far higher.** Dried textured soy retails at 10 to 16 times the Chinese border price [@BUY-36; @BUY-37; @FORM-01; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Dry soy pieces sold to kitchens cost about VND 20,000 to 25,000 per 100 g of protein, and branded tofu about VND 36,000 to 48,000 (our calculation) [@CPC-12; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

## 16.3 Who the buyers are: food makers, led by chay makers

**Chay makers: the most willing to switch.** Every protein-rich local chay product we audited uses imported textured soy or gluten ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]]). The makers, such as An Nhien, Au Lac, Thoai An, LC Foods and SG Food, are owner-run firms that can trial a new ingredient in weeks, but each buys small volumes [@FORM-01; @FORM-28; @EXP-38] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. One startup founder said over 90% of its raw materials were imported, because local ones had "two problems, quality and price" [@BUY-38; @AIS-41] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:stated}.

Chay makers also reach shelves through store brands. One co-packer makes all of Co.op's frozen chay dishes [@CPC-15; @ECO-35] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Unnamed small makers sell dry soy pieces online and to kitchens: a buyer group still to trace [@MKT-03; @MKT-04; @CPC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

**Meat processors: small amounts of added protein already.** A third of Vissan's meat recipes list soy protein, mostly in cheap lines [@BUY-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Added proteins bind water and fat and cut cost. As a binder buyer, a large processor needs tens to hundreds of tonnes a year, not thousands (our calculation) [@BUY-04; @BUY-05] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**Extension is a prize Vissan has not taken.** Extending 20% of the lean in its cheap lines would save VND 58 to 112 billion a year, against a 2025 pre-tax profit of VND 105 billion. Its labels suggest it does not do this (our calculation) [@BUY-04; @BUY-05; @TIC-12; @DIE-25] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. So what limits extension is product identity, texture and trust, not cost ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

Cheap imported pork lowers the saving for some processors [@MIM-09; @MIM-07] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The big meat groups are investing in animal protein: Vissan in a pig farm, Masan in branded processed meat [@AIB-14; @AIB-15; @BUY-10; @AIB-12] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. C.P.'s imported plant-based range is no longer orderable [@BUY-31; @AIB-19; @AIS-25] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Dairy and plant milks: the clearest pull for a non-soy isolate.** Vinamilk's high-protein nut milk gets its protein "mainly from pea", sold as soy-free and non-GMO [@BUY-16; @BUY-15] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. It is the only large Vietnamese buyer we found using a non-soy isolate. The pull comes from the product, not a company target, and the milk sells as a niche [@AFN-21; @AIB-03; @MKT-03; @MKT-04] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. The wider plant-milk business is growing [@AIB-05; @AIB-40] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Regional plant-milk imports are mostly duty-free, so local makers' edge is distribution [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. A sugar tax from 2027 may reach sweet plant milks, so isolate buyers will want recipes at or below 5 g of sugar per 100 ml ([[ch13-consumers]]) [@FTR-01; @FTR-02; @FTR-05; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Infant and medical nutrition: demanding and slow.** These makers buy whey and milk proteins, market "non-GMO" and are moving into clinical research [@BUY-02; @BUY-19] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. They are the buyers for precision-fermented dairy proteins, but approving a new supplier is slow and legal status is the gate ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

**Instant noodles: volume, not yet protein.** Vietnamese eat about 81 servings each a year [@BUY-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Acecook uses soy only in sachets, and already buys pea starch, a by-product of making pea protein [@BUY-22; @AIB-18] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. So a pulse-protein plant would have starch buyers waiting.

**Others.** Seasoning makers buy protein as flavour; bakery buys egg and dairy; sports nutrition imports finished goods [@BUY-26; @BUY-02; @BUY-08] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

## 16.4 What buyers specify

- **Meat pastes and sausages:** gel strength, binding, stability through canning heat, and a bland flavour [@BUY-05; @BUY-42] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. For extension, smooth pastes take a fine powder at 10% to 20% of the lean, not granules; coarse mince takes up to 30% [@HXE-09] {general|Medium} {dx:tested}. Graininess cut liking of *chả lụa* (pork roll) in a Vietnamese test [@HXE-02; @HXE-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:tested}. An extender keeps the meat name, but premium lines sold on "no filler" will not want one [@LBL-11; @LBL-06; @TRU-27] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.
- **Plant milks:** soluble, stable in long-life processing, neutral in taste, with a complete amino-acid profile and documents for soy-free and non-GMO claims [@BUY-16] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Mung bean isolates of 85% to 88% protein have been made from Thai beans [@BUY-47] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **Chay products:** set granule sizes and slices, in 20 to 25 kg bags; exporters want non-GMO and halal papers [@FORM-28; @FORM-11; @FORM-13; @EXP-38] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **All buyers:** certificates of analysis and traceable origin. Food-safety crises in 2025 and 2026 have raised the bar for new suppliers [@BUY-20; @BUY-45; @MAC-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. Vinamilk says traceability is now "mandatory rather than a competitive advantage" [@AIB-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:stated}.

No buyer publishes minimum orders or how long it takes to approve a new supplier. That approval time is the most important unknown for a founder.

## 16.5 How ingredients are bought

Large processors import major ingredients directly and buy minor ones through distributors, and they expect to meet suppliers in person [@MAC-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. Large buyers import plant proteins by the container; small firms buy 20 to 25 kg bags from distributors [@BUY-09; @BUY-35; @BUY-36; @BUY-37; @FORM-11] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Vietnam's main ingredients fair in 2026 had about 250 exhibitors, mostly Chinese [@BUY-33] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Fermented protein is already on offer.** A distributor has marketed a Chinese yeast protein to Vietnamese bakery, dairy and plant-based makers since 2025, with no public price or named user [@AIS-38; @CPC-21; @CPC-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Kitchens buy finished plant protein through supply platforms,** mainly tofu and dry soy pieces. None lists dry textured soy or a Western meat copy [@CPC-12; @CPC-13; @CPC-14] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

## 16.6 Novel protein: a clean negative, with two qualifications

We found no Vietnamese food company piloting fungal protein, precision-fermented dairy protein or a novel blend. No Vietnamese protein buyer publishes an alternative-protein target. A 2026 ranking of 100 Asian protein buyers found only 2 with such targets, none in Vietnam [@AFN-18; @AFN-21] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The big Vietnamese firms are diversifying into more animal protein [@AIB-01; @AIB-12; @AIB-19; @BUY-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:stated}.

**Two qualifications.** A small startup, Emmay, sells about 2 t a month of mushroom foods, including a fermented fungal protein [@AIS-39] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. And a Chinese yeast protein is on offer (section 16.5). The newer ingredients with proven uptake are pea isolate in one Vinamilk milk, and the soy, wheat and milk proteins already in meat pastes.

**Likely first customers, by product** {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The plays named here are set out in [[ch26-plays]].
- **Domestic textured soy (play T1):** chay makers such as An Nhien, Au Lac, Thoai An, LC Foods and SG Food first, for fast trials. Then the chay lines of the big frozen-food makers, store-brand co-packers and kitchen suppliers. A T1 line is best owned by a chay maker, co-packer or distributor and financed with debt. Its pool is up to about 12,800 t a year of cheap imports ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
- **Rice or mung bean isolate (play T10):** Vinamilk, Vinasoy and Nutifood, replacing imported pea, with starch sold to noodle makers. Vietnam imports most of its mung beans, so a "domestic" isolate may still rest on imports.
- **Mung bean isolate replacing egg white:** meat processors and bakeries. Vietnam imported 2,536 t of egg white and egg powder in 2025 [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Mung bean protein is accepted as safe in the US [@SLP-11] {general|High}. This job pays better than plant milk, but is small (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **Fungal protein (play T9), by job:** as a binder for cheap meat lines, bakery and chay seasonings, priced against egg and milk proteins. As an extender, it must reach the price of hydrated soy.
- **Precision-fermented dairy protein (play T7):** specialty nutrition at Nutifood and Vinamilk, once the Food Safety Law and the GMO question are settled.

## 16.8 What this means for new protein products

**For each reader.**
- **Startups making textured protein:** a line is an import-replacement plant, not a venture. Aim to be owned by, or contracted to, a chay maker, co-packer or distributor. Test two things first ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]): whether food-grade soy flour is delivered below about USD 700 to 940 per t, and whether three buyers will order 2,000 t a year.
- **Startups making isolates or fungal protein:** design isolates for dairy buyers, keep drinks low in sugar and plan to sell the starch. Pitch fungal protein by job: as a binder against egg and dairy, or as an extender against soy. Win trials with certificates, not claims.
- **Investors:** plant-protein imports are worth about USD 87 to 109 million a year, an unknown share of it feed. Duty-free Chinese supply caps prices, so returns depend on volume and co-products, not premiums. A textured-protein line installs for about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million, so the need is mostly working capital, suited to debt. Vietnam already offers invoice finance against an anchor buyer, equipment leases and loan guarantees for small firms. The anchor buyer matters most [@SLK-01; @SLK-05; @SLK-19; @SLK-20] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Manufacturers:** working with a local protein maker can secure soy-free, traceable supply, but no tariff edge. A co-packer can take a protein upgrade to a store brand. Test any new extender against your own soy recipe.
- **Researchers:** publish tests of local proteins in Vietnamese products, at equal cost to what they would replace. Offer samples in 20 to 25 kg lots, which is how small buyers test.
- **Policy makers:** finer customs codes for soy isolate, pea protein and textured protein would make demand visible. Settle whether an identical protein made by microbes counts as a detectable GMO product, and whether sweet plant milks are soft drinks for the new tax.
- **International organisations and funders:** fund an analysis of customs declarations, a price sheet of delivered soy flour quotes, and a standing panel of 10 to 15 food-industry buyers. Together they would tell every founder how much of the import pool is food, whether a textured-soy line pays, and how long buyers take to approve a supplier.
