# 16. Business buyers: the market for protein ingredients

**In one paragraph.** The clearest revealed demand for new protein in Vietnam comes from firms. Vietnam imported about USD 285 million of protein ingredients in 2023, including USD 87 million of plant proteins, and the plant proteins that China reports shipping to Vietnam nearly tripled in tonnage from 2019 to 2025, to about 54,000 t, at about USD 0.9 to 1.3 per kg. Most of that jump is a cheap HS 3504 flow with no lunar timing that Vietnamese customs mostly did not record as 3504, which fits feed better than food; textured protein, by contrast, rises a third before the Vu Lan *chay* season. Chinese, ASEAN and Korean protein ingredients enter at 0% duty, so a domestic maker has no tariff shelter against Chinese product landing at about USD 1.3 to 1.4 per kg. Industrial *đồ chay* (traditional vegetarian food) makers are the most import-dependent and most switchable buyers; meat processors already use soy, wheat, milk and egg proteins as binders, and 31% of one large processor's meat formulations list soy protein, though extension that would pay at every pork price is not done at scale; Vinamilk buys pea protein for a soy-free high-protein nut milk that sells as a niche; infant and medical nutrition makers pay dairy-protein prices of USD 8 to 14 per kg. We found no Vietnamese protein buyer that publishes an alternative-protein target, and no Vietnamese food company piloting mycoprotein, precision-fermented dairy protein or a novel-protein hybrid, though a Chinese yeast protein is now marketed to makers. The price ladder has two rungs, commodity plant proteins at about USD 2 per kg of protein and egg, dairy and premium plant proteins at USD 7 to 18 (mostly 7 to 11), and a new ingredient must choose its rung and its job.

**Plant proteins are the fastest-growing tonnage.** China-reported shipments of HS 3504, 2106.10 and 1109 to Vietnam rose from 18,375 t (2019) to 28,323 t (2023), 51,130 t (2024) and 53,976 t (2025), worth USD 59.7 million in 2025 [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Most of the 2024 jump came from HS 3504 at about USD 1 per kg (see below). Wheat gluten from all origins doubled, from 11,327 t (2019) to 21,991 t (2025) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. China-origin textured protein (2106.10) grew 54% from 2019 to 2025; it did not triple. In 2025 Vietnam imported 12,832 t of 2106.10 from its three low-cost origins, China, India and Serbia, which is the pool a domestic soy extrusion line could serve [@BUY-02; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-china-plant-protein-tonnage}}

{{chart:chart-plant-protein-imports}}

**Monthly data separate chay food from the new 3504 flow.** China's monthly shipments of textured protein to Vietnam run 33% above an average month in the two months before the Vu Lan month, the peak of *chay* eating in the seventh lunar month, in five of six years (2019 to 2024), against 4% for the same code to the world and 2% to Thailand; that seasonal excess is about 5.5% of annual tonnage, some 550 t in 2024 (our calculation) [@BRD-05; @BRD-06] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The 2024 jump in HS 3504 has no such timing. It began in October 2023, was priced at USD 1.03 per kg against USD 2.46 for all China's 3504 exports, and was mostly not recorded as 3504 by Vietnamese customs (38% of China's reported value in October to December 2023, against full-year ratios of 84 to 101% in 2019 to 2022) [@BRD-05; @BRD-06; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Price, timing and mirror records all support the reading that the jump is a feed, pet-food or technical protein, not a food isolate; the importers are still unidentified. If it is not food, the imported food pool is nearer 35 kt of product than the 40 kt the demand model uses ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]) (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. For an ingredient supplier the seasonal swing is modest: about a third more demand before Vu Lan and a trough in the *Tết* (lunar new year) month, not the two to three times swing of retail chay sales (`trade_seasonal_summary.csv`).

**What the trade data cannot tell us.** The split between food and feed, and between buyer segments, is not visible at six-digit level. Treat plant-protein imports as an upper bound for food demand until a declaration-level dataset (eight-digit code, description, importer) is analysed; this is one of the cheapest public goods on the demand side ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

The ladder has two rungs {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. Commodity plant proteins cost about USD 1.7 to 2.4 per kg of protein at the border; egg, dairy and premium plant proteins cost USD 7 to 11 and more [@BUY-02; @BUY-07; @BUY-09] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. The Chinese textured-protein price of USD 1.24 per kg is China's lowest to any of eight Asian and US partners (world average USD 1.95), and India (2,372 t at USD 0.98) and Serbia (259 t at USD 0.85) sold lower still, so a domestic line would meet the lowest-priced grade on the market [@TIC-14; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-border-protein-price}}

**No tariff shelter.** Chinese, ASEAN and Korean product pays no duty: HS 2106.10, 3504 and 1109 are at 0% under ACFTA, AKFTA and RCEP in 2025 to 2027 and under ATIGA (0% in the latest schedule we read, to 2022) with a certificate of origin, and pea protein (3504) also enters at 0% from France under EVFTA and Canada under CPTPP [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The most-favoured-nation rates (5% for 2106.10, 3% for 3504, 10% for gluten) apply only to other origins, chiefly the United States [@REG2-20; @REG2-23; @FTR-32; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. With freight, insurance and port costs, Chinese textured protein lands at about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg, or about USD 2.0 to 2.1 per kg of protein (our calculation, `t1_landed_competitor.csv`, assuming freight and insurance at 3 to 8% of FOB value, port and inland costs of USD 15 to 40 per t and 65% protein) [@TIC-12; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Whether every importer claims the preference is not known; declaration data would show it. {{kn:kn-fta-duty-china}}

A new ingredient must decide which rung, and which job, it competes on (our calculation) [@BUY-02; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}:
- **Commodity textured protein or isolate:** match Chinese landed cost.
- **Meat extender** (10 to 30% of the lean; hundreds of tonnes at a large processor): match hydrated soy, about USD 2.6 to 2.8 per kg of protein. For a fungal biomass at 45% protein that is about USD 0.7 to 1.2 per kg dry, unless it lets the processor replace more meat unnoticed than soy ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **Binder or functional protein** (1 to 3% of a recipe; tens of tonnes a buyer): price against egg and dairy proteins. Fungal biomass at 45% protein could reach about USD 3.3 per kg against egg white and USD 3.9 to 4.6 per kg against casein or whey concentrate if it delivers binding, texture, flavour or solubility. Here it meets a Chinese yeast protein already marketed in Vietnam (16.5).

Ingredient distributors list gluten as "contact for price", and retail prices of dried textured soy (VND 327,000 to 500,000 per kg) are about 10 to 16 times the Chinese border unit value of USD 1.24 per kg for HS 2106.10 (our calculation) [@BUY-36; @BUY-37; @FORM-01; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The first public business-to-business prices we found are for finished kitchen products: on Kamereo, a platform supplying restaurants and hotels, domestic dry soy-gluten chay pieces sell at VND 135,000 per kg (VND 118,692 by the carton), about VND 20,000 to 25,000 per 100 g of protein on a borrowed protein range, and firm branded tofu at about VND 36,000 to 48,000 per 100 g of protein (our calculation) [@CPC-12; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

## 16.3 Who the buyers are

**Chay makers: the most switchable.** Every protein-dense local chay product in our audit uses imported textured soy, soy concentrate, soy isolate or gluten ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]]). The makers are owner-managed firms that can trial a new ingredient in weeks but each buys small volumes: An Nhien (imported Serbian soy concentrate on its label), Au Lac (an exporter), Thoai An (35% gluten in its vegetarian pork roll), LC Foods (textured soy granules of 4 to 7 mm), SG Food and the chay lines of Vissan, CJ Cau Tre and Cholimex [@FORM-01; @FORM-28; @EXP-38] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. A Vietnamese plant-based startup founder said in 2021 that more than 90% of its raw materials were imported because domestic ones had "two problems, quality and price" [@BUY-38; @AIS-41] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:stated}. Chay makers also reach shelves through retailer private label: one certified co-packer, GN Foods, makes all five Co.op Select frozen chay dishes, using wheat protein, soybean or no protein ingredient [@CPC-15; @ECO-35] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. And they sell dry pieces to households and kitchens: small online shops have sold about 40,700 kg of dry soy-and-gluten "ribs" on Lazada, and a domestic brand, Panko, sells similar pieces to kitchens through Kamereo; the makers behind the online shops are not named, so they are a buyer group still to trace [@MKT-03; @MKT-04; @CPC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

**Meat processors: low-inclusion blends already.** In an online label audit of 118 Vissan products, 22 of 67 unique meat formulations (33%) list soy protein and 25 (37%) list at least one added protein, mostly in value lines such as canned meats, pâté, *giò lụa* (plain, and with head meat or skin) and nutrition sausages [@BUY-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The pattern is functional: added proteins bind water and fat and cut meat cost. Vissan made 18,547 t of processed food in 2025 and plans 20,350 t in 2026 [@BUY-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Illustratively, if 30 to 50% of that output carried soy protein at 1 to 3%, Vissan would buy about 55 to 280 t a year (our calculation) [@BUY-04; @BUY-05] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}: as a binder buyer, a large processor is a customer of tens to hundreds of tonnes, not thousands. Extension is a larger prize that Vissan has not taken. A 20% extension of the lean in the same lines would need 224 to 434 t of textured soy (or 249 to 482 t of fungal biomass) and save VND 58 to 112 billion a year at median hog prices, against a 2025 pre-tax profit of VND 105.1 billion; its labels suggest it does not extend at that scale (our calculation, `hybrid_prize.csv`) [@BUY-04; @BUY-05; @TIC-12; @DIE-25] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Because soy extension pays at every pork price ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]), what limits it is product identity, texture and trust, not cost. Vissan's 2026 shareholder meeting set no chay target, and one of its two large projects is a 32,000-head pig farm (the other relocates its slaughter and processing plant) [@AIB-14; @AIB-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The business press describes Masan MEATLife as a "consumer protein platform" [@BUY-10], and Masan says it is moving into marinated and processed branded meat [@AIB-12] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. C.P. Vietnam, whose revenue was THB 101.3 billion in 2025 (food THB 8.2 billion), runs a USD 250 million processed-chicken complex and owns a caterer, Dzine Food Solutions; its parent's Meat Zero plant-based range, imported from Thailand, was listed on a Vietnamese HoReCa (hotel, restaurant and catering) platform and is no longer orderable [@BUY-31; @AIB-19; @AIS-25] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Dairy and plant milks: the clearest pull for a non-soy isolate.** Vinamilk's high-protein nut milk delivers 9 g of protein per 180 ml "mainly from pea" and is sold as soy-free, lactose-free, non-GMO, gluten-free and glyphosate-free, aimed at "people wary of soy"; its page sets 5 g per 100 ml as the threshold for a high-protein claim [@BUY-16] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. A press summary of Vinamilk's H1 2026 results (total revenue VND 35,034 billion) names its high-protein and plant milks among the contributors to domestic results [@BUY-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. This is the only large Vietnamese buyer we found using a non-soy protein isolate. Its pull is product-led, not commitment-led: FAIRR records Vinamilk's plant-based products at 1.30% of its range with no timebound diversification target, and Vinamilk promotes its high-protein dairy milk on milk protein being "more easily absorbed" than plant protein [@AFN-21; @AIB-03] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. The high-protein nut milk sells as a niche: about 70,300 L on Vinamilk's Lazada store, about one-eighth of its standard nut milk by litres (our calculation on cumulative counters) [@MKT-03; @MKT-04] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. The wider plant-milk business is growing: Vinasoy sold 264 million litres in 2025 with its Veyo nut milks up 50%, and Vietnam imported USD 3.9 billion of tree nuts in 2025, up 40%, partly for plant milk [@AIB-05; @AIB-40] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Two fiscal rules shape the category. Regional plant-milk imports are duty-free (2202.99 at 0% under ACFTA, ATIGA and AKFTA, though 12 to 18% under RCEP in 2025 to 2027; most-favoured-nation rates of 20 to 30% apply to non-FTA origins), so local makers' advantage is distribution, not tariffs [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. From 1 January 2027 drinks above 5 g of total sugar per 100 ml pay an 8% excise (10% from 2028); dairy is exempt, whether soy and nut milks count is unresolved, and the reference high-protein nut milk lists 6.5% added sugar, so an isolate buyer will want formulations at or below 5 g ([[ch13-consumers]]) [@FTR-01; @FTR-02; @FTR-05; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Infant and medical nutrition: demanding and slow.** These makers use whey protein concentrate (Vietnam imported 1,634 t in 2025), demineralised whey, casein and milk protein concentrates, market "non-GMO" and are moving towards clinical research (Nutifood signed a memorandum of understanding on research and clinical trials with Military Hospital 175 in June 2026) [@BUY-02; @BUY-19] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. They are the buyers for precision-fermented dairy proteins, but qualification is slow and regulatory status is the gate; low-dose ingredients such as lactoferrin fall below the 5% trigger of the mandatory GMO label ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

**Instant noodles: volume, not yet protein.** Vietnam ate 8,231 million servings in 2025, about 81 per person, fourth in the world [@BUY-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Acecook uses soy protein only in seasoning and garnish sachets, but already buys pea starch, a co-product of pulse protein fractionation, and calcium-fortifies its best-selling brand; we found no protein-fortified noodle [@BUY-22] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Its July 2025 strategy moves into convenient seasonings, ready-to-eat foods and meal-replacement snacks; the nutrition steps it lists for existing lines are calcium, vitamin B12 and real meat and vegetables in instant pho and bun, and it makes no protein claim [@AIB-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. A pulse-protein plant would have starch buyers waiting.

**Seasoning, bakery and sports nutrition.** Seasoning makers buy protein as flavour (for example bone-and-meat extract in Aji-ngon) [@BUY-26] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}; bakery protein demand is likely egg and dairy (our inference from egg-albumin and dairy-protein imports) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}; sports nutrition imports finished products, not ingredients [@BUY-08] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

## 16.4 What buyers specify

- **Meat pastes and sausages:** gel strength, water and fat binding, emulsion stability through retort sterilisation, bland flavour and light colour, used with starch, fibre, phosphates and carrageenan [@BUY-05] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Two isolates at the same price can perform differently in the same paste [@BUY-42] {general|Medium}. For extension rather than binding, fine emulsions take a fine isolate (or a similar fine ingredient) at 10 to 20% of the lean, not granules, and coarse mince up to about 30% [@HXE-09] {general|Medium} {dx:tested}. We found no published Vietnamese study of soy protein in *giò lụa*; the nearest are a 2017 study of emulsion sausages, which found soy protein in all seven commercial sausages tested, and a 2021 study showing that graininess and uneven texture cut liking of *chả lụa* [@HXE-02; @HXE-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:tested}. No national standard sets a minimum meat content, and a meat named in the product name must be quantified, so an extender keeps the product name [@LBL-11; @LBL-06] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}; buyers whose premium lines sell on "no filler" will not want one [@TRU-27] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **Plant milks and nutrition drinks:** solubility, low viscosity, stability through UHT treatment, neutral flavour, a complete amino-acid profile, and documents for soy-free, non-GMO and glyphosate-free claims [@BUY-16] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Mung bean isolates of 85 to 88% protein have been made from Thai-grown beans (transfer assumption: similar beans and processes) [@BUY-47] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **Chay analogues:** granule size (for example 4 to 7 mm) and slices, in 20 to 25 kg bags [@FORM-28; @FORM-11; @FORM-13] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}; we infer that buyers also check rehydration ratio and chewiness, and that exporters want non-GMO claims and halal certificates (one exporter, Au Lac, is halal-certified) [@EXP-38] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **All buyers:** certificates of analysis and traceable origin. Food-safety crises in 2025 and 2026, including about 130 t of pork from pigs with African swine fever at a canned-meat maker whose H1 2026 revenue then fell 32%, and enforcement against a nut-milk maker whose product failed its own declared specification, have, we infer, raised the documentation bar for any new supplier [@BUY-20; @BUY-45; @MAC-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. Vinamilk's 2025 annual report says traceability and label compliance are becoming "mandatory rather than a competitive advantage" and names price competitiveness and quality as supplier criteria [@AIB-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:stated}.

Minimum order quantities, shelf-life requirements and qualification times were not published by any buyer we could read. Qualification time is the most important unknown for a founder ([[app-r1-open-questions]]).

## 16.5 How ingredients are bought

Large processors import major ingredients directly and buy minor ones through distributors, and in-person contact is expected [@MAC-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. Plant proteins sit between: large buyers import containers (16 t pea protein shipments in 2025), while small firms buy 20 to 25 kg bags from distributors such as VMC Group, TDGIMEX and Brenntag [@BUY-09; @BUY-35; @BUY-36; @BUY-37; @FORM-11] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Food Ingredients Vietnam (May 2026) listed about 250 exhibitors, mostly Chinese, including pea protein and US dairy protein suppliers and two Chinese soy protein makers (Sinoglory and Yuxin), but none of the global soy isolate brands (ADM, IFF, Fuji Oil, Yuwang) [@BUY-33] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Distributors now also sell fermentation-derived protein: ACC (Asia Ingredients Group) has marketed Angel Yeast's yeast protein (55 to 90% protein and a PDCAAS of 1.0, company claims) to Vietnamese bakery, beverage, dairy, plant-based and meat-alternative makers since May 2025, with sampling and application support; no price or named user is public [@AIS-38; @CPC-21; @CPC-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Kitchens buy finished plant protein through HoReCa distributors: Kamereo lists 37 tofu SKUs and dry soy-gluten pieces, and MM Mega Market about 39 chay prepared foods and 31 tofu items, but neither lists dry textured soy, gluten or a Western analogue [@CPC-12; @CPC-13; @CPC-14] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

## 16.6 Novel protein: a clean negative, with two qualifications

We found no Vietnamese food company piloting mycoprotein, precision-fermented dairy protein or a hybrid meat product using a novel protein, and no Vietnamese protein buyer publishing an alternative-protein or protein-diversification target. The Asia Protein Buyers 100 (February 2026) covers five Vietnamese buyers, placing Vinamilk in Tier 3, Masan in Tier 4, Vinh Hoan in Tier 5 and Minh Phu and Nam Viet in Tier 6, and finds only 2 of 100 Asian buyers publishing alternative-protein sourcing targets [@AFN-18; @AFN-21] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. In the 2025 annual reports and 2026 shareholder meeting records we read for Vinamilk, Masan, Vissan and C.P.'s parent, we found no such target for Vietnam, and incumbents' protein diversification is into animal protein: Vinabeef chilled beef, a Vissan pig farm and Masan processed meat [@AIB-01; @AIB-12; @AIB-19; @BUY-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:stated}. CP Foods sells Meat Zero with no quantified target.

Two qualifications. A small Vietnamese startup, Emmay, reported sales of 1.5 to about 2 t a month of mushroom and mycelium foods in 2023, including a shiitake mycoprotein made by fermentation, with exports about 10% of 2023 revenue (company report) [@AIS-39] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. And a Chinese yeast protein has been marketed to Vietnamese makers through ACC since May 2025, with no named user (16.5). The corporate moves on record remain the seafood exporters' cultivated-meat stakes, whose partners have since turned to non-food revenue, with no food product yet ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]). The one novel-ish ingredient with proven corporate uptake is a non-soy plant isolate in dairy (pea at Vinamilk); the one hybrid category with proven uptake is conventional (soy, wheat and milk proteins in meat pastes).

## 16.8 What this means for the frontier

**For each reader.**
- **Startups:** if you build a textured protein line, you are building an import-substitution plant, not a venture: aim to be owned by or contracted to a chay maker, co-packer or distributor, and test two things first, the delivered price of food-grade defatted soy flour (above about USD 700 to 940 per t the margin fails for a 1 t/h line at 70% use) and whether three buyers will order 2,000 t a year ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]). For an isolate, design for the dairy buyer, keep drinks at or below 5 g of sugar per 100 ml and plan to sell the starch co-product. Pitch fungal biomass by job: against egg and dairy proteins as a binder, against hydrated soy as an extender. Expect trials to be won with certificates, not claims.
- **Investors:** plant-protein imports are worth about USD 87 to 109 million a year at the border (2023 to 2025), an unknown share of it feed, and prices are capped by Chinese supply that pays no duty; returns depend on volume, co-products and distribution, not premiums. A Chinese dry textured-protein line lists at USD 11,000 to 100,000 and installs for about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million, so the funding need is mostly working capital, suited to debt and trade finance. Dairy and nutrition buyers pay 3 to 5 times more per kilogram of protein but qualify suppliers slowly.
- **Manufacturers:** joint development with a local protein maker can secure soy-free and traceable supply, but not a tariff advantage, since pea protein from China, France and Canada is duty-free too. A co-packer can take a protein upgrade to a retailer's private label without a consumer brand. Meat processors already own the cheapest extender, soy: test any new one against your own soy recipe.
- **Research bodies:** publish functional tests of local proteins in Vietnamese formats (industrial *xúc xích*, *giò lụa*, UHT nut milk, noodle garnish) at equal cost to the ingredient they would replace, and offer pilot samples in 20 to 25 kg lots, which is how small buyers test.
- **Policy makers:** national eight-digit subheadings for soy isolate, pea protein and textured protein would make demand visible; a clear answer on whether an identical recombinant protein is a detectable GMO product matters because leading buyers market "non-GMO"; a written ruling before 1 January 2027 on whether soy, nut and cereal milks above 5 g of sugar per 100 ml are *nước giải khát* (soft drinks) would settle plant-milk formulation.
- **International organisations and funders:** fund a declaration-level customs analysis, a price sheet of delivered quotes for food-grade defatted soy flour and Chinese textured soy, and a standing panel of 10 to 15 research and development buyers; together they would answer the food-and-feed split, the T1 margin and the qualification-time question for every founder.
