# 16. Business buyers: the market for protein ingredients

**In brief.**
- **Food firms are the clearest buyers of new protein.** Vietnam imported about USD 285 million of protein ingredients in 2023, including USD 87 million of plant proteins.
- **Plant-protein imports from China grew fast, but part of the growth is probably not food.** The plant proteins that China reports shipping to Vietnam nearly tripled in tonnage from 2019 to 2025, to about 54,000 t. They cost about USD 0.9 to 1.3 per kg. Most of the jump is a cheap flow under code 3504 of the harmonised system (HS) of customs codes, whose price and timing fit feed or technical uses better than food. So the pool of imports that food makers buy is smaller than the trade totals.
- **Domestic makers have no tariff shelter.** Chinese, Korean and Southeast Asian (ASEAN) protein ingredients enter at 0% duty, and Chinese product lands at about USD 1.3 to 1.4 per kg.
- **The buyers to win first are food makers.** Makers of industrial *đồ chay* (traditional vegetarian food) are the most import-dependent and the most willing to switch. Meat processors already use soy, wheat, milk and egg proteins as binders, but do not extend meat with soy at scale, though it would pay at every pork price. Vinamilk buys pea protein for a niche high-protein nut milk, and infant and medical nutrition makers pay dairy-protein prices of USD 8 to 14 per kg.
- **No buyer is trying novel protein.** We found no Vietnamese protein buyer with an alternative-protein target. We also found no Vietnamese food company piloting mycoprotein (fungal protein), precision-fermented dairy protein (made by engineered microbes) or a novel-protein hybrid. A Chinese yeast protein is now marketed to makers.
- **A new ingredient has to choose its price rung and its job.** Commodity plant proteins cost about USD 2 per kg of protein; egg, dairy and premium plant proteins cost USD 7 to 18 (mostly 7 to 11).

## 16.1 What the trade data reveal: a growing import pool, part of it not food

No customs code maps cleanly to one protein ingredient. Trade data use harmonised system (HS) codes, the international product codes for goods. Soy protein isolate (a refined soy protein) appears under both HS 3504 and HS 2106.10. HS 2106.10 also carries supplement premixes, and HS 3504 carries peptones (partly digested proteins) and collagen peptides. Vietnam's own reported quantities for these lines are flagged as estimated. So values are the better measure on the Vietnam side, and exports reported by partner countries give tonnages to 2025 [@BUY-01; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|High}.

**Plant proteins grew fastest by tonnage.** China-reported shipments of HS 3504, 2106.10 and 1109 to Vietnam rose from 18,375 t (2019) to 28,323 t (2023), 51,130 t (2024) and 53,976 t (2025). They were worth USD 59.7 million in 2025 [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Most of the 2024 jump came from HS 3504 at about USD 1 per kg (see below). Wheat gluten from all origins doubled, from 11,327 t (2019) to 21,991 t (2025) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. China-origin textured protein (2106.10) grew 54% from 2019 to 2025; it did not triple.

The pool a domestic soy extrusion line could serve is the textured protein from low-cost origins. Extrusion cooks and shapes the protein under pressure. In 2025 Vietnam imported 12,832 t of HS 2106.10 from its three low-cost origins, China, India and Serbia [@BUY-02; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-china-plant-protein-tonnage}}

{{chart:chart-plant-protein-imports}}

**Food imports follow the *chay* calendar.** China's monthly shipments of textured protein to Vietnam rise before the Vu Lan month, the peak of *chay* (vegetarian) eating in the seventh lunar month. In five of six years (2019 to 2024) they ran 33% above an average month in the two months before Vu Lan. For the same code, the rise was 4% to the world and 2% to Thailand. That seasonal excess is about 5.5% of annual tonnage, some 550 t in 2024 (our calculation) [@BRD-05; @BRD-06] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

For an ingredient supplier the seasonal swing is modest: about a third more demand before Vu Lan, and a trough in the *Tết* (lunar new year) month. Retail chay sales, by contrast, swing two to three times (`trade_seasonal_summary.csv`).

**The new HS 3504 flow is probably not food, so the food pool is smaller.** The 2024 jump in HS 3504 has no lunar timing. It began in October 2023 and was priced at USD 1.03 per kg, against USD 2.46 for all China's 3504 exports. Vietnamese customs mostly did not record it as 3504. Mirror records set China's reported exports against Vietnam's reported imports. In October to December 2023 Vietnam recorded 38% of China's reported value, against full-year ratios of 84 to 101% in 2019 to 2022 [@BRD-05; @BRD-06; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Price, timing and these mirror records all support the reading that the jump is a feed, pet-food or technical protein, not a food isolate. The importers are still unidentified. If the flow is not food, the imported food pool is nearer 35 kt of product than the 40 kt the demand model uses ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]) (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

**What the trade data cannot tell us.** At six-digit level the data do not split food from feed, or one buyer segment from another. Treat plant-protein imports as an upper bound for food demand until someone analyses the individual customs declarations (eight-digit code, description, importer). That analysis is one of the cheapest public goods on the demand side ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

The ladder has two rungs {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. Commodity plant proteins cost about USD 1.7 to 2.4 per kg of protein at the border. Egg, dairy and premium plant proteins cost USD 7 to 11 and more [@BUY-02; @BUY-07; @BUY-09] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

A domestic line would meet the lowest-priced grade on the market. The Chinese textured-protein price of USD 1.24 per kg is China's lowest to any of eight Asian and US partners (world average USD 1.95). India (2,372 t at USD 0.98) and Serbia (259 t at USD 0.85) sold lower still [@TIC-14; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-border-protein-price}}

**No tariff shelter.** Chinese, ASEAN and Korean product pays no duty. With a certificate of origin, HS 2106.10, 3504 and 1109 enter at 0% under these trade agreements:
- the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area (ACFTA), the ASEAN-Korea Free Trade Area (AKFTA) and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), in 2025 to 2027;
- the ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement (ATIGA), at 0% in the latest schedule we read, to 2022 [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Pea protein (3504) also enters at 0% from France under the EU-Vietnam Free Trade Agreement (EVFTA) and from Canada under the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP) [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The standard most-favoured-nation (MFN) rates (5% for 2106.10, 3% for 3504, 10% for gluten) apply only to other origins, chiefly the United States [@REG2-20; @REG2-23; @FTR-32; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}.

With freight, insurance and port costs, Chinese textured protein lands at about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg, or about USD 2.0 to 2.1 per kg of protein (our calculation, `t1_landed_competitor.csv`) [@TIC-12; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The calculation assumes freight and insurance at 3 to 8% of FOB value, port and inland costs of USD 15 to 40 per t and 65% protein. We do not know whether every importer claims the preference; declaration data would show it. {{kn:kn-fta-duty-china}}

A new ingredient has to choose which rung, and which job, it competes on (our calculation) [@BUY-02; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}:
- **Commodity textured protein or isolate:** match the landed cost of Chinese product.
- **Meat extender** (replaces 10 to 30% of the lean meat; hundreds of tonnes at a large processor): match hydrated soy, about USD 2.6 to 2.8 per kg of protein. For a fungal biomass at 45% protein that is about USD 0.7 to 1.2 per kg dry, unless it lets the processor replace more meat unnoticed than soy does ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **Binder or functional protein** (holds water and fat; 1 to 3% of a recipe; tens of tonnes a buyer): price against egg and dairy proteins. If it gives binding, texture, flavour or solubility, fungal biomass at 45% protein could reach about USD 3.3 per kg against egg white. Against casein or whey concentrate it could reach USD 3.9 to 4.6 per kg. Here it meets a Chinese yeast protein already marketed in Vietnam (section 16.5).

Ingredient distributors list gluten as "contact for price". Dried textured soy retails at VND 327,000 to 500,000 per kg. That is about 10 to 16 times the Chinese border unit value for HS 2106.10, USD 1.24 per kg (our calculation) [@BUY-36; @BUY-37; @FORM-01; @BUY-02] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

The first public business-to-business prices we found are for finished kitchen products. On Kamereo, a platform supplying restaurants and hotels, domestic dry soy-gluten chay pieces sell at VND 135,000 per kg (VND 118,692 by the carton). That is about VND 20,000 to 25,000 per 100 g of protein, using a protein range taken from similar products. Firm branded tofu costs about VND 36,000 to 48,000 per 100 g of protein (our calculation) [@CPC-12; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

## 16.3 Who the buyers are: food makers, led by chay makers

**Chay makers: the most willing to switch.** Every protein-dense local chay product in our audit uses imported textured soy, soy concentrate, soy isolate or gluten ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]]). The makers are owner-managed firms that can trial a new ingredient in weeks, but each buys small volumes. They include An Nhien, Au Lac, Thoai An, LC Foods, SG Food and the chay lines of Vissan, CJ Cau Tre and Cholimex. An Nhien's label shows imported Serbian soy concentrate; Au Lac is an exporter; Thoai An puts 35% gluten in its vegetarian pork roll; LC Foods uses textured soy granules of 4 to 7 mm [@FORM-01; @FORM-28; @EXP-38] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. A Vietnamese plant-based startup founder said in 2021 that more than 90% of its raw materials were imported, because domestic ones had "two problems, quality and price" [@BUY-38; @AIS-41] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:stated}.

Chay makers also reach shelves through retailer private label (a retailer's own brand), made by co-packers (firms that make products under another firm's brand). One certified co-packer, GN Foods, makes all five Co.op Select frozen chay dishes, using wheat protein, soybean or no protein ingredient [@CPC-15; @ECO-35] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. Chay makers also sell dry pieces to households and kitchens. Small online shops have sold about 40,700 kg of dry soy-and-gluten "ribs" on Lazada, and a domestic brand, Panko, sells similar pieces to kitchens through Kamereo. The makers behind the online shops are not named, so they are a buyer group still to trace [@MKT-03; @MKT-04; @CPC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

**Meat processors: small amounts of added protein already.** An online audit of the labels of 118 Vissan products found that 22 of 67 unique meat formulations (33%) list soy protein. In all, 25 (37%) list at least one added protein. Most are value lines such as canned meats, pâté, *giò lụa* (steamed pork roll; plain, and with head meat or skin) and nutrition sausages [@BUY-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The pattern is functional: added proteins bind water and fat and cut meat cost. Vissan made 18,547 t of processed food in 2025 and plans 20,350 t in 2026 [@BUY-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

As a binder buyer, a large processor is a customer of tens to hundreds of tonnes, not thousands. As an illustration, if 30 to 50% of Vissan's output carried soy protein at 1 to 3%, Vissan would buy about 55 to 280 t a year (our calculation) [@BUY-04; @BUY-05] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

Extension is a larger prize that Vissan has not taken. A 20% extension of the lean in the same lines would need 224 to 434 t of textured soy (or 249 to 482 t of fungal biomass). It would save VND 58 to 112 billion a year at median hog prices, against a 2025 pre-tax profit of VND 105.1 billion. Vissan's labels suggest it does not extend at that scale (our calculation, `hybrid_prize.csv`) [@BUY-04; @BUY-05; @TIC-12; @DIE-25] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Soy extension pays at every pork price ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]), so what limits it is product identity, texture and trust, not cost.

Imported pork lowers the saving for some processors. Imported frozen pork landed at about VND 42,600 per kg in January to July 2026 (cost, insurance and freight, before duty and VAT), about half the carcass-equivalent price of domestic pigs (our calculation) [@MIM-09; @MIM-07] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. With mechanically deboned meat, it is the only meat import blends could plausibly displace. On the benchmark path that is at most about 1.6% of imported meat protein by 2035 (our calculation; [[ch01-why-vietnam]]) [@MIM-04; @QNT-01] {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}.

Vissan's 2026 shareholder meeting set no chay target. One of its two large projects is a 32,000-head pig farm; the other relocates its slaughter and processing plant [@AIB-14; @AIB-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The business press describes Masan MEATLife as a "consumer protein platform" [@BUY-10], and Masan says it is moving into marinated and processed branded meat [@AIB-12] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}.

C.P. Vietnam had revenue of THB (Thai baht) 101.3 billion in 2025, of which food was THB 8.2 billion. It runs a USD 250 million processed-chicken complex and owns a caterer, Dzine Food Solutions. Its parent's Meat Zero plant-based range, imported from Thailand, was listed on a Vietnamese HoReCa (hotel, restaurant and catering) platform and is no longer orderable [@BUY-31; @AIB-19; @AIS-25] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

**Dairy and plant milks: the clearest pull for a non-soy isolate.** Vinamilk's high-protein nut milk gives 9 g of protein per 180 ml, "mainly from pea". It is sold as soy-free, lactose-free, non-GMO (not genetically modified), gluten-free and glyphosate-free, and aimed at "people wary of soy". Its page sets 5 g per 100 ml as the threshold for a high-protein claim [@BUY-16] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. A press summary of Vinamilk's results for the first half of 2026 (total revenue VND 35,034 billion) names its high-protein and plant milks among the contributors to domestic results [@BUY-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. This is the only large Vietnamese buyer we found using a non-soy protein isolate.

Vinamilk's pull comes from the product, not from a commitment. The investor network FAIRR records Vinamilk's plant-based products at 1.30% of its range, with no timebound diversification target. Vinamilk promotes its high-protein dairy milk on milk protein being "more easily absorbed" than plant protein [@AFN-21; @AIB-03] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. The high-protein nut milk sells as a niche: about 70,300 L on Vinamilk's Lazada store, about one-eighth of its standard nut milk by litres (our calculation on cumulative sales counters) [@MKT-03; @MKT-04] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

The wider plant-milk business is growing. Vinasoy sold 264 million litres in 2025, with its Veyo nut milks up 50%. Vietnam imported USD 3.9 billion of tree nuts in 2025, up 40%, partly for plant milk [@AIB-05; @AIB-40] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Two fiscal rules shape the plant-milk category:
- **Tariffs.** Regional plant-milk imports are duty-free: HS 2202.99 enters at 0% under ACFTA, ATIGA and AKFTA, though at 12 to 18% under RCEP in 2025 to 2027. MFN rates of 20 to 30% apply to origins without a trade agreement. So local makers' advantage is distribution, not tariffs [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.
- **Sugar tax.** From 1 January 2027 drinks above 5 g of total sugar per 100 ml pay an 8% excise (10% from 2028). Dairy is exempt, and whether soy and nut milks count is unresolved. The reference high-protein nut milk lists 6.5% added sugar, so an isolate buyer will want formulations at or below 5 g ([[ch13-consumers]]) [@FTR-01; @FTR-02; @FTR-05; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Infant and medical nutrition: demanding and slow.** These makers use whey protein concentrate (Vietnam imported 1,634 t in 2025), demineralised whey, casein and milk protein concentrates. They market "non-GMO" and are moving towards clinical research: Nutifood signed a memorandum of understanding on research and clinical trials with Military Hospital 175 in June 2026 [@BUY-02; @BUY-19] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. They are the buyers for precision-fermented dairy proteins, but approving a new supplier is slow and regulatory status is the gate. Low-dose ingredients such as lactoferrin fall below the 5% trigger of the mandatory GMO label ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

**Instant noodles: volume, not yet protein.** Vietnam ate 8,231 million servings in 2025, about 81 per person, fourth in the world [@BUY-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Acecook uses soy protein only in seasoning and garnish sachets. It already buys pea starch, a co-product of splitting pulses into protein and starch, and adds calcium to its best-selling brand; we found no protein-fortified noodle [@BUY-22] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

Its July 2025 strategy moves into convenient seasonings, ready-to-eat foods and meal-replacement snacks. The nutrition steps it lists for existing lines are calcium, vitamin B12 and real meat and vegetables in instant pho and bun, and it makes no protein claim [@AIB-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. A pulse-protein plant would have starch buyers waiting.

**Seasoning, bakery and sports nutrition.** Seasoning makers buy protein as flavour, for example bone-and-meat extract in Aji-ngon [@BUY-26] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Bakery protein demand is likely egg and dairy (our inference from egg-albumin and dairy-protein imports) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Sports nutrition imports finished products, not ingredients [@BUY-08] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}.

## 16.4 What buyers specify

- **Meat pastes and sausages:** gel strength, water and fat binding, emulsion stability through retort sterilisation (heating in the sealed pack), bland flavour and light colour. The protein is used with starch, fibre, phosphates and carrageenan [@BUY-05] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Two isolates at the same price can perform differently in the same paste [@BUY-42] {general|Medium}. For extension rather than binding, fine emulsions take a fine isolate (or a similar fine ingredient) at 10 to 20% of the lean, not granules. Coarse mince takes up to about 30% [@HXE-09] {general|Medium} {dx:tested}.
- **Vietnamese pork rolls and labels:** we found no published Vietnamese study of soy protein in *giò lụa*. The nearest are two studies. A 2017 study of emulsion sausages found soy protein in all seven commercial sausages tested. A 2021 study showed that graininess and uneven texture cut liking of *chả lụa* (steamed pork roll) [@HXE-02; @HXE-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:tested}. No national standard sets a minimum meat content, and a meat named in the product name must be quantified, so an extender keeps the product name [@LBL-11; @LBL-06] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. Buyers whose premium lines sell on "no filler" will not want one [@TRU-27] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **Plant milks and nutrition drinks:** solubility, low viscosity, stability through UHT (ultra-high-temperature) treatment, neutral flavour and a complete amino-acid profile. Buyers also want documents for soy-free, non-GMO and glyphosate-free claims [@BUY-16] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Mung bean isolates of 85 to 88% protein have been made from Thai-grown beans (transfer assumption: similar beans and processes) [@BUY-47] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- **Chay analogues:** granule size (for example 4 to 7 mm) and slices, in 20 to 25 kg bags [@FORM-28; @FORM-11; @FORM-13] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. We infer that buyers also check rehydration ratio and chewiness, and that exporters want non-GMO claims and halal certificates; one exporter, Au Lac, is halal-certified [@EXP-38] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.
- **All buyers:** certificates of analysis and traceable origin. We infer that food-safety crises in 2025 and 2026 have raised the documentation bar for any new supplier. They include about 130 t of pork from pigs with African swine fever at a canned-meat maker, whose revenue for the first half of 2026 then fell 32%. They also include enforcement against a nut-milk maker whose product failed its own declared specification [@BUY-20; @BUY-45; @MAC-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}. Vinamilk's 2025 annual report says traceability and label compliance are becoming "mandatory rather than a competitive advantage", and names price competitiveness and quality as supplier criteria [@AIB-01] {VN-direct|High} {dx:stated}.

No buyer we could read published minimum order quantities, shelf-life requirements or qualification times. Qualification time, how long a buyer takes to approve a new supplier, is the most important unknown for a founder ([[app-r1-open-questions]]).

## 16.5 How ingredients are bought

Large processors import major ingredients directly and buy minor ones through distributors, and they expect in-person contact [@MAC-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:stated}. Plant proteins sit between the two. Large buyers import by the container (16 t pea protein shipments in 2025). Small firms buy 20 to 25 kg bags from distributors such as VMC Group, TDGIMEX and Brenntag [@BUY-09; @BUY-35; @BUY-36; @BUY-37; @FORM-11] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

The trade fair Food Ingredients Vietnam (May 2026) listed about 250 exhibitors, mostly Chinese. They included pea protein and US dairy protein suppliers and two Chinese soy protein makers (Sinoglory and Yuxin). None of the global soy isolate brands (ADM, IFF, Fuji Oil, Yuwang) exhibited [@BUY-33] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Distributors now also sell protein made by fermentation. ACC (Asia Ingredients Group) has marketed Angel Yeast's yeast protein to Vietnamese bakery, beverage, dairy, plant-based and meat-alternative makers since May 2025, with sampling and application support. The company claims 55 to 90% protein and a PDCAAS (an older protein-quality score) of 1.0. No price or named user is public [@AIS-38; @CPC-21; @CPC-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Kitchens buy finished plant protein through HoReCa distributors. Kamereo lists 37 tofu products and dry soy-gluten pieces, and MM Mega Market about 39 chay prepared foods and 31 tofu items. Neither lists dry textured soy, gluten or a Western analogue [@CPC-12; @CPC-13; @CPC-14] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

## 16.6 Novel protein: a clean negative, with two qualifications

We found no Vietnamese food company piloting mycoprotein, precision-fermented dairy protein or a hybrid meat product using a novel protein. We also found no Vietnamese protein buyer publishing an alternative-protein or protein-diversification target. The Asia Protein Buyers 100 (February 2026) covers five Vietnamese buyers. It places Vinamilk in Tier 3, Masan in Tier 4, Vinh Hoan in Tier 5, and Minh Phu and Nam Viet in Tier 6. It finds only 2 of 100 Asian buyers publishing alternative-protein sourcing targets [@AFN-18; @AFN-21] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

We read the 2025 annual reports and 2026 shareholder meeting records of Vinamilk, Masan, Vissan and C.P.'s parent, and found no such target for Vietnam. The incumbents' protein diversification is into animal protein: Vinabeef chilled beef, a Vissan pig farm and Masan processed meat [@AIB-01; @AIB-12; @AIB-19; @BUY-04] {VN-direct|High} {dx:stated}. CP Foods sells Meat Zero with no quantified target.

There are two qualifications. First, a small Vietnamese startup, Emmay, reported sales of 1.5 to about 2 t a month of mushroom and mycelium foods in 2023. These included a shiitake mycoprotein made by fermentation. Exports were about 10% of its 2023 revenue (company report) [@AIS-39] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:revealed}. Second, a Chinese yeast protein has been marketed to Vietnamese makers through ACC since May 2025, with no named user (section 16.5).

The corporate moves on record are still the seafood exporters' stakes in cultivated meat. Their partners have since turned to non-food revenue, and there is no food product yet ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]). The one newer ingredient with proven corporate uptake is a non-soy plant isolate in dairy (pea at Vinamilk). The one hybrid category with proven uptake is conventional: soy, wheat and milk proteins in meat pastes.

Vietnam imports most of its mung beans, so a "domestic" isolate may still rest on imported beans.
- **Fungal biomass, split by job:** as a binder (1 to 3% of a recipe, tens of tonnes a buyer), priced against egg and milk protein at about USD 3.3 to 4.6 per kg dry. Buyers would be value lines at Vissan and Masan MEATLife, bakery egg replacement, and Ajinomoto Vietnam and Cholimex for a flavour-active ingredient in chay seasonings and dumplings. As an extender (10 to 30% of the lean, hundreds of tonnes a buyer), it has to reach hydrated-soy parity of about USD 0.7 to 1.2 per kg dry. The exception is if it allows more replacement than soy. This matches play T9 (koji and fungal foods).
- **Precision-fermented dairy protein:** Nutifood and Vinamilk specialty nutrition, for bioactive proteins rather than bulk whey. This waits until the Food Safety Law (passage considered in May 2027) and the question of whether such proteins are detectable GMO products are settled. Low-dose uses already fall outside the mandatory label. This matches play T7 (high-value, low-dose precision-fermentation ingredients).

## 16.8 What this means for new protein products

**For each reader.**
- **Startups making textured protein:** a textured protein line is an import-substitution plant, not a venture. Aim to be owned by, or contracted to, a chay maker, co-packer or distributor. Test two things first ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]). One is the delivered price of food-grade defatted soy flour: above about USD 700 to 940 per t, the margin fails for a 1 t/h line (one tonne an hour) at 70% use. The other is whether three buyers will order 2,000 t a year.
- **Startups making isolates or fungal biomass:** for an isolate, design for the dairy buyer, keep drinks at or below 5 g of sugar per 100 ml and plan to sell the starch co-product. Pitch fungal biomass by job: against egg and dairy proteins as a binder, against hydrated soy as an extender. Expect to win trials with certificates, not claims.
- **Investors:** plant-protein imports are worth about USD 87 to 109 million a year at the border (2023 to 2025), an unknown share of it feed. Chinese supply that pays no duty caps prices, so returns depend on volume, co-products and distribution, not premiums. A Chinese dry textured-protein line lists at USD 11,000 to 100,000 and installs for about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million. So the funding need is mostly working capital, suited to debt and trade finance. Dairy and nutrition buyers pay 3 to 5 times more per kilogram of protein but approve suppliers slowly.
- **Manufacturers:** joint development with a local protein maker can secure soy-free and traceable supply, but not a tariff advantage, since pea protein from China, France and Canada is duty-free too. A co-packer can take a protein upgrade to a retailer's private label without a consumer brand. Meat processors already own the cheapest extender, soy: test any new one against your own soy recipe.
- **Research bodies:** publish functional tests of local proteins in Vietnamese formats, at equal cost to the ingredient they would replace. Formats include industrial *xúc xích* (sausage), *giò lụa*, UHT nut milk and noodle garnish. Offer pilot samples in 20 to 25 kg lots, which is how small buyers test.
- **Policy makers:** national eight-digit customs subheadings for soy isolate, pea protein and textured protein would make demand visible. Leading buyers market "non-GMO", so a clear answer matters on whether an identical recombinant protein (one made by an engineered microbe) is a detectable GMO product. A written ruling would settle how plant milks are formulated. It would say, before 1 January 2027, whether soy, nut and cereal milks above 5 g of sugar per 100 ml are *nước giải khát* (soft drinks).
- **International organisations and funders:** fund three things: a customs analysis at declaration level, a price sheet of delivered quotes for food-grade defatted soy flour and Chinese textured soy, and a standing panel of 10 to 15 research and development buyers. Together they would answer three questions for every founder: how much of the import pool is food rather than feed, the T1 margin, and how long qualification takes.
