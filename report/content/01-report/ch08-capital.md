# 8. Capital

**In brief.**

- **No large deal yet.** No disclosed deal in Vietnam in the protein families this study covers has exceeded USD 5 million. The disclosed deals are angel money of about USD 250,000 and strategic minority stakes of undisclosed size. There is no proven local template for financing a plant, and the wider region offers none either: elsewhere in East and Southeast Asia, plants were built by corporate joint ventures and state-backed loans, not by venture rounds.
- **No venture track record in food.** We found no disclosed institutional venture round for any Vietnam-based plant-based, fermentation, microalgae or cultivated company. Vietnamese strategic money has gone abroad instead.
- **Little venture money reaches farming and food.** Vietnam's venture market raised about USD 509 million in 2025, and agriculture took about USD 1 million. There were no technology stock-market listings backed by venture capital or private equity in 2025 or the four years before. So investors exit by selling to larger companies.
- **State funds are new and small.** New state venture funds (national, Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi) can now accept losses, but their tickets fit pilot plants, not production plants.
- **Global money has moved to fermentation and ingredients.** Global alternative-protein investment fell to USD 881 million in 2025. What still flows goes to fermentation and upstream ingredients, the part of the value chain where Vietnam's advantages lie.

**We found no disclosed institutional venture round for any Vietnam-based plant-based meat, plant-based dairy, precision-fermentation, microalgae or cultivated-meat company.** Our search tools limited coverage, so absence in our log means "not found". Emmay, a mushroom-based protein maker, reported three angel rounds totalling about USD 250,000 by 2021 (company claim) [@CAP-15] {VN-direct|Low}. Vietnamese strategic money has gone abroad: Vinh Hoan into Singapore and Hong Kong cultivated-seafood companies, and VinaCapital Ventures into a Korean plant-based company [@CAP-16; @CAP-17; @CAP-18] {VN-direct|Low}.

**The region shows the same pattern.** Across East and Southeast Asia we logged about USD 355 million to 365 million of disclosed private alternative-protein money since 2020, about three quarters of it raised in 2020 to 2022 (our sum; section S11.6 of [[app-s11-capital]]) {VN-adjacent|Medium}. The largest round was Next Gen Foods' USD 100 million Series A in Singapore in 2022 [@RDL-28] {VN-adjacent|High}. Since 2023 the larger deals financed plants or ingredients: DAIZ in Japan built a plant-based ingredient factory with about USD 47 million, almost half of it low-cost loans under ministry schemes [@RDL-20] {VN-adjacent|Medium}; Changing Bio in China raised USD 22 million for precision-fermented milk proteins [@RDL-15] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. In Thailand, a joint venture between the state energy group's life-science arm and a listed food maker built a 3,000 t a year contract factory with a British brand [@RDL-30; @RDL-32] {VN-adjacent|High}. Several firms that raised money in the boom have since merged, paused or closed, among them Shiok Meats (merged into Umami Bioworks for stock), GOOD Meat's Singapore plant and Avant's Singapore unit [@RDL-06; @RDL-36; @RDL-07] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. The one new Vietnamese item is an on-air television offer of about USD 0.6 million for a stake in YERA, a yeast-protein startup; we found no confirmation that it closed [@RDL-01] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 8.3 Development finance: no direct ticket yet

We found no development finance institution (DFI) with a direct ticket in Vietnam in the protein families this study covers, nor anywhere else in East or Southeast Asia. The Asian Development Bank's only link to a Vietnamese alternative-protein firm is technical assistance to Emmay (December 2025), with no investment [@RDL-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. The Vietnamese agribusiness deals of the International Finance Corporation (IFC) that we found run from about USD 20 million to 40 million. One example is a USD 40 million warehouse-finance facility for TTC AgriS in 2023, which it helped arrange with SMBC. It also supported Vietnam's first blue bond (SeABank, USD 25 million, 2024) [@CAP-41; @CAP-43] {VN-direct|High}.

ADB Ventures, the Asian Development Bank's venture arm, writes tickets of USD 0.1 million to 4 million with a climate lens. It has backed microbial oils elsewhere in Asia but has no Vietnamese agrifood deal [@CAP-45; @CAP-46] {VN-direct|High}. Proparco reaches Vietnamese food businesses as a limited partner in local funds, for example Mekong Enterprise Fund IV [@CAP-47] {VN-direct|High}. We did not verify other DFIs (FMO, DEG, BII, JICA, KfW, Finnfund, Norfund).

## 8.4 State capital: new rules, small funds

- **Decree 264/2025** (in force 14 October 2025) lets national and local venture funds invest directly or as funds-of-funds. They can accept losses of up to 50% of charter capital per investment cycle and run cycles of up to 10 years (15 for strategic technology). Managers are exempt from liability for "objective risk" losses [@CAP-29; @CAP-30] {VN-direct|High}. The national fund (at least VND 500 billion of initial state capital) was still at proposal stage in June 2026 [@CAP-30; @CAP-31] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Ho Chi Minh City's venture fund** has VND 500 billion (about USD 19 to 20 million; 40% state). It lists biotechnology as a priority and planned its first one or two investments for the fourth quarter of 2026 [@CAP-32; @CAP-33] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Hanoi's venture fund** was set up in April 2026 with VND 230 billion (47.8% state). It caps each investment at 5% of the fund (about USD 0.45 million), and high-tech agriculture and biotechnology are among its priorities [@CAP-34; @CAP-35] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **NATIF** (National Technology Innovation Fund) pays up to 50% of loan interest for up to five years on loans from partner banks. It gives priority to profitable, low-risk borrowers. Round 1 of 2026 closed on 20 September [@CAP-37; @CAP-38] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Credit:** Decree 156/2025 gives organic and circular agriculture credit treatment similar to high-tech agriculture (unsecured loan limits, risk handling) [@CAP-39] {VN-direct|Medium}.

These tickets fit pilot plants, not production plants. The state has not yet named alternative protein or biomanufacturing as a fund priority.

## 8.5 Grants and philanthropy: no funder names Vietnam

- **Coefficient Giving's alternative-protein research and development request** offered up to USD 30 million across two rounds, at USD 0.1 million to 5 million per award. For-profits and international applicants were eligible. It closed on 10 August and 15 September 2026, with decisions due by the end of 2026. Its topics included fish flavour, off-flavours, fat alternatives and egg replacement [@CAP-57] {general|High}.
- The **Good Food Institute (GFI) Research Grant Program** is closed with no reopening date [@CAP-50] {general|High}. We found no Navigation Fund alternative-protein request on its pages [@CAP-58] {general|Medium}.
- The **Bezos Earth Fund** has promised USD 1 billion to its Future of Food programme, including grants on AI for alternative protein, with no open call found [@CAP-61] {general|Medium}. Its Bezos Centre for Sustainable Protein at the National University of Singapore cites potential impact for "East and South-East Asia's 2.3 billion consumers" [@AFN-15] {general|Medium}.
- **Nova Vista Foundation's Protein Innovation request for proposals** (USD 10 million; announced 7 August 2026, closed 15 September 2026) funds policy and market shaping. Examples are public procurement of innovative protein products, advance market commitments and retailer protein-ratio targets. It excludes whole-food plant-based work, culture change, individual diet change and shifts to smaller animals. Its priority places for added policy staff are "high-innovation Asian economies (including but not limited to Japan and South Korea)", Germany and the Nordics; Vietnam is not named [@AFN-04; @AFN-05] {general|High}.
- **No major alternative-protein funder names Vietnam as a priority.** The 2025 report card of GFI's Asia Pacific office (GFI APAC) does not mention Vietnam, and its team page lists no one covering it. The 2026 ReRoot Asia diet-change fund published no Vietnam-specific application guide. Animal Charity Evaluators' July 2025 Movement Grants had no Vietnamese grantee [@AFN-11; @AFN-12; @AFN-29; @AFN-28] {VN-direct|Medium}. See [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]] for which routes fit which funder.
- The **Australian Centre for International Agricultural Research (ACIAR)** funds 23 Vietnamese projects (AUD 5.4 million in 2025/26, in Australian dollars), commissioned through Australian partners, none on alternative protein [@CAP-67] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Accelerators:** Better Bite Ventures (USD 100,000 to 200,000 cheques) and Big Idea Ventures (USD 200,000 through its programme) are the regional early-stage specialists. The ProVeg Incubator offers up to USD 300,000 including in-kind support [@CAP-63; @CAP-62; @CAP-59] {general|Medium}.

Grant windows open and close, and none of the current alternative-protein programmes treats Vietnam as a priority. Vietnamese groups need an international co-applicant, a proposal that fits the funder's stated scope and a draft ready before windows open.

## 8.6 The global and regional context: money has moved to fermentation

- Global alternative-protein investment was **USD 881 million in 2025** (plant-based 450, fermentation 357, cultivated 74) and USD 359 million in the first half of 2026. In the second quarter of 2026, fermentation took about USD 132 million of roughly USD 197 million [@CAP-51] {general|High}. Synthesis Capital's managing partner says valuations "have reset" [@CAP-64] {general|Low}.
- Global agrifood-tech funding was flat at USD 16.2 billion in 2025, with upstream categories (farm, food production and biological systems) up 7% [@CAP-53] {general|Medium}.

**Reading.** Money that still flows goes to fermentation [@CAP-51] and to upstream ingredients with what AgFunder calls "tangible science, real unit economics and clear paths to revenue" [@CAP-53]. That is the part of the value chain where Vietnam's advantages (feedstock and processing skills) lie, and the opposite of a consumer-brand play.

## 8.7 What this means for food ventures

- **Investors:** there is no venture track record in Vietnamese alternative protein. A thesis needs business-to-business food-ingredient revenue, a named strategic buyer, and a structure that pairs a private-equity lead with DFI support and a buyer's offtake, that is, an agreement to buy the output (our suggestion). The regional precedents that did build plants are a corporate joint venture (Thailand) and equity with state-scheme loans (Japan), which points to a domestic strategic sponsor as the likeliest lead. Expect to underwrite capital intensity, not consumer growth ([[ch09-economics]]). A domestic textured-protein line is the exception: its equipment is cheap (Chinese lines listed at USD 11,000 to 100,000) and its main need is USD 0.6 to 3.5 million of working capital. So it suits bank debt and trade finance for an established maker or a small or medium-sized firm, not venture equity (our calculation; [[ch26-plays]]) [@TIC-02; @TIC-03] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Startups:** realistic first cheques come from angels, family offices and regional seed specialists (USD 0.1 to 0.3 million). Other options are ADB Ventures (up to USD 4 million, climate lens) and, from late 2026, the Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi funds. A Singapore holding company can ease foreign investment.
- **Policy makers:** naming "alternative protein and biomanufacturing" as a priority in the rules of the national and local venture funds would cost little. A grant or first-loss window for pre-revenue pilot plants would fill the gap between research grants and private equity. NATIF's priority for profitable borrowers leaves that gap open.
- **International bodies:** the IFC already invests in Vietnamese agribusiness, and ADB Ventures writes small climate tickets in the region. A pooled first-loss tranche would target the gap: money that takes losses first, so other investors carry less risk. It could back a first shared food-grade fermentation plant in Vietnam (play T5 in [[ch26-plays]]). DFI co-investment with a local fund would also help.
