# 8. Capital

**In brief.**

- **No large deal yet.** No disclosed deal in Vietnam in these protein families has exceeded USD 5 million. There is no proven way to finance a plant, locally or in the region.
- **No venture track record in food.** We found no institutional venture round for any Vietnam-based alternative-protein company. Vietnamese strategic money has gone abroad instead.
- **Little venture money reaches food and farming.** Vietnam's venture market raised about USD 509 million in 2025; agriculture took about USD 1 million. Investors exit by selling to larger companies, not by listing.
- **State funds are new and small.** They fit pilot plants, not production plants.
- **Global money has moved to fermentation and ingredients**, where Vietnam's advantages lie.

---

## 8.1 The deal record in Vietnam: small and mostly undisclosed

| Date | Company | What | Deal | Amount | Investor | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 to 2021 | Emmay | Mushroom-based protein foods | Three angel rounds | About USD 0.25 M (company claim) | Undisclosed | [@CAP-15] {VN-direct\|Low} |
| 2021 | Shiok Meats, Avant Meats | Cultivated seafood (abroad) | Minority stakes | Undisclosed | Vinh Hoan (Vietnam) | [@CAP-16; @CAP-17] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| 2024 | INNOHAS (Korea) | Plant-based meat | Stake | Undisclosed | VinaCapital Ventures | [@CAP-18] {VN-direct\|Medium} |

**We found no disclosed institutional venture round for any Vietnam-based alternative-protein company.** Our search tools limited coverage, so "not found" is not proof of absence. A yeast-protein startup, YERA, received an on-air television offer of about USD 0.6 million, with no confirmation that it closed [@RDL-01] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**The region shows the same pattern.** East and Southeast Asia raised about USD 360 million of disclosed private alternative-protein money since 2020, three quarters of it in 2020 to 2022 (our sum) {VN-adjacent|Medium}. Since 2023 the larger deals have financed plants and ingredients. In Japan, DAIZ built an ingredient plant with about USD 47 million, almost half of it in low-cost state loans [@RDL-20] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. In Thailand, a joint venture between an energy group and a food maker built a 3,000 t a year contract factory [@RDL-30; @RDL-32] {VN-adjacent|High}. Several boom-era firms have since merged, paused or closed [@RDL-06; @RDL-36; @RDL-07] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.

- **Exits are trade sales.** There were no technology stock-market listings in Vietnam from 2021 to 2025 [@CAP-21; @CAP-22] {VN-direct|Medium}. A food-ingredient company's realistic exit is a sale to a larger company or a private-equity buyout.
- **Likely buyers.** Masan, Vinamilk, TH, Nutifood, Vinasoy, C.P. Vietnam, Vinh Hoan, Ajinomoto and Vedan fit. Only Vinh Hoan, CJ CheilJedang and VinaCapital have shown interest [@CAP-16; @CAP-17; @CAP-18] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **No corporate venture arm in food.** Vingroup's venture fund invests in AI, chips and cloud, not food [@SLK-12] {VN-direct|Medium}. So a Vietnamese food group would act through a joint venture, a supply contract or an acquisition.

## 8.3 Development finance: no direct ticket yet

We found no development finance institution (DFI) with a direct investment in these protein families anywhere in East or Southeast Asia. The Asian Development Bank (ADB) gave Emmay technical help, not money [@RDL-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. The International Finance Corporation's (IFC) Vietnamese agribusiness deals run from about USD 20 million to 40 million [@CAP-41; @CAP-43] {VN-direct|High}. ADB's venture arm writes USD 0.1 million to 4 million cheques with a climate lens, but has no Vietnamese food deal [@CAP-45; @CAP-46] {VN-direct|High}. Other DFIs reach Vietnamese food businesses through local funds [@CAP-47] {VN-direct|High}.

## 8.4 State capital: new rules, small funds

- **A 2025 decree** lets national and local state venture funds accept losses of up to 50% per investment cycle [@CAP-29; @CAP-30] {VN-direct|High}. The national fund was still a proposal in June 2026 [@CAP-30; @CAP-31] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Ho Chi Minh City's fund** has about USD 19 million and lists biotechnology as a priority. It planned its first investments for late 2026 [@CAP-32; @CAP-33] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Hanoi's fund** (2026) caps each investment at about USD 0.45 million, with high-tech farming and biotechnology among its priorities [@CAP-34; @CAP-35] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **The national technology innovation fund** pays up to half the interest on bank loans, but favours profitable, low-risk borrowers [@CAP-37; @CAP-38] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Credit:** organic and circular farming now get credit terms like high-tech farming [@CAP-39] {VN-direct|Medium}.

These tickets fit pilot plants, not production plants. No fund names alternative protein as a priority.

## 8.5 Grants and philanthropy: no funder names Vietnam

- **Coefficient Giving's research request** offered up to USD 30 million, at up to USD 5 million per award, and closed in September 2026. Its topics included fish flavour and egg replacement, with open data any firm can use [@CAP-57; @CGG-03] {general|High}. None of its 61 earlier alternative-protein grants names Vietnam or Southeast Asia [@CGG-19; @CGG-02; @CGG-14] {general|High}.
- **The Good Food Institute's grant programme** is closed with no reopening date [@CAP-50] {general|High}.
- **The Bezos Earth Fund** funds a protein centre in Singapore that aims at East and Southeast Asia, with no open call found [@CAP-61; @AFN-15] {general|Medium}.
- **Nova Vista Foundation's 2026 call** (USD 10 million) funded policy and market shaping, with Japan and Korea as Asian priorities, not Vietnam [@AFN-04; @AFN-05] {general|High}.
- **No major alternative-protein funder names Vietnam as a priority** [@AFN-11; @AFN-12; @AFN-29; @AFN-28] {VN-direct|Medium}. See [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]] for which routes fit which funder.
- **Accelerators** in the region write early cheques of USD 0.1 million to 0.3 million [@CAP-63; @CAP-62; @CAP-59] {general|Medium}.

Vietnamese groups need an international co-applicant, a proposal that fits the funder's scope, and a draft ready before windows open.

## 8.6 The global and regional context: money has moved to fermentation

- Global alternative-protein investment was **USD 881 million in 2025**. In the second quarter of 2026, fermentation took about two thirds of the money [@CAP-51] {general|High}. Valuations "have reset" [@CAP-64] {general|Low}.
- Global agrifood-tech funding was flat at USD 16.2 billion in 2025, with upstream categories up 7% [@CAP-53] {general|Medium}.

{{chart:chart-global-altprotein-investment}}

**Reading.** The money that still flows goes to fermentation and to ingredients with "real unit economics and clear paths to revenue" [@CAP-51; @CAP-53]. That is where Vietnam's advantages, feedstock and processing skills, lie. It is the opposite of a consumer-brand play.

## 8.7 What this means for food ventures

- **Investors:** a thesis needs food-ingredient revenue from other businesses, a named strategic buyer, and a buyer's agreement to take the output. The regional plants that were built had a corporate joint venture or state loans behind them, so a Vietnamese strategic sponsor is the likeliest lead. Expect to fund heavy equipment, not consumer growth ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **The exception is textured protein.** Its equipment is cheap (Chinese lines at USD 11,000 to 100,000), and its main need is USD 0.6 to 3.5 million of working capital (our calculation) [@TIC-02; @TIC-03] {VN-direct|Low}. That suits bank debt and trade finance for an established maker, not venture equity ([[ch26-plays]]). Vietnam already has the tools: invoice finance against a large buyer, equipment leases and loan guarantees for small firms [@SLK-05; @SLK-01; @SLK-20; @SLK-19] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Startups:** realistic first cheques come from angels, family offices and regional seed funds (USD 0.1 to 0.3 million), ADB's venture arm, and, from late 2026, the city funds.
- **Policy makers:** naming alternative protein and biomanufacturing as a priority in the state venture funds would cost little. A grant or first-loss window for pilot plants would fill the gap between research grants and private equity.
- **International bodies:** a pooled first-loss fund, which takes losses before other investors, could back a first shared food-grade fermentation plant (play T5, [[ch26-plays]]).
