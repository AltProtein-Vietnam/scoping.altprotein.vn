# 8. Capital

**In one paragraph.** Disclosed investment in Vietnamese alternative protein amounts to one real deal: Entobel's USD 32.5 million insect-meal project, financed by Mekong Capital, Dragon Capital and the IFC, with a seafood exporter as anchor buyer. We found no disclosed institutional venture round for any Vietnam-based plant-based, fermentation, microalgae or cultivated company. Vietnamese strategic money has gone abroad instead. Vietnam's venture market raised about USD 509 million in 2025, of which agriculture took about USD 1 million, and there have been no VC- or PE-backed technology IPOs in 2025 or the four years before, so exits run through trade sales to strategic buyers. New state venture funds (national, Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi) can now accept losses but are small. Globally, alternative-protein investment fell to USD 881 million in 2025 and is shifting towards fermentation and upstream ingredients, which is the part of the value chain where Vietnam's advantages lie.

## 8.2 The Entobel template

Entobel is the only proven financing template for an alternative-protein asset in Vietnam [@CAP-01; @CAP-04; @ECO-05; @VCO-28] {VN-direct|Medium}:
1. **A feed product**, sold business-to-business into aquafeed, not a consumer brand.
2. **A local side-stream feedstock** (brewery by-products) next to the plant.
3. **Local private equity** as lead (Mekong Capital), with a local co-investor (Dragon Capital).
4. **A small development-finance ticket** to de-risk and signal (IFC, USD 2.5 million), with a second DFI, Proparco, backing the lead fund as a limited partner in Mekong Enterprise Fund IV [@CAP-01; @CAP-04; @CAP-47] {VN-direct|High}.
5. **A strategic offtake** from a seafood exporter (Vinh Hoan, at least 15,000 t over 2025 to 2027) and, later, a feed-major partnership (Skretting, November 2025).

The IFC noted in 2023 that private investors remained cautious because insect meal had not yet proved it could compete with fishmeal and soy on scale and price [@CAP-06] {VN-direct|Medium}. Replicating the template needs a company that can show a path to about USD 20 million or more of capital expenditure and a named anchor buyer.

## 8.4 Development finance

The IFC is the only development finance institution we found with a direct Vietnamese alternative-protein ticket (Entobel, USD 2.5 million). Its other Vietnamese agribusiness deals we found run from about USD 20 million to 40 million (for example a USD 40 million warehouse-finance facility for TTC AgriS in 2023, which it helped arrange with SMBC), and it supported Vietnam's first blue bond (SeABank, USD 25 million, 2024) [@CAP-04; @CAP-41; @CAP-43] {VN-direct|High}. ADB Ventures (tickets of USD 0.1 million to 4 million, climate lens) has backed insect protein and microbial oils elsewhere in Asia but has no Vietnamese agrifood deal [@CAP-45; @CAP-46] {VN-direct|High}. Proparco reaches Vietnamese food businesses as an LP in local funds, for example Mekong Enterprise Fund IV, Entobel's lead investor [@CAP-47; @CAP-01] {VN-direct|High}. Other DFIs (FMO, DEG, BII, JICA, KfW, Finnfund, Norfund) were not verified.

## 8.5 State capital: new rules, small funds

- **Decree 264/2025** (in force 14 October 2025) lets national and local venture funds invest directly or as funds-of-funds, accept losses of up to 50% of charter capital per investment cycle, run cycles of up to 10 years (15 for strategic technology), and exempts managers from liability for "objective risk" losses [@CAP-29; @CAP-30] {VN-direct|High}. The national fund (at least VND 500 billion of initial state capital) was still at proposal stage in June 2026 [@CAP-30; @CAP-31] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Ho Chi Minh City's venture fund** has VND 500 billion (about USD 19 to 20 million; 40% state), lists biotechnology as a priority and planned its first one or two investments for the fourth quarter of 2026 [@CAP-32; @CAP-33] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Hanoi's venture fund** was established in April 2026 with VND 230 billion (47.8% state) and a cap of 5% of the fund per investment (about USD 0.45 million); high-tech agriculture and biotechnology are among its priorities [@CAP-34; @CAP-35] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **NATIF** (National Technology Innovation Fund) subsidises up to 50% of loan interest for up to five years on loans from partner banks, with priority for profitable, low-risk borrowers; Round 1 of 2026 closed on 20 September [@CAP-37; @CAP-38] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Credit:** Decree 156/2025 gives organic and circular agriculture credit treatment similar to high-tech agriculture (unsecured loan limits, risk handling) [@CAP-39] {VN-direct|Medium}.

These tickets fit pilot plants, not production plants. The state has not yet named alternative protein or biomanufacturing as a fund priority.

## 8.6 Grants and philanthropy

- **Coefficient Giving's alternative-protein R&D request** (up to USD 30 million across two rounds; USD 0.1 million to 5 million per award; for-profits and international applicants eligible) closed on 10 August and 15 September 2026, with decisions due by the end of 2026. Its topics included fish flavour, off-flavours, fat alternatives and egg replacement [@CAP-57] {general|High}.
- The **GFI Research Grant Program** is closed with no reopening date [@CAP-50] {general|High}; we found no Navigation Fund alternative-protein request on its pages [@CAP-58] {general|Medium}.
- The **Bezos Earth Fund** has committed USD 1 billion to its Future of Food programme, including grants on AI for alternative protein, with no open call found [@CAP-61] {general|Medium}. Its Bezos Centre for Sustainable Protein at the National University of Singapore cites potential impact for "East and South-East Asia's 2.3 billion consumers" [@AFN-15] {general|Medium}.
- **Nova Vista Foundation's Protein Innovation request for proposals** (USD 10 million; announced 7 August 2026, closed 15 September 2026) funds policy and market shaping: public procurement of innovative protein products, advance market commitments and retailer protein-ratio targets. It excludes whole-food plant-based work, culture change, individual diet change and shifts to smaller animals. Its priority geographies for added policy staff are "high-innovation Asian economies (including but not limited to Japan and South Korea)", Germany and the Nordics; Vietnam is not named [@AFN-04; @AFN-05] {general|High}.
- **No major alternative-protein funder names Vietnam as a priority.** GFI APAC's 2025 report card does not mention Vietnam and its team page lists no one covering it; the 2026 ReRoot Asia diet-change fund published no Vietnam-specific application guide; ACE's July 2025 Movement Grants had no Vietnamese grantee [@AFN-11; @AFN-12; @AFN-29; @AFN-28] {VN-direct|Medium}. See [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]] for which routes fit which funder.
- **ACIAR** funds 23 Vietnamese projects (AUD 5.4 million in 2025/26), commissioned through Australian partners, none on alternative protein [@CAP-67] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Accelerators:** Better Bite Ventures (USD 100,000 to 200,000 cheques) and Big Idea Ventures (USD 200,000 through its programme) are the regional early-stage specialists; the ProVeg Incubator offers up to USD 300,000 including in-kind support [@CAP-63; @CAP-62; @CAP-59] {general|Medium}.

Grant windows are episodic, and none of the current alternative-protein programmes treats Vietnam as a priority. Vietnamese groups need an international co-applicant, a proposal that fits the funder's stated scope and a ready draft before windows open.

## 8.7 The global and regional context

- Global alternative-protein investment was **USD 881 million in 2025** (plant-based 450, fermentation 357, cultivated 74) and USD 359 million in the first half of 2026. In the second quarter of 2026, fermentation took about USD 132 million of roughly USD 197 million [@CAP-51] {general|High}. Synthesis Capital's managing partner says valuations "have reset" [@CAP-64] {general|Low}.
- Global agrifood-tech funding was flat at USD 16.2 billion in 2025, with upstream categories (farm, food production and biological systems) up 7% [@CAP-53] {general|Medium}.

**Reading.** Money that still flows goes to fermentation [@CAP-51] and to upstream ingredients with what AgFunder calls "tangible science, real unit economics and clear paths to revenue" [@CAP-53]. That is the part of the value chain where Vietnam's advantages (feedstock, feed demand, processing skills) lie, and the opposite of a consumer-brand play.

## 8.8 What this means

- **Investors:** there is no venture track record in Vietnamese alternative protein. A thesis must rest on B2B feed or ingredient revenue, a named strategic buyer, and a structure like Entobel's (private-equity lead, DFI support, offtake). Expect to underwrite capital intensity, not consumer growth ([[ch09-economics]]). A domestic textured-protein line is the exception that proves the rule: its equipment is cheap (Chinese lines listed at USD 11,000 to 100,000) and its main need is USD 0.6 to 3.5 million of working capital, so it suits bank debt and trade finance for an incumbent or SME, not venture equity (our calculation; [[ch26-plays]]) [@TIC-02; @TIC-03] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Startups:** realistic first cheques are angels and family offices, regional seed specialists (USD 0.1 to 0.3 million), ADB Ventures (up to USD 4 million, climate lens), and from late 2026 the Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi funds. A Singapore holding company, as Entobel used, eases foreign investment.
- **Policy makers:** naming "alternative protein and biomanufacturing" as a priority in the rules of the national and local venture funds would cost little. A grant or first-loss window for pre-revenue pilot plants would fill the gap between research grants and private equity that NATIF's priority for profitable borrowers leaves open.
- **International bodies:** the IFC and ADB Ventures precedents transfer directly; a pooled first-loss tranche for a first Vietnamese fermentation-feed plant, or DFI co-investment with a local fund, would target the gap.
