# 4. What Vietnam has: raw materials and side streams

**In brief.**
- Vietnam's raw materials are rich in carbohydrate and poor in protein. It grows about 10 Mt of cassava roots, harvests 43.5 Mt of paddy rice and has a sugar surplus.
- For protein for people, that carbohydrate matters as clean carbon for food fermentation: cassava starch, surplus sugar and broken rice.
- Protein-rich side streams, such as okara (soy pulp) and brewer's spent grain, are small. They suit koji and fungal foods made next to the plant that produces them.
- The largest residue, cassava pulp (about 6 Mt wet a year), holds almost no protein.
- Cassava roots rose 50% to 90% in price in the year to April 2026, and a new fuel-ethanol mandate competes for the crop.

## 4.1 The shape of the asset base: rich in starch, poor in protein

The streams that could become protein for people, or feed a process that makes it, fall into four groups.

| Group | Largest streams (2025) | Protein content | Use today | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starch and sugar | Cassava roots 10.24 Mt [@FS-01]; cassava pulp about 6 Mt wet (our estimate) [@FS-05; @FS-06]; paddy 43.5 Mt [@FS-25]; sugar 1.30 Mt (2025/26) [@FS-31; @FS-33] | Very low | Traded commodities or low-value feed | {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Protein-bearing residues | Brewer's spent grain up to 0.9 Mt wet; okara up to 0.66 Mt wet; seafood by-products (our estimates) [@FS-38; @FS-40; @FS-41; @FS-44; @FS-24; @FS-48] | 20% to 80% of dry matter | Cattle and pig feed, fishmeal | {VN-direct\|Low} |
| Oil and nut crops | Peanut about 380 kt; little crushed [@FS-24] | Press cakes 35% to 50% | Small, scattered, mostly food | {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Aquatic and fungal biomass | Seaweed, spirulina, duckweed, mushrooms: no reliable national volumes | 28% to 40% [@FS-55; @SCI-36] | Health food, research | {VN-direct\|Low} |

Brewer's spent grain and okara together hold at most about 75 kt of protein a year. That is about 2% of the protein in the soybean meal the feed sector uses (our calculation from [@FS-38; @FS-40; @FS-41; @FS-44; @MAC-04]). **Local protein-rich residues cannot replace imported protein at scale.** Only microbes grown on carbohydrate, or new protein crops, have the volume to matter.

## 4.2 Cassava: the largest carbon source, now contested

**The crop.** Vietnam harvested 10.24 Mt of fresh cassava roots in 2025 [@FS-01] {VN-direct|High}. It imports roughly 8 Mt more, mainly from Laos and Cambodia [@FS-14] {VN-direct|Medium}. About 85% of roots go to industry: starch, ethanol and monosodium glutamate (MSG) [@FS-08] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**The factories.** Vietnam has 100 to 150 starch factories, depending on the source [@FS-04; @FS-02; @FS-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. **Tay Ninh (former Long An) alone has 65 to 68 factories** [@FS-03; @FS-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**Exports to China.** In 2025 Vietnam exported 3.99 Mt of cassava products worth USD 1.27 billion, about 93% to China [@FS-01; @FS-13] {VN-direct|High}.

**The pulp.** Nobody publishes how much cassava pulp (*bã sắn*) Vietnam makes. From a Thai factory's material balance, we estimate **about 6 Mt of wet pulp in 2025, range 4 to 8 Mt** (our calculation from [@FS-05; @FS-06; @FS-53; @FS-01]) {VN-adjacent|Low}. The volume moves with starch exports: it was only about 3.7 Mt in 2024.

**Pulp is carbon, not protein.** It is mostly starch and only about 2% protein [@FS-18; @FS-19; @FS-20] {VN-adjacent|High}. Microbial protein grown on it needs nitrogen bought in. Growing food on it would also need safety work on cyanide, mould toxins and digestibility ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).

**Pulp is not free.** It is already sold to cattle and pig farms, dried for feed mills, or used for biogas [@FS-03; @FS-17; @FS-22] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**Cassava is no longer cheap.** By April 2026 fresh roots cost VND 3,000 to 3,550 per kg, 50% to 90% more than a year before. Starch export prices rose by about a third from January to April 2026 [@FS-01; @FS-11; @FS-12] {VN-direct|Medium}. Vedan, the largest cassava-based fermenter, reported sharp cassava price rises [@VCO-02] {VN-direct|High}.

**A new competing buyer.** Petrol with 10% ethanol (E10) has been mandatory nationwide since 1 June 2026 [@GT-10] {VN-direct|High}. Domestic ethanol plants use cassava, though most E10 ethanol was still imported in mid-2026 [@FS-09; @IND-20] {VN-direct|Medium}. Any plan that assumes cheap cassava needs testing against this.

**What cassava means for food.** Its value is as fermentation carbon. Turning part of the exported starch into fermentation sugar next to the starch factories, for food fermentation at home, is play T4 ([[ch26-plays]]).

## 4.3 Rice: large, concentrated, and already sold

- **Paddy and bran.** Vietnam harvested 43.5 Mt of paddy in 2025, yielding about 4.35 Mt of bran [@FS-25; @FS-27] {VN-direct|High}. Bran is exported or fed to animals, and sells for about VND 7,500 per kg [@FS-23; @FS-26] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Broken rice.** Vietnam exported 0.87 Mt of fully broken rice in 2025, at about USD 370 per tonne [@FS-23; @FS-26] {VN-direct|Medium}. It is a food-grade starch at a commodity price, so it can supply food fermentation.
- **Rice distillers' grain.** Vietnamese research shows this residue from rice liquor holds 55% to 80% protein on a dry basis, and extraction can recover much of it [@RD-22; @RD-23] {VN-direct|Medium}. The national volume is unknown.

**What it means for food.** Rice bran and broken rice are traded commodities. A rice-protein business, for example for plant drinks (play T10), buys at market prices.

## 4.4 Sugar: a surplus, and a clean carbon source for food fermentation

The 2025/26 crop yielded 1.3 Mt of sugar [@FS-31; @FS-33; @FS-34] {VN-direct|High}. In mid-2026 the domestic sugar price was at a three-year low, below prices in China, Indonesia and the Philippines [@FS-31; @FS-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. On 2026 prices, surplus sugar and broken rice are clean, food-grade carbon for fermentation. They are worth comparing against residues that need treatment first.

## 4.5 Protein-bearing side streams: small, and best used for food near the source

The food-grade side streams suit koji and fungal foods made next to the brewery or soy plant (play T9, [[ch26-plays]]). Koji is the food mould used for soy sauce and *tương* (fermented soybean paste). These streams are small and spoil fast.

- **Brewer's spent grain.** Vietnam's beer output implies at most 0.8 to 0.9 Mt of wet spent grain a year, about 32 to 35 kt of protein (our estimate from [@FS-38; @FS-40; @FS-41]) {VN-adjacent|Low}. Heineken and Sabeco brew about 77% of the beer [@FS-41] {VN-direct|Medium}. Fresh spent grain spoils in two to three days [@FS-42] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Okara (soy pulp from soy milk and tofu).** Vietnam's food soybeans yield at most about 0.6 Mt of wet okara a year (our estimate from [@FS-44; @FS-45]) {general|Low}. It is scattered across many makers, and its protein was already imported as soybeans.
- **Seafood by-products.** About two thirds of a filleted fish becomes by-product [@FS-48] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. These are animal streams, context only here.
- **Fermentation co-products.** The amino-acid makers sell their co-products into feed and fertiliser [@VCO-01; @IND-08] {VN-direct|Medium}. Context only for this study.

## 4.6 Crops for plant protein: none large at home

Vietnam has no large protein crop of its own.

- **Soybean:** about 43,000 t in 2025, and falling [@FS-24] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Peanut:** about 380 kt, of which only 35 kt is crushed [@FS-24] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Mung bean (*đậu xanh*):** output unknown. Vietnam imported USD 138 million of mung and black gram beans in 2023, mostly from Myanmar [@FORM-08] {VN-direct|Medium}. So even "domestic" chay protein is partly imported ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]]).
- **Cashew:** Vietnam is the world's largest cashew processor, mostly from imported nuts.

**What it means for food.** A domestic plant-protein industry cannot rest on a Vietnamese protein crop today. It would process imported beans, use rice and mung bean at a cost premium, or grow a new crop. Plays T1 and T10 both start from this position ([[ch26-plays]]).

## 4.7 Aquatic and fungal biomass: possible food, but unmeasured

- **Seaweed:** farmed and harvested commercially, but no production volume is published [@FS-51] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Spirulina:** small health-food producers making a few tonnes a year [@ECO-52; @ECO-53] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Duckweed (*bèo*):** Vietnamese researchers hold over 100 wild samples [@RD-44] {VN-direct|Medium}, but we found no commercial growing [@FS-54] {VN-direct|Low}. For food, manganese is the safety gate. Europe's food safety authority could not confirm whole duckweed powder as safe in 2021, but approved a duckweed protein concentrate in 2023 [@SCI-28; @SCI-29; @SCI-30] {general|High}. Duckweed grown on wastewater would also need heavy-metal testing ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **Mushrooms:** a large smallholder sector on rice straw and sawdust, with no verified national output [@NOV-78] {VN-direct|Low}.

Any plan built on these streams starts with collecting data.

## 4.8 Energy and gases: carbon-heavy power, unmeasured gases

Industrial power costs about USD 0.073 to 0.081 per kWh ([[ch09-economics]]) [@COST-01; @COST-03]. The grid emits about 0.66 kg of CO2 per kWh [@COST-08] {VN-direct|High}. So power-hungry processes carry a high carbon footprint unless they buy renewable power. Gas-fermentation routes have feedstocks in principle, but no measured supply [@FS-04; @FS-09] {VN-direct|Low}.

## 4.9 Five rules from the asset map

1. **Carbon is abundant; nitrogen and protein are scarce.** Microbial protein from Vietnamese carbohydrate needs bought nitrogen. Urea cost about USD 425 per tonne in Vietnam in March 2026 [@COST-33; @COST-30] {VN-direct|Medium}.
2. **Residues are traded, not free.** Bran, broken rice, pulp, spent grain and okara all have buyers.
3. **Wet residues must be used where they are made.** They spoil in days, so a plant should sit next to the starch factory, brewery or soy plant [@FS-18].
4. **Volumes swing with China.** Cassava pulp in 2024 was about 60% of the 2025 volume. Supply contracts need several factories or storage.
5. **Nobody measures by-products.** No agency publishes national data on pulp, spent grain, okara or molasses. A short yearly return from factories would fix this. It is the by-product atlas, public good P5 ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

**For each reader.**
- **Investors:** cheap cassava residue is real in volume, but weaker on price than it looked. Test plans against cassava price cycles and E10.
- **Startups:** for pulp-based fermentation, start in Tay Ninh, pay by dry weight and budget for nitrogen. For koji or fungal foods, plan one plant next to one brewery or soy plant.
- **Manufacturers:** domestic protein-rich residues suit niche foods made near the source, not bulk protein.
- **Policy makers and international bodies:** a yearly public by-product atlas is cheap and missing.
- **Researchers:** measure cassava pulp across factories and seasons, and duckweed and seaweed yields on Vietnamese sites, with manganese and heavy metals for food use.
