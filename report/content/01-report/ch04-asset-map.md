# 4. What Vietnam has: raw materials and side streams

**In brief.**
- Vietnam's raw materials are rich in carbohydrate and poor in protein. It grows about 10 Mt of cassava roots and imports roughly 8 Mt more for processing, harvests 43.5 Mt of paddy rice, and has a sugar surplus.
- For protein for people, that carbohydrate matters as clean carbon for food fermentation: cassava starch turned into fermentation sugar, surplus sugar and broken rice.
- Protein-rich side streams (brewer's spent grain, okara, seafood by-products) are small next to the 7.2 Mt of soybean meal the feed sector uses a year. The food-grade ones, okara and brewer's grain, suit koji and fungal foods made next to the plant that produces them.
- The largest residue is cassava pulp at starch factories: about 6 Mt wet a year (about 1.2 Mt dry matter), clustered in Tay Ninh (former Long An). It holds almost no protein.
- Cassava roots rose about 50 to 90% in price, depending on province, in the year to April 2026, and a new fuel-ethanol mandate now competes for the crop.
- Three rules follow: residues are traded, not free; wet residues have to be used where they are made; and any protein route from Vietnamese carbon has to buy nitrogen.

## 4.1 The shape of the asset base: carbon-rich, protein-poor

The streams that could become protein for people, or supply a process that makes it, fall into four groups. Full data sheets for 34 streams are in [[app-s4-feedstocks]].

| Group | Largest streams (2025 unless stated) | Protein content | What it is today | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starch and sugar | Cassava roots 10.24 Mt grown [@FS-01]; cassava pulp about 6 Mt wet (derived) [@FS-05; @FS-06]; paddy 43.5 Mt [@FS-25]; broken rice (0.87 Mt of 100% broken exported) [@FS-23]; sugarcane 13.3 Mt crushed, 1.30 Mt sugar (2025/26) [@FS-31; @FS-33] | Very low (pulp 1.55% to 2.6% of dry matter) | Traded commodities or low-value feed | {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Protein-bearing residues | Brewer's spent grain up to 0.8 to 0.9 Mt wet (derived) [@FS-38; @FS-40; @FS-41]; okara up to 0.6 to 0.66 Mt wet (derived) [@FS-44; @FS-24]; rice distillers' grain (volume unknown) [@RD-22]; seafood by-products (about 68% of the fish, derived from 3.1 kg of pangasius and tilapia per kg of fillet in Vietnam and Brazil) [@FS-48] | 20% to 80% of dry matter | Cattle and pig feed, fishmeal, some export | {VN-direct\|Low} |
| Oil and nut crops | Peanut about 380 kt, only 35 kt crushed; copra 31 kt crushed (USDA forecasts for MY2025/26) [@FS-24]; cashew processing (volumes not established) | Press cakes 35% to 50% | Small, scattered, mostly food | {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Aquatic and fungal biomass | Seaweed, spirulina, duckweed, mushrooms: we found no reliable national volumes | About 28% (straw mushroom); 35% to 40% (duckweed) [@FS-55; @SCI-36] | Health food, research collections | {VN-direct\|Low} |

USDA is the US Department of Agriculture; MY is its marketing year.

**What each group means for protein for people.**
- **Starch and sugar** are the carbon for food fermentation. Cassava starch, surplus sugar and broken rice can all supply it (sections 4.2 to 4.4).
- **Protein-bearing residues** are small. The food-grade ones, okara and brewer's spent grain, suit koji and fungal foods made near the source (section 4.5).
- **Oil and nut crops** are small and scattered, and mostly already go to food (section 4.6).
- **Aquatic and fungal biomass** could become food, but nobody measures it, and duckweed needs safety checks first (section 4.7).

For scale, Vietnam's brewer's spent grain holds at most about 35 kt of protein a year, and okara at most about 40 kt. Together that is about 2% of the protein in the soybean meal the feed sector uses (derived from [@FS-38; @FS-40; @FS-41; @FS-44; @MAC-04]). **Local protein-rich residues cannot replace imported soybean meal at scale.** Their best use is in food products made near the source. Only microbial conversion of carbohydrate streams, or new protein crops, have the volume to matter.

## 4.2 Cassava: the largest carbon source, now contested

**The crop.** Vietnam harvested 10.24 Mt of fresh cassava roots from 499,100 ha in 2025, down 18,600 ha from 2024 [@FS-01] {VN-direct|High}. Annual cassava supply exceeds 18 Mt, about 42% of it (roughly 8 Mt) imported, mainly from Laos and Cambodia [@FS-14] {VN-direct|Medium}. About 85% of roots go to industry: starch, ethanol, monosodium glutamate (MSG) and others [@FS-08] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**The factories.** Estimates of the number of industrial starch factories differ by source [@FS-04; @FS-02; @FS-07] {VN-direct|Medium}:
- about 100 (Vietnam Cassava Starch Association, reported by the Ministry of Industry and Trade, 2021);
- about 120 in 27 provinces (ASEAN Cassava Centre, reported January 2023);
- "150+" (Vietnam Cassava Association, 2025).

The ASEAN Cassava Centre gives 11.3 Mt of design capacity and 8.62 Mt of actual root capacity (data year not stated) [@FS-02]. **Tay Ninh alone has 65 to 68 factories and 6.4 Mt a year of root capacity** [@FS-03; @FS-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Tay Ninh now includes the former Long An province (see [[app-s10-admin-map]]).

**Exports to China.** Vietnam exported about 2.28 Mt of cassava starch to China in the first 11 months of 2025 (+70%), becoming China's largest supplier [@FS-01; @FS-13] {VN-direct|High}. Over the year it exported 3.99 Mt of cassava products worth USD 1.27 billion [@FS-01] {VN-direct|High}. China took about 93% [@FS-01] {VN-direct|High}.

**The pulp.** Nobody publishes the national tonnage of cassava pulp (*bã sắn* or *bã mì*). We estimate it from a Thai factory material balance: per tonne of starch, a factory uses 4.4 t of roots and makes 2.5 t of wet pulp at about 80% moisture. Applied to Vietnam's 2025 starch output, this gives **about 6 Mt of wet pulp in 2025, range 4 to 8 Mt**. That is **about 0.8 to 1.7 Mt of dry matter** (our calculation from [@FS-05; @FS-06; @FS-53; @FS-01]) {VN-adjacent|Low}. Pulp volume moves with starch exports: the 2024 equivalent was only about 3.7 Mt wet.

> **Correction.** Earlier drafts gave 1.7 to 2.1 Mt of wet pulp, which is too low unless it refers to pressed or partly dried pulp. The ratios in circulation disagree mainly because they use different moisture bases. See [[app-s4-feedstocks]] and [[app-m5-changelog]].

**Composition.** Pulp is about 54% to 70% starch and only 1.55% to 2.6% crude protein on a dry basis [@FS-18; @FS-19; @FS-20] {VN-adjacent|High}. All the pulp in Vietnam holds only about 20 to 35 kt of protein (our calculation). It is a **carbon source, not a protein source**: any microbial protein grown on it needs nitrogen bought in. Growing food on pulp would also need a food-grade safety package (cyanide, mycotoxins, RNA and digestibility), a research gap listed in [[ch06-knowledge-talent]].

**Price.** No current gate price is published. The only 2024 signal is an implied value of about VND 1,700/kg from one Tay Ninh factory's pulp revenue [@FS-03] {VN-direct|Low}. Pulp is already sold to cattle and pig farms, dried for feed mills, pelleted, or digested for biogas [@FS-03; @FS-17; @FS-22] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**Cassava is no longer cheap.** By April 2026 fresh roots (30% starch) sold for VND 3,000 to 3,550/kg in Tay Ninh, Dak Lak, Quang Ngai and Gia Lai. That was about 50 to 90% above April 2025, depending on province. Starch rose from USD 405 to 415/t free on board (FOB) in January 2026 to USD 520 to 540/t in April [@FS-01; @FS-11; @FS-12] {VN-direct|Medium}. Vedan, the largest cassava-based fermenter, reported that cassava prices "rose sharply" from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the first half of 2026 [@VCO-02] {VN-direct|High}.

**A new competing buyer.** E10 gasoline (petrol with 10% ethanol) has been mandatory nationwide since 1 June 2026 [@GT-10] {VN-direct|High}. Three fuel-ethanol plants have a combined design capacity of 830 m3 a day: Dung Quat in Quang Ngai, the Quang Nam plant now in Da Nang, and Tung Lam in Dong Nai. Dung Quat plans to switch its feedstock from cassava to maize [@FS-09] {VN-direct|High}. About 80% of E10 ethanol was still imported in mid-2026 [@IND-20] {VN-direct|Medium}. Any plan that assumes cheap cassava roots needs testing against this.

**What cassava means for protein for people.** Cassava's value for food is as fermentation carbon, not as protein. Today much of Vietnam's cassava starch is exported, mostly to China. Turning part of it into fermentation sugar next to the starch factories, for food fermentation at home, is play T4: a cassava-starch-to-fermentation-sugar hub, meaning a cluster of plants on one site ([[ch26-plays]]). Pulp could add more carbon, but for food it needs pre-treatment and the safety work above.

## 4.3 Rice: large, concentrated, and already sold

- **Paddy and bran.** Vietnam harvested 43.5 Mt of paddy in 2025 [@FS-25] {VN-direct|High}. Bran is about 4.35 Mt a year at 10% of paddy (derived from [@FS-27]). In 2025, 1.2 Mt of bran was exported (double 2024) and about 2.8 Mt of bran plus broken rice went into domestic feed [@FS-23] {VN-direct|High}. Bran sold for VND 7,300 to 7,650/kg in An Giang on 19 September 2026 [@FS-26] {VN-direct|Medium}. Full-fat bran is about 11% protein [@FS-56] and 12% to 24% oil [@FS-57] {general|Medium}.
- **Broken rice.** Vietnam exported 0.87 Mt of 100% broken rice in 2025 [@FS-23]. On 19 September 2026, fragrant 100% broken rice sold at USD 368 to 372/t FOB against USD 440 to 445/t for fragrant 5% broken [@FS-26] {VN-direct|Medium}. Broken rice is a food-grade starch at a commodity price, so it can supply food fermentation. The total volume is not published.
- **Rice distillers' grain.** Vietnamese research shows dried distillers' grain from rice ethanol and rice liquor holds 55% to 80% protein on a dry basis [@RD-22]. Alkaline extraction recovered 54% to 55% of it in one study [@RD-22] and 90% in another [@RD-23] {VN-direct|Medium}. The national volume of this stream is unknown. It is one of the few protein-rich residues with published Vietnamese extraction data, and a possible food protein ingredient ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).

**What it means for food.** Rice bran and broken rice are not free residues. They are traded feed and food commodities with export demand. A rice-protein business in Vietnam, for example for plant drinks (play T10), buys its raw material at market prices.

## 4.4 Sugar: a surplus, and a clean carbon source for food fermentation

The 2025/26 crop crushed 13.3 Mt of cane for 1.298 Mt of sugar [@FS-31; @FS-33]; about 25 mills were operating in 2024 [@FS-34] {VN-direct|High}. The average domestic sugar price was about VND 16,505/kg in mid-2026. That was a three-year low and below prices in China, Indonesia and the Philippines [@FS-31; @FS-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. Molasses volume is not published; we estimate 0.55 to 0.72 Mt a year from Indian yield ratios [@FS-37] {general|Low}.

Earlier drafts treated Vietnam as short of sugar for fermentation. On 2026 prices, surplus domestic sugar and broken rice are both clean, food-grade carbon sources for food fermentation. They are worth comparing against residues that need pre-treatment.

## 4.5 Protein-bearing side streams: small, and best used for food near the source

The food-grade side streams, brewer's spent grain and okara, suit koji and fungal foods made next to the brewery or soy plant (play T9, [[ch26-plays]]). Koji is the food mould used for soy sauce and Vietnamese *tương* (fermented soybean paste). These streams are small and spoil fast.

- **Brewer's spent grain (*bã bia*).** Beer output of more than 4 billion litres a year (2022) to 4.4 billion litres (2019) implies at most 0.8 to 0.9 Mt of wet spent grain. Assuming about 20% dry matter and 20% protein in the dry matter, that is about 32 to 35 kt of protein (derived from [@FS-38; @FS-40; @FS-41]) {VN-adjacent|Low}. Two groups dominate: Heineken (43%) and Sabeco (33.9%) held about 77% of the 2023 beer market [@FS-41] {VN-direct|Medium}. Spent grain already has buyers: Entobel's insect-feed plant in Ho Chi Minh City (former Ba Ria-Vung Tau) uses Heineken Vietnam by-products [@ECO-01; @ECO-03] {VN-direct|High}. Fresh spent grain spoils in two to three days [@FS-42] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Okara (*bã đậu nành*, the residue from soy milk and tofu).** Vietnam uses about 550 kt of soybeans for food a year [@FS-24]. At 1.1 to 1.2 kg of wet okara per kg of beans, that gives at most 0.6 to 0.66 Mt of wet okara (derived from [@FS-44; @FS-45]) {general|Low}. It is scattered across many tofu and soy-milk makers, and its protein was already imported as soybeans. Because it comes from food plants, it is a natural substrate for koji and fungal foods.
- **Seafood processing by-products.** Filleting uses about 3.1 kg of pangasius or tilapia per kg of fillet in exporting countries such as Vietnam and Brazil. So about 68% of the fish becomes by-product (derived) [@FS-48] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. Shrimp processing side streams (heads and shells) carry 40% to 65% protein on a dry basis [@FS-49] {general|Medium}. These are animal-origin streams, not alternative-protein feedstocks, and are context only here. Vietnam exported USD 430 million of fishmeal in 2023, 93.5% to China [@FM-38] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Fermentation co-products.** The amino-acid makers sell their fermentation co-products into feed and fertiliser. Vedan's "fertiliser and feed" segment earned USD 27.1 million in 2025 [@VCO-01], and Ajinomoto Vietnam sells liquid and solid feed ingredients [@IND-08] {VN-direct|Medium}. Chinese single-cell microbial biomass from glutamic-acid fermentation, at least 70% crude protein, is already imported and sold as a feed protein in Vietnam [@FM-12; @ECO-69] {VN-direct|Low}. For this study these streams are context only.

## 4.6 Crops for plant protein: none large at home

Vietnam has no large protein crop of its own.

- **Soybean:** about 43,000 t on 26,300 ha in 2025, falling [@FS-24] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Peanut:** about 380 kt (USDA forecast for MY2025/26; 388 kt in 2025), of which only 35 kt is crushed (forecast) [@FS-24] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Coconut:** only about 24 to 31 kt of copra crushed a year (USDA 2025 estimate to MY2025/26 forecast) [@FS-24] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Mung bean (*đậu xanh*):** national production was not established. Vietnam imported USD 138 million of dried mung and black gram beans in 2023, 65% from Myanmar [@FORM-08] {VN-direct|Medium}. Mung bean is the main protein ingredient in mass-market *đồ chay* (traditional vegetarian food) fillings ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]]). So even "domestic" chay protein is partly regional imports.
- **Cashew:** Vietnam is the world's largest cashew processor, largely from imported raw nuts. Broken-kernel tonnage was not established.
- **Coffee pulp:** about 450 kt of dried pulp a year (reported in a 2020 paper, which describes it as exported). It is mostly burned in dryers or used as fertiliser; caffeine limits its use as feed [@FS-46] {VN-direct|Low}.

**What it means for food.** A domestic plant-protein ingredient industry cannot rest on a Vietnamese protein crop today. It would process imported beans, as soy-milk and tofu makers already do. Or it would use rice and mung bean at a cost premium, or grow a new crop. Domestic textured plant protein (play T1) and mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks (play T10) both start from this position ([[ch26-plays]]).

## 4.7 Aquatic and fungal biomass: possible food, but unmeasured

- **Seaweed:** 827 recorded species. The commercially important genera (Caulerpa, Sargassum, Gracilaria, Ulva, Kappaphycus) are farmed and harvested commercially, including in Khanh Hoa (including former Ninh Thuan). No production volume was found [@FS-51] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Spirulina:** small health-food producers. Vinh Hao in Lam Dong (former Binh Thuan) reports more than 12,000 m2 of cultivation area and about 17 t a year (2013 figure). VN Vastcom in Nghe An makes about 5 t of biomass a year (2023) [@ECO-52; @ECO-53] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Duckweed (*bèo*):** Vietnamese researchers hold a collection of more than 100 wild duckweed samples (*Spirodela*, *Lemna*, *Wolffia*) [@RD-44] {VN-direct|Medium}. But we found no commercial cultivation, only feed trials [@FS-54] {VN-direct|Low}. For food, manganese is the safety gate. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) could not establish the safety of whole *Wolffia* powder in 2021 because of manganese intake. But it judged a *Lemna* protein concentrate safe in 2023 [@SCI-28; @SCI-29; @SCI-30] {general|High}. Duckweed grown on effluent would also need heavy-metal testing ([[ch10-technology-fit]]). Until Vietnamese field data exist, plan on a conservative 10 to 30 t of dry matter per hectare per year (our assumption). Reported averages are about 30 t and maxima up to 100 t [@SCI-36; @SCI-37] {general|Medium}. Duckweed is also a permitted livestock feed material; for this study that is context only ([[ch07-rules]]).
- **Mushrooms:** a large smallholder sector on rice straw and sawdust. National output and spent-substrate volumes were not verified in this study [@NOV-78] {VN-direct|Low}.

Any plan built on these streams starts with primary data collection.

## 4.8 Energy and gases: carbon-heavy power, unmeasured gases

Industrial power costs about USD 0.073 to 0.081/kWh for a plant running around the clock [@COST-01; @COST-03] ([[ch09-economics]]). The grid emits about 0.66 kg of CO2 per kWh (2023 official factor) [@COST-08] {VN-direct|High}. So power-intensive processes carry a high carbon footprint unless they buy renewable power. The revised national power plan targets 28% to 36% non-hydro renewable electricity by 2030 [@GT-11] {VN-direct|High}. Some starch factories use biogas from cassava starch wastewater, but we found no figure for how much. One ethanol plant plans research on low-carbon methanol from biogenic CO2 [@FS-04; @FS-09] {VN-direct|Low}. So gas-fermentation routes have feedstocks in principle, but no measured supply.

## 4.9 Five rules from the asset map

1. **Carbon is abundant; nitrogen and protein are scarce.** Microbial protein from Vietnamese carbohydrate needs bought nitrogen. Phu My urea was VND 553,100 per 50 kg bag in March 2026, about USD 425/t at 26,000 VND per USD, and world urea spiked to USD 726/t [@COST-33; @COST-30] {VN-direct|Medium}.
2. **Residues are traded, not free.** Bran, broken rice, pulp, spent grain and okara all have buyers. Model them at a positive price per tonne of dry matter.
3. **Wet residues have to be used where they are made.** Pulp, spent grain and okara spoil in days (pulp keeps well as silage in pits [@FS-18]). So a plant that uses them should sit next to a starch factory, brewery or soy plant.
4. **Volumes swing with China.** Cassava pulp in 2024 was about 60% of the 2025 volume because starch exports to China jumped. Supply contracts need several factories or storage.
5. **Nobody measures by-products.** No agency publishes national data on starch output, pulp, spent grain, okara, molasses or seafood by-products. A one-page annual return from starch factories, breweries, sugar mills and seafood processors would make the circular-economy case investable. This is the by-product atlas, public good P5 (see [[ch27-policy-options]]).

**For each reader.**
- **Investors:** the volume thesis for "cheap cassava residue" holds; the price thesis is weaker than earlier drafts assumed. Test plans against cassava price cycles and E10.
- **Startups:** for pulp-based fermentation, start in Tay Ninh, negotiate on a dry-matter basis and budget for nitrogen. For koji or fungal foods on spent grain or okara, plan one plant next to one brewery or soy plant.
- **Manufacturers:** domestic protein-rich residues are small. They suit niche food products made near the source, such as koji and fungal foods, rather than bulk protein.
- **Policy makers and international bodies:** a public Vietnam by-product atlas (volume, location, price, composition), updated yearly, is a cheap and missing public good.
- **Researchers:** priority measurements are a Vietnamese pulp material balance across factories and seasons, and duckweed and seaweed productivity on Vietnamese sites, with manganese and heavy metals for food use.
