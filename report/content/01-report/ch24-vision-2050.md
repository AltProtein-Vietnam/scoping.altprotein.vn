# 24. Vietnam 2050: a vision for a measured, more diverse and more secure protein supply

**In brief.**
- This chapter proposes one vision for the protein Vietnamese people eat in 2050 and works back to milestones in each five-year plan. It is a goal for discussion, not a forecast.
- In the vision, Vietnam feeds a larger, richer and older population from a protein supply that is measured, more diverse and more secure. Meat stays the main protein; new protein adds a second source.
- It has five pillars: measure what people eat; make more protein ingredients at home; diversify through new routes; specialise in regional niches; and secure supply against import and climate shocks.
- The numbers come from our demand model. By 2050, new routes supply about 67,000 to 172,000 t of protein a year, and about 59,000 to 147,000 t of it is made in Vietnam.
- The first steps are cheap: a yearly protein balance from 2028, a new-food route, a protein-quality laboratory, 10,000 to 30,000 L of open food-grade pilot capacity by 2030 and a public innovation line of USD 5 to 15 million a year.
- No country we checked has a protein plan that reaches 2050, so this vision needs a review at every five-year plan.

> **Vision, not forecast.** Everything in this chapter tagged *vision* is a goal that AltProtein Vietnam puts forward for discussion. It is not a prediction and not an official target. The benchmark evidence behind each goal is cited and tagged as usual. The ranges come from the demand model of [[ch18-demand-sizing]] and [[app-d8-demand-model]], which uses the same population and meat demand as [[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]. Section 24.7 tests how the vision fares in each 2050 world.

## 24.1 Why a vision, and why this framing

- **Nobody has a protein plan to 2050.** Protein horizons abroad mostly stop at 2030 to 2035: the EU, Canada and Singapore at 2035, Japan's basic plan at fiscal year 2030, and China, Israel and the Netherlands at 2030. Japan's MIDORI strategy (2050) and Denmark's land deal (2045) reach further but carry no protein indicator [@VIS-15; @VIS-08; @VIS-25; @VIS-13; @GEO-10; @VIS-23; @VIS-05; @VIS-49; @VIS-03] {general|Medium}. The one exception we found is a Japanese food-tech investment roadmap to 2040, reported only in the press, with about JPY 1 trillion for novel foods [@VIS-14] {VN-adjacent|Low}.
- **Vietnam's official 2050 plans cover inputs, not protein.** Vietnam has official 2050 numbers for non-hydro renewables (74 to 75% of power), clean hydrogen (10 to 20 Mt, million tonnes, a year), agricultural emissions and net zero. The emissions cap for agriculture is 56 MtCO2e (million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent). No official meat, feed or protein number goes beyond 2030 [@GT-11; @NTS-25; @NTS-24; @CLM-01; @NTS-07] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection} (official targets). A protein vision can anchor on those inputs.
- **Security and industry framing lead, with climate as a co-benefit.** The EU's July 2026 plan is titled for resilience and strategic autonomy. Japan and Korea state their targets as food security. Singapore left alternative protein out of its food-security targets for cost and acceptance reasons [@VIS-15; @VIS-13; @VIS-29; @VIS-25] {general|Medium} {fx:signal}. This vision follows that lead. It is about the security of the protein people eat and the industry that makes it. Diet change is not a goal this study sets.
- **Plans without a statistic cannot be judged.** The Dutch protein strategy published no quantitative targets. We found no published progress report for Canada's roadmap, and Denmark's action plan has no indicators. Japan, the EU and China are tracked even when they miss [@VIS-05; @VIS-08; @VIS-10; @VIS-01; @VIS-11; @VIS-12; @VIS-13; @VIS-15; @GEO-11] {general|Medium}.
- **Backcasting works only when embedded.** Backcasting means starting from a goal and working back to the steps needed now. Reviews name follow-up and monitoring as the weak link. A Dutch novel-protein backcast in the 1990s seeded a research programme, yet the country's 2020 strategy still published no quantified targets for its solution tracks [@VIS-37; @VIS-38; @VIS-41; @VIS-05] {general|Medium}.
- **Feed is context, not a goal.** Feed is the hidden import behind the meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish that people eat. On trend, the imported soybean meal behind domestic meat reaches about 10.4 Mt by 2050. Each tonne of meat protein replaced in diets avoids about 4.5 t of it (our calculation, [[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. So protein for people is the route that saves the most imports per tonne. But at the scale demand supports, it adds a second source rather than changing national exposure on its own {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.

## 24.2 The vision

> **Vision, not forecast.** By 2050 Vietnam feeds a larger, richer and older population from a protein supply that is **measured, more diverse and more secure**. It publishes a yearly protein balance of what people eat and where it comes from. Domestic makers supply 40 to 60% of the plant-protein ingredients, such as textured soy, gluten and isolates, that food makers now import. {fx:vision}
>
> New routes supply about 67,000 to 172,000 t of protein a year. They include upgraded *chay* (traditional vegetarian food), declared blends of meat and plant protein, canteen dishes, plant drinks and fermented foods. About 59,000 to 147,000 t of this protein is made in Vietnam. Most of it is plant protein processed at home in place of imported ingredients; some is grown on Vietnamese carbohydrate and side streams. {fx:vision}
>
> Vietnam has a working new-food route, open food-grade pilot capacity and a protein-quality laboratory. It supplies fermentation sugar and contract fermentation for food to the region. Meat stays the main protein; new protein adds a second source rather than replacing it. The vision is reviewed at every five-year plan. {fx:vision}

**The scale behind it.** On trend, the population grows from about 101.6 million in 2025 to about 110.0 million in 2050 (our model, on UN projections [@GT-12]). The meat protein people eat rises from about 1.01 Mt to about 1.45 Mt. That is about 27 to 36 g per person per day on a supply basis {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. People aged 65 and over rise from 9.5% to 20.0% of the population (UN medium variant) [@GEO-31] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:projection}. New protein displaces about 0.4% of meat protein in 2050 on the benchmark path and about 1.5% on the stretch path {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}. That is why the vision speaks of a second source, not a replacement.

Two terms need a gloss. Precision fermentation uses microbes to make one specific protein, such as an enzyme or a milk protein. Cultivated seafood is grown from fish or shellfish cells. The plays behind each pillar are in [[ch26-plays]].

How to read the model rows {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}:
- **"Made in Vietnam" is the model reading.** Only import substitution applies an explicit domestic share; the other routes count plant or novel protein of any origin. On a strict reading, the 2035 benchmark figure is about 11.4 kt, not 16.6 kt ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]; [[app-d8-demand-model]], D8.3).
- **Most of it is import substitution.** On the benchmark path, replacing imported ingredients is about two-thirds of the protein supplied in 2050 (44.6 of 66.7 kt). It replaces soy protein with soy protein, so it displaces no meat. Its value is the import bill replaced, processing capacity and jobs.
- **The stretch path is a stretch.** It assumes price parity per serving in local formats, openly declared blends, canteen protein defaults and more *chay* days. It is the top of a range, not a target to be judged on alone.
- **Meat displaced is not a diet target.** We track it to show the size of the second source; the study does not tell people what to eat. Existing *chay* days already avoid about 36 kt of meat protein a year in 2050 in the model, more than either path adds.

## 24.6 The map in 2050

The vision has a geography. Appendix F5 names six candidate hubs, each a cluster of plants in one area, which open at different times ([[app-f5-targets-hubs]], `candidate_hubs.csv`) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Five matter for protein for people; the sixth is now context only.
- **Ho Chi Minh City (including former Binh Duong and Ba Ria-Vung Tau) with Dong Nai (including former Binh Phuoc):** pilot plants, tolling and food-grade fermentation, now to 2035. Tolling means renting time on someone else's fermenter. In our assessment this is the only hub where most assets already operate, including Vedan's and Ajinomoto's fermentation plants in Dong Nai and Saigon Hi-Tech Park [@IND-01; @IND-04; @IND-08; @IND-57] {VN-direct|Medium}. It suits domestic textured plant protein (play T1), shared pilot and tolling fermentation (T5) and precision-fermentation ingredients (T7).
- **Tay Ninh (former Long An and Tay Ninh):** fermentation sugar from cassava for food fermenters (play T4), 2028 to 2040. It has the lowest industrial-park occupancy in the south (60.9% in the second quarter of 2026) and a large land bank [@FS-03; @COST-21] {VN-direct|Low}. Its roots are ample for food-scale fermentation. A protein plant of 20 kt a year would need 3.9 to 4.7% of former Tay Ninh's root capacity (our calculation, [[app-f5-targets-hubs]]) {VN-direct|Low}.
- **The Mekong river belt (Dong Thap, including former Tien Giang; Can Tho, including former Hau Giang; An Giang, including former Kien Giang):** side streams from rice and pangasius, 2027 to 2035 [@HUB-13; @HUB-14; @HUB-28] {VN-direct|Medium}. For protein for people the rice streams matter most: rice bran and broken rice for koji and fungal foods (T9) and rice protein for plant drinks (T10) (`candidate_hubs.csv`).
- **The north (Hanoi Biotechnology High-Tech Zone, Bac Tu Liem):** research now; industry in the 2040s (our judgement) [@HUB-19; @HUB-20] {VN-direct|Medium}. It is a candidate home for the protein-quality laboratory and for cultivated seafood research (T8) (`candidate_hubs.csv`).
- **The south-central coast (Khanh Hoa and Lam Dong, including former Ninh Thuan and Binh Thuan):** the best long-run solar site, 2035 to 2050 [@HUB-02] {VN-direct|Low}. For food, seaweed protein and spirulina (a microalga) are small niches there (`candidate_hubs.csv`).
- **The Mekong shrimp coast (Ca Mau, including former Bac Lieu; Vinh Long, including former Tra Vinh and Ben Tre): context only.** Its case rests on aquafeed buyers and planned green hydrogen for protein made from power [@HUB-15; @HUB-05] {VN-direct|Low}. Both are feed uses, outside this study's scope, so it is not a site for the plays this vision relies on. Any plant there would need a raised site that uses surface water, because the delta is sinking [@CLM-14] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 24.7 How the vision fares in each world

Chapter 23 builds four worlds for 2050 from two uncertainties. The first is stress on imported protein: the feed behind domestic meat, and imported protein foods. The second is whether Asia's fermentation market stays open or falls under Chinese price dominance.

| Pillar | A. Regional workshop | B. Comfortable price-taker | C. Security build-out | D. Squeezed importer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measure | Achievable | Achievable | Achievable | Achievable |
| Make | Upper half of the range | Near the drift path: Chinese and ASEAN protein ingredients enter duty-free [@FTR-32] {VN-direct\|High} | Upper half of the range | Lower half, in home-market niches |
| Diversify | Achievable | Achievable, with more imported ingredients | Achievable | Achievable, with more imported ingredients |
| Specialise | Strongest | Weak | Strong | Narrow (local niches) |
| Secure | Achievable | Achievable, but the second source stays small | Achievable, and urgent | Achievable, at higher cost |

Our judgement from [[ch23-scenarios-2050]] and the play scores in `play_robustness.csv` {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Three of the five pillars (Measure, Diversify and Secure) are achievable in every world. They rest on public goods and on demand conditions, such as accepted blends and canteen menus, that the two axes do not decide. Making at home and specialising depend on the world. Textured plant protein (T1), the cassava sugar hub (T4) and shared pilot fermentation (T5) hold in every world. But they thrive only in the open-market worlds, A and C, and T1 only in A. That is why their milestones are ranges, and why the vision needs a review cycle.

## 24.8 What would make us revise it

- **Upwards:**
  - an ASEAN reliance mechanism for novel foods;
  - a Vietnamese processor selling a declared meat-and-plant blend that buyers accept (no Vietnamese brand sells one today [@HXE-04; @HXE-09; @TRU-22; @TRU-07] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed});
  - a large caterer making a plant-protein dish a menu default;
  - plant-protein foods reaching price parity per serving in local formats;
  - a working cellulosic sugar plant in Asia.
- **Downwards:**
  - imports of yeasts and protein preparations from China growing above 20% a year for three years;
  - Chinese export dumping of microbial protein;
  - a serious novel-food safety scandal in Asia;
  - no protein balance published by 2030;
  - no new-food procedure in force by 2028.
- **Review cycle:** at each five-year plan (2030, 2035, 2040, 2045), using the 16 signposts in `signposts_2050.csv` and the method in [[app-m2-futures-method]]. Vietnam's own foresight studies advise small, high-quality scenario workshops because statistics are thin [@VIS-45] {VN-direct|Low}. *Vietnam 2035*, written jointly by the World Bank and the planning ministry, is the precedent for a jointly authored national vision [@VIS-46] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 24.9 What this means

- **Policy makers:** the 2026 to 2030 milestones are small, cheap and mostly administrative: a statistic, a route, named sites, a budget line, a laboratory and a pilot plant. The most time-bound is the new-food clause, which has to be in the Food Safety Law before the vote considered at the May 2027 session [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. The successor livestock and fisheries strategies (before 2030) are the natural home for targets from the protein balance.
- **International bodies:** the protein balance and the review cycle are where technical help adds most. The EU's new balance-sheet method is a ready template [@VIS-15] {general|Medium}.
- **Investors:** the vision is an ingredient business first. On the benchmark path, the protein supplied in 2050 is worth roughly USD 200 million a year, at about USD 3 per kg of protein (order of magnitude only). About two-thirds of it is import substitution {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}. Regional sugar and contract fermentation come later.
- **Manufacturers:** most of the vision's volume is your purchasing. Replacing imported textured soy, gluten and isolates is the largest route. Declared blends in processed meat displace the most meat per tonne. The first steps are quotes for food-grade soy flour delivered to the plant, conditional orders from *chay* makers, and tests of extenders against the soy recipes buyers already use.
- **Startups and researchers:** the vision rewards process know-how on Vietnamese carbohydrate, side streams, strains and species, not generic platforms. A protein-quality dataset for Vietnamese ingredients is a cheap early contribution ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
