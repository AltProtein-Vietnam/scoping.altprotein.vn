# 29. What frontier actors asked, and what the answers changed

**In brief.**

- We named 80 real actors who could move alternative protein in Vietnam. From their own documents we wrote 118 decision questions, and checked each against Part III, the demand chapters.
- Part III answered one question in full and 71 in part, mostly on weak evidence. It sized channels and prices well, but did not say who decides, which rules exist, who would pay or what a deal would look like.
- Sixteen further research lines followed. The core conclusion survives: demand pulls new protein as an ingredient inside familiar foods, sold to the firms that make them.
- What changed is how to act. Schools already have plant-protein days, filled with tofu. A domestic textured-protein line is a debt-financed import-replacement project. Soy, not meat, is the price a new meat extender has to beat.
- Most label rules Part III proposed are already law, and only four of nineteen demand moves have a funder that fits and accepts Vietnam.

The corrections are folded into chapters 11 to 18 and 25, the briefs and the summaries.

## 29.2 What Part III missed: different kinds of question

The misses share a pattern. Each is a different kind of question, not a harder version of the ones Part III asked.

- **Decisions, not attitudes.** One funder looks for "an identifiable decision-maker, a reason they might move, and a window" [@AFN-05] {general|High} {dx:stated}. Part III sized canteens and schools, but did not say who writes the menus or when the rules change.
- **Rules already on the books.** Several moves Part III proposed already exist. And a new sugar tax it missed bears on plant milk.
- **Transactions, not pools.** Investors now want "contracted revenue" and "clearer unit economics" [@AIS-01; @AIS-15] {general|Medium} {dx:stated}. Part III named likely buyers, but had no price, margin or order.
- **Who pays.** Part III told funders to pay for measurement, without checking their rules.
- **Who competes.** Cheap domestic dry soy pieces already sit in the channels Part III recommended.
- **Units.** Welfare and climate funders count animals and emissions, not tonnes of protein.

## 29.3 What the answers changed

**Schools already have plant-protein days, and tofu fills them.** The old target to raise the animal share of school protein ended in 2020 [@CHN-33] {VN-direct|High}. The 2025 guidance sets no animal share and asks for legumes or tofu twice a week [@APR-04; @AIB-39] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. In 48 school-weeks we read, plant protein appeared on 15% of days, almost always tofu beside meat [@PMR-10; @PMR-31] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

The menu software still prints "animal protein at least 48%" [@PMR-26] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. So a plant ingredient there replaces tofu, not meat. Only a labelled blend in minced dishes removes meat. The rule window is the School Health Programme 2026 to 2035 [@APR-12; @AFN-44] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-school-plant-days}} ([[ch15-channels]])

**The Food Safety Law window runs into 2027.** The vote is considered for May 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}, so recommendations tied to late 2026 now have until early 2027 ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch27-policy-options]]).

**A domestic textured-protein line is an import-replacement project, not a venture bet.** Chinese, Southeast Asian and Korean protein ingredients enter duty-free [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. Chinese lines list at USD 11,000 to 100,000, and installed in Vietnam cost about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million (our calculation) [@TIC-02; @TIC-03; @TIC-07] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Whether a line pays depends on the delivered price of food-grade soy flour, which we could not verify [@TIC-12; @TIC-13] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The benchmark path supports about one line by 2035, best owned by a chay maker, co-packer or distributor and financed with bank debt. {{kn:kn-fta-duty-china}} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]])

**Soy, not meat, is the price a new extender has to beat.** Replacing a fifth of the lean meat in *chả lụa* (pork roll) with soy saved money at every pork price from 2019 to 2024 (our calculation) [@DIE-25; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. So a new extender must replace more meat unnoticed than soy, or carry a better label. No standard sets a minimum meat content, and buyers punish hidden filler, not a declared ingredient [@LBL-11; @LBL-12; @LBL-13; @LBL-06; @LBL-07; @LBL-08; @TRU-02; @TRU-03; @TRU-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

**Most label moves are already law:** the high-protein claim, the vegetarian standard, the absence of reserved meat and milk words, and a GMO label only above 5% [@LBL-03; @LBL-15; @LBL-06; @LBL-10] {VN-direct|High}. What remains is enforcement, one definition (unqualified chay should mean vegan) and one ruling on proteins made by microbes ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

**A sugar tax may reach plant milks** from 2027; dairy is exempt [@FTR-01; @FTR-05] {VN-direct|High}. New plant milks should stay at or below 5 g of sugar per 100 ml ([[ch13-consumers]]).

**Store brands are a route to the shelf; corporate pledges add no volume.** A named co-packer makes Co.op's chay range, so a protein upgrade needs no consumer brand [@CPC-15; @AIB-28] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The one corporate pledge that binds Vietnamese units counts dishes, not protein [@AFN-40; @AFN-18; @AFN-21] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed} ([[ch15-channels]], [[ch16-business-buyers]]).

**The export base is wrapped foods,** sold to importers and brand owners who need European food-safety certificates [@XBA-29; @XBA-28] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. EU origin rules bind on the flour in the wrapper, not the soy in the filling, so they give domestic textured soy no export customer [@ORG-01] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

**For school-age children, protein is not the gap.** Stunting is a problem of the youngest children [@APR-27] {VN-direct|High}. In 13 upland boarding schools, lunches and dinners almost always had meat, fish or egg; breakfasts mostly did not [@UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Weighed diets met protein and zinc needs but fell short on calcium and vitamin A [@UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed} ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).

**Only four of nineteen demand moves have a payer.** The funders' own rules explain why. The main market-shaping funder excludes diet-change work and prioritises Japan and Korea in Asia [@AFN-05] {general|High} {dx:stated}. A regional diet-change fund has no Vietnam guide, and the leading animal charity evaluator's grants now exclude diet change [@AFN-29; @FUF-01] {general|Medium}. On the benchmark path, the routes spare about 2.5 million animals a year by 2035, nearly all chickens and ducks; import replacement spares none (our estimate) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-funder-fit}} {{kn:kn-animals-spared-2035}} ([[ch18-demand-sizing]])

**The regional firm record favours familiar foods sold to other firms.** Of 24 alternative-protein firms in Vietnam and the region since 2019, 7 are active, 4 pivoted, 2 exited and 11 show no recent activity. None of the four Vietnamese meat-copy makers is active, while all three makers of familiar foods are [@AIS-28; @BRD-08] {VN-adjacent|Low} {dx:revealed}. No founder has yet tried selling an ingredient to established food firms, so that route is the hypothesis to test.

## 29.4 What each actor should take away

- **Funders and field-builders:** fund what fits your unit and rules. Welfare funders: canteen menus, with Humane World, which trained chefs in Vietnam in 2025 [@AFN-23] {VN-direct|Medium}. Market-shaping funders: canteen buying and the naming and GMO rules. Research funders: ingredient function and Vietnamese taste tests. National survey budgets should carry the chay-day question ([[brief-international]]).
- **Investors:** treat domestic textured protein as a debt-financed import-replacement project, and drop it if soy flour is too dear. Price blends against soy ([[brief-investors]]).
- **Startups:** sell a familiar food with little capital, as an ingredient, store brand or co-packed product, with a chay maker or distributor as co-owner or anchor buyer ([[brief-startups]]).
- **Manufacturers and retailers:** you already own the cheapest extender and the store-brand route. Test a protein upgrade through a co-packer, and keep plant milks low in sugar ([[brief-manufacturers]]).
- **Policy makers:** use the School Health Programme to put protein quality, zinc and iron into the menu software. Enforce existing label rules. Rule on plant milks and the sugar tax before 2027 ([[brief-policy]]).
- **Researchers:** measure protein quality and zinc in tofu, textured soy and blends. Run a blind test of a fungal extender against soy at equal saving ([[brief-research]]).

## 29.5 What desk research could not answer

The questions that now decide most need a call, a quote, a trial or bought data, not more reading.

| Question | Why it matters | Who can answer |
|---|---|---|
| The delivered price of food-grade soy flour | Decides whether a textured-protein line pays | Five flour suppliers, through a buyer |
| Would three chay makers sign conditional orders at the Chinese import price? | Turns demand into orders | Chay makers and distributors |
| Is the menu software's 48% standard a default or a hard check? | Decides whether a plant ingredient can be written into school menus | The education ministry, the software makers, the nutrition institute |
| Are sweet soy and nut milks soft drinks under the new tax? | Sets plant-milk prices from 2027 | Ministry of Finance |
| Would Saigon Co.op list a 10 g-protein version of a store-brand chay dish? | Tests the cheapest route to the shelf | Co.op's store-brand team |
| Does a fungal extender beat the processor's soy recipe at equal saving? | Decides whether new extenders have a job | Two processors and a taste laboratory |
| Who would fund a Vietnamese canteen trial? | The welfare route has no payer today | Humane World, Coefficient Giving, the Navigation Fund |

## 29.6 Where we stopped

We stopped when desk research stopped changing conclusions. In the first two waves, almost every research line changed something. In the third, none changed a conclusion. The remaining questions need methods other than desk research. As before, new findings came from asking different kinds of question, not the same question harder.
