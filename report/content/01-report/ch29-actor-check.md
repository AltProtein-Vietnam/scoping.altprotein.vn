# 29. What frontier actors asked, and what the answers changed

**In brief.**

- We named 80 real actors who could move alternative protein in Vietnam, in four groups: funders, investors and startups, first buyers, and policy and research bodies. From their own documents we wrote 118 decision questions and checked each against Part III, the demand chapters (11 to 18).
- Part III answered one question in full and 71 in part, and the evidence was mostly weak or missing. It sized channels and prices well, but did not say who decides, which rules exist, who would pay, who competes or what a deal would look like.
- We then ran sixteen research lines in three waves, each asking a different kind of question. We stopped when the next candidates would add citations without changing a decision.
- The core conclusion survives: demand pulls new protein as an ingredient inside familiar foods, sold to the firms that already make them.
- What changed is how to act. Schools already have plant-protein days, and tofu fills them. A domestic textured-protein line is a debt-financed import-substitution project with no tariff shelter. Soy is the price a new meat extender, an ingredient that replaces part of the meat, has to beat.
- Most of the label rules Part III proposed are already law, and a sugar tax may reach sweetened plant milks from 2027. Only four of the original nineteen demand moves have a funder whose rules fit and that accepts Vietnam.

The method, the yield of each line and the reasons for stopping are in [[app-m4-actor-check-waves]]. We folded the corrections into chapters 11 to 18 and 25, the briefs and the executive summaries.

The evidence was thin. Across all 118 questions it was moderate for 27, weak for 49 and absent for 42; none was strong. Of the 72 questions answered in full or in part, 26 had moderate evidence, 41 weak and 5 none (our assessment) {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

## 29.2 What Part III missed: different kinds of question

The misses share a pattern. Each is a different kind of question from the ones Part III asked, not a harder version of them.

- **Decisions, not attitudes.** One market-shaping funder, Nova Vista, counts "an identifiable decision-maker, a reason they might move, and a window" [@AFN-05] {general|High} {dx:stated}. Part III sized canteens and schools but did not say who writes the menus, which rules govern them or when those rules change.
- **Rules already on the books.** Several moves Part III proposed already exist: school guidance without an animal share, percentage labels on blends, and definitions of *chay* (Vietnamese vegetarian food). A new tax it did not see, the sugary-drink excise, bears on its one proven modern category, plant milk.
- **Transactions, not pools.** Investors now favour "strong offtake agreements" and "contracted revenue" [@AIS-01] and "clearer unit economics" [@AIS-15] {general|Medium} {dx:stated}. An offtake agreement is a contract to buy future output. Part III named likely first buyers from labels and trade data, but had no price, cost, margin or order.
- **Who pays.** Part III told funders to pay for measurement, without checking whose rules allow it.
- **Who competes.** Part III framed competition as Chinese commodity protein. In fact, regional suppliers and cheap domestic dry soy pieces already sit inside the channels it recommends.
- **Units.** Welfare and climate funders count animals and carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e). Part III counted tonnes of protein.

## 29.3 What the answers changed

Sixteen research lines followed, listed in `expansion_lines.csv`. These are the findings that change what an actor should do.

**Schools already have plant-protein days, and tofu fills them.** Part III said school meals follow an old target to raise the animal share of children's protein. That target (Decision 1340, 2016) ended in 2020 [@CHN-33] {VN-direct|High}. The Ministry of Health's school-meal guidance of December 2025 sets no animal share and asks for legumes or tofu at least twice a week [@APR-04; @AIB-39] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. We looked at 48 published school-weeks from Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi and Hai Phong. Plant protein appeared on 15.1% of days and 9 weeks met the rule (our audit, `school_menu_weeks.csv`); tofu was served beside meat, and no dish used textured soy or mock meat [@PMR-10; @PMR-31] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

The menu software schools use still prints an "animal protein at least 48%" standard [@PMR-26] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. So a plant ingredient in the plant slot competes with tofu and replaces tofu, not meat. The only school route that removes meat is a labelled blend in minced-meat and fish-cake dishes (our reading of the menu audit, `school_menu_weeks.csv`) [@PMR-10; @PMR-31]. The rule window is the School Health Programme 2026 to 2035 and the menu tools that follow it [@APR-12; @AFN-44] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-school-plant-days}} ([[ch15-channels]])

**The Food Safety Law window runs into 2027.** The National Assembly takes first comments in October 2026 and considers passage in May 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Every supply-side and demand-side recommendation tied to "the October to November 2026 session" now has until early 2027 ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch27-policy-options]]).

**A domestic textured-protein line is an import-substitution project, not a venture bet.** Textured protein, isolates and gluten from China, other ASEAN countries and Korea enter Vietnam duty-free. The regional trade agreements that allow this are ASEAN's goods agreement, ASEAN's agreements with China and Korea, and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. Chinese textured-soy lines are listed at USD 11,000 to 100,000 [@TIC-02; @TIC-03; @TIC-07] {general|Low}. Installed in Vietnam, a line costs about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million, and working capital is the larger need (our calculation, `t1_margin_payback.csv`) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Whether it pays depends on the delivered price of food-grade soy flour, which we could not verify [@TIC-12; @TIC-13] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}.

The benchmark demand path supports about one line by 2035. The natural owner is a chay maker, co-packer or distributor that already has buyers, financed by bank debt and trade finance. A co-packer is a firm that makes products for other brands. {{kn:kn-fta-duty-china}} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]])

**Soy, not meat, is the price a new extender has to beat.** Replacing a fifth of the lean meat in *chả lụa* (Vietnamese pork roll) with hydrated textured soy saved money at every annual hog price from 2019 to 2024. This is our calculation from FAOSTAT hog prices and the Comtrade unit value of textured protein [@DIE-25; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. So processors already own the cheapest extender. A fungal or other novel extender has to replace more meat unnoticed than soy, or carry a label buyers prefer.

No national standard sets a minimum meat content. A named meat must already have its amount stated on the label under Decree 43/2017, a rule Decree 37/2026 probably carries over [@LBL-11; @LBL-12; @LBL-13; @LBL-06; @LBL-07; @LBL-08] {VN-direct|High}. Buyers punish hidden cost-cutting, called *độn* (filler), not a declared ingredient [@TRU-02; @TRU-03; @TRU-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

**Most of the label moves are already law.**

- A high-protein claim needs 10 g per 100 g, or 5 g per 100 kcal, under a voluntary standard based on Codex, the international food code [@LBL-03] {VN-direct|High}.
- The national vegetarian standard allows egg, milk and honey [@LBL-15] {VN-direct|High}.
- No rule reserves words such as "meat" or "milk" for animal products [@LBL-06].
- The label for genetically modified organisms (GMO) applies only above 5% of ingredients, so low-dose uses such as lactoferrin (a milk protein) are not caught [@LBL-10] {VN-direct|Medium}.

What remains is enforcement, one definition (chay with no qualifier should mean vegan) and one agency ruling on recombinant proteins, made by engineered microbes ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

**A sugar tax may reach plant milks.** From 2027 drinks above 5 g sugar per 100 ml pay an 8% excise tax (10% from 2028), and dairy is exempt [@FTR-01; @FTR-05] {VN-direct|High}. Whether soy and nut milks are in scope is not yet settled. New plant milks, and the protein isolates that go into them, should be designed at or below 5 g of sugar per 100 ml ([[ch13-consumers]]).

**Retailers' own labels are a route to the shelf; corporate pledges do not add volume.** Saigon Co.op's Co.op Select chay line is made by a named co-packer, so a protein upgrade can reach the shelf without a consumer brand [@CPC-15; @AIB-28] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Only one published corporate commitment binds Vietnamese units: Accor's 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030, which counts dishes, not protein. We found no Vietnamese protein buyer with a published target [@AFN-40; @AFN-18; @AFN-21] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed} ([[ch15-channels]], [[ch16-business-buyers]]).

**Feed demand exists but is priced low.** Vietnam imported about 9,200 t of inactive yeast (food and feed together) in 2025, 95% of it at about USD 1.1 per kg [@FBA-17] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. This finding pointed to play T2 (microbial feed ingredients for shrimp and pangasius), which was retired because feed is now context only ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

**The export base is wrapped foods.** Vietnam exported USD 128 million of dumplings and other stuffed pasta in 2023, against almost no textured protein [@XBA-29; @XBA-28] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. European retailers' protein targets are flat and met with European products. The buyers are importers and brand owners who need IFS or BRCGS food-safety certification.

Dumplings fall in chapter 19 of the harmonised system of tariff codes. Rules of origin decide whether a product counts as Vietnamese for tariff purposes. For dumplings, they bind on the imported flour in the wrapper, not on the soy in the filling [@ORG-01] {VN-direct|High}. So these rules give domestic textured soy no export customer ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

**For school-age children, protein is not the gap.** Stunting (being too short for one's age) is 18% among children under five (2023) and 32% among ethnic minority children [@APR-27] {VN-direct|High}. But it is a problem of the youngest children. We audited published menus from 13 upland boarding schools. Lunches and dinners carried meat, fish or egg in 240 of 242 meals, while 95 of 111 breakfasts had little or no animal food (our menu audit, `upland_school_weeks.csv`) [@UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}.

Where boarding diets were weighed, they met protein and zinc needs but fell short on calcium and vitamin A [@UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. A plant product is not the answer to a school protein gap that does not exist ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).

**Only four of the original nineteen demand moves have a payer.** The funders' own rules explain why:

- A new USD 10 million market-shaping request for proposals excludes whole-food plant-based work, culture change and individual diet change. It wants more policy staff, especially in high-innovation Asian economies such as Japan and Korea [@AFN-05] {general|High} {dx:stated}.
- The 2026 regional diet-change fund published no Vietnam application guide [@AFN-29] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- The leading animal charity evaluator's grants now exclude diet change [@FUF-01] {general|Medium}.

In the units funders count, the benchmark path spares about 2.4 to 2.7 million animals a year by 2035, nearly all of them chickens and ducks. It also avoids about 0.14 Mt CO2e, while import substitution spares no animals (our estimate) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-funder-fit}} {{kn:kn-animals-spared-2035}} ([[ch18-demand-sizing]])

**The regional firm record favours familiar foods sold to others.** Our register holds 24 alternative-protein firms in Vietnam and the region since 2019 (`firm_outcomes.csv`, our calculation). Of these, 7 are active, 4 pivoted (three of them towards private label, co-manufacturing or non-food), 2 exited and 11 are dormant (no news found, not proof of closure). None of the 4 Vietnam-based makers of meat analogues (products that copy meat) is active, while all 3 Vietnam-founded makers of traditional formats are {VN-adjacent|Low} {dx:revealed}. One dormant firm has an unknown first channel, so the channel rows in [[app-d4-channels]] sum to 23 firms and 10 dormant.

TiNDLE's move to private label [@AIS-28] and Karana's regional expansion [@BRD-08] are examples, not the source of the counts. In our register, no founder has yet tried selling an ingredient to established food firms, so that route is the hypothesis to test.

> **Correction.** In v0.6 this paragraph said all four pivots moved towards private label, co-manufacturing or non-food, and cited two news items for the firm counts; the counts come from our register, where three of the four pivots moved that way (Eat Just relaunched a retail product under its own brand).

## 29.4 What each actor should take away

Each takeaway is about protein for people. The feed finding in section 29.3 no longer leads to an action.

- **Funders and field-builders:** fund what fits your unit and your rules.
  - Welfare funders: canteen menus with Humane World, which trained chefs and nutritionists in Viet Nam in 2025 [@AFN-23] {VN-direct|Medium}.
  - Market-shaping funders: getting canteens to buy a protein ingredient, and the naming and GMO rules.
  - Research funders: how ingredients perform in food, and Vietnamese sensory (taste and texture) tests.
  - National survey budgets, not philanthropy, should carry the survey question on chay days. See [[brief-international]].
- **Investors:** treat domestic textured protein as an import-substitution project financed with debt. Drop it if the delivered soy-flour price is too high (the kill test), and price blends against soy. Pitch import substitution to development finance on the import bill, not on climate. See [[brief-investors]].
- **Startups:** sell a familiar food with little capital. Aim first to supply ingredients, private-label products or co-packing, and bring in a chay maker or distributor as co-owner or anchor buyer. See [[brief-startups]].
- **Manufacturers and retailers:** you already own the cheapest extender and the private-label route. Test a protein upgrade through a co-packer, and design plant milks at or below 5 g sugar per 100 ml. See [[brief-manufacturers]].
- **Policy makers:** use the School Health Programme to put protein quality, zinc and iron (not an animal share) into the menu software. Enforce the label rules that exist. Rule on plant milks and the excise before 2027. See [[brief-policy]].
- **Research bodies:** measure protein quality and zinc in tofu, textured soy and blends. Run a blind taste test of a fungal extender against soy, at the same cost saving. See [[brief-research]].

## 29.5 What the desk could not answer

The questions that now decide most need a call, a quote, a trial or bought data, not more reading. The full list is in [[app-r1-open-questions]] (open questions OQ-270 onwards) and [[app-m4-actor-check-waves]].

| Question | Why it matters | Who can answer |
|---|---|---|
| The delivered price of food-grade defatted soy flour | Decides whether a domestic textured-protein line pays | Five flour suppliers, through a partner buyer |
| Would three chay makers sign conditional orders at the price of Chinese imports? | Turns demand for play T1 (domestic textured plant protein) into orders | Chay makers and ingredient distributors |
| Is the menu software's 48% animal-protein standard a default or a hard check, and does its food list include textured soy? | Decides whether a plant ingredient can be written into school menus | MOET, Ajinomoto, National Institute of Nutrition |
| Are sweetened soy and nut milks *nước giải khát* (soft drinks) under the excise? | Sets plant-milk prices from 2027 | Ministry of Finance, Tax Policy Department |
| Would Saigon Co.op list a 10 g per 100 g version of a private-label chay dish? | Tests the cheapest route to the shelf | Co.op private-label team with its co-packer |
| Does a fungal extender beat the processor's own soy recipe at the same cost saving? | Decides whether novel extenders have a job | Two processors and a sensory laboratory |
| Who would fund a Vietnamese canteen trial? | The welfare route has no payer today | Humane World Viet Nam, Coefficient Giving, the Navigation Fund |

One further question was retired because feed is now context only. It asked which mills import feed yeast, and whether they would trial a Vietnamese product (the buyers for play T2).

## 29.6 Where we stopped

We stopped when desk research stopped changing conclusions. Wave 7 ran six research lines, and all six changed something. Wave 8 ran six, and five did. Wave 9 ran four: none changed a conclusion in Part III, one corrected a wave 8 proposal, one added a constraint and two gave clear negative answers.

Every remaining candidate was either a deeper version of a question already answered, or needed a method other than desk research. As in earlier rounds, new findings came from asking different kinds of question, not the same question harder. Details are in [[app-m4-actor-check-waves]].
