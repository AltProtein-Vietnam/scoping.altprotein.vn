# 29. What frontier actors asked, and what the answers changed

**In one paragraph.** Part III was written for the people who could move alternative protein in Vietnam, so we asked whether it answers their questions. We named 80 real actors in four groups, read what each says it funds, buys, builds or regulates, turned that into 118 decision questions and checked each against Part III. It answered one in full and 71 in part; for most, the evidence behind the answer was weak or missing. The gaps were of a kind: Part III sized channels and prices well but did not map who decides, which rules already exist, who would pay, who already competes, or what a deal would look like. We then ran sixteen research lines in three waves, each asking a different kind of question, and stopped when the next candidates would add citations without changing a decision. The core conclusion survives: demand pulls new protein as an ingredient inside familiar foods, sold to incumbents. What changed is how to act on it: schools already reserve plant-protein days that tofu fills; a domestic textured-protein line is a debt-financed import-substitution project with no tariff shelter; soy is the price a new extender must beat; most of the label rules Part III proposed are already law; a sugar tax may reach sweetened plant milks from 2027; and only four of the original nineteen demand moves have a funder whose rules fit and that accepts Vietnam.

The method, the yield of each line and the reasons for stopping are in [[app-m4-actor-check-waves]]. The corrections are folded into chapters 11 to 18 and 25, the briefs and the executive summaries.

Across all 118 questions the evidence was moderate for 27, weak for 49 and absent for 42; none was strong. Of the 72 answered in full or in part, 26 had moderate evidence, 41 weak and 5 none (our assessment) {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

## 29.2 What Part III missed

The misses share a pattern. Each is a different kind of question from the ones Part III asked, not a harder version of them.

- **Decisions, not attitudes.** One market-shaping funder, Nova Vista, counts "an identifiable decision-maker, a reason they might move, and a window" [@AFN-05] {general|High} {dx:stated}. Part III sized canteens and schools but did not say who writes the menus, which rules govern them or when those rules change.
- **Rules already on the books.** Several moves Part III proposed (school guidance without an animal share, percentage labels on blends, chay definitions) turned out to exist already, and a new tax it did not see (the sugary-drink excise) bears on its one proven modern category.
- **Transactions, not pools.** Investors now favour "strong offtake agreements" and "contracted revenue" [@AIS-01] and "clearer unit economics" [@AIS-15] {general|Medium} {dx:stated}. Part III named likely first buyers from labels and trade data but had no price, cost, margin or order.
- **Who pays.** Part III told funders to fund measurement, without checking whose rules allow it.
- **Who competes.** Part III framed competition as Chinese commodity protein; regional suppliers and cheap domestic dry soy pieces already sit inside the channels it recommends.
- **Units.** Welfare and climate funders count animals and CO2e; Part III counted tonnes of protein.

## 29.3 What the answers changed

Sixteen lines followed (listed in `expansion_lines.csv`). These are the findings that change what an actor should do.

**Schools already have plant-protein days, and tofu fills them.** Part III said school meals follow an old target to raise the animal share of children's protein. That target (Decision 1340, 2016) ended in 2020 [@CHN-33] {VN-direct|High}. The Ministry of Health's school-meal guidance of December 2025 sets no animal share and asks for legumes or tofu at least twice a week [@APR-04; @AIB-39] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. In 48 published school-weeks from Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi and Hai Phong, plant protein appeared on 15.1% of days, 9 weeks met the rule (our audit, `school_menu_weeks.csv`), tofu was served beside meat, and no dish used textured soy or mock meat [@PMR-10; @PMR-31] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. The menu software schools use still prints an "animal protein at least 48%" standard [@PMR-26] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. So a plant ingredient in the plant slot competes with tofu and replaces tofu, not meat; the only school route that removes meat is a labelled blend in minced-meat and fish-cake dishes (our reading of the menu audit, `school_menu_weeks.csv`) [@PMR-10; @PMR-31]; and the rule window is the School Health Programme 2026 to 2035 and the menu tools that follow it [@APR-12; @AFN-44] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. {{kn:kn-school-plant-days}} ([[ch15-channels]])

**The Food Safety Law window runs into 2027.** The National Assembly takes first comments in October 2026 and considers passage in May 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Every supply-side and demand-side recommendation tied to "the October to November 2026 session" now has until early 2027 ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch27-policy-options]]).

**A domestic textured-protein line is an import-substitution project, not a venture thesis.** Chinese, ASEAN and Korean textured protein, isolates and gluten enter duty-free under ACFTA, ATIGA, AKFTA and RCEP [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. Chinese textured-soy lines are listed at USD 11,000 to 100,000 [@TIC-02; @TIC-03; @TIC-07] {general|Low}; installed in Vietnam a line costs about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million, and working capital is the larger need (our calculation, `t1_margin_payback.csv`) {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. Whether it pays turns on the delivered price of food-grade soy flour, which we could not verify [@TIC-12; @TIC-13] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The benchmark path supports about one line by 2035. The natural owner is a chay maker, co-packer or distributor with buyers, financed by bank debt and trade finance. {{kn:kn-fta-duty-china}} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]])

**Soy, not meat, is the price a new extender must beat.** Replacing a fifth of the lean in *chả lụa* (Vietnamese pork roll) with hydrated textured soy saved money at every annual hog price from 2019 to 2024 (our calculation from FAOSTAT hog prices and the Comtrade unit value of textured protein), so processors already own the cheapest extender [@DIE-25; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. A fungal or novel extender must replace more meat unnoticed than soy, or carry a label buyers prefer. No national standard sets a minimum meat content, and a named meat must already be quantified on the label under Decree 43/2017, a rule Decree 37/2026 probably carries over [@LBL-11; @LBL-12; @LBL-13; @LBL-06; @LBL-07; @LBL-08] {VN-direct|High}. What buyers punish is hidden cost-cutting, called *độn* (filler), not a declared ingredient [@TRU-02; @TRU-03; @TRU-22] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

**Most of the label moves are already law.** A high-protein claim needs 10 g per 100 g, or 5 g per 100 kcal, under a voluntary Codex-based standard [@LBL-03] {VN-direct|High}; the national vegetarian standard allows egg, milk and honey [@LBL-15] {VN-direct|High}; no rule reserves the words for meat or milk [@LBL-06]; and the GMO label applies only above 5% of ingredients, so lactoferrin-type uses are not caught [@LBL-10] {VN-direct|Medium}. The remaining asks are enforcement, one definition (unqualified chay should mean vegan) and one agency ruling on recombinant proteins ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

**A sugar tax may reach plant milks.** From 2027 drinks above 5 g sugar per 100 ml pay an 8% excise (10% from 2028) and dairy is exempt [@FTR-01; @FTR-05] {VN-direct|High}. Whether soy and nut milks are in scope is unresolved. New plant milks and the isolates that go into them should be designed at or below 5 g ([[ch13-consumers]]).

**Retailers' own labels are a route to shelf; corporate pledges are not a volume lever.** Saigon Co.op's Co.op Select chay line is made by a named co-packer, so a protein upgrade can reach shelf without a consumer brand [@CPC-15; @AIB-28] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Only one published corporate commitment binds Vietnamese units (Accor's 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030, which counts dishes, not protein), and we found no Vietnamese protein buyer with a published target [@AFN-40; @AFN-18; @AFN-21] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed} ([[ch15-channels]], [[ch16-business-buyers]]).

**Feed demand is revealed but priced low.** Vietnam imported about 9,200 t of inactive yeast (food and feed together) in 2025, 95% of it at about USD 1.1 per kg, while the ASC feed standard neither requires nor rewards microbial protein and the BAP feed-mill standard only lets aquatic-microorganism meal count towards its marine-ingredient target [@FBA-17; @FBA-01; @FBA-05] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. A Vietnamese microbial feed ingredient must win head to head against the yeast mills already buy ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

**The export base is wrapped foods.** Vietnam exported USD 128 million of dumplings and other stuffed pasta in 2023, against almost no textured protein [@XBA-29; @XBA-28] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. European retailers' protein targets are flat and met with European products; the buyers are importers and brand owners who require IFS or BRCGS certification. For dumplings (Chapter 19), rules of origin bind on imported flour in the wrapper, not on the soy in the filling, so they give domestic textured soy no export customer [@ORG-01] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

**For school-age children protein is not the gap.** Stunting among children under five is 18% (2023) and 32% among ethnic minority children [@APR-27] {VN-direct|High}, but it is a problem of the youngest children. In 13 upland boarding schools, lunches and dinners carried meat, fish or egg in 240 of 242 published meals, while 95 of 111 breakfasts had little or no animal food (our menu audit, `upland_school_weeks.csv`) [@UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Weighed boarding diets met protein and zinc but fell short on calcium and vitamin A [@UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. A plant product is not the answer to a school protein gap that does not exist ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).

**Only four of the original nineteen demand moves have a payer.** A new USD 10 million market-shaping request for proposals excludes whole-food plant-based work, culture change and individual diet change, and wants more policy staff especially in high-innovation Asian economies such as Japan and Korea [@AFN-05] {general|High} {dx:stated}; the 2026 regional diet-change fund published no Vietnam application guide [@AFN-29] {VN-direct|Medium}; the leading animal charity evaluator's grants now exclude diet change [@FUF-01] {general|Medium}. In funder units, the benchmark path spares about 2.4 to 2.7 million animals a year by 2035, nearly all chickens and ducks, and avoids about 0.14 Mt CO2e; import substitution spares none (our estimate) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}. {{kn:kn-funder-fit}} {{kn:kn-animals-spared-2035}} ([[ch18-demand-sizing]])

**The regional firm record favours familiar foods sold to others.** Of 24 alternative-protein firms in Vietnam and the region since 2019 in our register (`firm_outcomes.csv`, our calculation), 7 are active, 4 pivoted (three of them towards private label, co-manufacturing or non-food), 2 exited and 11 are dormant (no news found, not proof of closure); none of the 4 Vietnam-based meat-analogue makers is active, while all 3 Vietnam-founded makers of traditional formats are {VN-adjacent|Low} {dx:revealed}. One dormant firm has an unknown first channel, so the channel rows in [[app-d4-channels]] sum to 23 firms and 10 dormant. TiNDLE's move to private label [@AIS-28] and Karana's regional expansion [@BRD-08] are examples, not the source of the counts. No founder has yet tried selling an ingredient to incumbents, so that route is the hypothesis to test.

> **Correction.** In v0.6 this paragraph said all four pivots moved towards private label, co-manufacturing or non-food, and cited two news items for the firm counts; the counts come from our register, where three of the four pivots moved that way (Eat Just relaunched a retail product under its own brand).

## 29.4 What each actor should take away

- **Funders and field-builders:** fund what fits your unit and your rules. Welfare funders: canteen menus with Humane World, which trained chefs and nutritionists in Viet Nam in 2025 [@AFN-23] {VN-direct|Medium}. Market-shaping funders: procurement of a canteen protein ingredient and the naming and GMO rules. R&D funders: ingredient function and Vietnamese sensory tests. National survey budgets, not philanthropy, should carry the chay-day survey question. See [[brief-international]].
- **Investors:** treat domestic textured protein as debt-financed import substitution with a flour-price kill test, and price blends against soy. Pitch import substitution to development finance on the import bill, not on climate. See [[brief-investors]].
- **Startups:** sell a familiar food with little capital, aim first for ingredient, private-label or co-packing supply, and bring a chay maker or distributor in as co-owner or anchor buyer. See [[brief-startups]].
- **Manufacturers and retailers:** you already own the cheapest extender and the private-label route; test a protein upgrade through a co-packer, and design plant milks at or below 5 g sugar. See [[brief-manufacturers]].
- **Policy makers:** use the School Health Programme to put protein quality, zinc and iron (not an animal share) into the menu software; enforce the label rules that exist; rule on plant milks and the excise before 2027. See [[brief-policy]].
- **Research bodies:** measure protein quality and zinc in tofu, textured soy and blends; run an equal-saving blind test of a fungal extender against soy. See [[brief-research]].

## 29.5 What the desk could not answer

The questions that now decide most need a call, a quote, a trial or purchased data, not more reading. The full list is in [[app-r1-open-questions]] (OQ-270 onwards) and [[app-m4-actor-check-waves]].

| Question | Why it matters | Who can answer |
|---|---|---|
| The delivered price of food-grade defatted soy flour | Decides whether a domestic textured-protein line pays | Five flour suppliers, through a partner buyer |
| Would three chay makers sign conditional orders at Chinese parity? | Turns the T1 market pull into offtake | Chay makers and ingredient distributors |
| Is the menu software's 48% animal-protein standard a default or a hard check, and does its food list include textured soy? | Decides whether a plant ingredient can be written into school menus | MOET, Ajinomoto, National Institute of Nutrition |
| Are sweetened soy and nut milks *nước giải khát* under the excise? | Sets plant-milk prices from 2027 | Ministry of Finance, Tax Policy Department |
| Would Saigon Co.op list a 10 g per 100 g version of a private-label chay dish? | Tests the cheapest route to shelf | Co.op private-label team with its co-packer |
| Does a fungal extender beat the processor's own soy recipe at equal saving? | Decides whether novel extenders have a job | Two processors and a sensory laboratory |
| Who would fund a Vietnamese canteen trial? | The welfare route has no payer today | Humane World Viet Nam, Coefficient Giving, the Navigation Fund |
| Which mills import feed yeast, and would they trial a Vietnamese product against it? | Names T2's buyers | Customs declaration data; mill nutritionists |

## 29.6 Where we stopped

Wave 7 ran six lines and all six changed something. Wave 8 ran six and five did. Wave 9 ran four: none changed a conclusion in Part III, one corrected a wave 8 proposal, one added a constraint and two were clean negatives. Every remaining candidate was either a deeper version of a question already answered or needed a method other than desk research. The signal came, as in earlier rounds, from asking different kinds of question rather than the same question harder. Details are in [[app-m4-actor-check-waves]].
