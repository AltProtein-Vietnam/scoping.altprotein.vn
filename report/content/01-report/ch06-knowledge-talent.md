# 6. Knowledge, talent and shared infrastructure

**In brief.**
- Vietnam is not starting from zero in the sciences behind alternative protein, but it is starting from almost zero in turning them into food.
- Its labs express proteins in yeast, culture stem cells, engineer koji mould and grow microalgae. They publish almost nothing on texturing plant protein, fungal protein, precision fermentation or food cell lines.
- Vietnam's research output on novel foods is about a quarter to a third of Thailand's [@HSC-01] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- The binding constraint is shared infrastructure: we found no open pilot fermenter, no food extruder and no laboratory offering the protein-quality tests a novel-food application needs.
- Training and research money are thin. We found no alternative-protein course in Vietnam, and a typical national science grant was about USD 36,500 in 2023 [@VCO-21; @SLW-03; @SLW-01] {VN-direct|High}.

## 6.1 Research output: small in novel food, regional-level in algae and aquafeed

We counted Vietnam-affiliated publications from 2015 to September 2026 and compared them with Southeast Asian peers. All counts are lower bounds.

**Reading.** Vietnam's weakness is specific to novel food technologies. In algae and fish feed it is at the regional level. Vietnam has **zero or near-zero** published work on precision fermentation, fungal protein, fish cell lines for food and cultivated meat [@BIB-01; @RD-43] {VN-direct|Medium}. About six papers address plant-based meat [@BIB-01; @RD-26; @RD-27] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Two groups stand out. The Industrial University of Ho Chi Minh City (IUH) published two meat-analogue papers in 2026 [@TIC-21; @SLW-18; @SLW-02] {VN-direct|Low}. And a university lab in Ho Chi Minh City reported culturing beef cells in 2022, though with no peer-reviewed paper [@SLC-01] {VN-direct|Low}.

**No Vietnamese study of protein quality scores has been found.** Some related safety and digestibility work exists, but none on a new microbial protein [@RD-31; @RD-61; @RD-23] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 6.2 Five adjacent strengths

1. **Fish and shrimp feed trials** (Can Tho University and others). They show a working research base in applied nutrition [@RD-46; @RD-47; @RD-48] {VN-direct|Medium}. But feed is context only in this study.
2. **Making proteins in yeast** at the University of Science, Ho Chi Minh City. The work includes growth factors used to grow cultivated meat, but no food proteins yet [@RD-36; @RD-37; @RD-38; @RD-35] {VN-direct|Medium}.
3. **Stem-cell culture** (Vinmec), including tests of growth media without animal serum [@RD-39; @RD-40] {VN-direct|Medium}.
4. **Genetic tools for koji mould** at the University of Science, Hanoi [@RD-33; @RD-34] {VN-direct|Medium}.
5. **Microalgae growing** at the Vietnam Academy of Science and Technology, including a spirulina strain with 48.7% protein [@RD-21] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Two more assets are under-used. Hanoi University of Science and Technology (HUST) has recovered 90% of the protein from rice distillers' grain, and made protein products from spent brewer's yeast [@RD-22; @RD-23; @RD-32] {VN-direct|Medium}. And Vietnamese researchers hold over 100 wild duckweed samples, with no protein work yet [@RD-44; @RD-71] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 6.3 Research gaps Vietnam is well placed to fill

Each gap combines a Vietnamese raw material, species or skill with no published work:

1. Food-grade cell lines from catfish and shrimp [@SCI-10; @SCI-12] {general|Medium}.
2. Low-cost growth factors made in yeast, tested on fish cells.
3. Fungal protein from cassava pulp, with a food safety package (cyanide, mould toxins, digestibility).
4. Vietnamese koji strains from *tương* (fermented soybean paste) as protein producers, with the safety data regulators will ask for [@SCI-19] {general|High}.
5. Texturing protein from rice distillers' grain and broken rice.
6. Duckweed protein for food, with manganese and heavy-metal checks.
7. Protein ingredients from salt-tolerant spirulina.
8. Seaweed protein for food.
9. Bacterial protein from biogas at pig farms and starch factories.
10. **A protein-quality dataset for Vietnamese ingredients** such as mung bean, rice and spirulina. Values measured abroad may not transfer: mung bean digestibility measured in children was about 20% lower than in pigs [@SCI-40; @SCI-41; @SCI-42] {general|High}.

## 6.4 Shared infrastructure: the binding constraint

- **Pilot fermentation.** We found no Vietnamese facility offering an open, food-grade pilot fermentation service [@INF-02; @INF-04; @INF-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Extrusion.** We found no food extruder at a Vietnamese university or institute [@RD-01; @INF-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. IUH's 2026 work suggests checking there first [@TIC-21] {VN-direct|Low}. Vietnam published 3 papers on texturing protein from 2015 to 2026, against Thailand's 31 [@SLP-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. A Thai public centre has extruded mung bean protein [@SLP-06] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. So Thailand is the nearest place to trial plays T1 and T10 ([[ch26-plays]]; our inference).
- **Testing.** Vietnamese laboratories test for basic composition, contaminants and microbes. None we found offers accredited amino-acid profiles or protein digestibility tests [@INF-03; @INF-10; @INF-11; @INF-13; @INF-14] {VN-direct|Medium}. That work will need an overseas laboratory for now.
- **Parks.** Saigon Hi-Tech Park has an incubator and a biotech centre [@INF-06] {VN-direct|High}. A 2026 decree lets provinces create high-tech agriculture zones [@INF-23] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 6.5 Talent

Several universities teach food technology, biotechnology and aquaculture. Can Tho University alone offers 860 places a year in these fields [@INF-15] {VN-direct|High}.

**The skills gap is specialist.** There is almost no Vietnamese work in extrusion, processing microbial protein for food, sensory work on meat analogues, protein-quality scoring or cell culture for food [@RD-01; @BIB-01] {VN-direct|Low}. Startups in these areas will import know-how and people, which adds cost and key-person risk.

**No alternative-protein teaching.** We found no alternative-protein course, research centre or student group in Vietnam [@VCO-21] {VN-direct|Medium}. The international student network on alternative protein had 70 chapters in 21 countries in 2024, none in Vietnam [@SLW-03] {general|Medium}.

**A low-cost teaching model exists.** From 2017 to 2024, the University of California, Berkeley, ran product-design classes on problems set by industry. At least three startups came out of them [@AML-21; @AML-22; @AML-11; @AML-13; @AML-15] {general|Medium}. Vietnam already has a national student food-innovation contest, with 144 projects in 2025, but no plant-protein winner [@AML-33] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**Nobody trains fermentation operators.** We found no fermentation short course for industry in Vietnam [@SLW-14; @INF-01; @SLW-20] {VN-direct|Medium}. Fees are not the barrier: a hands-on course in the United States costs about USD 3,100 [@SLW-09] {general|High}. The barrier is access to pilot-scale equipment. The skills move RM-09 responds with separate tracks for engineers and operators ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).

## 6.6 Research funding

- **The national science foundation is the main domestic funder, and its grants are small.** It is credited on 36 of 525 Vietnamese food-protein papers from 2015 to 2026 [@SLW-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. In 2023 its ordinary projects averaged about USD 36,500, and strong-group projects about USD 115,000 (our conversion) [@SLW-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- **It co-funds with foreign agencies** in Belgium, Germany, Switzerland, the UK and Australia [@SLW-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- **The Vingroup Innovation Foundation** mainly funds scholarships and fellowships, and showed no new call in 2025 or 2026 [@RD-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **International funders:** Australia's agricultural research centre funds 23 Vietnamese projects, none on alternative protein [@RD-04] {VN-direct|High}. The Good Food Institute (GFI) has funded no Vietnamese author [@RD-02; @RD-03; @SLW-02] {general|Medium}.
- **A 2025 science law** accepts research risk when procedures are followed, which a national protein research programme could use [@RD-08; @REG-48] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 6.7 What this means for protein for people

- **Researchers:** the fastest route to a track record is to point existing tools at food protein: yeast-made food proteins, koji grown on cassava residue, spirulina and duckweed protein, and a protein-quality dataset. Publish in indexed journals; much Vietnamese applied work is invisible abroad.
- **Policy makers:** three pieces of shared kit would unlock most gaps: a food-grade pilot fermentation line, a pilot extrusion line, and protein-quality tests in an accredited laboratory. A directed call on "protein from Vietnamese side streams and species" would turn scattered groups into a network ([[ch27-policy-options]]). A first round of about USD 0.35 million would fund 10 ordinary projects (our calculation) [@SLW-01] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:estimate}.
- **Investors and startups:** credible partners exist for side-stream protein (HUST) and koji genetics (University of Science, Hanoi). Do not assume any Vietnamese partner owns an extruder or a food-grade fermenter until confirmed.
- **International bodies:** the most fundable public goods are food-grade fish cell lines, a Vietnamese protein-quality database and duckweed protein screening.
