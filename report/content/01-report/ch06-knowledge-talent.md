# 6. Knowledge, talent and shared infrastructure

**In one paragraph.** Vietnam is not starting from zero in the sciences that feed alternative protein, but it is starting from almost zero in the technologies that turn them into food. Its universities and institutes publish fish and shrimp feeding trials at regional levels, express proteins (including growth factors used in cultivated-meat media) in yeast, culture human stem cells in serum-free media, engineer the koji mould, and grow microalgae. They publish almost nothing on texturisation, mycoprotein, precision-fermented food proteins or food-grade fish cell lines, and Vietnam's research intensity on novel foods is about a quarter to a third of Thailand's (a quarter on OpenAIRE; about 31% on a broader OpenAlex basket [@HSC-01] {VN-direct|Medium}). The binding constraint is not ideas but shared infrastructure: we found no open pilot fermentation facility, no food extrusion line and no laboratory offering the accredited amino-acid and digestibility tests a protein-quality claim or a novel-food dossier needs.

## 6.1 Research output: small in novel food, regional-level in feed

We counted Vietnam-affiliated publications from 2015 to September 2026 in the OpenAIRE scholarly database and compared them with Southeast Asian peers (OpenAlex was rate-limited; method in [[app-s7-research]]). All counts are lower bounds and include some off-topic records.

| Topic (2015 to Sept 2026) | Vietnam | Thailand | Malaysia | Philippines | Singapore |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plant-based or cultivated meat, records (per 10,000 publications) | 9 (0.37) | 49 (1.58) | 61 (1.02) | 10 (0.62) | 112 (2.94) |
| Fishmeal replacement in aquafeed | 10 (0.42) | 14 (0.45) | 28 (0.47) | 11 (0.68) | 6 (0.16) |
| Spirulina / *Arthrospira* | 87 (3.62) | 164 (5.29) | 178 (2.98) | 36 (2.23) | 28 (0.74) |

Source: [@BIB-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. Indonesia is excluded from the intensity comparison because the database indexes many Indonesian national journals.

**Reading.** Vietnam's weakness is specific to novel food technologies; in algae and in aquafeed protein replacement it is at the regional level (and the aquafeed count is certainly an undercount; broader term searches found at least 10 and up to about 20 Vietnamese replacement studies [@BIB-06; @RD-01] {VN-direct|Medium}). After reading titles, Vietnam has **zero or near-zero** published work in precision fermentation or recombinant food proteins, mycoprotein, fish or shrimp cell lines for food, and cultivated meat (one 2024 legal analysis), and at most one study on meat-analogue extrusion [@BIB-01; @RD-43] {VN-direct|Medium}. About six Vietnamese papers address plant-based meat products (reviews, a soy-burger shelf-life study, a partly meat-free sausage) [@BIB-01; @RD-26; @RD-27] {VN-direct|Medium}.

> **Correction.** Earlier drafts said no Vietnamese study had reported RNA reduction, mycotoxin testing or protein digestibility. That was too strong: nucleic-acid reduction in spent brewer's yeast (2018), aflatoxin testing in traditional fermented soy (2025) and in vitro digestibility of rice distillers'-grain protein (2025) are all published. What remains true is that none has been applied to a novel microbial biomass, and **no Vietnamese DIAAS or PDCAAS study was found** [@RD-31; @RD-61; @RD-23] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 6.2 Five adjacent strengths

1. **Aquafeed nutrition trials** (Can Tho University; Research Institute for Aquaculture No. 2; An Giang University). Measured Vietnamese benchmarks include: soy protein concentrate replacing 40% of fishmeal protein in snakehead with no loss of growth; fermented soybean meal with 88.8% protein digestibility in whiteleg shrimp; spent brewer's yeast replacing up to 60% of fishmeal protein in giant freshwater prawn [@RD-46; @RD-47; @RD-48] {VN-direct|Medium}. These are tank trials, but they are exactly the evidence a feed-first play needs.
2. **Recombinant protein expression in *Pichia*** (VNU-HCM University of Science), including three growth factors used in cultivated-meat media (FGF-2, KGF and PDGF-BB) and an alkaline phytase aimed at aquafeed [@RD-36; @RD-37; @RD-38; @RD-35] {VN-direct|Medium}. None of this work has targeted food proteins such as dairy, egg or heme proteins.
3. **Stem-cell culture** (Vinmec), including tests of serum-free and xeno-free media, where only one of four commercial kits worked for cord-blood cells [@RD-39; @RD-40] {VN-direct|Medium}.
4. **Fungal genetics for koji mould and *Cordyceps*** (VNU Hanoi University of Science): transformation systems and gene-deletion tools in *Aspergillus oryzae* [@RD-33; @RD-34] {VN-direct|Medium}.
5. **Microalgae cultivation** (VAST Institute of Biotechnology), including a lab-scale salt-tolerant spirulina strain reaching 48.7% protein (of dry cell weight) [@RD-21] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Two further assets are specific and under-used:
- **Hanoi University of Science and Technology (HUST)** has recovered 90% of the protein from rice distillers' grain (55% to 80% protein on a dry basis) and made protein products from spent brewer's yeast [@RD-22; @RD-23; @RD-32] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **A duckweed research collection** of more than 100 wild samples and a duckweed genome with a Vietnamese first author; we found no protein work on either [@RD-44; @RD-71] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 6.3 Research gaps Vietnam is well placed to fill

Each of these combines a Vietnamese raw material, species or skill with an absence of published work (detail in [[app-s7-research]]):

1. Food-grade adaptation of striped catfish (*tra*) and shrimp cells. Continuous striped-catfish lines exist in India, but they are serum-dependent virology lines [@SCI-10] {general|High}, and we know of no true continuous shrimp line (the "PmLyO-Sf9" line is part insect) [@SCI-12] {general|Medium}.
2. Low-cost *Pichia*-made growth factors tested on fish cells.
3. Fungal biomass from cassava pulp and peel with a food-grade safety package (cyanide, mycotoxins, RNA, digestibility).
4. Characterised Vietnamese *A. oryzae* (*tương*) strains as biomass-protein hosts, with the strain safety data regulators will ask for (cyclopiazonic acid and 3-nitropropionic acid) [@SCI-19] {general|High}.
5. Texturisation of rice distillers'-grain and broken-rice protein.
6. Protein extraction and feeding trials with Vietnamese duckweed clones.
7. Protein ingredients from salt-tolerant spirulina.
8. Seaweed protein for food and feed.
9. Bacterial protein from pig-farm and cassava-starch biogas.
10. **A DIAAS or PDCAAS reference dataset for Vietnamese protein ingredients** (mung bean, rice, spirulina, catfish side-stream isolates, spent yeast). Pig-based DIAAS values exist abroad (mung bean 86, soy isolate 97, brown rice concentrate 42), but human digestibility of mung bean measured in children was about 20% lower than the pig estimate [@SCI-40; @SCI-41; @SCI-42] {general|High}.
11. Bacterial single-cell protein and fungal meal in pangasius and whiteleg shrimp diets.

## 6.4 Shared infrastructure: the binding constraint

- **Pilot fermentation.** We found no Vietnamese facility that publishes an open, food-grade pilot fermentation service. FIRI's pilot centre in Hanoi lists soy-milk, tofu, juice and powder processes but no fermenters; the HCMC Biotechnology Center lists agriculture and aquaculture technology transfer, not contract fermentation [@INF-02; @INF-04; @INF-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Extrusion.** We found no food extruder listed by a Vietnamese university or institute [@RD-01; @INF-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. A team at the Industrial University of Ho Chi Minh City (IUH) published high-moisture meat-analogue work in 2026, so IUH should be checked first [@TIC-21] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Testing.** FIRI's NACEFA laboratory (VILAS 259) lists proximate, contaminant, mycotoxin and microbiology tests and HPLC equipment for amino-acid analysis; the NIFC page we read shows no test scope. No laboratory page we read lists accredited amino-acid profiles or protein digestibility (DIAAS) testing [@INF-03; @INF-10; @INF-11; @INF-13; @INF-14] {VN-direct|Medium}. PDCAAS and DIAAS work will probably need an overseas laboratory for now.
- **Feed trials.** MAE has restructured its state trial centres (a merged livestock and veterinary-drug testing centre in Hanoi, covering feed trials, from 1 October 2026; an aquatic-products centre based in Hanoi with regional branches in Can Tho and Ho Chi Minh City), and both may sell services [@INF-18; @INF-28] {VN-direct|High}. University wet labs (Can Tho, Nha Trang) and private contract labs (ShrimpVet) complement them.
- **Parks.** Saigon Hi-Tech Park (913 ha) has an incubator, an R&D centre and a biotech centre [@INF-06] {VN-direct|High}. A 2026 decree lets provinces create high-tech agriculture zones with R&D and interest-subsidy incentives [@INF-23] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 6.5 Talent

Degrees in food technology, biotechnology and aquaculture are offered at several universities. Can Tho University alone has a 2026 quota of 860 places in these three programme groups (300, 240 and 320), within a total quota of 11,850 [@INF-15] {VN-direct|High}. We could not sum a national intake (several admissions sites failed or showed quotas only as images) [@INF-15; @INF-21] {VN-direct|High}. The skills gap is specialist rather than general: the literature shows almost no Vietnamese work in extrusion, food-grade downstream processing of microbial biomass, sensory and structure work on meat analogues, protein-quality scoring, or cell culture of fish and livestock cells for food [@RD-01; @BIB-01] {VN-direct|Low}. Startups in those areas will import know-how and people, which adds cost and key-person risk. We found no salary survey for food or biotech engineers.

## 6.6 Research funding

- **NAFOSTED** (national science foundation) and VINIF are both frequently acknowledged in relevant Vietnamese papers; typical grant sizes were not established [@RD-05; @RD-06; @RD-12] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **VINIF** (Vingroup) mainly funds people: about 1,300 master's and doctoral scholarships, 180 postdoctoral fellowships and 117 projects in 2018 to 2023 [@RD-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **International:** ACIAR spends about AUD 5.4 million across 23 Vietnamese projects in 2025/26, none on alternative protein [@RD-04] {VN-direct|High}; the GFI research grant programme lists no featured grantee in Vietnam or elsewhere in Southeast Asia on the page we read, and is closed [@RD-02; @RD-03] {general|Medium}; the largest recent open call (Coefficient Giving's alternative-protein R&D request, up to USD 30 million, international applicants welcome) closed on 15 September 2026 [@CAP-57] {general|High}.
- **Law 93/2025** allows acceptance of research risk and liability exemption when procedures are followed, which a national protein-research programme could use [@RD-08; @REG-48] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## 6.7 What this means

- **Researchers:** the fastest route to a visible track record is to point existing tools at food protein: *Pichia* for a food protein, *A. oryzae* biomass on cassava residue, spirulina protein extraction, duckweed protein from the research collection, a DIAAS dataset. Publish in indexed journals with DOIs; much applied Vietnamese work is invisible abroad.
- **Policy makers:** three pieces of shared kit would unlock most gaps: a food-grade pilot fermentation train (about 50 to 3,000 litres with downstream processing), a pilot extrusion line, and amino-acid and digestibility methods added to an accredited laboratory's scope. A directed call on "protein from Vietnamese side streams and species" would turn scattered groups into a network ([[ch27-policy-options]]).
- **Investors and startups:** credible trial partners exist for feed (Can Tho, Nha Trang, the state aquafeed centre) and for side-stream protein (HUST); ask founders for their trial data. Do not assume any Vietnamese partner owns an extruder or a 100-litre-plus food-grade fermenter until confirmed.
- **International bodies:** the GFI research grant page we read lists no featured grantee in Vietnam; the most fundable public goods are a food-grade catfish or shrimp cell-line adaptation project, a Vietnamese protein-quality database and duckweed germplasm protein screening.
