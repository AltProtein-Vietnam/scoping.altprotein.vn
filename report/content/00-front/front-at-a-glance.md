# At a glance

The numbers that frame the report, in the order of its five parts. Each heading links to the chapters behind the numbers.

## Where the protein people eat comes from ([[ch01-why-vietnam]], [[ch11-protein-diet]])

About one fifth of the protein supply is imported food. Most meat, eggs and farmed fish are produced in Vietnam, but on imported feed. Counting that feed, about two thirds of the animal protein people eat rests on imports (our calculation).

## Food security: disease, wild fish and what new protein would change ([[ch01-why-vietnam]], [[ch20-drivers-2050]])

Disease, not feed, is the protein shock consumers feel. Wild fish is the protein food under most pressure. We found no evidence that alternative protein lowers disease or pandemic risk. At the scale demand supports, its effect on national import exposure is small (our calculation).

## Futures: 2035 and 2050 ([[ch19-outlook-2035]] to [[ch24-vision-2050]])

Every value below is a published projection, our estimate or, for the vision, a goal. None is a forecast.

On trend, the meat protein Vietnamese people eat rises from about 1.01 Mt in 2025 to about 1.45 Mt in 2050 (our model). The share resting on imports, directly or through feed, rises from 70% to 78% today to 79% to 87%. Replacing 1 t of meat protein avoids about 4.5 t of soybean meal and 9.5 t of maize imports (our calculation), unless the replacement is made from imported soy.

|---|---|---|---|
| A. Regional workshop | Cheap and ample | Open | Contract fermentation for regional brands; textured and fungal ingredients |

Seven moves pay off in all four worlds. The first are a national protein balance, a new-food approval route with deadlines and shared food-grade pilot capacity ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).

{{kn:kn-no-regret-moves}}

**The vision for 2050 is a goal, not a forecast** ([[ch24-vision-2050]]). New routes would provide about 67,000 to 172,000 t of protein a year, of which about 59,000 to 147,000 t would be made in Vietnam. Meat stays the main protein. For security, what counts is the share made from domestic inputs.

## What to do ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]] to [[ch30-unknowns]])

Of seven plays, three lead under almost every weighting: domestic textured plant protein (T1), a hub turning cassava starch into fermentation sugar (T4) and shared pilot and tolling fermentation (T5).

| Horizon | Businesses (plays) | Public steps |
|---|---|---|
| Now to 2028 | T1 textured plant protein; T4 cassava sugar hub (if a fermenter commits); koji and fungal food pilots (T9) | New-food clause in the Food Safety Law (vote considered May 2027) |
| 2028 to 2031 | T5 shared pilot fermentation; T10 mung bean and rice protein; T7 contract precision fermentation | Novel-food procedure, with a route that relies on trusted regulators' approvals; shared pilot line; protein-quality laboratory |
| 2031 onward | T8 cultivated seafood, if a food cell line exists | Regional reliance on novel-food approvals |
