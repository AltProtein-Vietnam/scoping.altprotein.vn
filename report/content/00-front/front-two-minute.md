# The report in two minutes

> **Draft for review.** Version 0.7. Not for citation.

**Why Vietnam.** Vietnam makes pork, poultry, eggs, fish and shrimp at scale on imported plant protein: about 99% of the soy protein in its feed is imported, for a feed-ingredient import bill of about USD 10 billion in 2025. The case for alternative protein here is food and feed security, industrial value and trade ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).

{{kn:kn-soy-protein-import}}

**What it could make first.** Domestic textured plant protein to replace Chinese imports, and functional microbial feed ingredients for shrimp and pangasius. A cassava-starch-to-sugar hub could keep a fermentation step in Vietnam, and shared pilot fermentation is what most other plays need. Precision fermentation fits only as contract-made, high-value ingredients; cultivated meat and seafood are research topics for this decade ([[ch26-plays]]).

**Who would buy it.** Firms, not shoppers. Chay makers, meat processors, plant-milk makers, canteens and exporters of dumplings and other wrapped foods would buy new protein as an ingredient. Branded meat replacements have little pull. On a benchmark path, the routes create demand for about 19,000 t of protein a year by 2035, of which about 11,000 to 17,000 t made in Vietnam for the home market, mostly replacing imports; they would displace about 0.2% of meat protein ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).

{{kn:kn-demand-bench-2035}}

**What stands in the way.** No route to approve new foods, no open pilot fermentation capacity that we could find, no tariff shelter against Chinese and ASEAN protein ingredients, and little risk capital ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch05-industrial-base]], [[ch16-business-buyers]], [[ch08-capital]]).

**What to do first.**

- Add a clause on new foods to the Food Safety Law while the draft is revised between the October 2026 first reading and the vote considered in May 2027.
- Clarify the GMO status of purified precision-fermented proteins, and fix the two feed lists.
- Share the expensive parts: a pilot fermentation line, public feed trials and a protein-quality laboratory.
- For firms: get delivered quotes for food-grade soy flour, win conditional orders from chay makers, and test feed ingredients head to head against the yeast mills already buy ([[ch27-policy-options]], [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

**By 2050.** On trend, soybean-meal need rises from 7.2 Mt to about 10.4 Mt. Better feed efficiency cuts that about twice as much as alternative protein, and nine no-regret moves pay off in every scenario we tested ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]], [[ch28-robust-moves]]).
