# The report in two minutes

**Protein for people first; feed only as context.** The study asks what Vietnam could make at home for people to eat, and who would buy it, now and to 2050.

**Why Vietnam.** Vietnamese people eat more meat each year: 136.4 g per person per day in 2020, against a recommended 50 to 80 g. Most of that meat, and the eggs and farmed fish, is produced in Vietnam on imported feed. About 99% of the soy protein in feed was imported in 2025, for a feed-ingredient bill of about USD 10 billion ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).

{{kn:kn-soy-protein-import}}

**Food security: real, but narrow.** Counting feed, about two thirds of the animal protein people eat (61% to 71% in 2023) rests on imports (our calculation). Disease is the shock consumers feel: pork prices rose 57% in 2020 after African swine fever. But alternative protein made from imported soy only moves the dependence. Only domestic inputs reduce it, and at the scale demand supports, the effect is small ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).

{{kn:kn-animal-protein-on-imports}}

**What it could make first.** Three of seven plays lead ([[ch26-plays]]):

- domestic textured plant protein, to replace imports from China (T1);
- a hub that turns cassava starch into fermentation sugar, so that step stays in Vietnam (T4);
- shared pilot and tolling fermentation, where firms rent time on someone else's fermenter (T5).

Fungal foods such as koji (T9), and mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks (T10), are worth proving.

**Who would buy it.** Firms, not shoppers. Makers of *chay* (traditional vegetarian) food, meat processors, plant-milk makers, canteens and dumpling exporters would buy new protein as an ingredient. Branded meat replacements have little pull. On a benchmark path, demand reaches about 19,000 t of protein a year by 2035, or about 0.2% of meat protein ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).

{{kn:kn-demand-bench-2035}}

**What stands in the way.** There is no route to approve new foods, and we found no open pilot fermentation capacity. Chinese and ASEAN protein ingredients enter without tariffs, and risk capital is scarce ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch05-industrial-base]], [[ch16-business-buyers]], [[ch08-capital]]).

**What to do first** ([[ch27-policy-options]], [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]):

- Add a clause on new foods to the Food Safety Law before the vote considered for May 2027.
- Clarify the genetically modified organism (GMO) status of purified proteins made by engineered microbes, and set a proportionate novel-food procedure in the implementing decree.
- Share the expensive parts: a food-grade pilot fermentation line and a protein-quality laboratory.
- Firms: get delivered quotes for food-grade soy flour, win conditional orders from chay makers, and test plant protein mixed into meat products against the soy recipe buyers already use.

**By 2050.** On trend, the meat protein Vietnamese people eat rises from about 1.01 Mt (million tonnes) in 2025 to about 1.45 Mt in 2050 (our model). The imported soybean meal behind it rises from 7.2 Mt to about 10.4 Mt. Seven moves pay off in every scenario we tested ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]], [[ch28-robust-moves]]).
