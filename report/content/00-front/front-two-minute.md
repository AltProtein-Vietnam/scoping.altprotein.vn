# The report in two minutes

> **Draft for review.** Version 0.8. Not for citation.

**Protein for people first; feed only as context.** The study asks what Vietnam could make at home for people to eat, and who would buy it, now and to 2050.

**Why Vietnam.** Vietnamese people eat more meat each year: 136.4 g per person per day in 2020, against a recommendation of 50 to 80 g. Most of that meat, and the eggs and farmed fish, is produced in Vietnam on imported feed. About 99% of the soy protein in feed was imported in 2025, for a feed-ingredient import bill of about USD 10 billion. So the case for making more protein for people at home is the security of that supply, industrial value and trade ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).

{{kn:kn-soy-protein-import}}

**Food security: real, but narrow.** Counting feed, about two thirds of the animal protein people eat (61% to 71% in 2023) rests on imports (our calculation). Disease is the shock consumers feel: pork prices rose 57% in 2020 after African swine fever. But the evidence does not show that Vietnam relies on subsidised imported meat, and we found no evidence that alternative protein lowers disease or pandemic risk. Made from imported soy, it only moves the dependence. Only domestic inputs reduce it, and at the scale demand supports the effect is small ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).

{{kn:kn-animal-protein-on-imports}}

**What it could make first.** Three of seven plays lead ([[ch26-plays]]):

- domestic textured plant protein to replace imports from China (T1);
- a cassava-starch-to-fermentation-sugar hub, a cluster of plants on one site, so a food-fermentation step stays in Vietnam (T4);
- shared pilot and tolling fermentation (renting time on someone else's fermenter), which most fermentation plays need first (T5).

Fungal foods such as koji (T9) and mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks (T10) are worth proving.

**Who would buy it.** Firms, not shoppers. Makers of *chay* (traditional vegetarian) foods, meat processors, plant-milk makers, canteens and exporters of dumplings would buy new protein as an ingredient. Branded meat replacements have little pull. On a benchmark path, the routes create demand for about 19,000 t of protein a year by 2035. About 11,000 to 17,000 t of it would be made in Vietnam, mostly replacing imports, and it would displace about 0.2% of meat protein ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).

{{kn:kn-demand-bench-2035}}

**What stands in the way.** There is no route to approve new foods, and we found no open pilot fermentation capacity. There is no tariff shelter against Chinese and ASEAN protein ingredients, and little risk capital ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch05-industrial-base]], [[ch16-business-buyers]], [[ch08-capital]]).

**What to do first** ([[ch27-policy-options]], [[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]):

- Add a clause on new foods to the Food Safety Law between the October 2026 first reading and the vote considered in May 2027.
- Clarify the genetically modified organism (GMO) status of purified precision-fermented proteins (made by engineered microbes), and write a proportionate novel-food procedure into the law's implementing decree.
- Share the expensive parts: a food-grade pilot fermentation line and a protein-quality laboratory.
- For firms: get delivered quotes for food-grade soy flour, win conditional orders from chay makers, and test extenders (plant protein mixed into meat products) against the soy recipe buyers already use.

**By 2050.** On trend, the meat protein Vietnamese people eat rises from about 1.01 Mt in 2025 to about 1.45 Mt in 2050 (our model). The imported soybean meal behind it rises from 7.2 Mt to about 10.4 Mt. Seven no-regret moves pay off in every scenario we tested ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]], [[ch28-robust-moves]]).
