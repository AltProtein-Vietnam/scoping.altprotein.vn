## M5.2 What changed in v0.6

No finding changed. The changes are to structure and readability:

- **Reader-first structure.** The 30 chapters are regrouped into five parts in the order a reader needs them: I Context, II Supply, III Demand, IV Futures and V What to do. Chapters are renumbered 1 to 30. Appendices are re-coded by theme: M (method and record), S (context and supply evidence), D (demand evidence), F (futures evidence) and R (registers). Every link, section number and reference was rewritten; M5.3 maps old to new.
- **Versions.** The earlier "editions 1.0 to 1.4" are now versions 0.1 to 0.5 (M5.1).
- **Markers moved here.** Notes in the text such as "new in edition 1.2" or "corrected in edition 1.3" were removed so pages read cleanly; what each version added is in M5.4, and each correction is in M5.5 to M5.12. Data files keep a version column (for example `version` in `key-numbers.json`).
- **New pages.** A two-minute summary ([[front-two-minute]]) and an FAQ ([[front-faq]]).
- **Draft labels.** The cover, the summaries and every page footer say "Draft for review, not for citation".
- **Reading aids.** How to read now has a map of the parts and a table of codes and scenario names; the executive summaries and at a glance follow the new part order; the briefs end with a "Read next" list.
- **Consistency fixes** from a reader review, listed in M5.2.1.

### M5.2.1 Consistency fixes in v0.6

These fix how things read or add up. None changes a finding.

- **Key numbers.** A duplicate key-number id was renamed (the 2023 food protein ingredient imports are now `kn-food-protein-ingredient-imports-2023`). The two retail protein price tiles are kept as separate observations (23 and 24 September 2026) and Chapter 9 now points to the wider 24 September check. The 2030 feed-target tile now says which feed the target covers. The retail price tile was removed from the startups brief, where it did not fit the text.
- **Counts.** Stale counts of open questions and disagreements in Chapter 30, Appendix R1 and Appendix S9 were brought up to date, as were the source, table and page counts on the cover and in the summaries. The plays are "ten" everywhere; "sixteen" refers only to the research lines of the actor check.
- **Law timing.** Every page now states the Food Safety Law window the same way: first comments in October 2026, a vote considered in May 2027, so a new-food clause needs to be in the draft revised between the two sessions, in practice by early 2027.
- **Codes.** Play, policy-option, demand-move and scenario codes are spelled out on first use in the summaries and briefs, and decoded in a table in [[front-how-to-read]].
- **Summaries and reading times.** 29 page summaries were shortened to one or two sentences, and every reading time was recomputed from the word count.
- **Briefs.** The six briefs now follow the new part order and end with a "Read next" list.
- **Terms and spelling.** TVP (textured vegetable protein) is defined where first used; spelling is British English throughout (for example yoghurt, sulphate); 13 abbreviations used in the report (HS code, MFN, ACFTA, RCEP, ATIGA, EVFTA and UKVFTA, CPTPP, FOB, CIF, IFS and BRCGS, ASC, TCVN, SFA) were added to [[app-r3-glossary]], which now has 236 terms.
- **Clean text.** Raw tag tokens that had leaked into running text were removed; the headings of [[app-s13-science]] are numbered; related-page links were added where pages cite each other but did not link.
- **Section codes.** Old appendix letters used as section codes inside three appendices now use the new codes: [[app-s5-facilities]] (was F1 to F9), [[app-s6-feed-market]] (was H1 to H13) and [[app-s12-costs]] (was I1 to I17). The futures question tree in [[app-m2-futures-method]] is Q1 to Q10, and the 2035 scenarios are Scenarios 1 to 3. The feedstock sheet codes C1 to C34 in [[app-s4-feedstocks]] are kept as identifiers.
- **Checks of the restructure.** An independent review of the renumbering restored one legal citation it had caught (Appendix I of Decree 111/2021), corrected two part ranges in the Vietnamese executive summary, one section reference in Chapter 24 and four section ranges in `actor_questions.csv`, and put the reading paths in `site-manifest.json` into part order. Tariff chapter numbers (HS chapters) were checked and are unchanged. The balance and demand models reproduce their published outputs byte for byte.

## M5.4 What each earlier version added

**v0.2 (futures).** New chapters on drivers, frontier technology, the protein and feed balance, the 2050 worlds, the vision and robust moves (now chapters 20 to 24 and 28), and Appendices M2 and F2 to F6. Futures sections were added to the executive summaries, at a glance, the cover, how to read and the six briefs. New tag: the foresight tag; new callouts: "Speculative" and "Vision, not forecast". Smaller edits corrected and cross-linked the knowledge, outlook and unknowns chapters and the directory, feed-market, research, regulation and science appendices; the registers gained the new rows. Corrections are in M5.9.

**v0.3 (demand).** New chapters on diet and prices, chay, consumers, frontier demand, channels, business buyers, export, demand sizing and product targets (now chapters 11 to 18 and 25), and Appendices M3 and D1 to D8. New tag: the demand evidence tag; new script: `tools/demand_model.py`. `plays.csv` was unchanged; the demand view of market pull is in `play_demand_check.csv`. An independent review corrected the draft before release (M5.10).

**v0.4 (actor check).** The demand side was checked against 80 named actors and expanded in three waves of research lines (now chapter 29 and Appendix M4). The check changed the school, textured-protein, blends, label, sugar-tax, export and funder conclusions and edited chapters 7 to 9, 11 to 19 and 25 to 27, several appendices, the summaries and the briefs. The demand model's numbers were frozen, with caveats stated beside them, and the plays' scores were unchanged. 50 data files were added. An independent review corrected the draft before release (M5.11).

**v0.5 (prologue).** A prologue for newcomers in English and Vietnamese, two charts, four key numbers, 81 sources and 11 glossary terms; the bibliography was completed to list every source (M5.12).

Sections M5.5 to M5.8 list corrections to the internal scoping drafts that preceded v0.1. Their claims were treated as leads and re-checked ([[app-m1-method]]); the corrections supersede the earlier figures everywhere. ## M5.14 Changes made in v0.8

Version 0.8 (October 2026) changes the study in three ways, all at the owner's request. It narrows the scope to protein for people, with feed only as context. It adds a research round, wave 11, that tested food-security hypotheses about the protein people eat. And it rewrites every page in clear language. [[app-m1-method]], section M1.14, describes how the work was done. The editors' page-by-page logs are in `working-papers/wave11/editorial/`.

Codes used below are decoded in [[front-how-to-read]]. T stands for plays, P for public goods and PO for policy options. RM stands for moves that pay off in any 2050, TPP for target product profiles and DMV for demand moves.

### M5.14.1 Why the study changed

The owner asked for three things:

1. **Test more food-security hypotheses.** Examples were reliance on subsidised imported meat, and animal disease and pandemic risk. Wave 11 did this in four research lines (M5.14.6).
2. **Concentrate on protein for human consumption, now and to 2050.** Prune the feed arguments, and stop treating fishmeal replacement, aquafeed and other feed ingredients as something to make (M5.14.2 and M5.14.3).
3. **Write in clear language to the GOV.UK standard.** Specialists and newcomers should both be able to read the study, and it should survive machine translation into Vietnamese (M5.14.11).

### M5.14.2 The new scope

The study is now about protein for people: what Vietnam could make at home for people to eat, from what, at what cost, under which rules, and who would buy it, now and to 2050. The short form is "protein for people first; feed only as context".

- **Feed is context.** It is the hidden import behind the meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish that people eat, so it stays part of the food-security picture.
- **Feed ingredients are no longer something to make.** The study no longer assesses fishmeal replacements, or microbial, insect or duckweed feed, as a production target. The version 0.7 assessment stays as the record in [[app-s6-feed-market]], [[app-f6-aquafeed-feedstock-futures]] and this appendix.
- **What stays the same.** Insect protein is still an incumbent and benchmark only. Diet change is not a goal: Part III sizes what people and firms would buy and does not tell people what to eat. Market-size forecasts stay out of scope.
- **Framing.** The case now leads with the security of the protein people eat, then industrial value and trade. Environment and animal welfare remain motivations for some actors.

### M5.14.6 Wave 11: what the food-security research found

Four research lines ran on 1 October 2026 and registered 90 sources, each line under its own source prefix (FBS, MIM, PAN and SEC). Each line tested a hypothesis, and each said whether alternative protein for human food would reduce the exposure. The findings were folded into chapters 1 (sections 1.1, 1.3, 1.6 and 1.7), 11, 14, 16, 20, 22, 23, 28 and 30, Appendix F2, the summaries, the prologue and the briefs.

- **Protein for people (FBS).** On the food balance sheets of the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), about 19% to 23% of the protein supply in 2023 was imported food, up from 11% to 13% in 2010 (our calculation) [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Counting the imported feed behind domestic meat, eggs and farmed fish, 61% to 71% of the animal protein people eat rested on imports (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Imported meat, offal and dairy (MIM).** Meat imports reached 978,000 t worth USD 2.0 billion in 2025 [@MIM-04; @MIM-05] {VN-direct|Medium}. Pork was only 3.7% to 4.5% imported [@MIM-03; @MIM-05; @MIM-06] {VN-direct|Medium}; beef and buffalo meat (32% to 48% imported) [@MIM-03; @QNT-01] {VN-direct|Medium} and milk are the most import-dependent. **Clean negative:** the evidence does not support the hypothesis that Vietnam relies on subsidised imported meat. Most of what Vietnam buys comes from origins that give no price support, India taxes its buffalo meat, and support where it exists works mainly through border protection at home [@MIM-15; @MIM-16] {general|High}. Imported meat is cheaper because costs differ: Vietnamese pig farm prices in 2024 were 1.6 to 2.8 times those in Brazil, Russia, the United States and the EU (our calculation) [@MIM-15] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Animal disease, zoonoses and pandemic risk (PAN).** Disease is the protein shock consumers feel: consumer pork prices rose 57.23% in 2020 [@SEC-08] {VN-direct|High}. In 2025, 79% of the African swine fever samples typed in the north were recombinant strains, against which the three licensed vaccines "have no protective effect" [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}. The 2019 shock was large for pork but small for protein: poultry output rose 16.5% [@PAN-03] {VN-direct|High}, and people switched to chicken, not tofu [@DIE-10; @DIE-05] {VN-direct|High}. **Clean negative:** we found no study that measures a fall in zoonotic, pandemic or antimicrobial-resistance risk from replacing meat with alternative protein; the claims come from reviews and opinion, several by advocacy or company authors [@PAN-27; @PAN-30; @PAN-31] {general|Medium}.
- **Other food-security exposures (SEC).** **Constraint:** only routes on domestic inputs reduce import dependence. Alternative protein made from imported soy, pea or wheat gluten moves the dependence, often from the Americas to China. China supplied about 84% of the tonnage of food plant-protein ingredients that reached Vietnam in 2025 (our calculation) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Wild fish is the protein food under most pressure: the marine fish stock in 2016 to 2020 was 22.1% below 2000 to 2005 [@SEC-06] {VN-direct|Medium}. The national food-security resolution to 2030 has no import, feed or protein indicator [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High}.

**What this changes in the case.** The security case is real but narrow. Chapter 1 keeps "security of the protein people eat" as the first argument, labelled "strong, with limits". It now says that only protein made from domestic inputs reduces the dependence. At the scale the demand evidence supports, new protein adds a second source; it does not cut national exposure or disease risk. On the benchmark path it would spare about 142,000 pigs a year in 2035. Swine fever culls in 2025 were 8.4 times that number (our calculation from [@MAC-01; @PAN-03]) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.
