# D2. Consumer and acceptance evidence register

**What this appendix contains.** This appendix holds the consumer and acceptance evidence behind [[ch13-consumers]] and [[ch14-frontier-demand]]. Each section is generated from the data files it names. Each row carries its source, an evidence tag and a demand evidence tag: stated (what people say), revealed (what people and firms do), tested (tastings and experiments) or inferred (our inference); see [[app-m3-demand-method]]. Read the stated rows with care. They come from online panels and small samples, and no Vietnamese study reaches our top quality grade.

We grade each consumer study for quality:

- **A:** a probability sample (a random sample that represents the population) with a full method.
- **B:** a large panel or academic sample with a disclosed method.
- **C:** a convenience or student sample, or a method only partly disclosed.
- **D:** anecdotal, unattributed, or social listening (reading public social media posts) of a few posts.

Short forms in the tables: GFI APAC is the Good Food Institute Asia Pacific, an advocacy non-profit. B2B means sold business to business. GM means genetically modified, GMO a genetically modified organism and GE genome-edited. SKU (stock-keeping unit) means one product in one pack size. *Chay* is Vietnamese vegetarian food and practice (no meat, poultry or fish), usually kept on set lunar days and at festivals. See [[app-r3-glossary]] for other terms.

**Not in the register: national diet surveillance.** The Vietnam round of the Global Diet Quality Project is the only probability sample with Vietnamese data on plant-protein eating. Gallup interviewed 1,007 adults by mobile telephone from 13 November to 12 December 2021. On the previous day, 40.0% of adults had eaten dried beans, soybeans, tofu, soy milk or bean flour, and 7.9% had eaten no meat, poultry or fish (our calculation from the published 92.1%). The survey asks nothing about plant-based meat, plant milk or new proteins. So it is a diet survey, not a consumer study, and it sits in [[app-d1-diet-chay-prices]] and `diet_quality_vietnam.csv` [@APR-36; @BRD-01; @BRD-03] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. The first sales counters for plant milks, chay foods and protein powders (Lazada, Tiki and a kitchen supply platform, September 2026) are in [[app-d3-retail-demand-signals]].

**What the register shows (our reading)** {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}:

- **We found no confirmed case of meat in a Vietnamese chay product from 2015 to 2026.** The one official case (An Giang, 2021) was a seizure on suspicion, with no published result. The other meat-in-chay stories are the relayed 2015 Taiwanese scandal and social-media videos seen only as search snippets (unverified) [@TRU-18; @TRU-01; @TRU-30]. The recorded problems with chay are borax, microbes and unlabelled goods ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]).
- ***Độn* is a fraud word for hidden, cost-cutting filler in *giò* and *chả*.** In search snippets, artisan sellers advertise "no starch filler" [@TRU-02; @TRU-03; @TRU-27]. Declared soy in branded processed meat draws no observed penalty. In our Dan Tri search log (25 September 2026, first result pages only), we found no story treating it as fraud. Masan sells a noodle cup "with a real meat stick" whose ingredient list includes soy protein [@TRU-22; @TRU-25]. The fraud frame attaches to how meat is extended and how that is named, not to an ingredient on the label ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **The same outlet uses *thịt giả* (fake meat) for plant-based meat and for crime.** Of Dan Tri's first 11 results for the term, 6 were about plant-based or cultivated meat and 4 about fraud [@TRU-22].
- **A survey of what chay products contain needs a plan for publishing the results.** The 2016 fish-sauce survey, as a critical commentary describes it, is the precedent that a chay composition and authenticity survey has to design around [@TRU-19]. We recommend publishing with the Vietnam Food Administration, publishing the methods, and not naming small traditional makers.
