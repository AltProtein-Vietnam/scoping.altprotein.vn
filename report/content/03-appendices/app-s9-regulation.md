# S9. Regulatory register and routes to market

This appendix is the detailed legal register behind [[ch07-rules]] and [[ch27-policy-options]]. It states the law as we found it on 23 September 2026. It covers food, genetically modified organisms (GMOs), feed, aquafeed, incentives, sandboxes, environment, genetic resources, intellectual property (IP) and import tariffs. It ends with a route-to-market table for 17 product types, a plain-language explainer, foreign precedents and the first 25 of the 33 policy options.

The study now concentrates on protein for people: food that Vietnam could make at home for people to eat. Feed appears only as context. The feed and aquafeed rules stay in this register as context and as the record (sections S9.10 and S9.11, and the feed rows in S9.19 and S9.22). Version 0.8 retires five feed policy options from the ranking: PO-007, PO-017, PO-018, PO-021 and PO-029.

> **Not legal advice.** This appendix is a research summary for orientation. It is not legal advice and must not be relied on as such. Vietnamese law changed often in 2025 and 2026 and will change again when the revised Food Safety Law and its decree take effect. Several texts were read only in part. Before investing, filing a dossier or selling a product, get advice from a lawyer licensed in Vietnam and, where this appendix says the position is unclear, a written opinion from the competent agency.

> **Method note.** We read legal texts on thuvienphapluat.vn, luatvietnam.vn and government portals. The fetch tool returns extracts, not the full verbatim text. "High" confidence means the wording was extracted from the primary text. "Medium" means government press, a legal publisher or a primary text read only in part. Several large appendices (EIA thresholds, the full MFN tariff schedule, parts of Decree 43/2026 and Decree 320/2025) did not render. The WTO notification of the draft Food Safety Law and the National Assembly draft portal could not be read. Where wave 2 (the REG2 notes) and wave 1 (the REG notes) differ, wave 2 wins, and we flag each change below. Instruments are listed in `instruments.csv` (78 rows). Conflicts are logged in [[app-r2-disagreements]].

Terms used in this appendix:

- *Tự công bố sản phẩm* (self-declaration): the business files its own product specification and a lab test report and may sell at once. No agency approval.
- *Đăng ký bản công bố sản phẩm* (registration of a product declaration): the agency reviews the dossier and issues a receipt before sale.
- *Khảo nghiệm* (feed trial): a controlled feeding trial under an approved protocol. A recognised result is needed before an unlisted feed ingredient can be used.
- Permitted list (*danh mục nguyên liệu được phép sử dụng*): the ministry list of raw materials that may be used in feed without a trial.
- Closed-production certificate: the new certificate that a GMO may be used in contained (*khép kín*) production.
- Sandbox, or controlled testing (*thử nghiệm có kiểm soát*): a time-limited, supervised permission to test a product or model that current law does not cover.
- MAE: Ministry of Agriculture and Environment. MOH: Ministry of Health. VFA: Vietnam Food Administration (MOH Food Safety Department). DLPAH: Department of Livestock Production and Animal Health (MAE). DFFI: Department of Fisheries and Fisheries Surveillance (MAE). MOST: Ministry of Science and Technology. MOF: Ministry of Finance. MOIT: Ministry of Industry and Trade.
- WD: working days. MFN: most-favoured-nation (standard) import duty.
- SCP: single-cell protein (protein from bacteria, yeasts, fungi or microalgae grown as single cells). CIT: corporate income tax. EIA: environmental impact assessment. HS code: the harmonised system code customs uses to classify goods.
- TCVN: a Vietnamese national standard. QCVN: a national technical regulation, which is mandatory.
- Trade agreements that set lower duties: ACFTA (ASEAN-China Free Trade Area), ATIGA (ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement), AKFTA (ASEAN-Korea Free Trade Area) and RCEP (Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership).

## S9.1 Ten things to know

Items 1 to 4 concern food rules, and items 8 and 9 incentives and sandboxes. Items 5 to 7 concern feed rules, which are now context only. Item 10 covers tariffs on both food and feed.

1. Food products still enter the market under Decree 15/2018/ND-CP (self-declaration or registration). Its replacement, Decree 46/2026/ND-CP, and Resolution 66.13/2026/NQ-CP are suspended until the revised Food Safety Law and its decree take effect [@REG-05; @REG-06] {VN-direct|High}.
2. Vietnam has no novel-food category. No law or decree defines novel food, history of safe use or a pre-market safety assessment for a new food source [@REG-01; @REG-02] {VN-direct|High}. The public summaries of the July 2026 draft Food Safety Law do not add one [@REG2-05; @REG2-06] {VN-direct|Medium}.
3. The current law has a hidden risk for new-technology food: Art 55(1)(c) lists "food that is a new-technology product not yet permitted for circulation" among foods to be recalled, but no procedure exists to obtain that permission [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}.
4. A precision-fermented protein is probably a "product of a GMO" under Decree 43/2026, because the definition covers products that contain "protein from the modified gene" [@REG-18; @REG2-11] {VN-direct|Medium}.
5. Insects, duckweed, algae and inactive yeasts are already on the livestock feed permitted list, so they need no feed trial for livestock [@REG-32; @REG-33] {VN-direct|High}.
6. The aquafeed permitted list has no raw-material section at all, so the legal status of new protein raw materials in shrimp and fish feed is ambiguous [@REG2-01; @REG2-02] {VN-direct|High}.
7. Using an unlisted livestock feed raw material has cost VND 10 to 20 million per material, plus 1 to 3 months' suspension of feed production, since 5 August 2026 [@REG2-10] {VN-direct|High}.
8. Decision 23/2026 items 60 (priority high technology) and 92 (encouraged high-tech product) fit precision fermentation (our mapping), but "alternative protein" as such is not listed [@REG-47] {VN-direct|High}.
9. There is no national sandbox procedure for food, but Hanoi's sandbox lists biotechnology as a priority field [@REG2-13; @REG2-17] {VN-direct|High}.
10. Chinese and ASEAN food protein ingredients, yeasts and single-cell protein enter at 0% under ACFTA, ATIGA and RCEP, and feed protein imports (soybean meal, feed maize, fishmeal) enter duty-free under MFN rates, so a domestic novel protein gets no tariff shelter [@REG2-21; @REG2-23; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}.

What this means in practice:

- After the mergers, one ministry (MAE) holds feed, aquafeed, GMO biosafety, genetic resources and carbon, and one ministry (MOST) holds standards, the sandbox, high-tech status and IP [@GT-04; @GT-06; @REG-42; @REG-48] {VN-direct|High}.

- Provinces now run most day-to-day feed procedures. The choice of province can change time to market [@REG-41; @REG-35] {VN-direct|High}.

## S9.3 Food law

### S9.3.1 Law on Food Safety 55/2010/QH12

- **Status.** Law 55/2010/QH12 was passed on 17 June 2010 and took effect on 1 July 2011. It was amended by Law 28/2018/QH14, and a consolidated text (61/VBHN-VPQH) was issued in August 2025 [@REG-01; @REG-17] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Art 2** defines food broadly, as products people eat or drink, fresh or processed [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Art 5(1)** prohibits "*sử dụng nguyên liệu không thuộc loại dùng cho thực phẩm để chế biến thực phẩm*" (using raw materials not of the type used for food to process food) [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Art 5(5)(h)** prohibits selling food that has not been registered where registration is required [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Art 15** sets safety conditions for genetically modified food (*thực phẩm biến đổi gen*); GM labelling is in Art 44(2)(d) [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Art 55(1)(c)** lists "*thực phẩm là sản phẩm công nghệ mới chưa được phép lưu hành*" (food that is a new-technology product not yet permitted for circulation) among foods subject to recall [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- **The gap.** We found no implementing text that defines "new-technology product" or says how permission to circulate is obtained [@REG-01; @REG-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. A new protein food therefore enters, in practice, through self-declaration against generic safety limits. An inspector could still argue that a fungus or alga with no food history is "not of the type used for food" (Art 5(1)) or an unpermitted new-technology product (Art 55(1)(c)). This is our interpretation. The result is legal uncertainty, not a clear route and not a ban [@REG-01; @REG-02] {VN-direct|Medium}.

### S9.3.2 Decree 15/2018/ND-CP: how market entry works today

Decree 15/2018/ND-CP of 2 February 2018 details the Food Safety Law. It applies as of 23 September 2026 [@REG-02; @REG-06] {VN-direct|High}.

**Self-declaration (Art 4 to 5).**

- It covers processed pre-packaged food, food additives, processing aids, and packaging in direct contact with food [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}.
- Exempt (Art 4(2)): products and raw materials made or imported only for export processing, or "*phục vụ cho việc sản xuất nội bộ*" (for the firm's own internal production) and not sold domestically [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}. This matters for imported protein ingredients used in-house.
- The dossier is a self-declaration form plus a test report from a designated or accredited lab, dated within 12 months [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}.
- The business may produce and sell right after filing with the authority designated by the provincial People's Committee, or after publishing the declaration [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}.

**Registration of the product declaration (Art 6 to 8).**

- Required for health supplements (*thực phẩm bảo vệ sức khỏe*), medical foods, special-diet foods, products for children up to 36 months, and "mixed additives with new uses or additives not on the MOH permitted list or not used as permitted" [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}.
- Health supplements are registered at MOH within 21 WD. The other categories take 7 WD [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}.
- The authority may ask for corrections once. The applicant then has 90 WD to respond [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}.

**GM food.** Art 9 refers to Decree 69/2010 for the GMO food-use certificate. Art 10 requires GM labelling when at least one GM ingredient is more than 5% of total ingredients [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}.

**What is missing.** No provision covers foods with no history of use, or new ingredients [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}.

**What the draft does, as described in Government press** [@REG2-05; @REG2-06] {VN-direct|Medium}:

- Four policy groups: reform of the management approach, stronger post-market control, digital transformation, and street food and catering.
- Two product groups. Group one: foods that must register a declaration or declare the applied standard (*công bố tiêu chuẩn áp dụng*) before circulation. Group two: foods that need no declaration. Both are inspected "throughout circulation on the market".
- The registration group named in the press is medical nutrition foods, foods for special diets, nutrition products for children up to 36 months, and health supplements.
- The drafters rejected requiring all pre-packaged processed foods to register a conformity declaration (the Decree 46/2026 approach) as "not suited to the management method".
- Advertising of medical nutrition foods and of products for children up to 36 months would be banned.

**What it lacks.** None of the draft summaries, the MOH submission or the stakeholder comments we read mentions *thực phẩm mới* (new food), new ingredients, history of use or a sandbox [@REG-08; @REG-10; @REG-16; @REG2-05; @REG2-06] {VN-direct|Medium}. We did not read the draft text itself or the WTO attachment. We do not know whether the recall ground in Art 55(1)(c) is carried over.

**Timing.** A trade source gave submission to the National Assembly in October 2026 and entry into force on 1 January 2027 [@REG2-04; @REG-12] {VN-direct|Medium}. The National Assembly Standing Committee settled the question on 24 September 2026: the law passes in two readings, with first comments at the October 2026 session and consideration for passage at the May 2027 session [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. So the 1 January 2027 start date cannot hold. The window to add a new-food clause runs from the October 2026 session into early 2027, while the draft is revised between readings (our reading) [@REG-12; @APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Our own search of the legal database on 23 September 2026 found no enacted law.

**What it means for new proteins.** Ordinary plant-based and fermented protein foods would fall in either the "declare the applied standard" group or the "no declaration" group. Neither involves pre-approval. So the new law, as described, keeps an open door but also keeps the uncertainty: there is still no way to get a new ingredient positively cleared. This is our reading [@REG2-05; @REG2-06] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## S9.4 GMO rules and precision fermentation (Decree 43/2026)

### S9.4.1 Instruments

The GMO rules sit in Decree 69/2010/ND-CP on biosafety, as amended by Decree 123/2018 and Decree 43/2026/ND-CP; Decree 43/2026 repealed Decrees 108/2011 and 118/2020 [@REG-18; @REG-24] {VN-direct|High}. Decree 43/2026 was issued on 26 January 2026 and took effect the same day. It amends Decree 65/2010 (Biodiversity Law), Decree 69/2010, Decree 59/2017 (genetic resources, Chapter III) and Decree 66/2019 (wetlands) [@REG-18; @REG-24] {VN-direct|High}.

> **Correction.** In v0.6 this said Decree 43/2026 does not amend Decree 59/2017 on genetic resources; the source says it does (Chapter III), and it also amends Decree 66/2019.

### S9.4.2 Definitions

- **Gene-edited organisms** (*sinh vật chỉnh sửa gen*) are now in scope. The Biodiversity Law, as amended by Law 146/2025/QH15, defines them as containing no new genetic material. Owners self-determine their status, report to MAE and keep records for 10 years [@REG-18; @REG-43] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Product of a GMO:** "*sản phẩm được tạo ra từ sinh vật biến đổi gen có chứa vật liệu di truyền hoặc protein từ gen biến đổi*" (a product created from a GMO that contains GM genetic material or protein from the modified gene) [@REG-18; @REG2-11] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Refined products.** USDA and ISAAA report that highly refined products such as soybean oil and corn starch are excluded from "GM food" [@REG-19; @REG-20] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. The text extract did not show an explicit exclusion, but the definition already excludes anything with no GM DNA and no GM protein. A precision-fermented protein is not excluded, because it is itself protein from the modified gene [@REG-18] {VN-direct|Medium}. See DG-050.

### S9.4.3 Certificate for food and feed use (merged procedure)

Decree 43/2026 merges the old Art 32 to 33 into one MAE procedure that certifies a GMO as eligible for use as food and feed [@REG-18; @REG-21] {VN-direct|High}.

- **Criteria.** A biosafety council finds no uncontrollable risk, or at least 5 developed OECD or G20 countries allow the event [@REG-18; @REG-23] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Steps.** 5 WD to accept the dossier and form the council; 67 WD for a full council review, or 17 WD on the 5-country route; for a food-use certificate, MOH is consulted after the council (3 WD to send, 7 WD to reply); then 5 WD to decide. Public comment runs 20 WD (10 WD on the 5-country route) alongside the council review [@REG-23; @REG-22; @REG-24] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Food implies feed.** A GMO approved as food qualifies as feed [@REG-22] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Who runs it.** MAE's procedure list places this procedure with the MAE Department of Science and Technology [@REG2-12] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **End-to-end time.** Agency steps add up to 37 WD (5-country route) or 87 WD (full review) for food use, and 32 or 82 WD for feed only, where the decision takes 10 WD and MOH is not consulted (our calculation). USDA gives 45 and 90 WD. We use about 45 or 90 WD end to end [@REG-19; @REG-23; @REG-24] {VN-direct|Medium}. See DG-049.

### S9.4.4 Closed-production certificate (new Art 37a)

Decree 43/2026 adds Art 37a to Decree 69/2010: "*Giấy chứng nhận sinh vật biến đổi gen đủ điều kiện sử dụng trong sản xuất khép kín*" (certificate that a GMO is eligible for use in closed production) [@REG-18] {VN-direct|High}. This is the certificate a precision-fermentation plant using a GM production strain in Vietnam would need.

| Item | What the text says | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Strain conditions (Art 37a(2) and (3)) | The GMO comes from safe donor and recipient organisms and has a safety history in closed production under OECD or G20 standards | [@REG2-11] {VN-direct\|High} |
| Facility conditions | A closed system that prevents release into the environment; waste and emission treatment licensed by the environmental authority; operation to OECD or G20 closed-production standards | [@REG2-11] {VN-direct\|High} |
| Dossier (Art 37a(4)) | Application form (*Mẫu* 32) and technical description (*Mẫu* 33) | [@REG2-11] {VN-direct\|High} |
| Filing | In person, by post or online, with MAE. Completeness check at once for in-person filing and within 1 WD otherwise. Incomplete dossiers are refused in writing | [@REG2-11] {VN-direct\|High} |
| Issuer | MAE issues, re-issues and revokes certificates | [@REG2-11] {VN-direct\|High} |

DG-111 recorded an issuing-level conflict; there is none, as both texts place the procedure at central level.

### S9.4.5 What this means for precision-fermented proteins

- A purified recombinant protein contains "protein from the modified gene", so it is probably a "product of a GMO" [@REG-18; @REG2-11] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- GM labelling then applies when it is more than 5% of total ingredients (Decree 15/2018 Art 10; Joint Circular 45/2015 Art 1(1)), but not to GM food in which neither the modified gene nor its product can be detected (Art 1(2)(a)) [@REG-02; @LBL-10] {VN-direct|High}. Lactoferrin in a Vietnamese formula is used at about 0.08% (80 mg per 100 g), about 60 times below the trigger (our calculation) [@LBL-25] {VN-direct|Medium}. So the label bites on high-inclusion uses, such as a drink whose main protein is precision-fermented; whether an identical recombinant protein counts as a "detectable product of the modified gene" is not settled by any text or letter.
- Making it in Vietnam with a GM microbe needs the Art 37a closed-production certificate [@REG-18; @REG2-11] {VN-direct|High}.
- No article covers products made with GMOs that contain no viable GMO. The food-use certificate is written for organisms, not products. So no text says whether a purified protein also needs a food-use certificate. Imported precision-fermented ingredients face the same labelling question [@REG2-11] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- Food-use certification of GMOs now sits fully with MAE, with MOH consulted [@REG-21; @REG-22] {VN-direct|Medium}.

> **Correction.** Earlier drafts did not treat precision-fermented proteins as GMO products. Under Decree 43/2026 they probably are, because the definition includes "protein from the modified gene" [@REG-18; @REG2-11] {VN-direct|Medium}. This is the single most important open legal question for precision fermentation in Vietnam. A joint written question to MAE and VFA is the cheapest way to close it.

## S9.7 Labelling, claims and fortification

- **The goods-label decree.** Decree 37/2026/ND-CP (23 January 2026) replaced Decree 43/2017/ND-CP and its amendment Decree 111/2021/ND-CP [@LBL-07; @LBL-08] {VN-direct|Medium}. Food labels still carry the name, ingredients or quantified ingredients, nutrition, *thông tin cảnh báo* (warning information), dates, storage and instructions for use [@REG-26; @LBL-09] {VN-direct|Medium}. We could not read the new label chapter (the text stopped at Art 32), so its article numbers are unknown; exact-phrase searches show that its text keeps the name, quantity and GM wording of the old decree [@LBL-09] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **The misleading-name and quantity rules, now verified.** Decree 43/2017 Art 11: "*Tên hàng hóa không được làm hiểu sai lệch về bản chất, công dụng và thành phần của hàng hóa*" (the name must not mislead about the nature, use or composition of the goods). Art 13(4): an ingredient whose name is used as, or as part of, the product name "*bắt buộc phải ghi định lượng*" (must be quantified), a rule carried into Decree 37/2026 [@LBL-06; @LBL-07; @LBL-08; @LBL-09] {VN-direct|Medium}. By our count from Vissanmart product pages, Vissan already prints the named meat's share on 28 of 29 meat-named formulations [@BUY-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **No allergen list.** The warning information in Appendix I item 2 of Decree 111/2021 had no defined content and no allergen list. Circular 05/2019/TT-BKHCN Art 9 covers technical specifications and warnings, also with no allergen list [@REG2-24; @REG2-25] {VN-direct|Medium}. So Vietnam has no mandatory allergen declaration list of the Codex type. Whether Circular 05/2019, which guided Decree 43/2017, still applies was not checked.
- **No reserved names.** We found no rule that reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese), and none that governs *chay* (vegetarian) or *thuần chay* (vegan) as names (our search of legal texts and standards, 25 September 2026). Decree 111/2021 had no such rule [@REG2-24] {VN-direct|Medium}; we did not read the label chapter of Decree 37/2026, which replaced it. TCVN 11216:2015 defines milk without a Codex-style clause restricting dairy terms, and QCVN 28:2026/BCT (Circular 09/2026/TT-BCT, in force 1 September 2026) covers liquid dairy products only, including skimmed milk blended with vegetable fat [@FTR-07; @LBL-16; @LBL-17] {VN-direct|Medium}. TCVN 12443:2018 is itself titled *Sữa đậu nành* (soy milk) and needs at least 2.0 g of protein per 100 ml for the name [@FTR-06] {VN-direct|Medium}. Makers qualify meat words, as in Vissan's *Heo hầm chay* (chay stewed pork) and *Ragu bò chay* [@BUY-05] {VN-direct|High}. The only state use of a new-meat name we found is *thịt nhân tạo* (artificial meat) for stem-cell meat in a 2025 Bac Ninh biotechnology scheme [@LBL-20] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Meat content.** TCVN 7049:2020 (heat-treated processed meat), TCVN 7050:2020 (not heat treated) and TCVN 7048:2020 (canned meat) set no minimum meat or protein content and do not mention plant protein [@LBL-11; @LBL-12; @LBL-13] {VN-direct|Medium}. A meat product with a plant or fungal extender keeps its name; if the name names the meat, the meat share must be printed.
- **Vegetarian and vegan claims.** TCVN 13625:2023 (ISO 23662:2021, voluntary) defines them. "Vegetarian" allows egg, milk and honey; "vegan" excludes every animal-derived input, including processing aids; the standard says nothing on alliums, so it is broader than Buddhist *chay* [@LBL-15] {VN-direct|High}. A false chay claim is already unlawful under the goods-label rules [@LBL-06] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **GM labelling:** above 5% of total ingredients, and not where neither the modified gene nor its product can be detected (Decree 15/2018 Art 10; Joint Circular 45/2015/TTLT-BNNPTNT-BKHCN Art 1) [@REG-02; @LBL-10] {VN-direct|High}. See S9.4.5.
- **Nutrition labelling.** Circular 29/2023/TT-BYT made energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat and sodium mandatory on non-exempt pre-packaged foods from 1 January 2026. Circular 30/2026/TT-BYT replaced it on 10 July 2026 with the same core rules: the protein reference value is 50 g, showing the percentage is optional, and foods made by small food businesses are exempt [@LBL-01; @LBL-02] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Claims.** TCVN 14429:2025 (voluntary, from Codex CAC/GL 23) sets "source of protein" at 10% of the reference value per 100 g, 5% per 100 kcal or 10% per serving, and "high" (*cao*) at twice that: 5 g and 10 g per 100 g, or 2.5 g and 5 g per 100 kcal (our calculation with the 50 g reference value in TCVN 7088:2025) [@LBL-03; @LBL-04] {VN-direct|High}. Claims for added micronutrients need 10% of the reference intake (Circular 43/2014 Art 8) [@LBL-05] {VN-direct|High}. A content claim needs no registration and no advertising approval; the 2025 Advertising Law amendment (Law 75/2025) and Decree 342/2025 add truthfulness and endorser duties, not approvals [@REG-02; @LBL-18; @LBL-19] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Fortification.** Decree 09/2016/ND-CP (in force) makes iodine, iron, zinc and vitamin A the mandatory fortificants: salt for eating and processing must be iodised, wheat flour used in food processing must carry iron and zinc, and soy, palm, rapeseed and peanut oil must carry vitamin A, except oil for industrial processing [@NQR-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. No protein food is a vehicle, so tofu, textured soy and soy milk carry nothing by rule. A 2025 amendment draft concerned iodised salt in fish sauce, and we found no amending decree [@NQR-03] {VN-direct|Low}. A voluntarily fortified food self-declares under Decree 15/2018 unless it is a health supplement, a medical food, a special-diet food or a nutrition product for children up to 36 months, which must register [@NQR-04] {VN-direct|High}.

The instruments are in `instruments.csv` (INS-067 to INS-078) and `label_rules.csv`.

> **Correction.** Wave 1 believed that the warning requirement covered allergens through guidance under Circular 05/2019/TT-BKHCN, but could not verify it. Wave 2 read Decree 111/2021 and Circular 05/2019 and found no allergen list in either [@REG2-24; @REG2-25] {VN-direct|Medium}. Voluntary allergen labelling is prudent for soy, wheat-gluten and insect-based foods because the law is silent.

> **Correction.** Earlier drafts cited Decree 43/2017 as amended by Decree 111/2021 as the label decree in force and Circular 29/2023 as the nutrition-label circular. Decree 37/2026 replaced the first on 23 January 2026 and Circular 30/2026 the second on 10 July 2026 [@LBL-08; @LBL-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. The rules that matter for new proteins carry over.

## S9.8 Halal

### S9.10.2 The permitted raw-material list

**Circular 21/2019/TT-BNNPTNT** (28 November 2019), amended by **Circular 31/2022/TT-BNNPTNT** (30 December 2022, effective 20 February 2023) [@REG-32; @REG-33] {VN-direct|High}:

### S9.10.3 Feed trials (Circular 94/2025/TT-BNNMT)

Circular 94/2025/TT-BNNMT (31 December 2025, effective 1 January 2026), Chapter III (Art 13 to 14) [@REG-34] {VN-direct|High}:

- Trial content: quality, safety or toxicity, and effects on animals. Methods suit each feed type, and duration is set in the protocol.
- DLPAH receives the protocol, forms an appraisal council within 15 WD, and approves within 5 WD after revision.
- Results recognition takes 15 + 5 WD.
- "*Kết quả khảo nghiệm được công nhận là căn cứ để đưa vào danh mục nguyên liệu được phép sử dụng làm thức ăn chăn nuôi*" (a recognised trial result is the basis for adding the material to the permitted list).
- It replaced parts of Circular 22/2019, Circular 21/2024 and Circular 09/2025.

The trial must be run at a qualified trial facility (Law Art 37(3)) [@REG-29] {VN-direct|High}. MAE merged its livestock trial and veterinary drug testing centres into the Central Livestock and Veterinary Trial and Testing Centre I (Hanoi) by Decision 3064/QĐ-BNNMT of 4 August 2026, effective 1 October 2026. The centre runs feed trials and may sell services [@INF-18] {VN-direct|High}.

### S9.10.4 Safety limits (QCVN 01-190:2020/BNNPTNT)

QCVN 01-190:2020/BNNPTNT (Circular 04/2020, amended by Circulars 08/2020 and 05/2021) sets maximum limits for arsenic, cadmium, lead, mercury, aflatoxin B1, *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, HCN, gossypol and other hazards in feed and raw materials. ### S9.10.6 Penalties (Decree 211/2026/ND-CP)

Decree 211/2026/ND-CP (16 June 2026, effective 5 August 2026) on administrative penalties in animal husbandry [@REG2-10] {VN-direct|High}:

- Art 22(1): "*Phạt tiền từ 10.000.000 đồng đến 15.000.000 đồng đối với hành vi sử dụng mỗi loại nguyên liệu không có trong Danh mục nguyên liệu được phép sử dụng*" (a fine of VND 10 to 15 million for using each type of raw material not in the permitted list), when the goods are worth under VND 200 million or the illicit profit is under VND 100 million.
- Art 22(2): VND 15 to 20 million at higher values.
- Additional penalty: suspension of feed production for 1 to 3 months.
- Remedies: surrender of illicit profit and forced repurposing of the material, or destruction if that is impossible.

At 26,000 VND per USD, VND 10 to 20 million is about USD 385 to 770 per material (our calculation). The fine is small, but the production suspension is not. For a maker of a new SCP or fungal biomass for feed, running the trial first (or waiting for a list update) is now clearly worth its cost.

### S9.10.7 Steps for a new single raw material (for example bacterial SCP or cassava-pulp fungal protein)

1. Check the permitted list (Circular 21/2019 Appendix VI as amended). If the material fits an existing entry (for example *nấm men*, yeast; *côn trùng*, insects; *bèo*, duckweed), no trial is needed [@REG-32; @REG-33] {VN-direct|High}.
2. If not, treat it as a "new substance". Run a trial under an approved protocol (Circular 94/2025 Chapter III) at a qualified facility. Importing trial samples may need a provincial import licence [@REG-34; @REG-35] {VN-direct|High}.
3. Get the result recognised by DLPAH, then wait for the annual list update or rely on the recognition decision [@REG-32; @REG-34] {VN-direct|High}. Whether recognition alone allows circulation before the list update is not stated in the texts read.
4. Get a certified factory (province), a declared standard, conformity with QCVN 01-190:2020, and a label [@REG-29; @REG-36; @REG-41] {VN-direct|High}.
5. If sold as supplement feed with a functional claim, get the province's appraisal (15 WD) [@REG-35] {VN-direct|High}.

**Documented agency time** is about 40 WD (protocol 15 + 5; results 15 + 5) [@REG-34] {VN-direct|High}. This excludes dossier preparation, the trial itself (likely one grow-out cycle per species) and the wait for the annual list update. **Our estimate of the total is 6 to 18 months.** It is an estimate, not a documented figure.

Law Art 37(2) exempts the recognised results of state research projects from separate trials [@REG-29] {VN-direct|High}. In version 0.7 this made publicly funded feed trials a candidate public good (policy option PO-021). Version 0.8 retires that option from the ranking, because feed is now context only.

### S9.11.3 Legal consequence: a trial trigger broader than practice

Read literally, Art 35(1) with a list that holds no raw materials catches every raw material, including fishmeal and soybean meal. Conventional feeds are evidently sold without trials, so practice differs from the text [@REG2-01; @REG2-02] {VN-direct|High}. The body of Circular 16/2026 does not say that livestock-list materials, or conventional materials, count as permitted in aquafeed [@REG2-01] {VN-direct|High}.

Our reading has two branches {VN-direct|Medium}:

- (a) the regulator treats conventional raw materials as outside the list requirement by practice, in which case insect meal and yeast protein probably are too; or
- (b) any unlisted raw material formally needs a trial.

The texts do not settle this. A written DFFI opinion is the cheapest fix. See DG-112. We found no penalty decree for aquafeed matching Decree 211/2026; this was not checked.

## S9.12 Tax and investment incentives

### S9.12.1 Corporate income tax

**Law on Corporate Income Tax 67/2025/QH15** (14 June 2025; effective 1 October 2025; applies from the 2025 tax period) [@REG-45] {VN-direct|Medium}:

- Art 12(2): incentives for (a) application of high technology under the High Technology Law and strategic technology; (e) high-tech enterprises, high-tech agriculture enterprises and science and technology enterprises; (i), (k), (l) agriculture and agricultural or aquatic processing meeting Art 4 conditions.
- Art 13(1): a 10% rate for 15 years for categories (a) to (e). Agricultural processing gets 10% or 15%, depending on location.
- Art 14(1): 4 years' exemption and a 50% cut for 9 years.
- Art 4: exempts some agricultural processing income in disadvantaged areas, and gives up to 3 years' exemption for products from newly applied technology.

**Decree 320/2025/ND-CP** (15 December 2025) guides the law and applies from the 2025 tax period [@REG2-18] {VN-direct|High}. Two tests matter for alternative protein:

- Art 4(1)(b): processed agricultural and aquatic products qualify for the exemption only if "*tỷ lệ giá trị nguyên vật liệu là nông sản, thủy sản trên chi phí sản xuất ... từ 30% trở lên*" (agricultural or aquatic raw materials are at least 30% of production cost), and the product is not subject to special consumption tax [@REG2-18] {VN-direct|High}. A cassava-based fermentation plant likely passes; a plant running on imported refined sugar may not. This is our interpretation {VN-direct|Medium}.
- Art 4(4)(b): "*Thu nhập từ bán sản phẩm làm ra từ công nghệ mới lần đầu tiên áp dụng ở Việt Nam được miễn thuế 03 năm*" (income from products made with technology applied for the first time in Vietnam is exempt for 3 years), subject to confirmation by the competent authority [@REG2-18] {VN-direct|High}. This could suit a first precision-fermentation or gas-fermentation plant.
- The incentive chapter (10% for 15 years; 4 years' exemption plus 9 years at 50% for new projects applying listed high technology) did not render, so the certification criteria remain unread [@REG2-18] {VN-direct|Medium}. MOF Decision 517/QĐ-BTC (20 March 2026) corrected Decree 320/2025 Art 20(1)(a), Art 23(10) and Art 24(2). We checked the decision on thuvienphapluat.vn; it is not yet in our source list {VN-direct|Medium}.

**VAT on feed.** Under the VAT Law 48/2024/QH15 (Art 5(3), in force 1 July 2025), animal feed and aquafeed are "not subject to VAT" [@COST-24] {VN-direct|High}. A plant making a feed protein therefore cannot reclaim VAT on its equipment, power and inputs; a food-ingredient plant can. Wave 1 estimated this adds roughly 5 to 10% to taxed input costs (our estimate; see [[app-s12-costs]]). This is now context: the related option (PO-029, input-VAT neutrality for new feed ingredients) is retired from the ranking.

### S9.12.3 Investment Support Fund (Decree 182/2024/ND-CP)

Decree 182/2024/ND-CP (31 December 2024), Art 18 [@REG-46] {VN-direct|High}:

- High-tech product projects qualify with at least VND 12,000 bn of capital or VND 20,000 bn of annual revenue. Semiconductor and AI projects qualify at VND 6,000 bn or VND 10,000 bn.
- R&D centres need at least VND 3,000 bn, of which VND 1,000 bn within 3 years.

At 26,000 VND per USD, the thresholds are about USD 460 million of capital or USD 770 million of revenue for a high-tech product project, and about USD 115 million for an R&D centre (our calculation). ## S9.15 Environment and carbon

- **Environmental impact assessment (EIA).** The Law on Environmental Protection 72/2020/QH14 is amended by Law 146/2025 from 1 January 2026. Decree 08/2022/ND-CP is amended by Decree 05/2025 (6 January 2025) and Decree 48/2026 (29 January 2026). Decree 48/2026 changes permit adjustment and phased EIA [@REG-58; @REG-43] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **The EIA gap.** The appendices with capacity thresholds for food, fermentation, feed and seafood processing (Appendices II to V) did not render in either wave. We can confirm only that Appendix II classifies activities by capacity (large, medium, small) and that Group I projects need an EIA. So we cannot say at what size a fermentation or feed plant needs a full EIA [@REG-58; @REG2-19] {VN-direct|Medium}. Decree 05/2025 moved some EIA appraisal and permitting to provinces from 6 January 2025 [@REG2-19] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Carbon market.** Decree 06/2022 as amended by Decree 119/2025 (9 June 2025) runs a pilot carbon market until the end of 2028, with full operation from 2029. Pilot quotas cover thermal power, steel and cement. Offsets can cover up to 30% of quotas, and Article 6.2 and 6.4 mechanisms are provided for. The extract says agriculture and food processing are not in the initial quota scope [@REG-59] {VN-direct|Medium}. Direct carbon revenue for alternative protein is unlikely before 2029, except through voluntary or Article 6 credits under an approved method. Whether any large food or feed facility is on the greenhouse gas inventory list is unresolved (DG-054).
- **Grid emissions.** The latest official grid emission factor, as of January 2026, is 0.6592 tCO2/MWh for 2023, published on 3 December 2024 [@COST-08; @COST-09] {VN-direct|High}. A power-intensive process in Vietnam carries a high Scope 2 footprint unless it buys renewable power.

## S9.16 Biodiversity, access and benefit sharing

- **Law on Biodiversity 20/2008/QH12**, amended by Law 146/2025/QH15 (effective 1 January 2026), adds a definition of gene-edited organisms and restructures Art 57: a permit is needed for foreign access, domestic commercial access and export of genetic resources [@REG-43; @REG-61] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Decree 59/2017/ND-CP** (12 May 2017) is still in force as amended, including by Decree 43/2026 (consolidated text 19/VBHN-BNNMT, 2026) [@REG-60; @REG-24] {VN-direct|High}. The points below are from the 2017 text and were not rechecked against the amendments:
  - Commercial research and product development need a licence (Art 7).
  - Registration is confirmed in 10 days. Non-commercial review takes 30 days. For commercial use, a council reviews within 90 days and a decision follows within 15 days.
  - A contract with the provider is certified by the commune People's Committee.
  - Benefit sharing is at least 1% of annual product revenue, or at least 2% of the value of an IP transfer (Art 22). The split is 30% to the provider and 70% to the state budget.
  - Derivatives are defined (Art 3(6)), and obligations pass on transfer (Art 14).
  - There is no provision on digital sequence information.
  - Microorganisms are not excluded, so microbes isolated from Vietnamese environments (for example from fermented foods or cassava waste) are in scope.
  - The licensing ministry is now MAE.
- **Practical point.** A startup that commercialises a strain isolated in Vietnam should budget for the licence and for benefit sharing of at least 1% of product revenue [@REG-60] {VN-direct|High}.

## S9.20 How a new protein gets to market: a plain-language explainer

These are the routes as the law stood on 23 September 2026. They may change when the new Food Safety Law and its decree take effect. The National Assembly considers passage at its May 2027 session, so not before mid-2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Sections S9.3 to S9.13 describe each step in detail, with sources. Routes 1 to 6 and 13 are for food. Routes 7 to 12 are for feed; they are now context and the record.

**1. Plant-based food from known ingredients** (tofu, textured soy, wheat gluten, jackfruit, *đồ chay*, plant milks).
Make it in a food-safe factory. Test the product at an accredited lab. File a self-declaration with the authority named by the province. You can sell right away [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}. We found no rule that stops you calling it *chay* or using words such as *sữa hạt* or *heo* with a qualifier, but do not mislead, and quantify any ingredient named in the product name [@REG2-24; @LBL-06] {VN-direct|Medium}. Print protein in the nutrition panel; a "high protein" claim needs 10 g per 100 g under the voluntary TCVN 14429:2025 [@LBL-02; @LBL-03] {VN-direct|High}. Because Vietnam has no mandatory allergen list, add soy and wheat warnings voluntarily. Many brands already use this route.

**2. Koji or other traditional mould fermentation on a food substrate** (*Aspergillus oryzae*, *Rhizopus*).
The route is the same as for plant-based food. The organisms have a long history of food use in Vietnam (*tương*, *nước tương*) and elsewhere, so legal risk is low [@REG-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Keep strain identity and safety data on file. The science matters here: *A. oryzae* cannot make aflatoxin but can make cyclopiazonic acid, so use a characterised strain (see [[app-s13-science]]).

**3. Fungal biomass or mycoprotein food.**
Legally, the only route is the same self-declaration. There is no way to get the ingredient pre-approved [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}. If the fungus has no food history, an inspector could argue it is "not a food-type raw material" (Law Art 5(1)) or a "new-technology product not yet permitted" (Art 55(1)(c)) [@REG-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. Edible-mushroom species carry lower risk than new strains. Build a safety dossier and talk to VFA before launch.

**4. Microalgae food** (spirulina, chlorella).
Either self-declare as an ordinary food, or register as a health supplement at MOH (21 WD, GMP-certified factory) [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}. Newer algae species carry the same uncertainty as new fungi.

**5. Precision-fermented protein food.**
An imported ingredient can be self-declared, or is exempt from declaration if used only in your own production [@REG-02] {VN-direct|High}. Label it as GM if it is more than 5% of total ingredients, because a recombinant protein is probably a "product of a GMO"; whether an identical recombinant protein counts as "detectable", which would decide the label, is unsettled [@REG-02; @REG2-11; @LBL-10] {VN-direct|Medium}. If it works as an additive or enzyme not on the MOH list, register it with VFA (7 WD) [@REG-02; @REG-25] {VN-direct|High}. Making it in Vietnam with a GM microbe needs MAE's new closed-production certificate: Forms 32 and 33, a contained facility that prevents release, licensed waste treatment and OECD or G20 standards [@REG2-11] {VN-direct|High}. The decree gives about 60 WD for the certificate (5 + 50 + 5 WD); the fee is not published [@REG-24] {VN-direct|Medium}. No text says whether a GMO food-use certificate is also needed, so ask MAE and MOH in writing before investing.

**6. Cultivated meat or seafood.**
There is no national route. The law implies that new-technology food needs "permission to circulate", but there is no procedure to obtain it [@REG-01] {VN-direct|High}. Any genetically modified cell line adds GMO questions. The most concrete option is a supervised pilot in Hanoi's sandbox under the biotechnology field, with MOH consulted [@REG2-16; @REG2-17] {VN-direct|Medium}. Whether a city sandbox can set aside national food law is untested.

**7. Insect meal for livestock and poultry feed.**
Insects are on the permitted raw-material list, so no feeding trial is needed [@REG-32] {VN-direct|High}. You need a certified feed factory (province), a declared standard, conformity with the QCVN 01-190:2020 limits, and a label [@REG-29; @REG-36; @REG-41] {VN-direct|High}. Entobel has used this route since 2019 [@REG-44] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**8. Insect meal, yeast protein, duckweed or microalgae for shrimp and fish feed.**
None of these is on the aquafeed list as a raw material, because the list has no raw-material section. The Fisheries Law, read literally, requires a trial for any unlisted raw material [@REG2-01; @REG2-02] {VN-direct|High}. Practice evidently differs, because conventional feeds are sold without trials. Ask DFFI in writing whether your ingredient needs an aquafeed trial before you build a product on it.

**9. Duckweed or algae meal for livestock feed.**
*Bèo* (duckweed) and *tảo* (algae) are on the livestock list, so the route is the same as for insect meal [@REG-32] {VN-direct|High}. If grown on wastewater, test heavy metals and pathogens against QCVN 01-190:2020 [@REG-36] {VN-direct|High}.

**10. Yeast protein for livestock feed.**
Inactive yeasts, including Torula or Candida yeast and yeast grown on molasses, and by-products of amino-acid fermentation, have been on the livestock list since February 2023. The route is the same as for insect meal [@REG-33] {VN-direct|High}.

**11. Bacterial SCP or another new microbial protein for livestock feed.**
This is probably a "new substance". Run a feed trial under a DLPAH-approved protocol (15 + 5 WD to approve), then get the result recognised (15 + 5 WD). The material then enters the permitted list at the next annual update [@REG-29; @REG-32; @REG-34] {VN-direct|High}. Our estimate of the total is 6 to 18 months; that is an estimate, not a legal deadline. Do not sell it before it is listed: since 5 August 2026 the fine is VND 10 to 20 million per unlisted material, plus 1 to 3 months' suspension of feed production [@REG2-10] {VN-direct|High}.

**12. SCP for aquafeed.**
Register an aquafeed trial (20-day dossier appraisal), run it, and get the results recognised by the provincial People's Committee chair (10 WD). Importing trial material needs a provincial licence. Then declare the aquafeed products that contain it [@REG-37; @REG-38; @REG-41] {VN-direct|High}.

**13. A new food additive, enzyme or processing aid.**
Register the product declaration at VFA (7 WD), or ask MOH to amend the additive list with safety evidence [@REG-02; @REG-25] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Three lessons from the regional record [@RGN-19; @RGN-21; @RGN-32] {VN-adjacent|Medium}:

- **Korea shows a route that does not need a new law.** A safety-assessment step can be added inside an existing instrument, with a published fee and review time.
- **Thailand shows how to borrow assessors.** An existing science body can be designated as the assessment unit instead of building a new agency.
- **A framework is not enough.** Thailand and Korea have routes but had no cultivated-meat approval by September 2026. Staff capacity and statutory timelines matter as much as the legal text.
