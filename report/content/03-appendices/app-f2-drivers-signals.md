# F2. Drivers, shocks, signals and wildcards to 2050

**What this appendix contains.** The evidence behind [[ch20-drivers-2050]] and [[ch23-scenarios-2050]]. Sections F2.1 to F2.5 give the drivers by domain (trade, disease, energy, demography, climate). Section F2.6 sorts them into near-certain trends and critical uncertainties and explains the two scenario axes. Sections F2.7 to F2.11 are the monitoring registers: 16 signposts, 43 signals, 26 wildcards, 22 external forecasts and 17 past shocks.

**How to use it.** To see which 2050 world is unfolding, start with F2.7. To check a number in chapter 20, use F2.1 to F2.5. Status is as of 24 September 2026 unless stated.

> **Method note.** Foresight tags follow [[app-m2-futures-method]]. We give probabilities only where a named forecaster published them (NOAA, Tigchelaar et al., Rethink Priorities). Our arithmetic is marked "our calculation" and shown with its inputs; the geo-macro series are written by `working-papers/wave3/geo_macro/build_csvs.py`. Disagreements show the position taken and are logged in [[app-r2-disagreements]].

### F2.1.2 Purchase commitments

Vietnamese firms signed 20 memorandums to buy "over $2.9 billion" of US farm goods [@GEO-02] {VN-direct|High}; the United States already supplied 43.6% of Vietnam's soybeans and 72% of its DDGS in 2025 [@MAC-09; @MAC-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. China committed to buy at least 25 Mt of US soybeans a year in 2026 to 2028 (compliance not verified) [@GEO-06] {general|High} {fx:projection} (official commitment), which pushes Brazilian soy towards other buyers, including Vietnam. In 2026 to 2028, deals as well as prices will set Vietnam's origins {general|Medium} {fx:signal}.

### F2.1.6 EU rules

- **EUDR** applies from 30 December 2026 (large and medium operators, and micro and small operators already under the EU Timber Regulation) and 30 June 2027 (other micro and small operators) to soy entering the EU, not to meat or fish from soy-fed animals (our reading) [@GEO-07; @GEO-08] {general|High}. Uncertified soy sold to Asia could be slightly cheaper in 2027 to 2030; no study measures this {general|Low} {fx:estimate}.
- **CBAM** covers cement, steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen; the proposed extension adds 180 downstream, metal-intensive goods (Commission proposal; 200 in the Council position and 457 in the Parliament's ENVI committee report), with a yearly review from 2028 in the Council position, and no food or feed [@GEO-09; @HSC-32] {general|High} {fx:projection}. The 2028 review is the signpost (WC-15).

### F2.1.8 Import duties

Soybean meal and feed maize carry 0% MFN duty since 31 March 2025 [@REG2-21] {VN-direct|Medium}. Food-protein ingredients carry MFN duties of 3% (HS 3504), 5% (HS 2106.10) and 7 to 10% (yeasts and dead single-cell organisms, HS 2102) [@REG2-20; @REG2-23] {VN-direct|Medium}, but these apply only to origins without a trade agreement. Chinese and ASEAN product pays 0% on HS 1109, 1208.10, 2106.10, 3504, 2102.10, 2102.20 and 2304 under ACFTA, ATIGA and RCEP in 2025 to 2027, and Korean protein ingredients pay 0% under AKFTA [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. So SCP from China and ASEAN enters at 0%, and local SCP has no tariff edge over Chinese product; with these schedules already at 0%, we expect no tariff shelter to reappear after 2027 (our reading) {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:trend}. This matters for WC-08: dumping of Chinese microbial protein would meet no duty barrier. China shipped 4,236 t of HS 2102.20 to Vietnam in 2025, up from 1,899 t in 2019 [@FBA-17] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## F2.2 Disease

### F2.2.1 African swine fever

| Year | Outbreaks (provinces) | Other magnitude | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 8,553 of 10,614 communes; all provinces | Nearly 6 million pigs died or culled (over 20% of the herd); modelled price rises of 45% (traditional sector) and 11% (modern); pigs traded halved (trader interviews) | [@GEO-14; @GEO-15] {VN-direct\|High} |
| 2020 to 2022 | 1,569 (50); 3,029 (59); 1,229 (53) | | [@GEO-15] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| 2023 | 952 (46) | Recombinant genotype I/II virus in 6 northern provinces | [@GEO-15; @GEO-16] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| 2024 | 1,669 (48) | Recombinant share of positive samples (north and central) 14.1% (2023) to 42.2% (2024) | [@GEO-15; @GEO-17] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| 2025 | Returned; most provinces by the third quarter | About 1.2 million pigs culled in the year | [@MAC-37; @MAC-01; @MAC-12] {VN-direct\|Medium} |

Two live attenuated vaccines were licensed in 2022 and a 2026 review lists three; nearly 6 million doses were produced, distributed or exported by June 2024, but adoption is "limited" [@GEO-15; @GEO-19] {VN-direct|Medium}. Genotype II vaccine strains were "unable to completely protect" pigs against the recombinant strain [@GEO-18] {VN-direct|Medium}; reversion, shedding and sow safety remain concerns [@HSC-31] {VN-direct|Medium}. Official promotion implies control; peer-reviewed work shows partial protection. **Position:** assume a major wave every two to three years to 2035, each cutting feed demand for a few quarters and speeding consolidation {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:trend}.

### F2.2.3 The antibiotic ban and the additive market

- Growth promoters were banned in feed from 2018; from 1 January 2026 antibiotics may only treat diagnosed disease [@GEO-24] {VN-direct|Medium}. About 71.7% of antimicrobials used in Vietnam in 2015 went to animals [@MAC-36] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- World use in food animals is projected to rise 8%, from 99,502 t (2020) to 107,472 t (2030), with 67% of hotspots in Asia [@GEO-25] {general|High} {fx:projection} (published model).
- The livestock strategy encourages "biological products that replace antibiotics" [@REG-54] {VN-direct|Medium}. We found no 2030 target for antimicrobial use in animals.
- **Reading.** The ban opens a 2026 to 2030 market for fermentation-made probiotics, yeast products and enzymes, the lowest-regulatory-risk use of Vietnamese fermentation capacity {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Its effect is not yet measured.

### F2.4.4 Just transition: feedstock incomes, not factory jobs

- Small pig and poultry households are already exiting (F2.2, F2.4.3). A shift on the S-ALT scale (10% of meat demand replaced by 2050) would add little to that pressure {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.
- Fermentation creates few jobs: labour is 4 to 5% of cost in regional plant models [@RGN-01] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.
- The income at stake is feedstock. If all S-ALT glucose came from cassava, 2050 would need about 5.37 Mt of fresh roots on about 262,000 ha, mostly from smallholders (`balance_outputs.csv`, BLO-1130, BLO-1131) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Contract terms for cassava, rice by-products and brewery residues decide who gains; about 58.8 million people will still be rural in 2050 [@GEO-32] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:projection}.

> **Correction.** In v0.6 this said labour is 7% (biomass) to 17% (precision fermentation) of cost; the source gives 5% and 4%, and 7% and 17% are the utilities shares.

### F2.5.4 Humid heat and fermentation cooling

Heat-stress days rose 0.8 to 4.2 per decade (interquartile range across stations) in 1979 to 2018 [@CLM-17] {VN-direct|Medium}. **The cultivated-meat forecast record.**
- **Consultancy forecasts ran far too high.** McKinsey's 2030 path needs 220 to 440 million L of capacity; in 2026 the largest run is 20,000 L and nothing sells at commercial scale [@HSC-21; @FTB-24; @HSC-12] {general|Medium}.
- **Past timelines were optimistic:** "very systematic overconfidence" [@HSC-19] {general|Medium}. A fetched summary said about 3% were right; **position:** use the source's counts (11%).
- **The only probabilistic forecast is sober:** a 54% chance that world output stays below 100,000 t a year through 2051 (2021 panel, conditional on no transformative AI) [@HSC-18] {general|Medium} {fx:projection}.
- EAT-Lancet 2019 and 2025 figures must not be mixed. We use these forecasts as benchmarks, never as inputs.

**Lessons.** Vietnam's protein supply met a large shock every one to two years in 2019 to 2026, so plan for recurring shocks, not a smooth trend, to 2035 {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:trend}. Each shock moved output from households to integrators, the buyers best able to specify new ingredients. Energy and freight mattered as much as grain in 2026. Policy shocks move both ways. Losses fell: an incomplete April 2024 count found 1,580 ha of rice at risk of lower yield (43 ha lost) in a milder season, against 26,000 ha of winter-spring rice lost in 2020 [@CLM-36; @CLM-37] {VN-direct|Medium}.
