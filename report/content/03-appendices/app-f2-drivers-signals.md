# F2. Drivers, shocks, signals and wildcards to 2050

**In brief.**
- **Most drivers reach the protein people eat through three doors:** the imported feed behind domestic meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish; animal disease; and climate at home. Feed appears here as context, as the hidden import behind that food.
- **African swine fever (ASF) is now structural.** In 2025, recombinant strains made up 79% of typed virus samples in the north. The Ministry of Agriculture and Environment (MAE) says the licensed vaccines give no protection against them [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}. Disease is a shock to pork supply, prices and food safety, not a national protein shortfall (F2.2).
- **Consumers felt disease more than imported feed.** Pork prices rose 57.23% in 2020 after ASF; in 2022, the year world feed prices peaked, they fell 10.68% [@SEC-08; @SEC-09] {VN-direct|High}.
- **Wild fish is under more pressure from fishing and policy than from climate to 2050** (F2.5.6).
- **We found no study that measures lower disease or pandemic risk from replacing animal-source food.** At the scale demand supports, alternative protein does not change disease exposure (F2.2.4).
- **The evidence points both ways on both scenario axes,** so we assign no probabilities (F2.7).

**What this appendix contains.** The evidence behind [[ch20-drivers-2050]] and [[ch23-scenarios-2050]].
- Sections F2.1 to F2.5 give the drivers by domain: trade, disease, energy, population and climate.
- Section F2.6 sorts them into near-certain trends and critical uncertainties, and explains the two scenario axes.
- Sections F2.7 to F2.11 are the monitoring registers: 16 signposts, 43 signals, 26 wildcards, 22 external forecasts and 20 past shocks.

The study is about protein for people. Signals, wildcards and shocks that matter only for feed ingredients stay in the registers as the record, marked as context. Row codes come from the data files: SP (signposts), SIG (signals), WC (wildcards), EXF (external forecasts) and SHK (shocks). DG numbers are disagreements in [[app-r2-disagreements]] and OQ numbers are open questions in [[app-r1-open-questions]]. Plays (T codes) and other codes are decoded in [[front-how-to-read]].

**How to use it.** To see which 2050 world is unfolding, start with F2.7. To check a number in chapter 20, use F2.1 to F2.5. Status is as of 24 September 2026 unless a later date is given.

> **Method note.** Foresight tags follow [[app-m2-futures-method]]. We give probabilities only where a named forecaster published them: the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), Tigchelaar et al. and Rethink Priorities. Our arithmetic is marked "our calculation" and shown with its inputs; the geo-macro series are written by `working-papers/wave3/geo_macro/build_csvs.py`, and the disease scale comparisons by `working-papers/wave11/L2-disease-pandemic/calc_scale.py`. Disagreements show the position taken and are logged in [[app-r2-disagreements]].

### F2.1.6 EU rules

- **The EU deforestation regulation (EUDR)** applies from 30 December 2026 (large and medium operators, and micro and small operators already under the EU Timber Regulation) and 30 June 2027 (other micro and small operators) to soy entering the EU, not to meat or fish from soy-fed animals (our reading) [@GEO-07; @GEO-08] {general|High}. - **The EU carbon border adjustment mechanism (CBAM)** covers cement, steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen; the proposed extension adds 180 downstream, metal-intensive goods (Commission proposal; 200 in the Council position and 457 in the report of the Parliament's environment committee), with a yearly review from 2028 in the Council position, and no food or feed [@GEO-09; @HSC-32] {general|High} {fx:projection}. The 2028 review is the signpost (WC-15).

### F2.1.8 Import duties

Soybean meal and feed maize carry 0% most-favoured-nation (MFN) duty, the rate for origins without a trade agreement, since 31 March 2025 [@REG2-21] {VN-direct|Medium}. Food-protein ingredients carry MFN duties of 3% (harmonised system code HS 3504), 5% (HS 2106.10) and 7 to 10% (yeasts and dead single-cell organisms, HS 2102) [@REG2-20; @REG2-23] {VN-direct|Medium}, but these apply only to origins without a trade agreement. Products from China and the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) pay 0% on HS 1109, 1208.10, 2106.10, 3504, 2102.10, 2102.20 and 2304 in 2025 to 2027. They do so under the ASEAN-China, ASEAN and Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership agreements (ACFTA, ATIGA and RCEP). Korean protein ingredients pay 0% under the ASEAN-Korea agreement (AKFTA) [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. So single-cell protein (SCP) from China and ASEAN enters at 0%, and local SCP has no tariff edge over Chinese product; with these schedules already at 0%, we expect no tariff shelter to reappear after 2027 (our reading) {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:trend}. This matters for WC-08: dumping of Chinese microbial protein would meet no duty barrier. China shipped 4,236 t of HS 2102.20 to Vietnam in 2025, up from 1,899 t in 2019 [@FBA-17] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**Vietnam's official frame is production volume, rice first, with more animal food.** Resolution 34/NQ-CP (25 March 2021) on national food security to 2030 sets these targets for 2030 [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection} (official target):

|---|---|---|---|
| Rice land; paddy | 3.5 million ha; at least 35 Mt a year | Not checked here | [@SEC-01] {VN-direct\|High} |
| Carcass meat | 6.0 to 6.5 Mt | Not checked here | [@SEC-01] {VN-direct\|High} |
| Fresh milk | 2.6 Mt | 1.3 Mt in 2025; over 1.37 Mt planned for 2026, about 53% of the target (our calculation) | [@SEC-01; @SEC-02; @MAC-14] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Eggs | 23 billion | 21.4 billion in 2025 | [@SEC-01; @MAC-14] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Aquatic products | 9 to 10 Mt | 9.95 Mt in 2025, inside the band | [@SEC-01; @MAC-15] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| Nutrition | Energy above 2,500 kcal per person per day; under-5 stunting below 19%; less rice and more meat, milk, eggs, fish, vegetables and fruit in diets | Not checked here | [@SEC-01] {VN-direct\|High} |

- The resolution names climate change, disasters, pollution and transboundary epidemics as threats. It does not name imports, trade disruption or feed. It treats imports only as a "flexible" management tool, and sets no import, feed, soybean, protein or self-sufficiency indicator [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- MAE's plan for 2026 (official letter 6034/BNNMT-KHTC, 26 August 2025) adds a livestock task: "step by step reduce import dependence by developing domestic raw-material areas" [@SEC-02] {VN-direct|High}. It is the nearest official hook for a measure of how much of the protein people eat comes from domestic inputs. We found no official text that names alternative protein as a food-security tool, and no mid-term review of Resolution 34 (OQ-455).
- **Peers.** China's 2024 opinions on the "big food view" seek "calories and protein from plants, animals and microbes". They promote microbial cell protein mainly for feed, advance new foods "steadily and cautiously" and push sugar from non-grain biomass. They aim for a diversified food supply "fully built" by 2035 [@SEC-04] {general|High} {fx:projection} (official target, qualitative). China's 2026 No. 1 policy document keeps biomanufacturing but does not repeat "new food resources" [@SEC-03] {general|High}.
- **Reading.** Peers that import feed (China, the EU) treat new protein mainly as a way to replace imported feed protein with domestic resources. We read none of them as counting alternative protein made from imported inputs as security [@SEC-03; @SEC-04; @VIS-15] {general|Medium}.

## F2.2 Disease

Disease reaches the protein people eat in three ways: pork and poultry supply and prices; food safety; and, rarely, infection of people. The events in detail, with losses, price effects and human cases, are in `disease_shocks.csv`.

### F2.2.1 African swine fever

|---|---|---|---|

A 2026 study counts 8,509 of 11,055 (77%) (DG-353) | Nearly 6 million pigs died or culled (over 20% of the herd); modelled price rises of 45% (traditional sector) and 11% (modern); pigs traded halved (trader interviews) | [@GEO-14; @GEO-15; @PAN-36] {VN-direct\|High} |
| 2020 to 2022 | 1,569 (50); 3,029 (59); 1,229 (53) | | [@GEO-15] {VN-direct\|Medium} |
| 2023 | 952 (46) | Recombinant genotype I/II virus in 6 northern provinces | [@GEO-15; @GEO-16] {VN-direct\|Medium} |

**The 2019 shock in protein terms: large for pork, small for protein.** USDA, citing official data, puts the 2019 culls at over 5.9 million pigs, about 23% of the herd. Live-weight pig output fell 13.8%, and pork supplied in the first quarter of 2020 was 19.3% below a year earlier. In 2019 poultry output rose 16.5%, and pork's share of meat output fell from 71.5% to 65.6% [@PAN-03] {VN-direct|High}. In early 2020 the government ordered about 100,000 t of pork imports for the quarter and asked for "pork substitutes and frozen pork" (Notice 132/TB-VPCP) [@PAN-03] {VN-direct|High}.

**People switched to other animal protein, not plant protein.** In 2019 and again in 2025, consumers moved to chicken [@PAN-03; @MAC-01] {VN-direct|High}. Hanoi traders reported a month of pork avoidance and a shift to tofu and vegetables in March 2019 [@PAN-34] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:stated}. But household tofu use was the same in 2018 and 2020 [@DIE-05] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}. **Position:** follow the revealed figure; the press report shows a short panic shift (DG-355).

**Prices moved both ways.** Live pig prices first collapsed to VND 24,000 to 35,000 per kg in May 2019, on panic selling and pork avoidance [@PAN-04] {VN-direct|Medium}. They then reached a record above VND 90,000 per kg in late December 2019 [@PAN-03] {VN-direct|High}. In 2020 consumer pork prices rose 57.23% (F2.11, SHK-018). In 2025 the effect ran the other way: culling sales caused local oversupply, and live pigs fell to about VND 50,000 per kg in November 2025 [@MAC-01] {VN-direct|High}.

**The modelled cost.** The only national estimate is a published model. A 10% fall in pig output cuts GDP by 0.36% (USD 0.88 billion, 2018 base) and costs about 247,000 jobs. Falls of 25% and 50% cut GDP by 0.90% and 1.80% [@GEO-14] {VN-direct|Medium}. The actual 2019 fall (13.8%) lies between the 10% and 25% cases (our reading). Smallholders draw 20% to 30% of their income from pigs, medium and large farms 50% to 100% [@GEO-14] {VN-direct|Medium}. We found no peer-reviewed loss estimate for the 2025 wave.

**Vaccines.** Two live attenuated vaccines were licensed in 2022 and a 2026 review lists three; nearly 6 million doses were produced, distributed or exported by June 2024, but adoption is "limited" [@GEO-15; @GEO-19] {VN-direct|Medium}. About 7.8 million doses had been supplied by August 2025, but MAE called the vaccination rate "still low" [@PAN-06] {VN-direct|Medium}. Genotype II vaccine strains were "unable to completely protect" pigs against the recombinant strain [@GEO-18] {VN-direct|Medium}; reversion, shedding and sow safety remain concerns [@HSC-31] {VN-direct|Medium}.

In September 2025 the MAE Department of Livestock Production and Animal Health went further. Its official letter says the three licensed vaccines (NAVET-ASFVAC, AVAC ASF LIVE and Dacovet-ASF2) are made from genotype II virus and "have no protective effect" against other genotypes. It now calls biosecurity "the foundation" of control and vaccination "an important supporting tool", chosen by local genotype surveillance [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}. A 2026 review finds that genotype II vaccine candidates give "limited cross-protective immunity", and that tests built for genotype II may miss recombinants [@PAN-08] {general|Medium}. A 2026 comparison describes Vietnam's control as "reliance on partial live-attenuated vaccination", against Thailand's physical biosecurity [@PAN-37] {VN-adjacent|Medium}.

> **Correction.** In v0.7 this section said: "Official promotion implies control; peer-reviewed work shows partial protection." MAE's own letter of 12 September 2025 says the licensed vaccines give no protection against the recombinant strains, which made up most typed samples in 2025 [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}.

**A signpost has been crossed.** The study's 2026 horizon scan set a signpost of a recombinant share above 50% of outbreaks. The 2025 typed surveillance samples crossed it in the north (79%) and the centre (76%) [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}. These are samples, not outbreaks, but they are the official measure. Escape has happened without a die-off on the 2019 scale so far: the 2025 wave culled about 3.8% of the herd, against about 23% in 2019 (our calculation) [@MAC-01; @MAC-12; @PAN-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. Signal and wildcard rows SIG-009 and WC-03 are updated (F2.8, F2.9).

**The 2026 to 2030 national plan.** Decision 779/QD-TTg (2 May 2026) covers avian influenza, foot-and-mouth disease and ASF. By 2030 it aims for at least 30% fewer ASF outbreaks and culls than the 2020 to 2025 average. For avian influenza it aims for 30% fewer outbreaks than the 2019 to 2025 average [@PAN-10] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:projection} (official target). The summary we read gives no vaccination target (OQ-449).

**Position:** assume a major wave every two to three years to 2035. Each would cut pork supply, move pork prices and cut feed demand for a few quarters, and speed consolidation {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:trend}. The vaccine statement makes this more likely, not less, to 2030 [@PAN-33; @GEO-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:trend}.

Vietnam holds about 585 million poultry and 31 million pigs [@MAC-12] {VN-direct|Medium}. We found no report of H5N1 in Vietnamese pigs or cattle {VN-direct|Low}. A mammal-adapted H5N1 lineage spreading in pigs or cattle in 2030 to 2050 has no published probability (WC-02) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:wildcard}. **Reading.** Avian influenza is a recurring but small shock to poultry supply and a rare risk to people. On current evidence it does not threaten protein supply at national scale [@PAN-09; @PAN-01] {VN-direct|Medium}.

**The steady burden comes from pork, through food and handling.**
- ***Streptococcus suis*** (a bacterium carried by pigs) was the leading cause of adult bacterial meningitis at a northern tertiary hospital in 2018 to 2024 [@PAN-13] {VN-direct|Medium}. The Ministry of Health (MOH) counted 123 human cases and 2 deaths in 2025, and named *tiết canh* (raw pig blood pudding) as a main route [@PAN-15] {VN-direct|Medium}. Provincial press reports may show more deaths (DG-354, OQ-451). For 2011 to 2014, a study estimates 1,437 to 1,866 disability-adjusted life years (DALYs, years of healthy life lost) a year. It puts indirect costs at USD 2.27 to 2.88 million a year [@PAN-11] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- ***Salmonella*** was found in 58.1% of 671 retail pork samples in three northern provinces [@PAN-17] {VN-direct|Medium}. Of 69 *Salmonella* isolates from pork outlets, 59.4% resisted several classes of antibiotics [@PAN-19] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Reading.** This is a food-safety problem, concentrated in slaughter, handling and raw-blood dishes, with food-safety fixes: slaughter hygiene, cold chains and cooking. A plant protein product avoids these pathogens but has its own food-safety record [@HSC-34] {general|Medium}. We found no Vietnamese estimate of the full burden of pork- and poultry-borne disease (OQ-452).

**Pandemic risk in Vietnam sits at wildlife trade, live-bird markets and backyard poultry.** Coronavirus RNA was found in 20.7% of field rats at traders and 55.6% in restaurants in southern Vietnam [@PAN-25] {VN-direct|Medium}. Vietnamese poultry is "usually reared outside" and exposed to viruses from wild waterbirds [@PAN-39] {VN-direct|Medium}. Directive 29/CT-TTg (2020) stopped most wildlife imports and ordered illegal wildlife markets closed; we found no evaluation of its results [@PAN-32] {VN-direct|Medium}.

### F2.2.3 Antimicrobials: the ban, farm practice and resistance

- Growth promoters were banned in feed from 2018; from 1 January 2026 antibiotics may only treat diagnosed disease [@GEO-24] {VN-direct|Medium}. About 71.7% of antimicrobials used in Vietnam in 2015 went to animals [@MAC-36] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- World use in food animals is projected to rise 8%, from 99,502 t (2020) to 107,472 t (2030), with 67% of hotspots in Asia [@GEO-25] {general|High} {fx:projection} (published model).
- The livestock strategy encourages "biological products that replace antibiotics" [@REG-54] {VN-direct|Medium}. The national strategy on antimicrobial resistance to 2030 (Decision 1121/QD-TTg, 25 September 2023) leaves the veterinary action plan for 2024 to 2030 to MAE [@PAN-24] {VN-direct|Medium}. We still found no 2030 target for antimicrobial use in animals (OQ-449).
- **Use tracks how animals are farmed.** On Mekong chicken farms, chickens received about 19 times the antimicrobials that people on the same farms did [@PAN-22] {VN-direct|Medium}. Preventive use "never reduced the probability of disease" [@PAN-23] {VN-direct|Medium}. Veterinary advice cut use by 66% and weekly deaths by 40%, though only 35 of 102 recruited farms stayed in the trial [@PAN-21] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Reading.** Resistance risk depends on how much antibiotic each animal gets, and the 2026 ban and the farm trial show that lever works. Fewer animals would cut total use roughly in proportion. But at about 0.2% of meat protein displaced on the benchmark path in 2035, the effect is far smaller than the ban's (our reading) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.
- **Feed context.** The ban opens a 2026 to 2030 market for fermentation-made probiotics, yeast products and enzymes, the lowest-regulatory-risk use of Vietnamese fermentation capacity {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Its effect is not yet measured. These are feed additives, which the study no longer treats as a target.

### F2.2.4 Does alternative protein reduce disease risk?

**We found no study that measures it.** We found no study that measures a fall in zoonotic, pandemic or antimicrobial-resistance risk from replacing animal-source food with alternative protein. Peer-reviewed claims that it does are reviews or opinion, several by advocacy or company authors [@PAN-30; @PAN-31; @PAN-27] {general|Medium}. Costed plans to prevent pandemics target surveillance, wildlife trade and deforestation, not diet change [@PAN-28] {general|Medium}. Risk depends on how animals are farmed and traded (F2.2.1 to F2.2.3). We log this as disagreement DG-356.

**Scale settles the question for this study.** The benchmark demand path spares about 142,000 pigs and 2.5 million chickens and ducks a year in 2035 ([[app-d8-demand-model]]). ASF culls were about 8.4 times that number of pigs in 2025 and about 41.5 times in 2019 (our calculation) [@MAC-01; @PAN-03] {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. We found no evidence that a change of that size would move herd-level disease risk.

**What the study can say.** Plant and fermentation supply chains do not depend on animal health. A larger sector would add a source of protein that does not fail when animals do. The study does not say that alternative protein reduces zoonotic, pandemic or resistance risk in Vietnam.

- Fish and seafood supplied 11.1 g of the 102.2 g of protein per person per day in 2023. That is 25.7% of animal protein supply (our calculation; food balance sheets measure supply, not intake) [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|High}.
- Catches rose while the stock fell (rows above). The fisheries strategy cuts capture to 2.8 Mt by 2030, about 27% below 2025 in five years (our calculation) [@NTS-08; @MAC-15] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:projection} (official target).
- Climate takes less to 2050: 2.8% to 11.7% of catch potential across the two FAO models and two pathways. After 2050 under RCP8.5 the models diverge, minus 40.8% against minus 6.5% by 2100, so we give no central figure [@SEC-07] {general|High} {fx:projection} (published model results). Each figure is the average across the climate models that drove the run; the published ranges around them are wide.
- The gap the capture cut leaves will be filled by aquaculture, which runs on imported feed, or by imports (our reading) {VN-direct|Low}. Alternative protein for food does not touch this exposure at the study's scale: the demand routes replace meat and *chay* (traditional vegetarian food) protein, not fish.
- We found no source on how much of the fish people eat comes from capture (OQ-454). Climate rows: CI-35 to CI-38 in `climate_impacts_2050.csv`.

### F2.5.7 Macro costs and the national climate scenario

- Climate change could cost Vietnam 12 to 14.5% of GDP a year by 2050, against 3.2% in 2020 [@CLM-18; @CLM-19] {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:projection} (World Bank model).
- The official scenario in use is the 2020 RCP edition: mid-century warming of 1.2 to 1.7 °C (RCP4.5) and 1.7 to 2.3 °C (RCP8.5) [@CLM-08] {VN-direct|High} {fx:projection}.
- Its replacement (fifth edition, SSP-based, urban model at up to 4.4 km, district sea-level rise) was due in Q1 2026 [@CLM-09; @CLM-39; @CLM-43] {VN-direct|Medium}. We found no evidence of publication by September 2026 {fx:signal}. One preliminary figure: plus 2 to 3.7 °C by 2100 under SSP3-7.0 [@CLM-11] {VN-direct|Low} {fx:projection}.

**Lessons.**
- **Plan for recurring shocks, not a smooth trend.** Vietnam's protein supply met a large shock every one to two years in 2019 to 2026. We expect this to continue to 2035 {VN-direct|Medium} {fx:trend}.
- **Disease reaches consumers; imported-input shocks reach farmers.** Pork prices rose 57.23% in 2020 after ASF. In 2022, the year world feed prices peaked, consumer food prices rose 1.62% and pork fell 10.68% [@SEC-08; @SEC-09] {VN-direct|High}. 2026 is not yet clear. Food and food-service prices rose 4.76% in January to August, but no breakdown separates feed, energy and the 2025 ASF wave [@SEC-17] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **People bridged disease shocks with other animal protein and imports, not plant protein** (F2.2.1).
- **Each shock moved output from households to integrators.** Energy and freight mattered as much as grain in 2026. Policy shocks move both ways.
- **Losses fell.** An incomplete April 2024 count found 1,580 ha of rice at risk of lower yield (43 ha lost) in a milder season, against 26,000 ha of winter-spring rice lost in 2020 [@CLM-36; @CLM-37] {VN-direct|Medium}.
