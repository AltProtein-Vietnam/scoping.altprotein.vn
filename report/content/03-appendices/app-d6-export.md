# D6. Export markets and market access

**What this appendix contains.** The data behind [[ch17-export-demand]]. Trade values are Vietnam-reported UN Comtrade data unless stated; 2023 is the latest year at six-digit level that Vietnam had reported when we queried it. For 2024 and 2025 we use mirror data (what partner countries report importing from Vietnam, or exporting to it), which differ from Vietnam-reported figures and may be revised. Tariff lines for the EU and the UK were read from the official tariff databases on 24 and 25 September 2026; Japanese lines were read from the customs schedule and should be confirmed line by line. Rules of origin were read from the EU and Viet Nam agreement's Protocol 1, the UK Trade Tariff and the RCEP Secretariat. Sections D6.4 to D6.7 come from the actor check ([[app-m4-actor-check-waves]]).

| None (US) | Most-favoured-nation duty whatever the recipe | MFN | No effect | No effect | [@ORG-09] {VN-direct\|High} |

All rows are rules, read as revealed requirements {dx:revealed}. Four details matter for recipes [@ORG-01] {VN-direct|High}:

- **Caps count only non-originating materials.** Vietnamese farmed shrimp and pork, rice and cassava are wholly obtained, so a shrimp dumpling with Vietnamese prawns and a rice or cassava wrapper meets every cap.
- **The tolerance cannot lift a cap.** The 10% general tolerance may not be used to exceed the 20% flour cap.
- **Milling counts.** Flour bought from a Vietnamese mill that grinds imported wheat is non-originating (chapter 11 requires wholly obtained cereals); Vietnamese rice flour and cassava starch are originating.
- **The tolerance text offers weight or value.** It reads "10 per cent of the weight of the product or ex-works price"; we read this as the exporter's choice, which an official view should confirm. On the value reading, Chinese textured soy at USD 1.27 to 1.34 per kg CIF may make up about 32 to 46% of the weight of a product selling at USD 4.27 to 5.87 per kg (our calculation) {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:inferred}.

Classification decides the rule: a spring roll with a pasta-type wrapper is stuffed pasta; one classed as prepared vegetables needs only a change of heading; one with more than 20% meat classed in 1602 needs Vietnamese-raised meat. For domestic textured soy (T1) the rules set a design condition, not a demand: extruded from imported defatted soy flour or soybean meal it is originating under all four agreements; extruded from imported concentrate it is not [@ORG-01; @ORG-04; @ORG-06] {VN-direct|High} {dx:revealed}.

**Does the recipe switch pay?** Replacing 10 to 20% dry Chinese textured soy with domestic supply changes cost by minus USD 0.14 to plus 0.06 per kg of product. In an EU-bound chay preparation with 15 to 20% dry textured soy, where the weight reading binds, the duty saved (USD 0.38 to 0.53 per kg plus an agricultural component) is at least seven times the worst-case switch cost; but cutting imported textured soy to 10% and adding isolate or gluten meets the rule at similar cost, and in dumplings the rule never binds on soy. Only seasoned dry textured soy exported as such needs domestic soy to be originating, and the EU imported USD 0.008 million from Vietnam under HS 2106.10 (all lines, not only seasoned textured soy) in 2025 (our calculation) [@TIC-12; @ORG-02; @XBA-28] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. The duty at stake by line is in `export_tariff_at_stake.csv` and the rules in `origin_rules_by_agreement.csv`.
