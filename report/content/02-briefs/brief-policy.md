# Brief for policy makers

**The short version.** Vietnam produces animal protein at scale on imported plant protein: about 99% of the soy protein (our calculation from USDA supply data) and three-quarters of the maize in its feed [@MAC-04; @MAC-08; @MAC-01], roughly USD 10 billion of feed-ingredient imports in 2025 (our calculation) [@MAC-07; @MAC-08; @MAC-09; @MAC-10]. The 2030 livestock strategy targets 30 to 32 Mt of industrial feed, 8.5 to 10.5 Mt more in 2030 than in 2024 [@REG-54; @MAC-28]. Domestic alternative proteins can reduce that exposure where they meet a price or function. Vietnam has the carbohydrate, the fermentation skills and the buyers, but ranks joint last in the region (with Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines) on regulatory readiness [@RGN-01]. Most of the fix is cheap, and the most valuable step must be taken while the Food Safety Law is being revised: the National Assembly takes first comments at its October 2026 session and considers passage at its May 2027 session, so a new-food clause needs to be in the draft revised between the two sessions, in practice by early 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## What you need to know

1. **The strongest case is supply security, industrial value and trade** ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]). Environment and animal welfare are narrower motivations.
2. **Food rules: an open door but no gate.** New protein foods enter through self-declaration, yet "new-technology food not yet permitted for circulation" is recallable and no procedure grants permission. Firms face enforcement-discretion risk, not a ban ([[ch07-rules]]).
3. **Precision-fermented proteins probably count as GMO products** under Decree 43/2026; production with a GM strain needs a closed-production certificate with no published timeline or fee ([[ch07-rules]]).
4. **Feed rules are more open but have two gaps.** The aquafeed list has no raw-material section, and the livestock list has no entry for bacterial single-cell protein or filamentous fungal biomass. Since Decree 211/2026, unlisted use draws fines of VND 10 to 20 million per material and one to three months' suspension ([[ch07-rules]]).
5. **The region is moving.** Singapore has approved 18 novel foods; Thailand has a novel-food route with recognised assessment units and a 2026 to 2035 biotechnology framework that cites the same regional study that scores Vietnam 0 on rules; Korea amended an existing rule in about nine months ([[ch03-regional]]).
6. **Tax holidays are the weakest incentive for this industry.** Capital is most of the cost; a regional incentive study ranks capital grants and loan guarantees far above tax holidays ([[ch09-economics]]).

## Alignment with national strategy

The options serve Resolution 36-NQ/TW on biotechnology (biotech 7% of GDP by 2030; replace imported biotech products), Resolution 57 on science and innovation (R&D 2% of GDP), and the livestock strategy (Decision 1520: feed and by-product use). None of these names alternative protein; naming it in biotechnology programmes and state venture-fund priorities is low-cost ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch08-capital]]).

## Trade-offs to weigh

Speed against caution in a reliance lane; the risk that a new gate slows products that are legal today (hence transition rules); tariffs that help food makers but weaken domestic ingredient makers; public spending on a pilot line that needs an anchor host to avoid becoming idle ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

## For provinces

Tay Ninh and the Central Highlands hold the cassava-starch industry that could host fermentation-sugar hubs (play T4). Ho Chi Minh City, Dong Nai and Hanoi hold the industrial parks, research institutes, city venture funds and the sandbox. Mekong Delta provinces hold the aquafeed buyers. The provincial map changed on 1 July 2025 ([[app-s10-admin-map]]).

## The demand side

- **Most label rules already exist; enforce them and settle two questions.** TCVN 13625:2023 already defines vegetarian and vegan claims, but its vegetarian class allows egg, milk and honey, so ask the Vietnam Food Administration to state that an unqualified *chay* or *thuần chay* (strict vegetarian) claim means the vegan criteria. Any meat named in a product name must already be quantified (Decree 37/2026 carries the rule of Decree 43/2017), so blends need only a voluntary industry code for generic names such as *giò lụa* and *xúc xích*. We found no rule that reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese); defend that permissive regime in the new law. The GMO label applies only above 5% of ingredients, so one question remains for MAE and VFA: is a recombinant protein identical to the native one a detectable product of the modified gene? [@LBL-15; @LBL-06; @LBL-07; @LBL-10; @LBL-16] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]])
- **School meals: the animal-share target has expired, but the menu software still prints one.** Decision 1340 (2016) set targets that ended in 2020 [@CHN-33]. The Ministry of Health's Decision 3958/QĐ-BYT (December 2025) sets no animal share and asks for plant protein (legumes, tofu) at least twice a week. Our audit of published menus (`school_menu_weeks.csv`) finds that only 9 of 48 school-weeks meet that; menus serve tofu beside meat, and the MOET, Ajinomoto and NIN menu software prints a standard of "animal protein at least 48%" [@APR-04; @PMR-10; @PMR-26] {VN-direct|High}. Use the School Health Programme 2026 to 2035 (Decision 973/QĐ-TTg), the MOET *bán trú* (school lunch and midday care) circular due in 2026 and the digital menu tools due in 2027 to 2028 to replace that default with protein-quality, zinc, iron and sodium targets per lunch, and to add textured soy and legume products to the software's food list [@PMR-01] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch15-channels]]).
- **Children's nutrition: aim at the gaps that exist.** Stunting affects 18% of children under five (2023) and 32% of ethnic minority children, so it is a problem of the youngest children [@APR-27] {VN-direct|Medium}. For school-age children protein is not the gap: published Hanoi primary-school lunches carry about 31.8 g (our calculation), upland boarding lunches and dinners carried meat, fish or egg in 240 of 242 published meals (our menu coding), and weighed boarding diets met protein and zinc; the documented shortfalls are calcium and vitamin A, and our menu coding shows breakfast as the thin meal [@PMR-26; @UPL-01; @UPL-04; @UPL-09; @UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium}. A plant dish that removes all meat needs added zinc; blends keep protein quality high ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).
- **Canteens and procurement.** Factory canteens serve up to 1.2 billion meals a year (our calculation from about 4.15 million industrial-park workers) [@CHN-37] and are a large, price-set protein channel. A procurement standard for innovative protein in public and state-enterprise canteens is the form of this move a market-shaping funder can back. Corporate pledges are a weak lever here: Accor's target of 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030 is the only one we found that binds Vietnamese units, and we found no Vietnamese protein buyer with a published target [@AFN-05; @AFN-40; @AFN-18] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Rule on plant milks before the sugary-drink excise starts.** From 1 January 2027 drinks above 5 g of total sugar per 100 ml pay 8% (10% from 2028); dairy is exempt, and whether sweetened soy, nut and cereal milks count as *nước giải khát* (soft drinks) is unresolved. A written Ministry of Finance ruling before the tax starts would let makers plan recipes [@FTR-01; @FTR-02; @FTR-05] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch13-consumers]]).
- **Trade: tariffs give no shelter; origin rules and data matter more.** Chinese, ASEAN and Korean protein ingredients enter duty-free under ACFTA, ATIGA, AKFTA and RCEP, so changing most-favoured-nation rates cannot protect a domestic maker [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. Protect the EU, UK, Japanese and CPTPP preferences on wrapped foods (Japan's edge on Vietnamese dumplings is 17.4 points), publish an origin note on the EU cap of 20% non-originating flour and starch in wrappers, and create eight-digit customs subheadings for protein ingredients so that demand is visible [@ORG-01; @ORG-05] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).
- **Measure before targeting.** No probability survey asks how many people keep chay days, although existing chay days probably avoid as much meat protein as the Part IV alternative-protein scenario assumes for 2030 to 2035 ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]). National survey budgets, not philanthropy, should carry that question, starting with a lunar-date tabulation of the 2021 diet survey, in which 7.9% of adults ate no meat, poultry or fish on the previous day [@APR-36] {VN-direct|Medium}. Do not set national targets from consultancy forecasts ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **Vietnam's 2050 plans cover inputs, not protein.** Power, hydrogen and emission caps have 2050 numbers; meat, feed and aquaculture targets stop at 2030. The successor livestock and fisheries strategies and the national biotechnology strategy are the natural homes for protein targets ([[ch20-drivers-2050]], [[app-f5-targets-hubs]]).
- **The 2030 feed target appears to cover livestock feed only** (Decision 1625/QD-TTg) and looks out of reach on our model: 26.4 Mt against 30 to 32 Mt.
- **Feed efficiency does about twice the work of alternative protein** in cutting 2050 soybean-meal imports; the two act through different channels, and the combined case is not modelled ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]).
- **Nine no-regret moves pay off in all four 2050 worlds.** Eight can start by 2028 with existing institutions: a new-food route with deadlines, a feed-efficiency programme, climate-proof siting rules, a skills programme, a national protein and feed balance, residue-carbon rules, a protein-quality laboratory with an open price series, and an omega-3 sourcing plan. One cheap option belongs alongside them: naming fermentation and feed-protein sites in the MAE biotechnology-cluster plan (December 2026) ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **A vision to debate, not a forecast:** 7 to 9 Mt of imported soybean meal in 2050 instead of 10.4 Mt, 4 to 8% microbial feed protein and 15 to 50% non-marine aquafeed omega-3, reviewed at every five-year plan ([[ch24-vision-2050]]).
