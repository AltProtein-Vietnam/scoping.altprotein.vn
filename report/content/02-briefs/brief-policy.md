# Brief for policy makers

**The short version.** Vietnam has the carbohydrate, the fermentation skills and the buyers to make more protein for people at home. But it ranks joint last in the region on regulatory readiness [@RGN-01]. Most of the fix is cheap, and the most valuable step has a deadline: a new-food clause in the revised Food Safety Law, which must be in the draft by early 2027, before the vote considered for May 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## Why it matters: the security of the protein people eat

Vietnamese people eat more meat each year: 136.4 g per person per day in 2020, against a recommended 50 to 80 g [@DIE-01; @DIE-02; @DIE-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. About 90% of the meat is produced at home, but on imported feed [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|High}. About 99% of the soy protein and three quarters of the maize in that feed are imported, roughly USD 10 billion a year (our calculation) [@MAC-04; @MAC-08; @MAC-01; @MAC-07; @MAC-09; @MAC-10]. Disease shocks hit supply too: pig prices rose 70% after African swine fever in 2019 [@DIE-25] {VN-direct|Medium}.

Protein made at home, from domestic inputs where possible, adds a second source. The strongest case is supply security, industrial value and trade ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]). But at the scale demand supports, about 0.2% of meat protein by 2035, it does not change national exposure on its own ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).

## Food security: measure first, and be precise about what new protein does

- **Most of the animal protein people eat rests on imports, but no target measures it.** Counting feed, 61% to 71% of animal protein rested on imports in 2023 (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low}. The food-security resolution to 2030 counts production volumes, with no import or protein indicator [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High}.
- **So the first move is a national protein balance,** by food, source and origin. Chapter 1 builds a first version from FAO data. The agriculture ministry's 2026 plan to "step by step reduce import dependence" is the official hook [@SEC-02] {VN-direct|High}. Define the indicator by where inputs come from: about 84% of plant-protein food ingredients came from China in 2025, so protein made from them moves the dependence (our calculation) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Disease is the shock consumers feel, and the vaccine gap is open.** Pork prices rose 57% in 2020 [@SEC-08] {VN-direct|High}. In 2025 most swine fever samples in the north were new strains that the licensed vaccines do not cover [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}. New protein is a second source, not disease control: we found no study showing it lowers disease risk [@PAN-27; @PAN-30; @PAN-31] {general|Medium}. It would spare about 142,000 pigs a year in 2035, against 2025 culls 8.4 times that (our calculation from [@MAC-01; @PAN-03]) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.
- **Imported meat is not a subsidy problem.** Meat imports reached 978,000 t in 2025 [@MIM-04; @MIM-05] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}, but most exporting countries give no price support [@MIM-15; @MIM-25] {general|High}.

## What could be made first

Three plays lead under almost every weighting ([[ch26-plays]]):

- domestic textured plant protein to replace imports from China (T1);
- a hub that turns cassava starch into fermentation sugar (T4);
- shared pilot and tolling fermentation, where firms rent time on someone else's fermenter (T5).

## What you need to know

1. **Food rules: an open door but no gate.** New protein foods are self-declared, but "new-technology food not yet permitted" can be recalled, and no procedure grants permission ([[ch07-rules]]).
2. **Precision-fermented proteins probably count as GMO products,** and making them needs a certificate with no published timeline or fee ([[ch07-rules]]).
3. **The region is moving.** Singapore has approved 18 novel foods. Thailand has a route with recognised assessors. Korea changed an existing rule in about nine months ([[ch03-regional]]).
4. **Tax holidays are the weakest incentive here.** Capital is most of the cost, and capital grants and loan guarantees work far better ([[ch09-economics]]).

## Options, ranked

The three top options are cheap and time-critical. MOH is the Ministry of Health, MAE the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment, MOST the Ministry of Science and Technology and MOF the Ministry of Finance.

| Rank | Option | Owner | Effort | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A clause in the Food Safety Law: "The Government shall regulate the management of new foods and new food ingredients" | MOH; National Assembly | Low | Before the May 2027 vote [@APR-07] |
| 2 | A joint note on purified precision-fermented proteins: no GMO food certificate if no living GMO or GM DNA remains | MAE; MOH | Low | 3 to 6 months |
| 3 | A novel-food decree: "not novel" rulings, a traditional-food lane, reliance on trusted regulators, recognised assessors, a public approved list | MOH; Government | Medium | From late 2026 |
| 4 to 9 | Hanoi sandbox pilot; GMO certificate guidance; high-tech tax guidance; a shared pilot fermentation line; capital grants over tax holidays; venture-fund priorities | MOST, MAE, MOF, cities | Low to high | 2027 to 2028 |

## Alignment with national strategy

The options serve the 2023 biotechnology resolution, the 2024 science and innovation resolution, and the livestock strategy's push for by-products. None names alternative protein. Naming it in biotechnology programmes and state venture funds costs little ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch08-capital]]).

## Trade-offs to weigh

Speed against caution in relying on other regulators; the risk that a new gate slows products legal today (so add transition rules); and a pilot line that needs an anchor host to avoid standing idle ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

## For provinces

Tay Ninh and the Central Highlands hold the cassava starch industry and could host the sugar hub (T4). Ho Chi Minh City, Dong Nai and Hanoi hold the industrial parks, institutes, city venture funds and the sandbox.

## The demand side

- **Most label rules already exist: enforce them and settle two questions.** Ask the food safety authority to state that an unqualified *chay* or *thuần chay* (vegan) claim means vegan. Keep the permissive naming rules: no rule reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese). And settle one GMO question: does a protein made by microbes, identical to the natural one, count as a detectable GMO product? [@LBL-15; @LBL-06; @LBL-07; @LBL-10; @LBL-16] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]])
- **School meals: the animal-share target has expired, but the menu software still prints one.** The 2025 guidance sets no animal share and asks for plant protein twice a week. Only 9 of 48 school-weeks we read met that, with tofu beside meat. The menu software still prints "animal protein at least 48%" [@CHN-33; @APR-04; @PMR-10; @PMR-26] {VN-direct|High}. Replace that default with protein-quality, zinc, iron and sodium targets, and add textured soy and legumes to the food list. Use the School Health Programme 2026 to 2035, the 2026 school-meal rule and the 2027 to 2028 digital menu tools [@PMR-01] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch15-channels]]).
- **Children's nutrition: aim at the gaps that exist.** Stunting is a problem of the youngest children [@APR-27] {VN-direct|Medium}. School lunches already carry enough protein; the gaps are breakfast, calcium and vitamin A [@PMR-26; @UPL-01; @UPL-04; @UPL-09; @UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).
- **Canteens.** Factory canteens serve up to 1.2 billion meals a year (our calculation) [@CHN-37]. A buying standard for innovative protein in public and state-enterprise canteens is a move a market-shaping funder could back. Corporate pledges are a weak lever here [@AFN-05; @AFN-40; @AFN-18] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Rule on plant milks before the sugar tax starts.** From 2027, sweet drinks above 5 g of sugar per 100 ml are taxed; dairy is exempt. A finance ministry ruling on whether sweet plant milks are soft drinks would let makers plan [@FTR-01; @FTR-02; @FTR-05] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch13-consumers]]).
- **Trade: tariffs give no shelter.** Chinese, Southeast Asian and Korean protein ingredients enter duty-free, so changing standard duties cannot protect a domestic maker [@FTR-32; @FTR-33] {VN-direct|Medium}. Instead, protect the EU, UK and Japanese preferences on wrapped foods; publish a guide on the EU flour rule; and create finer customs codes for protein ingredients [@ORG-01; @ORG-05] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).
- **Measure before targeting.** No national survey asks about chay days, though they may avoid as much meat as Part IV's alternative-protein scenario assumes ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]) [@APR-36] {VN-direct|Medium}. Do not set targets from consultancy forecasts ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **Vietnam's 2050 plans cover inputs, not protein.** Meat, feed and fish-farming targets stop at 2030. The next livestock, fisheries and biotechnology strategies are the natural homes for protein targets ([[ch20-drivers-2050]]).
- **Protein for people saves the most imports per tonne,** about 4.5 t of soybean meal per tonne of meat protein replaced. But at the scale demand supports, it displaces about 0.4% of meat protein by 2050 (our model) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]).
- **Feed efficiency is a larger lever,** about twice as large, but outside this study {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}.
- **Seven no-regret moves pay off in all four 2050 worlds,** and most can start by 2028: a national protein balance; a new-food route with deadlines; shared pilot capacity; a protein-quality laboratory and price series; residue rules and a by-product atlas; climate-proof siting; and process-engineering skills. Naming food-fermentation sites in the December 2026 biotechnology cluster plan is a cheap option alongside them ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **A vision to debate:** by 2050 Vietnam has a measured, more diverse and more secure protein supply, reviewed at every five-year plan {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} ([[ch24-vision-2050]]).
