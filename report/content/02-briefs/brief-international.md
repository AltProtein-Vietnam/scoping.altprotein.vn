# Brief for international organisations and development finance

**The short version.** Vietnam is a protein-processing economy that imports its plant protein. It has cheap inputs and a strong feed industry, but ranks joint last in the region on regulatory readiness, has no open pilot fermentation and almost no venture capital for this sector. The leverage points are public goods and first-plant finance. Several can be supported through existing channels (regulator-to-regulator cooperation, research grants, development-finance co-investment) at modest cost. On the demand side, fund what fits your unit and your rules: welfare money fits canteen menus, market-shaping money fits procurement and label rules, and development finance fits import substitution and feed.

## Why Vietnam matters

- About 99% of the soy protein in Vietnamese feed and three-quarters of the maize supply are imported, a feed-ingredient import bill of roughly USD 10 billion in 2025 ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).
- Vietnam exported USD 11.3 billion of seafood in 2025; aquafeed is where fishmeal prices bite, and where alternative feed proteins have their clearest market ([[ch01-why-vietnam]], [[ch09-economics]]).
- The regional comparison scores Vietnam 100 on labour and 0 on regulatory readiness. Its recommended fix is a published novel-food procedure with statutory timelines ([[ch03-regional]]).

## Precedents to build on

- **Entobel:** IFC's USD 2.5 million upstream collaboration alongside Mekong Capital and Dragon Capital, with Vinh Hoan's offtake ([[ch08-capital]]).
- **Decree 43/2026:** Vietnam already fast-tracks GMOs approved by five developed countries; the same reliance logic can apply to novel foods ([[ch07-rules]]).
- **Thailand's recognised assessment units** and **Korea's published review time of about nine to 12 months** ([[ch03-regional]]).

## Motivations and framing

AltProtein Vietnam frames the case around food and feed security, industrial value and trade. Climate and animal welfare are motivations for many international funders; in Vietnam, livestock accounted for about 5.8% of national greenhouse-gas emissions in 2016, and the grid emits about 0.66 kg CO2 per kWh, so climate claims for power-intensive processes need care ([[ch01-why-vietnam]], [[ch09-economics]]). The documented demand routes avoid about 0.01 to 0.05 Mt CO2e a year by 2030 and about 0.14 Mt (0.08 to 0.25) by 2035 on the benchmark path (0.33 to 0.99 Mt on the stretch path), about half of it from displacing beef and buffalo (our estimate) [@FUF-04; @FUF-05; @FUF-07] {general|Low}. Treat climate as a co-benefit, not the case; the welfare impact is overwhelmingly in chickens and ducks.

## Coordination

After the March 2025 mergers, MAE holds feed, aquafeed, GMO biosafety and genetic resources; MOST holds standards, the sandbox, high-tech status and IP; MOH holds food safety. A joint programme across these three ministries is administratively simpler than before ([[ch07-rules]]).

## The demand side

- **Separate the protein problems.** Cities eat far more meat than guidance advises. Stunting (18% of children under five in 2023, 32% among ethnic minority children) is a problem of the youngest children, where plant products face the young-child protein-quality pattern and registration as foods for children under 36 months [@APR-27; @NQR-04] {VN-direct|Medium}. For school-age children protein is not the gap: upland boarding lunches and dinners carried meat, fish or egg in 240 of 242 published meals, and breakfast is the thin meal (our menu coding) [@UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-04; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17]; weighed boarding diets met protein and zinc, and the documented shortfalls are calcium and vitamin A [@UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium}. Fund complementary-food standards for the under-twos, and protein quality, zinc and iron in school food standards ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).
- **In funder units.** On the benchmark path the displacing routes spare about 2.4 to 2.7 million animals a year by 2035, 93 to 94% of them chickens and ducks, and avoid about 0.14 Mt CO2e (0.08 to 0.25). Import substitution, most of the protein delivered, spares none (our estimate) [@FUF-03; @FUF-04] {VN-direct|Low}. The routes deliver about 19,000 t of protein a year by 2035, of which about 11,000 to 17,000 t made in Vietnam for the home market, and displace about 0.2% of meat protein ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).
- **Match the route to your unit and your rules.** Welfare funders: a canteen trial with Humane World, which trained chefs and nutritionists in Viet Nam in 2025, that logs the dish species replaced; chicken leads only 13% of caterers' sample-menu protein dishes (our calculation, `canteen_dish_summary.csv`) [@UPL-27; @UPL-28; @UPL-29; @UPL-30; @UPL-32]. Market-shaping funders: procurement of a canteen protein ingredient and the naming and GMO rules; Nova Vista's USD 10 million programme funds procurement, advance market commitments and retailer ratio targets, and prioritises policy staff in Japan, South Korea, Germany and the Nordics. R&D funders: ingredient functionality and Vietnamese sensory tests. Development finance: import substitution and feed, on the import bill and jobs. Nutrition agencies: protein quality, zinc and iron in school food standards [@AFN-05; @AFN-23; @UPL-27] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Why the chay route is hard to fund.** Only 4 of the original 19 demand moves have a funder whose scope fits and that accepts Vietnam (our assessment, `funder_fit.csv`). Culture-change and diet-change work is excluded by Nova Vista and now by ACE Movement Grants; ReRoot Asia has no Vietnam track; Lever's Asia expansion names India and Thailand; and we found no Vietnam work by Sinergia or Good Food Fund [@AFN-05; @FUF-01; @AFN-29; @AFN-39; @AFN-26; @AFN-27] {VN-direct|Medium}. Existing chay days already avoid about as much meat protein as the Part IV scenario assumes, but that is in the baseline; the chay-day survey question belongs in national survey budgets (NSO, NIN) or GAIN's Diet Quality Questionnaire ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]).
- **Corporate commitments are a weak lever here.** Accor's target of 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030 is the only one we found that binds Vietnamese units (43 hotels; it counts dishes, not protein), we found no Vietnamese protein buyer with a published target, and only 2 of 100 Asian protein buyers have targets to boost plant-protein sales. If you fund commitments work, fund implementation measurement at Accor's Vietnamese hotels and a disclosure ask to Vinamilk, not a new pledge campaign [@AFN-40; @AFN-41; @AFN-18; @AFN-21] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch15-channels]]).

What the actor check changed for funders and field-builders, and who could answer the open questions, are in [[ch29-actor-check]].

## Looking to 2050

- **Where long-horizon support has leverage:** a national protein and feed balance (the EU's new balance-sheet method is a ready template); a review cycle for the vision at each five-year plan; climate-proof siting in a Mekong Delta that sinks about 1.1 cm a year; concessional finance windows ready to open when signposts trip; and Vietnam's part in any ASEAN reliance on novel-food approvals ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Just transition is about farm incomes, not factory jobs.** A 10 kt a year plant employs 60 to 100 people; the households most affected by a protein shift are the remaining small pig and poultry farms, and the ones who could gain are cassava, rice-straw and brewery-residue suppliers.
- **Climate finance hardly reaches food** (about 7% of climate finance in 2021 and 2022), no approved carbon-credit method exists for protein substitution, and green-bond criteria for alternative proteins appeared in 2025.
- **A jointly authored vision has a precedent:** *Vietnam 2035* was written by the World Bank and the planning ministry. Our Vietnam 2050 protein vision is offered for that kind of discussion ([[ch24-vision-2050]], [[app-f5-targets-hubs]]).
