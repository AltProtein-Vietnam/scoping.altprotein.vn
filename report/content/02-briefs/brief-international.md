# Brief for international organisations and development finance

**The short version.** Vietnam is a protein-processing economy that imports its plant protein. It has cheap inputs, but ranks joint last in the region on regulatory readiness, has no open pilot fermentation and almost no venture capital for this sector. Outside help goes furthest on public goods and finance for first plants, through existing channels at modest cost. On the demand side, fund what fits your unit and rules: welfare money fits canteen menus, market-shaping money fits public buying and label rules, and development finance fits import replacement.

## Why Vietnam matters

- Vietnamese people eat more meat each year: 136.4 g per person per day in 2020, against a recommended 50 to 80 g [@DIE-01; @DIE-02; @DIE-03] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- About 99% of the soy protein and three quarters of the maize in Vietnamese feed are imported, roughly USD 10 billion a year ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).
- Counting that feed, about two thirds of the animal protein people eat rests on imports (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low}. No national target measures this [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High}. A national protein balance is a public good that statistical cooperation could fund ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- Only protein made from domestic inputs reduces import dependence: about 84% of plant-protein food ingredients came from China in 2025 (our calculation) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- A regional study scores Vietnam 100 on labour and 0 on rules. Its fix is a published novel-food procedure with deadlines ([[ch03-regional]]).

## What the study recommends

- **Three plays lead:** domestic textured plant protein (T1), a hub that turns cassava starch into fermentation sugar (T4), and shared pilot and tolling fermentation, where firms rent time on someone else's fermenter (T5) ([[ch26-plays]]).
- **Three policy steps first:** a new-food clause in the Food Safety Law, a note on the GMO status of purified fermented proteins, and a novel-food procedure ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

## Precedents to build on

Vietnam already fast-tracks GMOs approved by five developed countries; the same logic can apply to novel foods ([[ch07-rules]]). Thailand uses recognised assessors, and Korea publishes its review time ([[ch03-regional]]).

## Motivations and framing

AltProtein Vietnam frames the case around protein security, industrial value and trade. Climate and animal welfare motivate many international funders. But livestock caused about 5.8% of Vietnam's emissions in 2016, and the grid is carbon-heavy, so climate claims need care ([[ch01-why-vietnam]], [[ch09-economics]]). The demand routes avoid about 0.14 Mt of CO2e a year by 2035 on the benchmark path, about half of it from beef (our estimate) [@FUF-04; @FUF-05; @FUF-07] {general|Low}. Treat climate as a co-benefit, not the case.

**The pandemic claim is not supported by measured evidence.** No study we found measures lower pandemic or drug-resistance risk from replacing meat. Claims that it does come from reviews and opinion pieces [@PAN-27; @PAN-30; @PAN-31] {general|Medium}. Risk depends on how animals are farmed and traded [@PAN-28; @PAN-29] {general|Medium}; in Vietnam the evidence points to the wildlife trade and live-bird markets [@PAN-25; @PAN-26] {VN-direct|Medium}. New protein would spare about 142,000 pigs a year in 2035, against 2025 swine fever culls 8.4 times that (our calculation from [@MAC-01; @PAN-03]) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Fund surveillance, farm biosecurity and wildlife-trade control directly, and do not present alternative protein as pandemic prevention.

## Coordination

Three ministries share the field: agriculture and environment (GMO safety, genetic resources), science and technology (standards, sandbox, high-tech status) and health (food safety). A joint programme across them is simpler to run since the 2025 mergers ([[ch07-rules]]).

## The demand side

- **Separate the protein problems.** Cities eat far more meat than advised. Child stunting is a problem of children under two, where plant products face a high bar [@APR-27; @NQR-04] {VN-direct|Medium}. For school-age children, protein is not the gap; calcium, vitamin A and breakfast are [@UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-04; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17; @UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium}. Fund weaning-food standards, and protein quality, zinc and iron in school food standards ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).
- **In funder units.** On the benchmark path, the routes spare about 2.5 million animals a year by 2035, nearly all chickens and ducks, and displace about 0.2% of meat protein. Import replacement, most of the protein delivered, spares none (our estimate) [@FUF-03; @FUF-04] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).
- **Match the route to your unit and rules** [@AFN-05; @AFN-23; @UPL-27] {VN-direct|Medium}:
  - *Welfare funders:* a canteen trial with Humane World, which trained chefs in Vietnam in 2025, logging which meat each dish replaces. Chicken leads only 13% of canteen protein dishes [@UPL-27; @UPL-28; @UPL-29; @UPL-30; @UPL-32].
  - *Market-shaping funders:* public buying of a canteen protein ingredient, and the naming and GMO rules.
  - *Research funders:* ingredient function and Vietnamese taste tests.
  - *Development finance:* import replacement, argued on the import bill and jobs.
  - *Nutrition agencies:* protein quality, zinc and iron in school food standards.
- **Why the chay route is hard to fund.** Only 4 of 19 demand moves have a funder that fits and accepts Vietnam. The main diet-change funders exclude Vietnam or this kind of work [@AFN-05; @FUF-01; @AFN-29; @AFN-39; @AFN-26; @AFN-27] {VN-direct|Medium}. A chay-day survey question belongs in national survey budgets ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]).
- **Corporate pledges are a weak lever here.** Only one, Accor's, binds Vietnamese units, and it counts dishes, not protein. No Vietnamese protein buyer has a target. Fund measurement of how Accor's Vietnamese hotels meet their pledge, not a new pledge campaign [@AFN-40; @AFN-41; @AFN-18; @AFN-21] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch15-channels]]).

What the actor check changed for funders is in [[ch29-actor-check]].

## Looking to 2050

- **Seven no-regret moves pay off in any 2050:** a national protein balance; a new-food route with deadlines; shared pilot capacity; a protein-quality laboratory and price series; residue rules and a by-product atlas; climate-proof siting; and process-engineering skills ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Where long-horizon support goes furthest:** the protein balance (the EU's new method is a template); a review cycle for the vision; climate-proof siting in a sinking Mekong Delta; cheap-finance windows ready to open when signposts trip; and Vietnam's part in regional approval systems.
- **Just transition is about farm incomes, not factory jobs.** A 10,000 t a year plant employs 60 to 100 people. Those most affected are small pig and poultry farms; those who could gain are suppliers of cassava, rice straw and brewery residues.
- **Climate finance hardly reaches food:** about 7% of climate finance in 2021 and 2022.
- **A vision to debate:** by 2050, a measured, more diverse and more secure protein supply, with new protein as a second source beside meat {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. *Vietnam 2035*, written jointly by the World Bank and the planning ministry, is a precedent for a shared vision ([[ch24-vision-2050]]).
