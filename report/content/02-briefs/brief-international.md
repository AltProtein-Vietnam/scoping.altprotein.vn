# Brief for international organisations and development finance

**The short version.** Protein for people first; feed only as context. Vietnam is a protein-processing economy that imports its plant protein. Feed is the hidden import behind the meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish people eat. Vietnam has cheap inputs. But it ranks joint last in the region on regulatory readiness, has no open pilot fermentation and almost no venture capital for this sector. Outside help goes furthest on public goods and first-plant finance.

Several of these can be supported through existing channels at modest cost: regulator-to-regulator cooperation, research grants and development-finance co-investment. On the demand side, fund what fits your unit and your rules. Welfare money fits canteen menus, market-shaping money fits procurement and label rules, and development finance fits import substitution.

## Why Vietnam matters

- Vietnamese people eat more meat each year. Meat intake was 136.4 g per person per day in 2020, against a recommendation of 50 to 80 g [@DIE-01; @DIE-02; @DIE-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. Animal foods supply 42% of protein [@DIE-07; @DIE-08] {VN-direct|High}.
- About 99% of the soy protein in Vietnamese feed and three-quarters of the maize supply are imported, a feed-ingredient import bill of roughly USD 10 billion in 2025 ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).
- Counting that feed, about two thirds of the animal protein people eat (61% to 71% in 2023) rests on imports (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low}. The national food-security resolution to 2030 sets production volumes and has no import or protein indicator [@SEC-01] {VN-direct|High}, and we found no food balance published by a Vietnamese agency. A national protein balance, by food, source and origin, is a public good that statistical cooperation could fund ([[ch01-why-vietnam]], [[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- Protein for people saves the most imports per tonne. Replacing 1 t of meat protein in diets avoids about 4.5 t of soybean-meal and 9.5 t of maize imports (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]). But only protein made from domestic inputs reduces import dependence: about 84% of the food plant-protein ingredients reaching Vietnam in 2025 came from China (our calculation) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- The regional comparison scores Vietnam 100 on labour and 0 on regulatory readiness. Its recommended fix is a published novel-food procedure with statutory timelines ([[ch03-regional]]).

## What the study recommends

- **Seven plays; three lead under almost every weighting:** domestic textured plant protein (T1), a cassava-starch-to-fermentation-sugar hub (T4; a hub is a cluster of plants on one site) and shared pilot and tolling fermentation, where firms rent time on someone else's fermenter (T5). Koji and fungal foods (T9) and mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks (T10) are worth proving. Precision fermentation fits only as contract-made, high-value ingredients (T7). Cultivated seafood is research for this decade (T8) ([[ch26-plays]]).
- **Three policy steps first:** a new-food clause in the Food Safety Law, a joint note on the status of purified precision-fermented proteins as genetically modified organisms (GMO), and a novel-food procedure in the law's implementing decree ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

## Precedents to build on

- **Entobel**, an insect-feed deal outside this study's focus, but a deal structure to copy: the IFC's USD 2.5 million "upstream collaboration" alongside Mekong Capital and Dragon Capital, with Vinh Hoan's offtake ([[ch08-capital]]).
- **Decree 43/2026:** Vietnam already fast-tracks GMOs approved by five developed countries. The same reliance logic can apply to novel foods ([[ch07-rules]]).
- **Thailand's recognised assessment units** and **Korea's published review time of about nine to 12 months** ([[ch03-regional]]).

## Motivations and framing

AltProtein Vietnam frames the case around the security of the protein people eat, industrial value and trade. Climate and animal welfare are motivations for many international funders. In Vietnam, livestock accounted for about 5.8% of national greenhouse-gas emissions in 2016, and the grid emits about 0.66 kg CO2 per kWh. So climate claims for power-intensive processes need care ([[ch01-why-vietnam]], [[ch09-economics]]).

The documented demand routes avoid about 0.01 to 0.05 Mt of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) a year by 2030. By 2035 they avoid about 0.14 Mt (0.08 to 0.25) on the benchmark path (0.33 to 0.99 Mt on the stretch path). About half of it comes from displacing beef and buffalo (our estimate) [@FUF-04; @FUF-05; @FUF-07] {general|Low}. Treat climate as a co-benefit, not the case. The welfare impact is overwhelmingly in chickens and ducks.

**The pandemic claim is not supported by measured evidence.** We found no study that measures a fall in zoonotic (animal-to-human), pandemic or antimicrobial-resistance risk from replacing animal-source food with alternative protein. The claims that it does come from reviews and opinion pieces, several by advocacy or company authors [@PAN-27; @PAN-30; @PAN-31] {general|Medium}. The risk depends on how animals are farmed and traded [@PAN-28; @PAN-29] {general|Medium}. In Vietnam the spillover evidence points to the wildlife trade and live-bird markets [@PAN-25; @PAN-26] {VN-direct|Medium}.

One Health, the approach that treats human, animal and environmental health together, has its own levers: disease surveillance, farm biosecurity and control of the wildlife trade. At the scale demand supports, new protein would spare about 142,000 pigs a year in 2035, against 2025 swine fever culls 8.4 times that (our calculation from [@MAC-01; @PAN-03] and [[app-d8-demand-model]]) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Fund those levers directly, and do not present alternative protein as pandemic prevention ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).

## Coordination

After the March 2025 mergers, three ministries share the field. MAE holds GMO biosafety and genetic resources. The Ministry of Science and Technology (MOST) holds standards, the sandbox, high-tech status and intellectual property. MOH holds food safety. A joint programme across these three ministries is simpler to run than before ([[ch07-rules]]).

## The demand side

- **Separate the protein problems.** Cities eat far more meat than guidance advises. Stunting (18% of children under five in 2023, 32% among ethnic minority children) is a problem of the youngest children. There, plant products face the young-child protein-quality pattern and registration as foods for children under 36 months [@APR-27; @NQR-04] {VN-direct|Medium}. For school-age children protein is not the gap. Upland boarding lunches and dinners carried meat, fish or egg in 240 of 242 published meals, and breakfast is the thin meal (our menu coding) [@UPL-01; @UPL-02; @UPL-03; @UPL-04; @UPL-05; @UPL-06; @UPL-07; @UPL-08; @UPL-09; @UPL-10; @UPL-11; @UPL-12; @UPL-13; @UPL-14; @UPL-15; @UPL-16; @UPL-17]. Weighed boarding diets met protein and zinc, and the documented shortfalls are calcium and vitamin A [@UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium}. Fund complementary-food standards for the under-twos, and protein quality, zinc and iron in school food standards ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).
- **In funder units.** On the benchmark path the displacing routes spare about 2.4 to 2.7 million animals a year by 2035, 93 to 94% of them chickens and ducks. They avoid about 0.14 Mt CO2e (0.08 to 0.25). Import substitution, most of the protein delivered, spares none (our estimate) [@FUF-03; @FUF-04] {VN-direct|Low}. The routes supply about 19,000 t of protein a year by 2035, of which about 11,000 to 17,000 t made in Vietnam for the home market. They displace about 0.2% of meat protein ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).
- **Match the route to your unit and your rules.**
  - Welfare funders: a canteen trial with Humane World, which trained chefs and nutritionists in Viet Nam in 2025, that logs the dish species replaced. Chicken leads only 13% of caterers' sample-menu protein dishes (our calculation, `canteen_dish_summary.csv`) [@UPL-27; @UPL-28; @UPL-29; @UPL-30; @UPL-32].
  - Market-shaping funders: procurement of a canteen protein ingredient, and the naming and GMO rules. Nova Vista's USD 10 million programme funds procurement, advance market commitments and retailer ratio targets. It gives priority to policy staff in Japan, South Korea, Germany and the Nordics.
  - Research and development funders: ingredient functionality and Vietnamese sensory tests.
  - Development finance: import substitution, on the import bill and jobs.
  - Nutrition agencies: protein quality, zinc and iron in school food standards [@AFN-05; @AFN-23; @UPL-27] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Why the chay route is hard to fund.** Only 4 of the original 19 demand moves have a funder whose scope fits and that accepts Vietnam (our assessment, `funder_fit.csv`). Nova Vista excludes culture-change and diet-change work, and ACE Movement Grants now does too. ReRoot Asia has no Vietnam track, Lever's Asia expansion names India and Thailand, and we found no Vietnam work by Sinergia or Good Food Fund [@AFN-05; @FUF-01; @AFN-29; @AFN-39; @AFN-26; @AFN-27] {VN-direct|Medium}. Existing *chay* (vegetarian) days already avoid about as much meat protein as the Part IV scenario assumes, but that is in the baseline. The chay-day survey question belongs in national survey budgets (the National Statistics Office and the National Institute of Nutrition). Another home is the Diet Quality Questionnaire of the Global Alliance for Improved Nutrition ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]).
- **Corporate targets are a weak lever here.** Accor's target of 50% vegetarian or plant-based dishes by 2030 is the only one we found that binds Vietnamese units (43 hotels; it counts dishes, not protein). We found no Vietnamese protein buyer with a published target, and only 2 of 100 Asian protein buyers have targets to boost plant-protein sales. If you fund work on corporate targets, fund measurement of implementation at Accor's Vietnamese hotels and a disclosure request to Vinamilk, not a new campaign for targets [@AFN-40; @AFN-41; @AFN-18; @AFN-21] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch15-channels]]).

What the actor check changed for funders and field-builders, and who could answer the open questions, are in [[ch29-actor-check]].

## Looking to 2050

- **Seven no-regret moves pay off in any 2050:** a national protein balance; a new-food route with deadlines; shared food-grade pilot capacity; a protein-quality laboratory and price series; residue-carbon rules and a by-product atlas; climate-proof siting; and process-engineering skills ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Where long-horizon support goes furthest:**
  - a national protein balance (the EU's new balance-sheet method is a ready template);
  - a review cycle for the vision at each five-year plan;
  - climate-proof siting in a Mekong Delta that sinks about 1.1 cm a year;
  - concessional finance windows, ready to open when signposts trip;
  - Vietnam's part in any ASEAN reliance on novel-food approvals ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Just transition is about farm incomes, not factory jobs.** A 10 kt a year plant employs 60 to 100 people. The households most affected by a protein shift are the remaining small pig and poultry farms. Those who could gain are cassava, rice-straw and brewery-residue suppliers.
- **Climate finance hardly reaches food** (about 7% of climate finance in 2021 and 2022). No approved carbon-credit method exists for protein substitution, and green-bond criteria for alternative proteins appeared in 2025.
- **A vision to debate, not a forecast.** By 2050 Vietnam feeds a larger, richer and older population from a protein supply that is measured, more diverse and more secure. Domestic makers supply most of the plant-protein ingredients that food makers now import. New routes provide about 67,000 to 172,000 t of protein a year. About 59,000 to 147,000 t of it is made in Vietnam (benchmark and stretch paths of our demand model) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Meat stays the main protein; new protein adds a second source ([[ch24-vision-2050]]).
- **A jointly authored vision has a precedent.** *Vietnam 2035* was written by the World Bank and the planning ministry. Our Vietnam 2050 protein vision is offered for that kind of discussion ([[ch24-vision-2050]], [[app-f5-targets-hubs]]).
