# Brief for investors

**The short version.** Vietnam runs a large animal-protein economy on imported plant protein, so the case for making protein at home is real. But there is almost no deal history, and the near-term theses are business-to-business, not classic venture deals:

- **Domestic textured plant protein (T1)** is an import-replacement plant, best owned by a *chay* (vegetarian food) maker, co-packer or distributor and financed with debt. It has no tariff shelter, and the delivered price of soy flour decides its margin.
- **A hub that turns cassava starch into fermentation sugar (T4)** and **shared pilot and tolling fermentation (T5)**, where firms rent time on someone else's fermenter, need strategic partners and public co-funding.
- **Fermentation is decided by capital cost,** not by cheap labour and power. Price every deal against Chinese imports, and expect exits through sales to larger firms.

## What you need to know

1. **No precedent yet.** No disclosed deal in these protein families in Vietnam has exceeded USD 5 million, so there is no local template for financing a plant ([[ch08-capital]]).
2. **A thin venture market.** Vietnam's venture capital was about USD 509 million in 2025; agriculture took about USD 1 million. There have been no tech listings in five years, so exits are trade sales or buyouts ([[ch08-capital]]).
3. **Capital decides fermentation economics.** Capital and maintenance are 54% to 67% of the cost of fungal protein made in Vietnam, which costs USD 4,050 to 14,700 per tonne of protein ([[ch09-economics]]).
4. **The competitor is Chinese.** Chinese microbial protein, textured soy, isolates and gluten enter duty-free, and Chinese plants run at 11,000 to 20,000 t a year [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch03-regional]]).
5. **Food rules have no gate yet.** Familiar plant foods are self-declared, but new ingredients cannot be cleared in advance, and precision-fermented proteins probably count as GMO products ([[ch07-rules]]).
6. **Watch the Food Safety Law.** A new-food clause has to be in the draft by early 2027, before the vote considered for May 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.
7. **The security case is real but narrow, and it favours domestic inputs.** About two thirds of the animal protein Vietnamese people eat rests on imports, directly or through feed (our calculation) [@DIE-07; @MAC-01; @FS-23] {VN-direct|Low}. A plant running on imported soy or gluten moves that dependence, mostly to China, rather than reducing it [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Only domestic inputs reduce it, such as fermentation on cassava or sugar, and rice protein [@SEC-05; @SEC-14; @FS-24; @FS-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. Do not pitch alternative protein as lowering disease risk; we found no evidence ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).

## Theses and how to test them

Three plays lead under almost every weighting: T1, T4 and T5. Koji and fungal foods (T9) and mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks (T10) are worth proving. Precision fermentation fits only as contract-made, high-value ingredients (T7).

| Play | Thesis | Diligence question (kill test) |
|---|---|---|
| T1 Domestic textured plant protein | An import-replacement plant for chay and frozen-food makers, financed with bank debt. About USD 0.3 to 1.4 million installed, plus USD 0.6 to 3.5 million of working capital (our calculation). No patents | Is food-grade soy flour delivered below about USD 700 to 940 per t? Have three buyers signed conditional orders for 2,000 t a year? Does factory cost beat Chinese product landed at USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg [@TIC-02; @TIC-12; @FTR-32] {VN-direct\|Low}? Will the anchor buyer's bank finance the supplier's invoices [@SLK-01; @SLK-05; @SLK-19] {VN-direct\|Medium}? |
| T4 Cassava sugar hub | Keep the starch-to-glucose step in Vietnam, with food fermenters on the same site, built with a starch group and a fermenter | Has a fermenter agreed to co-locate? Can sugar be contracted despite cassava price swings? |
| T5 Shared pilot and tolling fermentation | A food-grade pilot line plus rented time on industrial tanks, with an anchor host, an operator and public co-funding | Does a host accept rental terms? Is use above a third of capacity in two years? |
| T9 Koji and fungal foods | Fermented foods from a characterised food mould on food-grade side streams; low capital | Can strain safety data be obtained? Do taste trials pass? |
| T10 Mung bean and rice protein for drinks | Isolates replacing imported pea protein in high-protein plant drinks | Does a buyer accept it? Does someone pay for the starch by-product [@SLP-14] {VN-direct\|Medium}? |
| T7 Precision fermentation (niche) | High-value, low-dose ingredients made under contract abroad | Does it pay at low yields? Is freedom to operate clear? |

## Who else is here

Local private equity and venture funds (Mekong Capital, Dragon Capital, VinaCapital Ventures); development finance (the International Finance Corporation; the Asian Development Bank's venture arm); regional seed funds (Better Bite Ventures, Big Idea Ventures, ProVeg Incubator); new state venture funds in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi; and dairy, seafood and amino-acid groups as strategic buyers ([[ch08-capital]]).

## What would make this more investable

- A new-food clause in the Food Safety Law, a note on the GMO status of purified fermented proteins, and a novel-food procedure with deadlines ([[ch27-policy-options]]).
- Rented time on existing fermentation tanks, or a shared pilot line (T5).
- A first-loss development-finance tranche for a first plant, argued on imports and jobs, not climate.
- For T1: a delivered soy flour price sheet and conditional orders from chay makers. They cost tens of hours and decide the kill test ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

## The demand side

- **Real demand is business-to-business, and a soy line can serve only part of it.** Plant-protein imports from China nearly tripled from 2019 to 2025, to about 54,000 t. But most of the rise looks like feed protein. The pool a textured-soy line can serve was at most about 12,800 t in 2025 [@BUY-02; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch16-business-buyers]]).
- **Size on the import pool, not consumer forecasts.** On the benchmark path, demand reaches about 19,000 t of protein a year by 2035. For textured soy, that supports about one line (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low}. Published market sizes have no method ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).
- **T1 has cheap equipment and one decisive input.** Soy flour is 63% to 85% of cost, so its price decides the margin. Export rules give it no foreign customer [@FTR-32; @TIC-02; @TIC-13; @ORG-01] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Price blends and plant milks against what incumbents use.** A new extender has to beat hydrated soy, not pork [@HXE-01; @DIE-25; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low}. A sugar tax from 2027 may hit sweet plant milks, a strong reason for makers to reformulate [@FTR-01; @CON-26; @AIB-05] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Impact screens.** T1 spares no animals and avoids no emissions, because soy replaces soy. So it will not count towards climate-screened targets [@AIS-22] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. Pitch it on the import bill, about USD 16 to 23 million a year replaced by 2035, and jobs ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]). Climate or welfare money fits blends and canteen ingredients instead [@FUF-03; @FUF-04] {general|Low}.
- **Look for orders, not surveys.** Investors now weigh contracted revenue and unit economics [@AIS-01; @AIS-15] {general|Medium}. T1 and T10 can show these; consumer meat copies cannot ([[ch13-consumers]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **Price two outside questions:** whether imported protein comes under chronic stress, and whether Asia's fermentation market stays open or falls under Chinese price dominance. T4, T5 and T9 hold in all four 2050 worlds. T7 is a bet: scale it only when its signposts trip ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]]).
- **Costs fall slowly.** Fermentation industries cut costs 13% to 20% per doubling of output ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]).
- **Cheaper capital is a first-order lever.** Cutting the cost of capital from 12% to 9% does as much as the cost learning expected by 2030 (our calculation) ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Second-hand fermentation tanks** are a 2027 to 2030 window.
- **A vision to debate:** by 2050, new routes provide about 67,000 to 172,000 t of protein a year, mostly made in Vietnam {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Meat stays the main protein ([[ch24-vision-2050]]).
