# Brief for investors

**The short version.** Vietnam has a real supply-side case for alternative protein (it runs a huge animal-protein economy on imported plant protein) but almost no deal history: one insect-meal plant has raised more than USD 5 million. The near-term theses that survive our tests are business-to-business, and neither is a classic venture deal. Domestic textured plant protein (T1) is an import-substitution plant best owned by a chay maker, co-packer or distributor and financed with debt; it has no tariff shelter, and the delivered price of soy flour decides its margin. Functional microbial feed ingredients for shrimp and pangasius (T2) must beat the inactive yeast that mills already import. Everything that relies on fermentation at scale is decided by capital cost, not by Vietnam's cheap labour and power, and must be priced against Chinese imports. Exits are trade sales.

## What you need to know

1. **One precedent.** Entobel's USD 32.5 million insect-meal plant was financed by Mekong Capital, Dragon Capital and a USD 2.5 million IFC ticket, with Vinh Hoan (a seafood exporter) as anchor buyer. B2B feed product, local private-equity lead, development finance alongside, a named offtake: that is the template ([[ch08-capital]]).
2. **Thin venture market for this sector.** Vietnam's venture capital was about USD 509 million in 2025, of which agriculture took about USD 1 million. No technology IPOs in five years; realistic exits are trade sales to strategics or private-equity buyouts ([[ch08-capital]]).
3. **Capital decides fermentation economics.** On our indicative stack, capital charges and maintenance are 54 to 67% of the cost of fungal feed protein, which lands at USD 4,050 to 14,700 per tonne of protein against USD 2,625 to 3,846 for fishmeal protein ([[ch09-economics]]).
4. **The competitor is Chinese by-product protein.** Bacterial biomass from Chinese amino-acid fermentation is already sold into Vietnamese feed [@FM-12], and Chinese microbial protein enters duty-free [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}; a Chinese yeast-protein plant runs at 11,000 t a year and a 20,000 t mycoprotein plant is under construction ([[ch03-regional]]).
5. **Rules: feed is open, food is uncertain.** Yeasts, duckweed, algae and insects are listed feed materials; bacterial and fungal biomass need trials; the aquafeed list has a drafting gap. For food, there is no route to positive clearance of a new ingredient, and precision-fermented proteins probably count as GMO products ([[ch07-rules]]).
6. **Watch October 2026 to May 2027.** The Food Safety Law revision is the event that most changes food-side risk. The National Assembly takes first comments at its October 2026 session and considers passage at the May 2027 session, so a new-food clause needs to be in the draft revised between the two sessions, in practice by early 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch19-outlook-2035]]).

## Theses and how to test them

|---|---|---|
| T1 Domestic textured plant protein | An import-substitution ingredient plant replacing Chinese textured soy for *chay* and frozen-food makers, best owned by a chay maker, co-packer or distributor and financed with bank debt and trade finance, not venture equity. Installed capex about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million; working capital USD 0.6 to 3.5 million (our calculations); no intellectual property; it displaces almost no meat | Is food-grade defatted soy flour delivered below about USD 700 to 940 per t (1 t/h line at 70% utilisation)? Have at least three buyers placed conditional orders totalling 2,000 t a year? [@TIC-02; @TIC-12; @FTR-32] {VN-direct\|Low} |
| T2 Functional microbial feed ingredients | Sell survival and feed-efficiency gains at 1 to 5% inclusion to about six shrimp-feed makers and integrated pangasius groups | Do two independent tank or pond trials show a gain at 5% inclusion or less? ## Who else is here

Local private equity (Mekong Capital, Dragon Capital), VinaCapital Ventures, the IFC, ADB Ventures (no Vietnamese agrifood deal yet), regional seed specialists (Better Bite Ventures, Big Idea Ventures, ProVeg Incubator), and new state venture funds in Ho Chi Minh City (VND 500 billion) and Hanoi (VND 230 billion). Strategic acquirers by fit include feed, dairy, seafood and amino-acid groups ([[ch08-capital]], [[app-s11-capital]], [[app-s1-directory]]).

## What would make this more investable

- A new-food procedure with timelines in the Food Safety Law and its decree ([[ch27-policy-options]], options 1 and 5).
- Aquafeed and livestock feed-list fixes (options 3 and 4).
- Tolling access to existing fermentation tanks or a shared pilot line (T5).
- A development-finance first-loss tranche for a first fermentation-feed plant, made on the feed case (T2) or, for T1, on the import and jobs case, not on a climate label.
- For T1: a delivered soy-flour price sheet and conditional orders from chay makers, which cost tens of hours and decide the kill test ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).

## The demand side

- **The revealed demand is business-to-business, and a soy line can serve only part of it.** Plant proteins shipped from China to Vietnam nearly tripled from 2019 to 2025, to about 54,000 t, but the rise is mostly HS 3504 protein substances at about USD 1 per kg, priced like feed or technical protein rather than food isolate. Textured-type imports, the pool a soy extrusion line can serve, were at most about 12,800 t in 2025 [@BUY-02; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Medium}. Chay makers are the most switchable buyers ([[ch16-business-buyers]]).
- **Size on the import-substitution pool, not on consumer forecasts.** On the benchmark path the routes create demand for about 19,000 t of protein a year by 2035, of which about 11,000 to 17,000 t made in Vietnam for the home market. For soy extrusion that is about one 1 t/h line or two 0.5 t/h lines, not three to four; the rest is gluten and isolates, which need other plants (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low}. Published Vietnamese market sizes have no traceable method, and global forecasts from 2019 to 2021 overshot by 10 to 100 times ([[ch18-demand-sizing]]).
- **T1 has no tariff shelter, cheap equipment and one decisive input.** Chinese, ASEAN and Korean textured protein, isolates and gluten enter duty-free. Chinese extrusion lines are listed at USD 11,000 to 100,000, working capital is the larger need, and the delivered price of food-grade soy flour (63 to 85% of cost, our calculation, [[ch09-economics]]) sets the sign of the margin. Export origin rules give domestic textured soy no customer: a dumpling made with Chinese textured soy keeps its origin [@FTR-32; @TIC-02; @TIC-13; @ORG-01] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **Feed demand is revealed but priced low.** Vietnam imported about 9,200 t of inactive yeast for food and feed in 2025, 95% of it at about USD 1.1 per kg, and the leading aquafeed maker Skretting reached 1.5% novel ingredients in 2025, against a 5 to 10% target for its parent Nutreco, which reached 0.6% group-wide. Export feed standards do not require microbial protein and reward only algae and aquatic microorganisms (BAP) ([[ch17-export-demand]]) [@FBA-17; @FBA-07; @FBA-01; @FBA-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Price blends and plant milks against what incumbents already use.** Soy extension already pays at every annual hog price from 2019 to 2024, so a new extender must beat hydrated soy (about USD 2.6 to 2.8 per kg of protein, our calculation), not pork [@HXE-01; @DIE-25; @TIC-12] {VN-direct|Low}. From 2027 drinks above 5 g sugar per 100 ml pay an 8% excise (10% from 2028) if plant milks count; the upper bound for the Vinasoy soy-milk business is about VND 384 to 480 billion a year, a strong reason for incumbents to reformulate (our calculation) [@FTR-01; @CON-26; @AIB-05] {VN-direct|Low}. Pea protein enters duty-free from China, France and Canada, so a domestic isolate has no tariff shelter either [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Impact screens.** T1 spares about no animals and avoids about no CO2e (soy replaces soy), so it will not count towards climate-screened targets such as ADB Ventures' 80% strong climate impact [@AIS-22] {VN-adjacent|Medium}. Pitch it to development finance on the import bill (about USD 16 to 23 million of imports replaced a year by 2035 on the benchmark path, gross; our model, [[ch18-demand-sizing]]), jobs and value added. Per tonne of protein delivered, a hybrid extender avoids about 57 t CO2e and spares about 540 animals; a canteen ingredient about 45 t and 750 to 1,100 animals (our calculation) [@FUF-03; @FUF-04] {general|Low}. Climate or welfare money fits blends and canteen ingredients, not T1.
- **Look for offtake, not surveys.** Lenders and investors increasingly weigh offtake agreements and operating economics [@AIS-01; @AIS-15] {general|Medium}, which T1 and plant-drink proteins (T10) can show and consumer analogues cannot (our assessment). 74% of urban online respondents say they would buy plant-based meat at parity, but only 15% intend to eat less meat ([[ch13-consumers]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **Price two external questions:** whether imported feed protein comes under chronic stress, and whether Asia's fermentation market stays open or falls under Chinese price dominance. Plays T2 (functional feed ingredients), T4 (cassava sugar hub), T5 (shared pilot fermentation), T6 (duckweed feed) and T9 (koji and fungal foods) hold in all four 2050 worlds and thrive in two; T3 (bulk fishmeal replacement) and T7 (high-value precision fermentation) are bets that should scale only when their signposts trip ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]]).
- **Cost curves are engineering estimates, not observed trends.** Fermentation industries have cut costs 13 to 20% per doubling of output. Price feed-protein deals against fishmeal: on our estimates soybean-meal parity is out of reach to 2050 ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]).
- **Cheaper capital is a first-order lever.** Cutting the capital charge from 12% to 9% does as much for fungal feed protein as the cost learning expected by 2030 (our calculation). Concessional debt and guarantees matter more than grants ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Mind the scale.** Our alternative-protein scenario implies microbial feed-protein capacity of about nine times Entobel's current Vietnamese capacity (11 kt a year) on a protein basis by 2030 (about 4.5 times by tonnage; our calculation) and about 78 times by 2050; the highest national feed-sector share we found is 0.4% novel ingredients (Norwegian salmon feed, 2020) ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]).
- **Timing.** Second-hand fermentation tanks are a 2027 to 2030 window; protein from renewable power is a 2040s option on the Mekong shrimp coast, and only if five conditions align.
