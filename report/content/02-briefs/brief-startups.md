# Brief for startups and founders

**The short version.** Vietnam offers cheap operating inputs, abundant carbohydrate, co-packers and concentrated feed buyers, but no novel-food route, no open pilot fermenter and very little venture capital. The founders most likely to reach revenue in two years are those who pick a familiar product with a clear route today, need little capital, aim first to supply incumbents (as an ingredient, a private label or a co-packed line) rather than launch their own analogue brand, and borrow capacity rather than build it. No founder here has yet tested the ingredient route, so treat it as the hypothesis to test ([[ch29-actor-check]]).

## Pick a product with a route that exists today

| Product | Route | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Plant-based food from known ingredients (textured protein, *chay*) | Self-declaration | Immediate |
| Koji and traditional mould fermentation on food substrates | Self-declaration; keep strain and mycotoxin data | Immediate |
| Insect, duckweed, algae or yeast meal for livestock feed | Listed: factory certificate and conformity declaration | About 35 working days for the factory |
| The same materials in aquafeed | Unclear: ask the Department of Fisheries in writing | Trial may be required |
| Bacterial single-cell protein or new fungal biomass in feed | Trial, recognition, annual list update | About 40 working days plus the trial; our estimate is 6 to 18 months in total |
| Fungal biomass or mycoprotein food | Self-declaration, but no way to get pre-clearance | Immediate, with legal risk |
| Precision-fermented protein made in Vietnam | Closed-production GMO certificate, then a food route | Not published |
| Cultivated meat or seafood | No national route; Hanoi sandbox is the nearest option | Not published |

Source: [[ch07-rules]]. Unlisted feed materials are now fined (VND 10 to 20 million each, plus suspension), so run the trial first.

## Plays that suit founders

- **T1. Textured plant protein.** Buyers are *chay* brands, frozen-food makers and co-packers who now import Chinese textured soy and gluten. Full cost is about VND 3,800 to 7,900 per 100 g of protein (USD 0.76 to 1.57 per kg), against Chinese product landing duty-free at USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg (2025) and Indian at about USD 1.08 to 1.15 (our calculations, `t1_cost_per_kg.csv` and `t1_landed_competitor.csv`, from Comtrade prices and the 0% ACFTA rate). Equipment is cheap: Chinese lines are listed at USD 11,000 to 100,000 and install for about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million (our calculation, `t1_margin_payback.csv`). Food-grade soy flour and working capital are the real needs. Do not raise venture money to build a line alone: bring in a chay maker or distributor as co-owner or anchor buyer, and start with delivered flour quotes and conditional orders [@TIC-02; @TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
- **T2. Functional microbial feed ingredients.** Sell survival and feed-efficiency gains at 1 to 5% of shrimp or pangasius diets. Get species-specific trial data first; most shrimp feed is made by about six firms ([[ch26-plays]]). Vietnam already imports about 9,200 t of inactive yeast a year for food and feed, 95% of it at about USD 1.1 per kg, so plan head-to-head trials against the yeast they use [@FBA-17] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **T9. Koji and fungal foods.** Use a characterised *Aspergillus oryzae* strain (no cyclopiazonic acid or 3-nitropropionic acid risk) on food-grade side streams ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **T6. Duckweed feed.** Low capital, listed for livestock; test heavy metals and manganese on your water ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **T7. Precision fermentation.** Only high-value, low-dose products, made under contract abroad ([[ch26-plays]]).

## Where to locate

- **Cassava and starch residues:** Tay Ninh (65 to 68 starch factories) and the Central Highlands. Cassava roots rose about 50 to 90% in price, depending on province, in the year to April 2026; build on residues, not roots ([[ch04-asset-map]], [[app-s4-feedstocks]]).
- **Industry, co-packers, parks and labs:** Ho Chi Minh City (including former Binh Duong and Ba Ria-Vung Tau) and Dong Nai. Industrial land about USD 186 per m² for the lease term; ready-built factories about USD 5 per m² a month ([[ch09-economics]], [[app-s5-facilities]]).
- **Aquafeed buyers and trials:** the Mekong Delta and Ho Chi Minh City.
- **Sandbox, research institutes, the Hanoi venture fund:** Hanoi ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch08-capital]]).

## Borrow capacity

- **Co-packers:** frozen-food processors already run vegetarian lines next to meat ([[ch05-industrial-base]]).
- **Feed mills:** about 269 mills at roughly half their capacity can host trials ([[ch05-industrial-base]]).
- **Fermentation:** Vedan, Ajinomoto and Daesang own the aerobic tanks; we found no published tolling offer, so ask ([[ch30-unknowns]]). For scale-up, ScaleUp Bio (Singapore, food-licensed, up to 10,000 L), EECi (Thailand, two 15,000 L tanks, food status to confirm) or Chinese CDMOs (largest and cheapest, more exposure of know-how) ([[ch03-regional]]).
- **Testing:** FIRI's NACEFA and NIFC for proximate analysis, contaminants and microbiology. Amino-acid and digestibility testing may need to go abroad ([[app-s8-labs-talent]]).

## Design for Vietnam

- **Cooling.** Cooling-tower water in the south arrives at about 31 to 33 °C (our engineering assumption). Chilled water adds roughly USD 70 to 180 per tonne of biomass; thermotolerant strains that grow at 40 to 45 °C avoid most of it ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **Power.** About 1,952 VND per kWh blended at 22 kV for a 24/7 plant, but the grid emits about 0.66 kg CO2 per kWh; a low-carbon claim needs renewable supply ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **VAT.** Feed is not subject to VAT, so a feed-ingredient maker cannot reclaim input VAT; budget 5 to 10% more on taxed inputs ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **Names and addresses.** Use the post-July 2025 province names in licences and investor documents ([[app-s10-admin-map]]).

## Money

Angels and family offices; regional seed specialists (USD 0.1 to 0.3 million: Better Bite Ventures, Big Idea Ventures, ProVeg Incubator); ADB Ventures (up to USD 4 million, climate lens); the Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi venture funds from late 2026; episodic international grants. A Singapore holding company, as Entobel used, can ease foreign investment ([[ch08-capital]], [[app-s11-capital]]).

## The demand side

- **Sell a familiar food with little capital, and supply incumbents first.** Aim for ingredient, private-label or co-packing supply to chay makers, meat processors, retailers and caterers. A restaurant menu listing for your own analogue brand is not a business customer: of 12 regional firms in our register (`firm_outcomes.csv`) that launched through restaurants, 7 are dormant and 3 pivoted. No founder has yet tested the ingredient route, so treat it as the hypothesis to test, not a proven path [@AIS-28; @BRD-08] {VN-adjacent|Low}.
- **Know the base rates.** Of 24 regional and Vietnamese alternative-protein firms since 2019 in our register (`firm_outcomes.csv`), 7 are active, 4 pivoted, 2 exited and 11 are dormant. Every pivot moved towards private label, co-manufacturing, a retail blend or non-food. None of the 4 Vietnamese meat-analogue startups is active; all 3 Vietnamese makers of traditional formats (mushroom floss, cashew cheese, tempeh) are [@AIS-28; @AIS-39; @BRD-08] {VN-adjacent|Low} ([[ch15-channels]]).
- **Price per gram of protein is the first gate, and kitchens already buy cheap plant protein.** Eggs and chicken sell protein at VND 34,000 to 45,000 per 100 g and lean pork at about VND 71,000 at retail [@COST-39; @COST-40; @COST-41; @DIE-12]; 20 g of protein from a packaged chay wrapper costs more than a whole street meal. Domestic dry soy-gluten pieces already reach kitchens at about VND 4,000 to 5,100 per 20 g of protein (protein assumed from similar labels), against VND 7,200 to 9,500 for tofu (our calculation) [@CPC-12; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch11-protein-diet]], [[ch13-consumers]]).
- **Private label is an open door.** Saigon Co.op's Co.op Select chay dishes are all made by one co-packer, GN Foods, and carry no protein claim. A version at 10 g of protein per 100 g [@DIE-04] qualifies for a high-protein claim and can reach the shelf without a consumer brand; Co.op's supplier portal answers a pitch within 10 working days [@CPC-15; @CPC-18; @LBL-03] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch16-business-buyers]]).
- **Build for the dish and the calendar.** Protein sits in Vietnamese meals as toppings, fillings, rolls and mince. Chay sales in specialist shops roughly double in the seventh lunar month, and one shop reported three times [@CHY-16]; for an ingredient maker the swing is smaller: China-reported textured-protein shipments to Vietnam run about a third above normal in the two months before *Vu Lan* (the seventh lunar month) and fall to about 40% of normal in the *Tết* (lunar new year) month, while Vietnam's own import records show no pre-Vu Lan rise [@BRD-05; @BRD-06] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]).
- **Words matter.** Our recommendation: lead with taste in familiar dishes, protein, safety and origin; avoid *nhân tạo* (artificial), "lab" and "future of meat", since the press uses *thịt nhân tạo* loosely [@NOV-69; @NOV-74]; *lên men* (fermented) reads well [@NOV-63; @NOV-64]. In a blend, name the plant part as an ingredient and avoid "x% meat replaced" wording (our recommendation) and *độn* (filler), which buyers read as cheating in *giò chả* [@TRU-02; @TRU-03] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **Windows open in sequence.** Functional feed ingredients, AI-assisted enzymes and tolling on existing tanks now; cheap second-hand tanks in 2027 to 2030; contract precision fermentation and food mycoprotein in 2030 to 2035 once a new-food route exists; residue sugar and protein from power in the 2040s ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]).
- **Edges that survive Chinese price dominance** are species-specific function, local feedstocks and freshness ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]]).
- **Design for the 2050 climate.** Use strains that grow at 40 °C or more, raised Mekong sites with surface water, and residue carbon if you want a climate claim: cassava-based microbial protein is not low-carbon by default.
- **Omega-3 may be the sharper opportunity than protein.** Vietnamese aquafeed needs about 11 kt of EPA plus DHA today and about 21 kt by 2050 (our estimate), and Vietnam's own fish oil is not an omega-3 source ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]).
