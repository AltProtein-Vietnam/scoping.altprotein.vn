# Brief for startups and founders

**The short version.** Vietnam offers cheap operating inputs, plenty of carbohydrate and co-packers. But it has no novel-food route, no open pilot fermenter and very little venture capital. The founders most likely to earn revenue within two years:

- pick a familiar food with a clear legal route today;
- need little capital;
- supply established firms first, as an ingredient, store brand or co-packed line, rather than launch their own meat-copy brand;
- borrow capacity rather than build it.

No founder here has yet tested the ingredient route, so treat it as the hypothesis to test ([[ch29-actor-check]]).

## Pick a food with a route that exists today

| Product | Route | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Plant-based food from known ingredients | Self-declaration | Immediate |
| Koji and traditional mould fermentation | Self-declaration; keep strain and mould-toxin data | Immediate |
| Fungal protein food | Self-declaration, but no advance clearance | Immediate, with legal risk |
| Precision-fermented protein made in Vietnam | A GMO certificate for closed production, then a food route | Not published |
| Cultivated meat or seafood | No national route; the Hanoi sandbox is closest | Not published |

Source: [[ch07-rules]]. Three policy steps would open a route for new ingredients: a new-food clause in the Food Safety Law, a note on the GMO status of fermented proteins, and a novel-food procedure ([[ch27-policy-options]]). The law's vote is considered for May 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## Plays that suit founders

Three plays lead under almost every weighting: textured plant protein (T1), a cassava sugar hub (T4), and shared pilot and tolling fermentation (T5). T4 and T5 are shared infrastructure, where founders would be early users ([[ch26-plays]]). The plays a founder can lead:

- **T1. Textured plant protein,** for *chay* (vegetarian food) brands and frozen-food makers now importing Chinese textured soy. It costs USD 0.76 to 1.57 per kg to make, against Chinese product landed duty-free at USD 1.29 to 1.38 (our calculation). Equipment is cheap: Chinese lines install for about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million. Soy flour and working capital are the real needs. Do not raise venture money to build a line alone. Bring in a chay maker or distributor as co-owner or anchor buyer, and start with flour quotes and conditional orders [@TIC-02; @TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
- **T9. Koji and fungal foods,** using a characterised, toxin-free koji strain on food-grade side streams ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **T10. Mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks,** replacing imported pea protein. Be careful with protein-quality claims: mung bean digestibility in people is about 20% below the pig values often quoted ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **T7. Precision fermentation,** only for high-value, low-dose products made under contract abroad.
- **T8. Cultivated seafood,** research for this decade, with foreign partners.

## Where to locate

- **Cassava residues:** Tay Ninh and the Central Highlands. Cassava roots rose 50% to 90% in price in the year to April 2026, so build on residues, not roots ([[ch04-asset-map]]).
- **Industry, co-packers and labs:** Ho Chi Minh City and Dong Nai. Ready-built factories cost about USD 5 per m² a month ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **Sandbox, institutes and the city venture fund:** Hanoi ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch08-capital]]).

## Borrow capacity

- **Co-packers:** frozen-food makers already run vegetarian lines next to meat ([[ch05-industrial-base]]).
- **Fermentation:** Vedan, Ajinomoto and Daesang own the tanks. None advertises rental, so ask ([[ch30-unknowns]]). Abroad, Singapore's ScaleUp Bio is food-licensed, Thailand's public pilot plant is larger, and Chinese contract makers are cheapest but expose more know-how ([[ch03-regional]]).
- **Testing:** local laboratories do basic food testing; protein-quality tests may need to go abroad.

## Design for Vietnam

- **Cooling.** Warm cooling water in the south adds cost. Strains that grow at 40 to 45 °C avoid most of it ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **Power.** Cheap, but the grid is carbon-heavy, so a low-carbon claim needs renewable supply ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **Inputs, if security is in your pitch.** A product made from imported soy, pea or gluten moves Vietnam's import dependence, mostly to China [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Cassava, sugar, rice, peanuts and Vietnamese soybeans are domestic inputs [@SEC-05; @SEC-14; @FS-24; @FS-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. Do not claim your product lowers disease risk; we found no evidence ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).
- **Names.** Use the new province names (since July 2025) in licences and investor papers.

## Money

Angels and family offices; regional seed funds at USD 0.1 to 0.3 million (Better Bite Ventures, Big Idea Ventures, ProVeg Incubator); the Asian Development Bank's venture arm (up to USD 4 million, climate lens); and the Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi funds from late 2026. A Singapore holding company can ease foreign investment ([[ch08-capital]]).

## The demand side

- **Sell a familiar food with little capital, to established firms first.** Of 12 regional firms that launched their own brand through restaurants, 7 are dormant and 3 pivoted [@AIS-28; @BRD-08] {VN-adjacent|Low}.
- **Know the base rates.** Of 24 regional alternative-protein firms since 2019, 7 are active. None of the four Vietnamese meat-copy startups is active; all three makers of familiar foods (mushroom floss, cashew cheese, tempeh) are [@AIS-28; @AIS-39; @BRD-08] {VN-adjacent|Low} ([[ch15-channels]]).
- **Price per gram of protein is the first gate.** Eggs and chicken cost VND 34,000 to 45,000 per 100 g of protein [@COST-39; @COST-40; @COST-41; @DIE-12]. Kitchens already buy dry soy pieces at about VND 4,000 to 5,100 per 20 g of protein, cheaper than tofu (our calculation) [@CPC-12; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch11-protein-diet]], [[ch13-consumers]]).
- **Store brands are an open door.** One co-packer makes all of Saigon Co.op's chay dishes, none with a protein claim. A version with 10 g of protein per 100 g could claim "high protein" and reach the shelf without a consumer brand. Co.op answers a supplier pitch within 10 working days [@CPC-15; @CPC-18; @LBL-03; @DIE-04] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch16-business-buyers]]).
- **Build for the dish and the calendar.** Protein sits in Vietnamese meals as toppings, fillings, rolls and mince. Chay sales roughly double in the seventh lunar month [@CHY-16]. Ingredient demand swings less: up about a third before that month, and down in the *Tết* (lunar new year) month [@BRD-05; @BRD-06] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]).
- **Words matter.** Lead with taste in familiar dishes, protein, safety and origin. Avoid *nhân tạo* (artificial), "lab" and "future of meat" [@NOV-69; @NOV-74]. *Lên men* (fermented) reads well [@NOV-63; @NOV-64]. In a blend, name the plant part as an ingredient, never *độn* (filler) [@TRU-02; @TRU-03] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **Windows open in sequence:** AI-assisted enzymes and renting tanks now; cheap second-hand tanks in 2027 to 2030; contract precision fermentation and fungal protein in 2030 to 2035, once a new-food route exists; sugar from residues and protein from power in the 2040s ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]).
- **The edges that survive Chinese price dominance** are local raw materials and freshness ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]]).
- **Design for the 2050 climate:** heat-tolerant strains, raised sites in the delta, and residue carbon if you want a climate claim.
- **Seven no-regret moves** would help every founder, from a new-food route to shared pilot capacity ([[ch28-robust-moves]]). A vision for 2050 is in [[ch24-vision-2050]].
