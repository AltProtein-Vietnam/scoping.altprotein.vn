# Brief for startups and founders

**The short version.** Protein for people first; feed only as context. Vietnam offers cheap operating inputs, plenty of carbohydrate and co-packers. But it has no novel-food route, no open pilot fermenter and very little venture capital. The founders most likely to reach revenue in two years:

- pick a familiar food with a clear legal route today;
- need little capital;
- supply incumbents first, as an ingredient, a private label or a co-packed line, rather than launch their own analogue brand;
- borrow capacity rather than build it.

No founder here has yet tested the ingredient route, so treat it as the hypothesis to test ([[ch29-actor-check]]).

## Pick a food with a route that exists today

| Product | Route | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Plant-based food from known ingredients (textured protein, *chay* or vegetarian food) | Self-declaration | Immediate |
| Koji and traditional mould fermentation on food substrates | Self-declaration; keep strain and mycotoxin data | Immediate |
| Fungal biomass or mycoprotein food | Self-declaration, but no way to get pre-clearance | Immediate, with legal risk |
| Precision-fermented protein made in Vietnam | Closed-production certificate for genetically modified organisms (GMO), then a food route | Not published |
| Cultivated meat or seafood | No national route; the Hanoi sandbox is the nearest option | Not published |

Source: [[ch07-rules]], which also sets out the routes for feed materials. Feed is outside this study's focus.

Three policy steps would open a route for new food ingredients ([[ch27-policy-options]]):

- a new-food clause in the Food Safety Law;
- a joint note on the GMO status of purified precision-fermented proteins;
- a novel-food procedure in the law's implementing decree.

The National Assembly takes first comments on the law at its October 2026 session and considers passage at the May 2027 session [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## Plays that suit founders

The study keeps seven plays. Three lead under almost every weighting: domestic textured plant protein (T1), a cassava-starch-to-fermentation-sugar hub, a cluster of plants on one site (T4), and shared pilot and tolling fermentation, where firms rent time on someone else's fermenter (T5). T4 and T5 are shared infrastructure, and founders are among their first users ([[ch26-plays]]). The plays a founder can lead are these:

- **T1. Textured plant protein.** Buyers are *chay* brands, frozen-food makers and co-packers who now import Chinese textured soy and gluten. Full cost is about VND 3,800 to 7,900 per 100 g of protein (USD 0.76 to 1.57 per kg). Chinese product lands duty-free at USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg (2025), and Indian product at about USD 1.08 to 1.15 (our calculations). We used Comtrade prices and the 0% rate under the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area (`t1_cost_per_kg.csv` and `t1_landed_competitor.csv`). Equipment is cheap: Chinese lines are listed at USD 11,000 to 100,000 and install for about USD 0.3 to 1.4 million (our calculation, `t1_margin_payback.csv`). Food-grade soy flour and working capital are the real needs. Do not raise venture money to build a line alone. Bring in a chay maker or distributor as co-owner or anchor buyer, and start with delivered flour quotes and conditional orders [@TIC-02; @TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
- **T9. Koji and fungal foods.** Use a characterised *Aspergillus oryzae* strain (no cyclopiazonic acid or 3-nitropropionic acid risk) on food-grade side streams ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **T10. Mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks.** Replace the imported pea protein in high-protein plant drinks. The first buyers are plant-milk makers ([[ch26-plays]]). Take care with protein-quality claims: human digestibility of mung bean is about 20% below the pig values often quoted ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
- **T7. Precision fermentation.** Only high-value, low-dose products, made under contract abroad ([[ch26-plays]]).
- **T8. Cultivated seafood.** Research for this decade, with foreign partners ([[ch26-plays]]).

## Where to locate

- **Cassava and starch residues:** Tay Ninh (65 to 68 starch factories) and the Central Highlands. Cassava roots rose about 50 to 90% in price, depending on province, in the year to April 2026. Build on residues, not roots ([[ch04-asset-map]], [[app-s4-feedstocks]]).
- **Industry, co-packers, parks and labs:** Ho Chi Minh City (including former Binh Duong and Ba Ria-Vung Tau) and Dong Nai. Industrial land costs about USD 186 per m² for the lease term; ready-built factories about USD 5 per m² a month ([[ch09-economics]], [[app-s5-facilities]]).
- **Sandbox, research institutes, the Hanoi venture fund:** Hanoi ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch08-capital]]).

## Borrow capacity

- **Co-packers:** frozen-food processors already run vegetarian lines next to meat ([[ch05-industrial-base]]).
- **Fermentation:** Vedan, Ajinomoto and Daesang own the aerobic tanks. We found no published tolling offer, so ask ([[ch30-unknowns]]). For scale-up abroad there are three options ([[ch03-regional]]):
  - ScaleUp Bio (Singapore, food-licensed, up to 10,000 L);
  - EECi (Thailand, two 15,000 L tanks, food status to confirm);
  - Chinese contract development and manufacturing organisations (CDMOs): the largest and cheapest, but they expose more of your know-how.
- **Testing:** the Food Industries Research Institute's National Center for Food Analysis and Assessment (NACEFA) and the National Institute for Food Control (NIFC) do proximate analysis, contaminants and microbiology. Amino-acid and digestibility testing may need to go abroad ([[app-s8-labs-talent]]).

## Design for Vietnam

- **Cooling.** Cooling-tower water in the south arrives at about 31 to 33 °C (our engineering assumption). Chilled water adds roughly USD 70 to 180 per tonne of biomass. Thermotolerant strains that grow at 40 to 45 °C avoid most of it ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **Power.** About 1,952 VND per kWh blended at 22 kV for a 24/7 plant. But the grid emits about 0.66 kg CO2 per kWh, so a low-carbon claim needs renewable supply ([[ch09-economics]]).
- **Inputs, if security is part of your pitch.** A product made from imported soy, pea or wheat gluten moves Vietnam's import dependence, often to China, rather than reducing it. About 84% of the food plant-protein ingredients reaching Vietnam in 2025 came from China (our calculation) [@BUY-02] {VN-direct|Medium}. Cassava or sugar for fermentation, rice and peanut protein, and Vietnamese soybeans are domestic inputs [@SEC-05; @SEC-14; @FS-24; @FS-01] {VN-direct|Medium}. Do not claim that your product lowers disease or pandemic risk: we found no study that measures it ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]).
- **Names and addresses.** Use the post-July 2025 province names in licences and investor documents ([[app-s10-admin-map]]).

## Money

- Angels and family offices.
- Regional seed specialists, at USD 0.1 to 0.3 million: Better Bite Ventures, Big Idea Ventures, ProVeg Incubator.
- ADB Ventures, the Asian Development Bank's venture arm (up to USD 4 million, climate lens).
- The Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi venture funds from late 2026.
- Occasional international grants.

A Singapore holding company can ease foreign investment; Entobel, an insect-feed maker, used one ([[ch08-capital]], [[app-s11-capital]]).

## The demand side

- **Sell a familiar food with little capital, and supply incumbents first.** Aim for ingredient, private-label or co-packing supply to chay makers, meat processors, retailers and caterers. A restaurant menu listing for your own analogue brand is not a business customer. Of 12 regional firms in our register (`firm_outcomes.csv`) that launched through restaurants, 7 are dormant and 3 pivoted. No founder has yet tested the ingredient route, so treat it as the hypothesis to test, not a proven path [@AIS-28; @BRD-08] {VN-adjacent|Low}.
- **Know the base rates.** Of 24 regional and Vietnamese alternative-protein firms since 2019 in our register (`firm_outcomes.csv`), 7 are active, 4 pivoted, 2 exited and 11 are dormant. Every pivot moved towards private label, co-manufacturing, a retail blend or non-food. None of the 4 Vietnamese meat-analogue startups is active; all 3 Vietnamese makers of traditional formats (mushroom floss, cashew cheese, tempeh) are [@AIS-28; @AIS-39; @BRD-08] {VN-adjacent|Low} ([[ch15-channels]]).
- **Price per gram of protein is the first gate, and kitchens already buy cheap plant protein.** Eggs and chicken sell protein at VND 34,000 to 45,000 per 100 g and lean pork at about VND 71,000 at retail [@COST-39; @COST-40; @COST-41; @DIE-12]. 20 g of protein from a packaged chay wrapper costs more than a whole street meal. Domestic dry soy-gluten pieces already reach kitchens at about VND 4,000 to 5,100 per 20 g of protein (protein assumed from similar labels). Tofu costs VND 7,200 to 9,500 for the same protein (our calculation) [@CPC-12; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch11-protein-diet]], [[ch13-consumers]]).
- **Private label is an open door.** Saigon Co.op's Co.op Select chay dishes are all made by one co-packer, GN Foods, and carry no protein claim. A version at 10 g of protein per 100 g [@DIE-04] qualifies for a high-protein claim and can reach the shelf without a consumer brand. Co.op's supplier portal answers a pitch within 10 working days [@CPC-15; @CPC-18; @LBL-03] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch16-business-buyers]]).
- **Build for the dish and the calendar.** Protein sits in Vietnamese meals as toppings, fillings, rolls and mince. Chay sales in specialist shops roughly double in the seventh lunar month, and one shop reported three times [@CHY-16]. For an ingredient maker the swing is smaller. China-reported textured-protein shipments to Vietnam run about a third above normal in the two months before the *Vu Lan* month (the seventh lunar month, named after its Buddhist festival for parents and ancestors). They fall to about 40% of normal in the *Tết* (lunar new year) month, while Vietnam's own import records show no pre-Vu Lan rise [@BRD-05; @BRD-06] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]).
- **Words matter.** Our recommendation: lead with taste in familiar dishes, protein, safety and origin. Avoid *nhân tạo* (artificial), "lab" and "future of meat", since the press uses *thịt nhân tạo* loosely [@NOV-69; @NOV-74]; *lên men* (fermented) reads well [@NOV-63; @NOV-64]. In a blend, name the plant part as an ingredient. Avoid "x% meat replaced" wording (our recommendation) and *độn* (filler), which buyers read as cheating in *giò chả* [@TRU-02; @TRU-03] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **Windows open in sequence** ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]):
  - now: AI-assisted enzymes and tolling on existing tanks;
  - 2027 to 2030: cheap second-hand tanks;
  - 2030 to 2035, once a new-food route exists: contract precision fermentation and food mycoprotein;
  - the 2040s: residue sugar and protein from power.
- **Edges that survive Chinese price dominance** are local feedstocks and freshness ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]]).
- **Design for the 2050 climate.** Use strains that grow at 40 °C or more, and raised Mekong sites with surface water. If you want a climate claim, use residue carbon: cassava-based microbial protein is not low-carbon by default.
- **Seven no-regret moves pay off in any 2050:** a national protein balance; a new-food route with deadlines; shared food-grade pilot capacity; a protein-quality laboratory and price series; residue-carbon rules and a by-product atlas; climate-proof siting; and process-engineering skills ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **A vision to debate, not a forecast.** By 2050, domestic makers supply most of the plant-protein ingredients that food makers now import. Vietnam has a working new-food route, open food-grade pilot capacity and a protein-quality laboratory. New routes provide about 67,000 to 172,000 t of protein a year. About 59,000 to 147,000 t of it is made in Vietnam (benchmark and stretch paths of our demand model) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Meat stays the main protein ([[ch24-vision-2050]]).
