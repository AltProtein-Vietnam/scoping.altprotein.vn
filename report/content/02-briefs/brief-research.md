# Brief for universities and research institutes

**The short version.** Vietnam's research output on novel foods is about a quarter to a third of Thailand's. It is at the regional level in fish feed and microalgae, and has real skills in making proteins in yeast, stem-cell culture and koji genetics. But it has almost no published work on texturing protein, fungal protein, precision-fermented food proteins or food cell lines. The most useful research pairs a Vietnamese raw material or species with a missing food study. The most useful infrastructure is shared.

## Where Vietnam stands

{{chart:chart-research-benchmark}}

- **Strengths:** fish and shrimp feeding trials; making proteins in yeast; stem-cell culture without animal serum; genetic tools for koji mould; microalgae, including a 48.7% protein spirulina ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **Gaps:** extrusion, fungal protein, precision-fermented food proteins, fish cell lines for food, and any Vietnamese study of protein quality scores ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **Infrastructure:** no open pilot fermenter, no food extruder at any institute we found, and no accredited protein-digestibility testing ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).

## Research questions Vietnam is well placed to answer

1. **Protein quality, phytate and zinc** for Vietnamese proteins: mung bean, rice, spirulina, spent yeast, and the plant proteins children eat (tofu, textured soy, blended dishes). Mung bean digestibility in children is about 20% below pig values [@SCI-42] ([[ch10-technology-fit]]). A fortified soy breakfast item for upland boarding schools is a research option to test on calcium and vitamin A as well as zinc [@NQR-05; @UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium}.
2. **Characterised koji strains** from *tương* (fermented soybean paste), with mould-toxin data: the safety evidence koji foods (play T9) need.
3. **Fungal protein from cassava pulp**, with a food safety package.
4. **Heat-tolerant yeasts** on raw cassava starch, with measured cooling savings.
5. **Duckweed food safety:** yields, manganese and heavy metals on Mekong water.
6. **Texturing** rice and legume proteins.
7. **Cultivated seafood:** cheap growth media from Vietnamese soy, rice bran or fish by-products [@COST-47] {general|High}; growth factors made in yeast; and fish cells that grow without serum, starting with seabass or grouper [@SLS-14; @SLS-15] {general|Low} (play T8).
8. **Bacterial protein** from farm and starch-factory biogas.

Source: [[ch06-knowledge-talent]], section 6.3. These questions support the plays in [[ch26-plays]], especially T9 and T10.

## Four gaps that decide the food-security case

Each is among the unknowns in [[ch30-unknowns]].

- **How much of the soy in tofu and soy milk is Vietnamese.** Soybeans are 97.5% imported [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|High}. A survey of the main soy-food makers would answer it.
- **Where the inputs of Vietnamese alternative-protein foods come from.** "Made in Vietnam" is not "from domestic inputs": a textured-protein line runs on imported soy flour [@TIC-12; @TIC-13] {VN-direct|Low}.
- **A Vietnamese food balance.** We found none published by a Vietnamese agency; chapter 1 builds a first version from FAO data [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **A swine fever vaccine for the new strains.** None of the licensed vaccines covers them [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}.

## Public goods research bodies could lead

- **A protein-quality and function laboratory,** testing amino acids, digestibility, zinc and iron absorption, and the functions buyers specify, such as binding and gelling [@NQR-16; @NQR-20; @SLP-10; @SLP-17] {general|Medium}.
- **A yearly by-product atlas** of volume, location, price and composition.
- **An open fermentation cost model** with Vietnamese inputs, and a public price series for protein ingredients.
- **Shared food-grade pilot fermentation,** with a university or the national food research institute as host (play T5, [[ch26-plays]]).

These sit beside the study's three top policy steps on new-food rules ([[ch27-policy-options]]).

## Funding and partners

- **Domestic:** the national science foundation, the main funder of food-protein research, where a typical project was about USD 36,500 in 2023 [@SLW-01; @SLW-02] {VN-direct|Medium}; the Vingroup Innovation Foundation, mainly for scholarships; and state science tasks under the new science law ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **International:** Australia's agricultural research centre funds 23 Vietnamese projects, none on alternative protein. Alternative-protein grant windows open only now and then, and need a ready proposal and often a foreign co-applicant ([[ch08-capital]]).
- **Partners:** Thai groups for heat-tolerant yeasts and pilot plants; Indian fish cell collections; Singapore's pilot and regulatory ecosystem ([[ch03-regional]]).

## Talent

Can Tho University alone offers 860 places a year in food technology, biotechnology and aquaculture. The gap is specialist: extrusion, processing microbial protein for food, taste and texture work, protein-quality scoring, and cell culture for food ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).

## The demand side

- **The biggest gap is tested evidence.** Vietnam has one large online consumer survey, but no choice experiment, in-store price test, label test or national survey of chay (vegetarian) practice ([[ch13-consumers]]).
- **Test new extenders against soy, not all-meat.** Processors already extend pork with soy, which saves money at every pork price [@BUY-05; @DIE-25]. The study that decides whether a fungal extender has a job is a blind test at equal saving against the processor's own soy recipe, in factory sausage or a coarse filling, at 10% to 30% replacement [@HXE-01; @HXE-09] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **Other studies that would change decisions:** a blind and repeat test of a 10 g-protein chay food; a price choice experiment; a label test of key words such as *chay*, *giàu đạm* (high protein), *lên men* (fermented) and *biến đổi gen* (genetically modified); a canteen trial that logs which meat each dish replaces; and a chay safety and composition survey [@TRU-24] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
- **Protein quality and zinc, where children actually fall short.** Tofu and extruded soy score well below meat, egg and milk on protein quality, but a 20% to 30% soy blend keeps a minced dish's quality high (our calculation) [@NQR-05; @NQR-07; @NQR-08; @NQR-09; @COST-41] {general|Medium}. For school-age children, the gaps are calcium, vitamin A and breakfast, not protein [@UPL-18]. Stunting is a problem of children under two, where plant products face a high bar [@APR-27; @NQR-04] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).
- **Know who pays.** Only 4 of 19 demand moves have a funder that fits and accepts Vietnam. The Bezos Centre for Sustainable Protein in Singapore fits consumer and label tests with a Vietnamese partner (our assessment) [@AFN-15]. The chay-day survey question belongs in national survey budgets [@AFN-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Stop running small student surveys** of stated intentions; they add little.

## Looking to 2050

- **Vietnam is nearly absent from frontier fields:** 2 of 494 gas-fermentation papers, and none on fungal protein ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]).
- **Futures questions you could answer best:** treating high-silica Mekong straw; life-cycle data for cassava fermentation; strains that grow at 40 °C; and cell lines from Vietnamese species.
- **Seven no-regret moves** pay off in any 2050. Researchers could lead two related public goods: a yearly protein balance and a published tracker of the scenario signposts ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **A vision for 2050** includes open pilot capacity and a protein-quality laboratory ([[ch24-vision-2050]]). A small scenario workshop in Vietnam would test it.
