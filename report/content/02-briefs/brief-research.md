# Brief for universities and research institutes

**The short version.** Vietnam's novel-food research intensity is about a quarter to a third of Thailand's, but it is at the regional level in aquafeed protein replacement and microalgae, and has real skills in yeast protein expression, stem-cell culture and koji genetics. Vietnam has almost no published work on texturisation, mycoprotein, precision-fermented food proteins or food cell lines. The most useful research combines a Vietnamese raw material or species with a missing study, and the most useful infrastructure is shared.

## Where Vietnam stands

{{chart:chart-research-benchmark}}

- **Strengths:** fish and shrimp feeding trials; protein expression in yeast (including growth factors); serum-free stem-cell culture; gene tools for *Aspergillus oryzae* and *Cordyceps*; microalgae cultivation, including a salt-tolerant spirulina at 48.7% protein ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **Gaps:** extrusion and texturisation (at most one study), mycoprotein, precision-fermented food proteins, fish or shrimp food cell lines, and any Vietnamese DIAAS or PDCAAS study ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **Infrastructure:** no open pilot fermenter, no food extruder at any university or institute we found, and no laboratory page listing accredited amino-acid profiles or protein-digestibility testing ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]], [[app-s8-labs-talent]]).

## Research questions Vietnam is well placed to answer

1. A DIAAS or PDCAAS reference dataset for Vietnamese proteins: mung bean, rice, spirulina, catfish side-stream isolates, spent yeast, and the plant proteins children already eat (tofu, textured soy and blended minced dishes), scored on both the young-child and the older-child patterns with phytate and zinc measured. Human mung bean digestibility, measured in children, is about 20% below pig values [@SCI-42] ([[ch10-technology-fit]]). A fortified soy item for upland boarding schools is a research option, not a product profile: test it at breakfast, and on calcium and vitamin A as well as zinc [@NQR-05; @UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium}.
2. Bacterial single-cell protein and fungal meal in pangasius and whiteleg shrimp diets, including early life stages and functional endpoints (survival, disease challenge).
3. Mixed-culture protein from tofu and soy-milk wastewater in shrimp (a 2025 preprint reports 50% fishmeal replacement consistently and up to 90% on some outcomes).
4. Thermotolerant yeasts (40 to 45 °C) on raw cassava starch and pulp, with measured cooling savings in a tropical plant.
5. Fungal biomass from cassava pulp and peel with a food-grade safety package (cyanide, mycotoxins, RNA, digestibility).
6. Characterised Vietnamese *tương* (*A. oryzae*) strains with cyclopiazonic acid and 3-nitropropionic acid data.
7. Duckweed: yields, manganese and heavy metals on Mekong water; protein concentrates.
8. Texturisation of rice distillers'-grain and broken-rice protein; extrusion of local legume blends.
9. Serum-free, suspension adaptation of striped catfish cells; *Pichia*-made growth factors tested on fish cells.
10. Bacterial protein from pig-farm and cassava-starch biogas.

Source: [[ch06-knowledge-talent]] section 6.3 and [[app-s7-research]].

## Public goods research bodies could lead

- **Protein-quality laboratory** (amino acids, DIAAS, and phytate, zinc and iron with phytate-to-mineral molar ratios) at a national reference laboratory (policy option PO-027) [@NQR-16; @NQR-20] {general|Medium}.
- **Public feed trials** of microbial protein as state science tasks, whose results then qualify for the legal exemption from separate trials (PO-021).
- **Yearly by-product atlas** of volume, location, price and composition (PO-028).
- **Open cost model** for fermentation with Vietnamese inputs, and a public series of domestic feed-ingredient prices ([[ch26-plays]], P6).

## Funding and partners

- **Domestic:** NAFOSTED (most acknowledged), VINIF (mainly scholarships and fellowships), state science tasks under the new science law, which allows acceptance of research risk ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **International:** ACIAR funds 23 Vietnamese projects, none on alternative protein; alternative-protein grant windows (for example Coefficient Giving's USD 30 million request) are episodic and need a ready proposal and often an international co-applicant ([[ch08-capital]]).
- **Partners:** Thai groups (cassava-pulp thermotolerant yeasts; EECi pilot plant), Chinese groups (cassava-to-protein and gas-to-protein feed), Indian fish cell repositories, and Singapore's pilot and regulatory ecosystem ([[ch03-regional]]).

## Talent

Can Tho University alone has a 2026 quota of 860 places in food technology, biotechnology and aquaculture. The gap is specialist: extrusion, food-grade downstream processing, sensory and structure work on analogues, protein-quality scoring, and cell culture for food ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).

## The demand side

- **The biggest gap is still tested evidence.** Vietnam has one large online consumer survey, no choice experiment or in-store price test for plant-based products, no name or label test and no probability survey of chay practice ([[ch13-consumers]], [[app-m3-demand-method]]).
- **Test new extenders against soy, not against all-meat.** Processors already extend pork with soy: one large processor lists soy protein in 31% of its meat formulations [@BUY-05]. This saved money at every annual hog price from 2019 to 2024 (our calculation from hog prices, `hybrid_savings.csv`) [@DIE-25]. The study that decides whether a fungal or novel extender has a job is a blind test at equal saving against the processor's own soy recipe, in industrial *xúc xích* (sausage) or a coarse filling rather than *giò lụa* (steamed pork roll), at 10 to 20% replacement in fine emulsions and up to 30% in coarse mince [@HXE-01; @HXE-09] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **Other studies that would change decisions:** a blind and repeat test of a chay food at 10 g of protein per 100 g; a price choice experiment; a randomised label test (*chay*, *giàu đạm* (high protein), *thịt nuôi cấy* (cultivated meat), *lên men* (fermented), *biến đổi gen* (genetically modified)); a canteen and school trial that logs the dish species replaced; and a chay safety and composition survey that tests borax, preservatives, microbes and protein first, with animal DNA as a cheap PCR screen [@TRU-24] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
- **Protein quality and zinc, where children actually fall short.** Tofu scores 64 (in vivo) to 98 (in vitro) and extruded soybean 65 to 97 on DIAAS (textured soy protein has a single in-vitro PDCAAS of 65), against 100 or more for pork, chicken, egg and milk [@NQR-05; @NQR-07; @NQR-08; @NQR-09]. Textured soy costs far less per gram of quality-adjusted protein than eggs, and a 20 to 30% soy extender keeps a minced pork dish at a DIAAS of about 97 or more (our calculation, `cost_per_quality_protein.csv` and `protein_quality.csv`) [@COST-41] {general|Medium}. For school-age children protein is not the documented gap; calcium and vitamin A are [@UPL-18], and breakfast is the thin meal (our menu coding, `upland_school_weeks.csv`). Stunting (18% of children under five in 2023, 32% among ethnic minority children) [@APR-27] is a problem of the youngest children, where plant products face the young-child pattern and registration as foods for children under 36 months [@NQR-04] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).

> **Correction.** In v0.6 this said textured soy scores 65 to 97 on DIAAS; the source gives 65 to 97 for extruded soybean and only a single in-vitro PDCAAS of 65 for textured soy protein.

- **Know who pays.** Only 4 of the original 19 demand moves have a funder whose scope fits and that accepts Vietnam (our assessment, `funder_fit.csv`). The Bezos Centre for Sustainable Protein at NUS appears to fit consumer-acceptance and label tests with a Vietnamese institute (our assessment) [@AFN-15]; the chay-day survey question has no philanthropic payer and belongs in national survey budgets [@AFN-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Stop running small student surveys** based on intention models; they add little to what is known.

## Looking to 2050

- **Vietnam is nearly absent from the frontier fields:** 2 of 494 gas-fermentation protein papers (2016 to 2026), none on mycoprotein or molecular farming. The 2025 to 2026 wave of Vietnamese work is mostly domestic food technology ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]).
- **The futures questions research bodies could answer best:** feed conversion and soybean-meal inclusion by species; EPA plus DHA needs of Vietnamese species; pretreatment of high-silica Mekong straw; life-cycle data for cassava fermentation with biogas; strains that grow at 40 °C; and cell lines from Vietnamese species for the 2040s ([[app-r1-open-questions]]).
- **Public goods:** a yearly national protein and feed balance, and a published tracker of the 16 scenario signposts ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Foresight capacity:** Vietnam's own foresight studies advise small, high-quality scenario workshops; one on the Part IV scenarios would test our axes and vision ([[app-m2-futures-method]]).
