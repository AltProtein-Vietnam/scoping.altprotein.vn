# Brief for universities and research institutes

**The short version.** Protein for people first; feed only as context. Vietnam's novel-food research intensity is about a quarter to a third of Thailand's. It is at the regional level in aquafeed protein replacement and microalgae, and has real skills in yeast protein expression, stem-cell culture and koji genetics. But Vietnam has almost no published work on texturisation, mycoprotein, precision-fermented food proteins or food cell lines. The most useful research pairs a Vietnamese raw material or species with a missing food study, and the most useful infrastructure is shared.

## Where Vietnam stands

{{chart:chart-research-benchmark}}

- **Strengths:** fish and shrimp feeding trials (feed, now context for this study); protein expression in yeast (including growth factors); serum-free stem-cell culture; gene tools for *Aspergillus oryzae* and *Cordyceps*; microalgae cultivation, including a salt-tolerant spirulina at 48.7% protein ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **Gaps:** extrusion and texturisation (at most one study), mycoprotein, precision-fermented food proteins, fish or shrimp food cell lines, and any Vietnamese study using DIAAS or PDCAAS, the two standard scores of protein quality ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **Infrastructure:** no open pilot fermenter, and no food extruder at any university or institute we found. No laboratory page lists accredited amino-acid profiles or protein-digestibility testing ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]], [[app-s8-labs-talent]]).

## Research questions Vietnam is well placed to answer

1. **Protein quality, phytate and zinc.** Build a DIAAS or PDCAAS reference dataset for Vietnamese proteins: mung bean, rice, spirulina, catfish side-stream isolates, spent yeast, and the plant proteins children already eat (tofu, textured soy and blended minced dishes). Score them on both the young-child and the older-child patterns, with phytate and zinc measured. Human mung bean digestibility, measured in children, is about 20% below pig values [@SCI-42] ([[ch10-technology-fit]]). A fortified soy item for upland boarding schools is a research option, not a product profile. Test it at breakfast, and on calcium and vitamin A as well as zinc [@NQR-05; @UPL-18] {VN-direct|Medium}.
2. **Strain characterisation.** Characterise Vietnamese *tương* (fermented soybean paste) strains of *A. oryzae*, with cyclopiazonic acid and 3-nitropropionic acid data. This is the safety evidence koji and fungal foods (play T9) need.
3. **Fungal biomass from cassava pulp and peel**, with a food-grade safety package (cyanide, mycotoxins, RNA, digestibility).
4. **Thermotolerant yeasts** (40 to 45 °C) on raw cassava starch and pulp, with measured cooling savings in a tropical plant.
5. **Duckweed food safety:** yields, manganese and heavy metals on Mekong water, and protein concentrates.
6. **Texturisation** of rice distillers'-grain and broken-rice protein, and extrusion of local legume blends.
7. **Cultivated seafood:** serum-free, suspension adaptation of striped catfish cells, and *Pichia*-made growth factors tested on fish cells (play T8).
8. **Bacterial protein** from pig-farm and cassava-starch biogas.

Sensory and blind tests are in "The demand side" below. Source: [[ch06-knowledge-talent]] section 6.3 and [[app-s7-research]].

**How this research fits the plays.** The study keeps seven plays. Three lead under almost every weighting: domestic textured plant protein (T1), a cassava-starch-to-fermentation-sugar hub, a cluster of plants on one site (T4), and shared pilot and tolling fermentation (T5). Mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks (T10) and koji and fungal foods (T9) are worth proving, and they need the protein-quality and strain data above. Precision fermentation fits only as contract-made, high-value ingredients (T7), and cultivated seafood is research for this decade (T8) ([[ch26-plays]]).

**Feed-trial questions are now context.** Two questions on the earlier list concern feed trials:

- bacterial single-cell protein and fungal meal in pangasius and whiteleg shrimp diets;
- mixed-culture protein from tofu and soy-milk wastewater in shrimp.

They stay in the record in [[ch06-knowledge-talent]], but this study no longer treats feed ingredients as something to make.

## Four gaps that decide the food-security case

The food-security research of 2026 left four gaps that decide how far the security case reaches. Each is among the eighteen unknowns in [[ch30-unknowns]].

- **The domestic share of soybeans in tofu and soy milk** (OQ-463). Soybeans are 97.5% import-dependent, and domestic beans could cover at most 9% of food use (2023; our calculation) [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|High}. A survey of the main soy-food makers would show whether they buy Vietnamese beans.
- **The input origin of alternative-protein foods made in Vietnam, by route** (OQ-453). "Made in Vietnam" is not "from domestic inputs": a domestic textured-protein line runs on imported soy flour [@TIC-12; @TIC-13] {VN-direct|Low}. A route-by-route survey of makers would close it.
- **A Vietnamese food balance** (OQ-462). We found no food or protein balance published by a Vietnamese agency. Chapter 1 builds a first version from data of the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO): 19% to 23% of the protein supply was imported food in 2023 (our calculation) [@DIE-07] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **An African swine fever vaccine for the recombinant strains** (OQ-448). In 2025, 79% of the samples typed in the north were recombinant strains, and the agriculture ministry says the three licensed vaccines give no protection against them [@PAN-33] {VN-direct|High}.

## Public goods research bodies could lead

- **Protein-quality laboratory** (amino acids, DIAAS, and phytate, zinc and iron with phytate-to-mineral molar ratios) at a national reference laboratory (policy option PO-027) [@NQR-16; @NQR-20] {general|Medium}.
- **Yearly by-product atlas** of volume, location, price and composition (PO-028).
- **Open cost model** for fermentation with Vietnamese inputs, and a public price series for food protein ingredients ([[ch26-plays]], public good P6).
- **Shared food-grade pilot fermentation**, with tolling on existing industrial tanks (play T5, PO-026). Tolling means renting time on someone else's fermenter. A university or the Food Industries Research Institute (FIRI) could be the anchor host ([[ch26-plays]]).

These sit beside the study's three top policy steps ([[ch27-policy-options]]):

- a new-food clause in the Food Safety Law;
- a joint note on whether purified precision-fermented proteins count as genetically modified organisms (GMO);
- a novel-food procedure in the law's implementing decree.

## Funding and partners

- **Domestic:** NAFOSTED, the National Foundation for Science and Technology Development (most acknowledged); VINIF, the Vingroup Innovation Foundation (mainly scholarships and fellowships); and state science tasks under the new science law, which allows research risk to be accepted ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).
- **International:** ACIAR, the Australian Centre for International Agricultural Research, funds 23 Vietnamese projects, none on alternative protein. Alternative-protein grant windows (for example Coefficient Giving's USD 30 million request) open only now and then. They need a ready proposal and often an international co-applicant ([[ch08-capital]]).
- **Partners:** Thai groups (cassava-pulp thermotolerant yeasts; the EECi pilot plant), Indian fish cell repositories, and Singapore's pilot and regulatory ecosystem ([[ch03-regional]]).

## Talent

Can Tho University alone has a 2026 quota of 860 places in food technology, biotechnology and aquaculture. The gap is specialist: extrusion, food-grade downstream processing, sensory and structure work on analogues, protein-quality scoring, and cell culture for food ([[ch06-knowledge-talent]]).

## The demand side

- **The biggest gap is still tested evidence.** Vietnam has one large online consumer survey. It has no choice experiment or in-store price test for plant-based products, no name or label test and no probability survey of chay (vegetarian) practice ([[ch13-consumers]], [[app-m3-demand-method]]).
- **Test new extenders against soy, not against all-meat.** Processors already extend pork with soy: one large processor lists soy protein in 22 of 67 meat formulations (33%) [@BUY-05]. This saved money at every annual hog price from 2019 to 2024 (our calculation from hog prices, `hybrid_savings.csv`) [@DIE-25]. The study that decides whether a fungal or novel extender has a job is a blind test at equal saving against the processor's own soy recipe. Run it in industrial *xúc xích* (sausage) or a coarse filling rather than *giò lụa* (steamed pork roll). Use 10 to 20% replacement in fine emulsions and up to 30% in coarse mince [@HXE-01; @HXE-09] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **Other studies that would change decisions:**
  - a blind and repeat test of a chay food at 10 g of protein per 100 g;
  - a price choice experiment;
  - a randomised label test (*chay*, *giàu đạm* (high protein), *thịt nuôi cấy* (cultivated meat), *lên men* (fermented), *biến đổi gen* (genetically modified));
  - a canteen and school trial that logs the dish species replaced;
  - a chay safety and composition survey that tests borax, preservatives, microbes and protein first, with animal DNA as a cheap PCR screen (a standard DNA test) [@TRU-24] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
- **Protein quality and zinc, where children actually fall short.** On DIAAS, tofu scores 64 (in vivo) to 98 (in vitro) and extruded soybean 65 to 97, against 100 or more for pork, chicken, egg and milk. Textured soy protein has a single in-vitro PDCAAS of 65 [@NQR-05; @NQR-07; @NQR-08; @NQR-09]. Textured soy costs far less per gram of quality-adjusted protein than eggs. A 20 to 30% soy extender keeps a minced pork dish at a DIAAS of about 97 or more (our calculation, `cost_per_quality_protein.csv` and `protein_quality.csv`) [@COST-41] {general|Medium}. For school-age children protein is not the documented gap; calcium and vitamin A are [@UPL-18], and breakfast is the thin meal (our menu coding, `upland_school_weeks.csv`). Stunting (18% of children under five in 2023, 32% among ethnic minority children) [@APR-27] is a problem of the youngest children. There, plant products face the young-child pattern and registration as foods for children under 36 months [@NQR-04] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch11-protein-diet]]).

> **Correction.** In v0.6 this said textured soy scores 65 to 97 on DIAAS; the source gives 65 to 97 for extruded soybean and only a single in-vitro PDCAAS of 65 for textured soy protein.

- **Know who pays.** Only 4 of the original 19 demand moves have a funder whose scope fits and that accepts Vietnam (our assessment, `funder_fit.csv`). The Bezos Centre for Sustainable Protein at NUS (National University of Singapore) appears to fit consumer-acceptance and label tests with a Vietnamese institute (our assessment) [@AFN-15]. The chay-day survey question has no philanthropic payer and belongs in national survey budgets [@AFN-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Stop running small student surveys** based on intention models; they add little to what is known.

## Looking to 2050

- **Vietnam is nearly absent from the frontier fields:** 2 of 494 gas-fermentation protein papers (2016 to 2026), and none on mycoprotein or molecular farming. The 2025 to 2026 wave of Vietnamese work is mostly domestic food technology ([[ch21-frontier-technology]]).
- **The futures questions research bodies could answer best:** pretreatment of high-silica Mekong straw; life-cycle data for cassava fermentation with biogas; strains that grow at 40 °C; and cell lines from Vietnamese species for the 2040s ([[app-r1-open-questions]]).
- **Seven no-regret moves pay off in any 2050:** a national protein balance; a new-food route with deadlines; shared food-grade pilot capacity; a protein-quality laboratory and price series; residue-carbon rules and a by-product atlas; climate-proof siting; and process-engineering skills. Research bodies could provide two related public goods: a yearly national protein balance and a published tracker of the 16 scenario signposts ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **A vision to debate, not a forecast.** By 2050 Vietnam publishes a yearly protein balance of what people eat and where it comes from, and has open food-grade pilot capacity and a protein-quality laboratory. New routes provide about 67,000 to 172,000 t of protein a year. About 59,000 to 147,000 t of it is made in Vietnam (benchmark and stretch paths of our demand model) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Meat stays the main protein ([[ch24-vision-2050]]).
- **Foresight capacity:** Vietnam's own foresight studies advise small, high-quality scenario workshops. One on the Part IV scenarios would test our axes and vision ([[app-m2-futures-method]]).
