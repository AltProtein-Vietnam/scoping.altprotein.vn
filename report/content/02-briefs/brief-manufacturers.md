# Brief for food and feed manufacturers

**The short version.** Vietnam's non-animal protein market is yours already: soy milk, tofu and industrial *đồ chay* made on the same lines as meat. But the protein in those products is imported, and so is the protein in your feed. Fishmeal at its highest price since at least 2020 and a feed-output target of 30 to 32 Mt by 2030 make protein supply a strategic question. The alternatives most likely to reach you within two years are domestic textured protein, which you are better placed to own or anchor than any startup, and functional microbial feed ingredients, which must beat the yeast you already buy. Both need you as a buyer or partner.

## Food and beverage

1. **Your shelf is low in protein and high in imported inputs.** Across the 43 audited foods with a label value, median protein was 4.7 g per 100 g. Every locally made product with 10 g or more per 100 g ran on imported textured soy, soy protein or gluten, much of it from China. About 77% of products relied on an imported protein ingredient, and 11% partly ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]], [[app-s2-retail-audit]]).
2. **Chay is often cheaper than meat per pack, not per gram of protein.** Filled wrappers hold 3 to 5 g of protein per 100 g. Chicken and eggs set the retail protein floor at about 34,000 to 45,000 VND per 100 g of protein ([[ch09-economics]]).
3. **Domestic textured protein is the nearest new supply (T1), and you are its natural owner.** Chinese textured soy enters duty-free under ACFTA; it left China at USD 1.24 per kg FOB in 2025 and lands at about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg with freight, port and inland costs. A Chinese-equipped 0.5 t/h line costs about USD 0.28 to 1.0 million installed and needs 2,000 to 3,600 t a year of orders, 16 to 28% of the 12,800 t of HS 2106.10 imports from China, India and Serbia in 2025 (an upper bound for textured protein, since the code also covers concentrates and isolates). At Chinese parity it clears a 15% margin only if food-grade soy flour is delivered below about USD 590 to 900 per t at 70% utilisation (USD 700 to 940 for a 1 t/h line). Freshness, shapes for local dishes and supply security are the other arguments (our calculation) [@TIC-02; @TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
4. **High-protein plant drinks use imported pea protein (T10).** Mung bean and rice proteins could replace it, with care on protein-quality claims: human digestibility of mung bean is about 20% below the pig values often quoted ([[ch10-technology-fit]]). Pea protein from China, France and Canada enters duty-free, so a domestic isolate competes on price and function, not on tariff [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}.
5. **Rules to watch.** The Food Safety Law revision takes first comments at the October 2026 National Assembly session and is considered for passage at the May 2027 session [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. Watch its allergen rules; no current rule reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese) for animal products. From 1 January 2027 a sugary-drink excise may reach sweetened plant milks ([[ch07-rules]], [[ch27-policy-options]]).

## Feed

1. **Fishmeal reached USD 2,500 a tonne in August 2026**, about 6 times the price of soybean meal. At 15% inclusion, fishmeal adds about USD 375 per tonne of shrimp feed at that price [@FM-01] ([[ch09-economics]]). Vietnamese mills paid less than that benchmark but the gap is closing: imported fishmeal averaged about USD 1,610 per t FOB in January to July 2026, up about 38% on 2025 (our calculation; partner-reported, and 2025 lacks Peru and Ecuador) [@FBA-20] {VN-direct|Low}.
2. **Novel proteins replace part of fishmeal, not all of it.** Most trials support about 25 to 60% replacement per ingredient without growth loss (for the best-studied microbial protein in shrimp, growth matched the control at 30% of a test diet); early life stages need their own trials ([[ch10-technology-fit]], [[app-s6-feed-market]]).
3. **Function is worth more than protein.** Survival after disease challenge, palatability and winter survival are where microbial ingredients earn a premium at 1 to 5% inclusion (T2) ([[ch26-plays]]).
4. **Check the legal route before using a new material.** Aquafeed's permitted list has no raw-material section, and unlisted use now draws fines ([[ch07-rules]]).
5. **Chinese microbial protein is already on the market**, as a by-product of amino-acid fermentation. Compare any new supplier against it, not only against fishmeal ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]]).
6. **You already buy microbial ingredients.** Vietnam imported about 9,200 t of inactive yeast for food and feed in 2025, 95% of it at about USD 1.1 per kg. Ask any new supplier for head-to-head trials against the yeast you use, priced per unit of performance [@FBA-17] {VN-direct|Medium}.
7. **Certification will tighten, but it does not reward microbial protein.** From 1 May 2028, ASC farm audits require feed from ASC-certified mills; 15 Vietnamese mills are certified. The ASC feed standard sets no requirement and gives no credit for microbial ingredients; BAP credits only algae and aquatic microorganisms [@FBA-02; @FBA-03; @FBA-04; @FBA-01; @FBA-05] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## What you could do in the next 12 months

- Pool offtake from two or three chay makers or co-packers, with a distributor, behind one textured-protein line; or give a startup a specification, a trial order and a volume commitment. Start with five delivered quotes for food-grade soy flour (T1).
- Host a head-to-head trial of a Vietnamese functional microbial ingredient against the yeast product you already buy, at 1 to 2% inclusion, and state the price per unit of performance you would pay (T2).
- Write a pea-protein replacement specification for plant drinks at or below 5 g of sugar per 100 ml (T10). Ask the Ministry of Finance for a written ruling on whether your sweetened plant milks are *nước giải khát* (soft drinks), and plan recipes below 5 g.
- Blind-test any fungal extender at equal saving against your own soy recipe, in industrial *xúc xích* (sausage) or a coarse filling (T9).
- Pitch a 10 g of protein per 100 g version of a private-label chay dish through your co-packer.
- Share by-product data (okara, spent grain, broken rice, processing residues) for the national by-product atlas.
- If you own fermentation tanks, consider tolling for third parties; it is the single biggest missing piece in the ecosystem (T5).

## Export angles

A Singapore approval is the regional credential for novel foods; Malaysia has ruled that cultivated meat can be halal; halal acceptance of Vietnamese fermentation-derived ingredients is an open question ([[ch03-regional]], [[ch30-unknowns]]).

Rules of origin shape wrapped-food recipes. For EU buyers, check the flour share of wrappers: non-originating flour or starch may be at most 20% of product weight, and flour milled in Vietnam from imported wheat counts as non-originating. Use Vietnamese rice flour or cassava starch where needed, and ship to the UK, Japan and Canada under CPTPP, which accepts imported flour and textured soy. Switching to domestic textured soy does not change origin for dumplings [@ORG-01; @ORG-04; @ORG-05; @ORG-07] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

## The demand side

- **Your shelves already hold the demand, and a protein claim is open.** Chay sales in specialist shops roughly double in the seventh lunar month (one shop reported three times), and Vissan's chay line sells 38% below its meat version ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]). A high-protein claim needs 10 g per 100 g (TCVN 14429:2025 with the 50 g NRV of TCVN 7088:2025, voluntary); 20 of 43 audited foods meet the per-100 g source condition, and none makes the claim [@LBL-03; @LBL-04; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|High}. Chay's live trust issues are borax, microbes and unlabelled goods; we found no confirmed case of meat in a Vietnamese chay product in our news searches (2015 to 2026) [@TRU-14; @TRU-16; @LBL-23; @TRU-18; @TRU-22] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Private label and co-packing are working routes.** All five Co.op Select frozen chay dishes are made by one co-packer, GN Foods, and Thai Corp Inter.VN and Vi Nguyen make its tofu; Lotte Mart sells its own Choice L chay. These products are starch and vegetable led and carry no protein claim, so a co-packer can take a protein upgrade to the retailer without a consumer brand. HoReCa (hotel, restaurant and catering) distributors already sell tofu to kitchens in volume and list domestic dry soy-gluten pieces, and the imported analogue that tried this route is no longer orderable [@CPC-15; @CPC-12; @AIS-25; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch15-channels]]).
- **Blends are already yours, and soy is the benchmark.** You already own the cheapest extender: soy at 20% saves about VND 9,100 to 16,600 per kg of *chả lụa* (steamed pork roll), or USD 350 to 640 per tonne at VND 26,000 per USD, at 2019 to 2020 hog prices, and extension has paid at every annual hog price from 2019 to 2024. Pilot a fungal extender only if it lets you replace more meat unnoticed than soy, or carries a label your buyers prefer, and test it against your soy recipe, not against all-meat. Keep replacement to 10 to 20% in fine emulsions and 30% in coarse mince, name the plant part as an ingredient, and avoid *độn* (filler) and "x% meat replaced" wording (our calculation) [@HXE-01; @DIE-25; @TIC-12; @AIB-15; @HXE-04; @HXE-09; @TRU-02] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **Plant milk: design at or below 5 g of sugar.** Plant milk is still the modern plant category that sells: the best plant-milk SKUs sell 60 to 79% of the litres of the best dairy SKUs on Lazada and Tiki [@MKT-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. Check whether your sweetened plant milks are *nước giải khát* under TCVN 12828:2019. From 2027 those above 5 g of total sugar per 100 ml would pay an 8% excise (10% from 2028), and since January 2026 they have lost the 2% VAT cut; dairy is exempt [@FTR-01; @FTR-05; @FTR-09] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch13-consumers]]).
- **Export: wrapped foods, not textured protein.** Vietnam exported USD 128.2 million of dumplings and other stuffed pasta in 2023, against almost no textured protein. The European route runs through importers and brand owners of Asian wrapped foods supplying retailers that require IFS or BRCGS certification; European retailers meet their protein targets with European products. Under CPTPP, Japan gives Vietnamese dumplings, except those with mainly shrimp fillings, a 17.4-point tariff edge over China and Thailand [@XBA-29; @XBA-04; @XBA-07; @ORG-05] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **On trend, soybean-meal need rises from 7.2 Mt (2025) to about 10.4 Mt (2050).** The two largest levers, soybean-meal inclusion and feed conversion, are in your hands, and nobody publishes them. Sharing anonymised data would make a national protein balance possible ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]).
- **Plan for recurring shocks.** In the stressed 2050 worlds, fishmeal shortages, joint crop failures in exporting countries and shipping scares recur; the 2026 Middle East conflict helped raise Vietnamese feed prices 10 to 14% between January and April 2026. Formulation flexibility, multi-origin sourcing and working stocks are the proven hedges ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]], [[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **Qualify one non-marine omega-3 oil** for shrimp and marine-fish feeds; alternatives could supply 15 to 50% of aquafeed omega-3 by 2050.
- **Watch trash-fish replacement and offshore cages:** they turn marine-fish growth into formulated, protein- and fishmeal-dense feed demand.
