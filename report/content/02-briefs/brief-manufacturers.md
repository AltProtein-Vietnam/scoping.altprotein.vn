# Brief for food manufacturers

**The short version.** Protein for people first; feed only as context. Vietnam's non-animal protein market is already yours: soy milk, tofu and industrial *đồ chay* (traditional vegetarian food), made on the same lines as meat. But the protein in those products is imported. So is the protein behind the meat you process: about 99% of the soy protein in Vietnamese feed is imported ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]). Two kinds of new supply could reach you within two years:

- **Domestic textured plant protein (T1).** You are better placed to own or anchor a line than any startup.
- **New extenders and plant-drink proteins.** They have to beat the soy and pea protein you already buy.

Both need you as a buyer or partner. Start by getting delivered quotes for food-grade soy flour, winning conditional orders from chay makers, and testing extenders against the soy recipe you already use.

## Food and beverage

1. **Your shelf is low in protein and high in imported inputs.** Across the 43 audited foods with a label value, median protein was 4.7 g per 100 g. Every locally made product with 10 g or more per 100 g ran on imported textured soy, soy protein or gluten, much of it from China. About 77% of products relied on an imported protein ingredient, and 11% partly ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]], [[app-s2-retail-audit]]).
2. **Chay is often cheaper than meat per pack, not per gram of protein.** Filled wrappers hold 3 to 5 g of protein per 100 g. Chicken and eggs set the retail protein floor at about 34,000 to 45,000 VND per 100 g of protein ([[ch09-economics]]).
3. **Domestic textured protein is the nearest new supply (T1), and you are its natural owner.** Chinese textured soy enters duty-free under the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area (ACFTA). It left China at USD 1.24 per kg free on board (FOB) in 2025. With freight, port and inland costs it lands at about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg. A Chinese-equipped 0.5 t/h line costs about USD 0.28 to 1.0 million installed. It needs 2,000 to 3,600 t a year of orders. That is 16 to 28% of the 12,800 t imported under customs code HS 2106.10 from China, India and Serbia in 2025. The 12,800 t is an upper bound for textured protein, since the code also covers concentrates and isolates. At Chinese parity, a line clears a 15% margin only if food-grade soy flour is delivered below about USD 590 to 900 per t at 70% utilisation. For a 1 t/h line the threshold is USD 700 to 940. Freshness, shapes for local dishes and supply security are the other arguments (our calculation) [@TIC-02; @TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
4. **High-protein plant drinks use imported pea protein (T10).** Mung bean and rice proteins could replace it. Take care with protein-quality claims: human digestibility of mung bean is about 20% below the pig values often quoted ([[ch10-technology-fit]]). Pea protein from China, France and Canada enters duty-free, so a domestic isolate competes on price and function, not on tariff [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}.
5. **Rules to watch.** The Food Safety Law revision takes first comments at the October 2026 National Assembly session and is considered for passage at the May 2027 session [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. The study's three top policy steps would open a route for new ingredients ([[ch27-policy-options]]):
   - a new-food clause in the law;
   - a joint note on whether purified precision-fermented proteins count as genetically modified organisms (GMO);
   - a novel-food procedure in the law's implementing decree.

   Watch the allergen rules too. No current rule reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese) for animal products. From 1 January 2027 a sugary-drink excise may reach sweetened plant milks ([[ch07-rules]]).

## What could be made first

The study keeps seven plays. Three lead under almost every weighting:

- domestic textured plant protein to replace imports from China (T1);
- a cassava-starch-to-fermentation-sugar hub, a cluster of plants on one site, so a food-fermentation step stays in Vietnam (T4);
- shared pilot and tolling fermentation, which most fermentation plays need first (T5). Tolling means renting time on someone else's fermenter.

Koji and fungal foods (T9) and mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks (T10) are worth proving. Precision fermentation fits only as contract-made, high-value ingredients (T7). Cultivated seafood is research for this decade (T8). Your role: owner or anchor buyer for T1; tolling host for T5 if you own tanks; specification and test kitchen for T9 and T10 ([[ch26-plays]]).

## Feed: context only

This study no longer treats feed ingredients as something to make. Feed matters as the hidden import behind the meat, eggs, milk and farmed fish people eat. About 269 feed mills run at roughly half their capacity ([[ch05-industrial-base]]); the feed market is described in [[app-s6-feed-market]]. One fact spans food and feed: Vietnam imported about 9,200 t of inactive yeast for food and feed in 2025, 95% of it at about USD 1.1 per kg [@FBA-17] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## What you could do in the next 12 months

- Pool offtake from two or three chay makers or co-packers, with a distributor, behind one textured-protein line. Or give a startup a specification, a trial order and an agreed volume. Start with five delivered quotes for food-grade soy flour (T1).
- Write a pea-protein replacement specification for plant drinks at or below 5 g of sugar per 100 ml (T10). Ask the Ministry of Finance for a written ruling on whether your sweetened plant milks are *nước giải khát* (soft drinks), and plan recipes below 5 g.
- Blind-test any fungal extender at equal saving against your own soy recipe, in industrial *xúc xích* (sausage) or a coarse filling (T9).
- Pitch a 10 g of protein per 100 g version of a private-label chay dish through your co-packer.
- Share by-product data (okara, spent grain, broken rice, processing residues) for the national by-product atlas.
- If you own fermentation tanks, consider tolling for third parties. It is the single biggest missing piece in the ecosystem (T5).

## Export angles

A Singapore approval is the regional credential for novel foods. Malaysia has ruled that cultivated meat can be halal. Whether halal bodies accept Vietnamese fermentation-derived ingredients is an open question ([[ch03-regional]], [[ch30-unknowns]]).

Rules of origin shape wrapped-food recipes:

- For EU buyers, check the flour share of wrappers. Non-originating flour or starch may be at most 20% of product weight, and flour milled in Vietnam from imported wheat counts as non-originating.
- Use Vietnamese rice flour or cassava starch where needed.
- Ship to the UK, Japan and Canada under the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), which accepts imported flour and textured soy.
- Switching to domestic textured soy does not change origin for dumplings [@ORG-01; @ORG-04; @ORG-05; @ORG-07] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

## The demand side

- **Your shelves already hold the demand, and a protein claim is open.** Chay sales in specialist shops roughly double in the seventh lunar month (one shop reported three times). Vissan's chay line sells 38% below its meat version ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]). A high-protein claim needs 10 g per 100 g (TCVN 14429:2025, a voluntary national standard, with the 50 g nutrient reference value of TCVN 7088:2025). 20 of 43 audited foods meet the per-100 g source condition, and none makes the claim [@LBL-03; @LBL-04; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|High}. Chay's live trust issues are borax, microbes and unlabelled goods. We found no confirmed case of meat in a Vietnamese chay product in our news searches (2015 to 2026) [@TRU-14; @TRU-16; @LBL-23; @TRU-18; @TRU-22] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Private label and co-packing are working routes.** All five Co.op Select frozen chay dishes are made by one co-packer, GN Foods, and Thai Corp Inter.VN and Vi Nguyen make its tofu. Lotte Mart sells its own Choice L chay. These products are led by starch and vegetables and carry no protein claim. So a co-packer can take a protein upgrade to the retailer without a consumer brand. Distributors to hotels, restaurants and caterers already sell tofu to kitchens in volume and list domestic dry soy-gluten pieces. The imported analogue that tried this route is no longer orderable [@CPC-15; @CPC-12; @AIS-25; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch15-channels]]).
- **Blends are already yours, and soy is the benchmark.** You already own the cheapest extender. At 2019 to 2020 hog prices, soy at 20% saves about VND 9,100 to 16,600 per kg of *chả lụa* (steamed pork roll). That is USD 350 to 640 per tonne at VND 26,000 per USD. Extension has paid at every annual hog price from 2019 to 2024. Pilot a fungal extender only if it lets you replace more meat unnoticed than soy, or carries a label your buyers prefer. Test it against your soy recipe, not against all-meat. Keep replacement to 10 to 20% in fine emulsions and 30% in coarse mince. Name the plant part as an ingredient, and avoid *độn* (filler) and "x% meat replaced" wording (our calculation) [@HXE-01; @DIE-25; @TIC-12; @AIB-15; @HXE-04; @HXE-09; @TRU-02] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **Imported pork sets a lower bar for some blends.** Imported frozen pork landed at about VND 42,600 per kg in January to July 2026, including cost, insurance and freight (CIF) but before duty and VAT. That is about half the carcass-equivalent price of domestic pigs in early 2026 (our calculation) [@MIM-09; @MIM-07] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. If you buy imported trimmings, you save less from extension. Frozen pork and mechanically deboned meat bought by processors are the only meat imports a blend could plausibly displace; poultry feet, offal and buffalo meat for *phở* have no alternative-protein substitute (our inference) [@MIM-05; @MIM-06] {VN-direct|Low} {dx:inferred}. On the benchmark path, new protein would displace at most about 1.6% of imported meat protein by 2035 (our calculation) [@MIM-04; @QNT-01] {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}.
- **Plant milk: design at or below 5 g of sugar.** Plant milk is still the modern plant category that sells. On Lazada and Tiki, the best plant-milk products sell 60 to 79% of the litres of the best dairy products [@MKT-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. Check whether your sweetened plant milks are *nước giải khát* under TCVN 12828:2019. From 2027 those above 5 g of total sugar per 100 ml would pay an 8% excise (10% from 2028). Since January 2026 they have lost the 2% VAT cut; dairy is exempt [@FTR-01; @FTR-05; @FTR-09] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch13-consumers]]).
- **Export: wrapped foods, not textured protein.** Vietnam exported USD 128.2 million of dumplings and other stuffed pasta in 2023, against almost no textured protein. The European route runs through importers and brand owners of Asian wrapped foods. They supply retailers that ask suppliers for IFS or BRCGS food-safety certification, and European retailers meet their protein targets with European products. Under CPTPP, Japan gives Vietnamese dumplings, except those with mainly shrimp fillings, a 17.4-point tariff edge over China and Thailand [@XBA-29; @XBA-04; @XBA-07; @ORG-05] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **Protein for people saves the most imports per tonne.** On trend, the meat protein Vietnamese people eat rises from about 1.01 Mt in 2025 to about 1.45 Mt in 2050. The imported soybean meal behind it rises from 7.2 Mt to about 10.4 Mt (our model) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Replacing 1 t of meat protein in diets avoids about 4.5 t of soybean-meal and 9.5 t of maize imports (our calculation) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. At the scale demand supports (benchmark path), new protein displaces about 0.4% of meat protein in 2050. It adds a second source rather than replacing meat ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]).
- **Plan for recurring shocks.** In the stressed 2050 worlds, joint crop failures in exporting countries and shipping scares recur. The 2026 Middle East conflict helped raise Vietnamese feed prices 10 to 14% between January and April 2026. Formulation flexibility, multi-origin sourcing and working stocks are the proven hedges ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]]).
- **Seven no-regret moves pay off in any 2050:** a national protein balance; a new-food route with deadlines; shared food-grade pilot capacity; a protein-quality laboratory and price series; residue-carbon rules and a by-product atlas; climate-proof siting; and process-engineering skills ([[ch28-robust-moves]]).
- **A vision to debate, not a forecast.** By 2050, domestic makers supply most of the plant-protein ingredients that food makers now import. New routes (upgraded chay, declared blends, canteen dishes, plant drinks and fermented foods) provide about 67,000 to 172,000 t of protein a year. About 59,000 to 147,000 t of it is made in Vietnam, much of it on Vietnamese carbohydrate and side streams (benchmark and stretch paths of our demand model) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate}. Meat stays the main protein ([[ch24-vision-2050]]).
