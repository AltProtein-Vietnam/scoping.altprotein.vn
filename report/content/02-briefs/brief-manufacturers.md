# Brief for food manufacturers

**The short version.** Vietnam's non-animal protein market is already yours: soy milk, tofu and factory-made *đồ chay* (vegetarian food), made on the same lines as meat. But the protein in those products is imported, and so is the protein behind the meat you process ([[ch01-why-vietnam]]). Two kinds of new supply could reach you within two years:

- **Domestic textured plant protein (T1).** You are better placed to own or anchor a line than any startup.
- **New extenders and plant-drink proteins.** They have to beat the soy and pea protein you already buy.

Start by getting delivered quotes for food-grade soy flour, winning conditional orders from chay makers, and testing extenders against your own soy recipe.

## Food and beverage

1. **Your shelf is low in protein and high in imported inputs.** Audited plant-protein foods had a median of 4.7 g of protein per 100 g. Every local product with 10 g or more ran on imported soy or gluten ([[ch02-alt-protein-today]]).
2. **Chay is cheaper than meat per pack, not per gram of protein.** Chicken and eggs set the protein price floor at VND 34,000 to 45,000 per 100 g of protein ([[ch09-economics]]).
3. **Domestic textured protein (T1) is the nearest new supply, and you are its natural owner.** Chinese textured soy lands duty-free at about USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg. A Chinese-equipped half-tonne-an-hour line costs about USD 0.28 to 1.0 million installed and needs 2,000 to 3,600 t a year of orders. It pays only if food-grade soy flour is delivered below about USD 590 to 940 per t, depending on line size. Freshness, local shapes and supply security are the other arguments (our calculation) [@TIC-02; @TIC-12; @TIC-13; @BUY-02; @FTR-32] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch25-demand-to-frontier]]).
4. **High-protein plant drinks use imported pea protein (T10).** Mung bean and rice proteins could replace it, but must compete on price and function, since pea protein enters duty-free [@FTR-32] {VN-direct|Medium}. Mung bean digestibility in people is about 20% below the pig values often quoted ([[ch10-technology-fit]]).
5. **Rules to watch.** The Food Safety Law is due for a vote in May 2027 [@APR-07] {VN-direct|Medium}. A new-food clause, a note on fermented proteins and a novel-food procedure would open a route for new ingredients ([[ch27-policy-options]]). No rule reserves *thịt* (meat), *sữa* (milk) or *phô mai* (cheese) for animal products. A sugar tax from 2027 may reach sweet plant milks ([[ch07-rules]]).

## What could be made first

Three plays lead: textured plant protein (T1), a cassava sugar hub (T4) and shared pilot and tolling fermentation (T5), where firms rent time on someone else's fermenter. Koji and fungal foods (T9) and mung bean and rice protein for drinks (T10) are worth proving. Your role: owner or anchor buyer for T1; tank host for T5 if you own tanks; specification and test kitchen for T9 and T10 ([[ch26-plays]]).

## Feed: context only

This study does not treat feed ingredients as something to make. Feed matters as the hidden import behind the meat, eggs, milk and fish people eat. Vietnam's 269 feed mills run at about half capacity ([[ch05-industrial-base]]). Vietnam imported about 9,200 t of inactive yeast for food and feed in 2025 [@FBA-17] {VN-direct|Medium}.

## What you could do in the next 12 months

- **Pool orders behind one textured-protein line,** with two or three chay makers or co-packers and a distributor, through separate supply contracts or a cooperative. Competing makers must not agree on the price they pay, which competition law bans (our reading, not legal advice) [@SLK-08; @SLK-09] {VN-direct|Medium}. Start with five delivered soy flour quotes.
- **Write a pea-replacement specification** for plant drinks with 5 g of sugar or less per 100 ml, and ask the Ministry of Finance whether your sweet plant milks count as soft drinks.
- **Blind-test any fungal extender** against your own soy recipe at equal saving, in factory sausage or a coarse filling.
- **Pitch a 10 g-protein version** of a store-brand chay dish through your co-packer.
- **Share by-product data** (okara, spent grain, broken rice) for the national by-product atlas.
- **If you own fermentation tanks, consider renting time to others.** It is the biggest missing piece in the ecosystem.

## Export angles

A Singapore approval is the regional credential for novel foods ([[ch03-regional]]). For EU buyers of wrapped foods, imported flour or starch may be at most 20% of product weight. Use Vietnamese rice flour or cassava starch where needed, and ship to the UK, Japan and Canada under the Pacific trade agreement, which has no such rule [@ORG-01; @ORG-04; @ORG-05; @ORG-07] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

## The demand side

- **Your shelves hold the demand, and a protein claim is open.** Chay sales roughly double in specialist shops in the seventh lunar month ([[ch12-chay-baseline]]). A "high protein" claim needs 10 g per 100 g; nearly half of audited foods already meet the 5 g "source" level, and none makes a claim [@LBL-03; @LBL-04; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|High}. Chay's trust problems are borax, microbes and unlabelled goods, not hidden meat [@TRU-14; @TRU-16; @LBL-23; @TRU-18; @TRU-22] {VN-direct|Medium}.
- **Store brands and co-packing work.** One co-packer makes all of Co.op's frozen chay dishes, none with a protein claim, so a co-packer can take a protein upgrade to the retailer. Kitchen suppliers already sell tofu and dry soy pieces in volume [@CPC-15; @CPC-12; @AIS-25; @FORM-01] {VN-direct|Medium} ([[ch15-channels]]).
- **Blends are already yours, and soy is the benchmark.** Soy at 20% of the lean saves about USD 350 to 640 per tonne of pork roll, at every pork price since 2019. Pilot a fungal extender only if it replaces more meat unnoticed than soy, or carries a better label. Keep replacement to 10% to 20% in smooth products and 30% in mince. Name the plant part as an ingredient, never *độn* (filler) (our calculation) [@HXE-01; @DIE-25; @TIC-12; @AIB-15; @HXE-04; @HXE-09; @TRU-02] {VN-direct|Low} ([[ch14-frontier-demand]]).
- **Imported pork lowers the bar for some blends.** It landed at about half the price of domestic pork in 2026, so processors using it save less from extension [@MIM-09; @MIM-07] {VN-direct|Medium} {dx:revealed}. Blends could displace at most about 1.6% of imported meat protein by 2035 (our calculation) [@MIM-04; @QNT-01; @MIM-05; @MIM-06] {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} {dx:inferred}.
- **Plant milk: design at or below 5 g of sugar.** Online, the best plant milks sell 60% to 79% of the litres of the best dairy milks [@MKT-03] {VN-direct|Medium}. Sweet plant milks may pay an 8% tax from 2027 (10% from 2028); dairy is exempt [@FTR-01; @FTR-05; @FTR-09] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch13-consumers]]).
- **Export wrapped foods, not textured protein.** Vietnam exported USD 128 million of dumplings and similar foods in 2023. The route to Europe runs through importers and brand owners who need European food-safety certificates. Japan gives Vietnamese dumplings a 17.4-point tariff edge over China and Thailand [@XBA-29; @XBA-04; @XBA-07; @ORG-05] {VN-direct|High} ([[ch17-export-demand]]).

## Looking to 2050

- **Demand for meat keeps rising,** and new protein grows beside it as ingredients, extenders and plant drinks. At the scale demand supports, it displaces about 0.4% of meat protein by 2050 (our model) {VN-direct|Low} {fx:estimate} ([[ch22-protein-balance-2050]]).
- **Plan for recurring shocks.** Feed prices rose 10% to 14% in early 2026. Flexible recipes, buying from several origins and working stocks are the proven hedges ([[ch23-scenarios-2050]]).
- **Seven no-regret moves** pay off in any 2050, from a protein balance to shared pilot capacity ([[ch28-robust-moves]]). A vision for 2050 is in [[ch24-vision-2050]].
