# L8: Berkeley Alt:Meat Lab, screened for Vietnam

Line: L8 Berkeley Alt:Meat Lab (wave 13). Source prefix: AML. Date: 2026-10-15.

## Verdict

The Berkeley Alt:Meat Lab is a teaching and venture-building programme, not a research lab. From 2017 to 2026 its affiliated authors published one closed-access book chapter on alternative protein, and no open data or methods, despite its 2017 aim to "freely distribute findings". Its recent peer-reviewed papers are about *Quillaja* saponins (vaccines and salmon health) and are out of scope. So on research the line is a **clean negative**: nothing changes a technical conclusion in `ch10-technology-fit` or any play's kill test. What transfers is the teaching model: industry-set product challenges for student teams, about 32 students a semester, which produced at least three startups. Vietnam already has a national student food-innovation contest (FID at HUIT) but no credit-bearing course of this kind that we found. Yield class: **added a candidate** (one low-priority public good, AML-C1, balanced score 2.95), plus two moderate strengthenings: RM-09 and T5 (a pilot-line teaching capstone) and T9 (a commercial koji precedent).

## Headline findings

1. **Little citable research.** The only alternative-protein output by lab-affiliated authors that we found is a 2021 book chapter on design imperatives (closed access, metadata only). We found no open data, methods or reports on the lab site or in OpenAlex, 2017 to 2026 [general, Medium, AML-24; AML-25].
2. **The lab's recent papers are out of scope.** The director's Berkeley-era papers (2020 to 2026) and the postdoctoral scholar's 2026 paper are on *Quillaja saponaria* saponins: salmon disease, the vaccine adjuvant QS-21 and leaf polyphenols [general, Medium, AML-25; AML-26; AML-27].
3. **The teaching model is the asset.** Challenge Labs are 4-unit, team-based classes for students of all backgrounds. Teams act as simulated startups on a broadly defined need set by an industry partner, then pitch at a twice-yearly showcase (the Collider Cup) [general, High, AML-21; AML-04]. The lab paired a technical foundations course with a product-design challenge lab [general, Medium, AML-07].
4. **Scale.** Nine food challenge labs from Fall 2019 to Spring 2024 enrolled 292 students, about 32 a semester (our calculation) [general, Medium, AML-22]. The lab received USD 125,000 from the Open Philanthropy Project in 2019 to expand the programme; the grant period is not stated [general, Medium, AML-04].
5. **Outcomes.** At least three startups came out of the classes. Prime Roots (koji mycelium deli meats) raised USD 50 million in total by May 2023 [general, Medium, AML-11; AML-29]. Impact Food (plant-based raw tuna) reached a three-outlet restaurant chain in 2023 [general, Medium, AML-13]. Uncracked moved from plant-based crab to seaweed-derived ingredients by 2024 [general, Low, AML-15; AML-30].
6. **Activity has slowed since 2024.** The newest blog post is dated 8 January 2024. The last class the lab taught that we found is Spring 2024. A Spring 2026 food challenge lab has another instructor and does not name the lab [general, Medium, AML-02; AML-22; AML-23].
7. **Localisation by sponsored projects.** A Brazilian egg producer, Mantiqueira, had a student team rebuild its plant egg powder from Brazilian plants (2020). The 2024 course asked for minimally processed foods from locally available raw materials and by-products [general, Medium, AML-05; AML-22]. This is a cheap first-test format for T10 and TPP-05.
8. **Vietnam has a platform but not the course.** The FID 2025 contest at Ho Chi Minh City University of Industry and Trade (HUIT) drew nearly 500 contestants and 144 projects from 58 universities and high schools. It ran a firm-judged table with 10 enterprise prizes. No plant-protein product was among the winners [VN-direct, Medium, AML-33]. A 2023 season had a "Plant-based food" theme [VN-direct, Low, AML-34, snippet only]. One Vietnamese search found no credit-bearing alternative-protein course; L6 owns the full inventory.
9. **No Vietnamese link to the Berkeley centre.** Its global partners include Chulalongkorn University (Thailand) and Sunway University (Malaysia), but none in Vietnam [VN-adjacent, Medium, AML-31].
10. **A pilot line can be a classroom.** Berkeley's Master of Bioprocess Engineering runs its capstone at a pilot unit with a 300 L bioreactor and downstream equipment, co-taught by industry. It lists the Alt:Meat product-design course as an elective [general, High, AML-32]. This gives RM-09 a concrete design and gives T5 an anchor user.
11. **Research topics screened.** We screened a plant-based seafood challenge (with catfish and tilapia among the species options), a fat-and-water formulation challenge, a legume comparison, a gene-editing project on pea off-flavour and a food neuroscience partnership. None produced a result we could retrieve [general, Medium, AML-03; AML-06; AML-08; AML-17; AML-19; AML-25].

## Detailed findings

### 1. What the lab is

- **Identity.** The Alternative Meats (Alt:Meat) X-Lab sits in the Sutardja Center for Entrepreneurship and Technology (SCET), College of Engineering, UC Berkeley. It calls itself "first and foremost an academic resource" that aims "to research and educate". Its scope has widened from meat to eggs, dairy and seafood, and to less intensive processing such as fermentation [general, Medium, AML-01].
- **Start.** The first plant-meat challenge lab ran in spring 2017, and the lab was announced in August 2017 [general, Medium, AML-03; AML-04]. The launch post says the spring 2017 class was the world's first course solely on plant-meat products. That is the lab's own claim, and we did not verify it.
- **People (roles only).** The People page lists 16 people [general, Medium, AML-01]:
  - a director, a lecturer, an instructor and a programme manager;
  - three programme coordinators and one postdoctoral scholar;
  - SCET's founding director;
  - four advisory committee members, including the executive director of Berkeley's Master of Bioprocess Engineering;
  - three industry fellows, from MilliporeSigma, Louis Dreyfus Company and Nestle.

  A co-director with plant-protein R&D experience in industry served from January 2022 and is described as "former" by January 2024 [general, Medium, AML-07; AML-02].
- **Partners.** The Partners page shows only logos: the Open Philanthropy Project, Louis Dreyfus Company, Nestle, Thimus, Mantiqueira and one unnamed logo. It does not say what partners do [general, Medium, AML-01]. Other partners named in blog posts:
  - Givaudan, which set a technical challenge in 2017;
  - the Good Food Institute (GFI), which provided mentors;
  - SeaCo, which sponsored the 2017 seafood challenge;
  - the Berkeley Food Institute.

  Sources: [general, Medium, AML-03; AML-06; AML-09].
- **Money.** The only figure found is USD 125,000 from the Open Philanthropy Project (now Coefficient Giving) in 2019 [general, Medium, AML-04]. This makes the lab a grantee in L9's population; the lead session may want to cross-reference it.

### 4. The teaching and venture-building model

- **Format.** Challenge Labs are 4-unit classes "for students of all academic backgrounds". Students form startup teams that "create technology solutions or services". An industry partner or social challenge poses a broadly defined problem [general, High, AML-21].
- **Entry and workload.** In Spring 2024: entry by application and resume with the instructor's consent; 4 hours a week in class and 8 hours outside; capacity 45, enrolled 27 [general, High, AML-22].
- **Two strands.** One strand is product design of plant-based foods (spring), led by the director with an instructor or co-director. The other is customer needfinding (fall), led by a design lecturer. A technical foundations course in Fall 2022 prepared students for the spring challenge lab. Projects become pitches to investors and industry partners [general, Medium, AML-07; AML-21].
- **Industry role.** Alumni describe group projects "on behalf of different corporate partners" and direct contact with company leaders [general, Medium, AML-18]. Industry fellows from three multinationals sit in the lab [general, Medium, AML-01].
- **Showcase.** Teams present at SCET's twice-yearly Collider Cup. Both Impact Food and Uncracked won prizes there [general, Medium, AML-04; AML-14; AML-15].

### 6. Could a Vietnamese university copy it?

**What Vietnam has.**
- **A national contest.** HUIT runs the Food Innovation and Development (FID) contest. In 2025 its theme was "The Future of Food": nearly 500 contestants, 144 projects from 58 universities and high schools, and 30 finalist teams in three tables [VN-direct, Medium, AML-33].
- **A firm-judged table.** One table (Problem Solving Innovation) is judged by firms, with five "Enterprise Choice" and five "Enterprise Encouragement" prizes [VN-direct, Medium, AML-33].
- **Co-organisers.** The co-organisers are the Vietnam Association of Food Science and Technology (VAFoST, already registered as BUY-44), the National Startup Support Center, GITP and BambuUp [VN-direct, Medium, AML-33].
- **What won in 2025.** The winners included fermented and side-stream products, for example a fermented cracker, a tofu-whey vinegar and a fish-scale collagen. No plant-protein product was among them [VN-direct, Medium, AML-33].
- **A 2023 plant-based theme.** A search summary of the season 5 (2023) plan says it had a "Plant-based food" theme. We could not read the PDF: it returned HTTP 503 and then failed TLS verification [VN-direct, Low, AML-34].
- **The skills gap.** The study already finds a specialist skills gap: sensory and structure work on meat analogues, extrusion and protein-quality scoring (`ch06-knowledge-talent` section 6.5).
- **No course found.** One Vietnamese-language search found no syllabus for an alternative-protein course. L6 is building the inventory of Vietnamese courses. Its folder held only a stub at the time of writing, so this comparison should be rechecked at fold-in.

**What a contest lacks and a course adds.** A contest runs for weeks; a course runs for a semester, with credit, a lecturer and a firm's problem from the start. Berkeley's spin-outs came from semester-long work with partner contact, not from the showcase alone [general, Medium, AML-10; AML-18]. Vietnam's FID already supplies the showcase and the firm-judged table.

**Transfer route.** SCET offers three-year global partnerships with partner-based pricing. Faculty can learn its teaching method and send venture teams to Silicon Valley. Its Southeast Asian partners are in Thailand and Malaysia [VN-adjacent, Medium, AML-31]. A Vietnamese faculty would not need the partnership to copy the format; it is a choice, not a requirement (our judgement).

### 7. Deep evaluation of the shortlist

1. **AML-I01, the product-design challenge lab, becomes candidate public good AML-C1** (section 8).
2. **AML-I16, a pilot-line teaching capstone, strengthens RM-09 and T5.**
   - RM-09 ("Process-engineering skills": operators and senior process engineers, industry placements) has no design and no costing.
   - Berkeley's two-semester Master of Bioprocess Engineering offers one: upstream and downstream courses, a lab course, a quality-by-design course co-taught by an industry advisory board, and a pilot-scale capstone [general, High, AML-32]. The capstone runs at the Advanced Biofuels and Bioproducts Process Development Unit, with a 300 L bioreactor, disc-stack centrifuge, filtration skid and chromatography [general, High, AML-32].
   - For Vietnam, a T5 pilot line that also hosts a university capstone gains a steady user. That helps T5's kill test of use below about a third of capacity in the first two years.
   - We have not costed it. We found no open food-grade pilot fermenter in Vietnam [VN-direct, Medium, INF-02; INF-05].
3. **AML-I05, sponsored localisation projects, strengthens T10 and TPP-05.**
   - TPP-05's cheapest first test is "functional samples to two dairy research teams".
   - The Mantiqueira case adds a format: the buyer sets a specification, here the plant-protein function it now gets from imported pea protein, and a student team characterises local protein against it [general, Medium, AML-05; VN-direct, Low, FORM-01].
   - This is weak evidence. No result from the Berkeley projects was published.
4. **AML-I11, Prime Roots, strengthens T9 as a signal only** (section 5).

### 8. Candidate AML-C1: buyer-set protein product challenge lab

- **What.** A credit-bearing, one-semester product-design course at one or two food-technology faculties, with a short technical foundations module first. Teams of three to five work on problems set, and partly paid for, by Vietnamese buyers: chay makers, plant-milk makers, ingredient distributors and fermenters. The problems are T1, T9 and T10 products and specifications, using local raw materials and food-grade side streams. Teams present at an annual showcase, for example a table at FID.
- **Why Vietnam.** It fills a gap that no public good covers. P1, P4, P5 and P6 are rules, a laboratory and data. RM-09 is process engineering. A host platform (FID), co-organisers (VAFoST, the National Startup Support Center) and named buyers already exist.
- **What must be true.** A host faculty with a pilot kitchen and basic analysis; at least two firms that set problems and give samples and staff time; a lecturer with industry R&D experience; and access to an extruder or pilot line for T1 work (links to T5 and P4).
- **Kill tests.** AML-C1 fails if any of these is true:
  - no Vietnamese firm sets and co-funds a problem for the first cycle;
  - fewer than 15 students enrol in each of the first two cycles;
  - no firm runs its own trial of a team's prototype or specification within two cycles.
- **First customers.** Hosts: HUIT, IUH, HUST, Can Tho University, Nong Lam University. Problem setters: chay makers (An Nhien, Au Lac), plant-milk makers (Vinamilk, TH) and ingredient distributors.
- **Horizon and capital.** 0 to 2 years. Low capital: the Berkeley lab's only disclosed grant was USD 125,000 [general, Medium, AML-04]. We did not estimate the Vietnamese cost.

**Scores** (our judgement, calibrated against `plays.csv`; weighted scores are our calculation in `calc_l8.py`):

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Market pull | 2 | Firms engage with FID's firm-judged table, but no firm has asked for a course |
| Vietnam advantage | 2 | Local raw materials and chay formats give the problems, but any neighbour could run it; Thailand and Malaysia already partner with SCET |
| Technical readiness | 4 | Proven teaching format; Vietnamese faculties and a contest exist; extrusion access is the weak point |
| Route to market | 4 | A university can run a course under its own rules; products from it go through ordinary self-declaration |
| Capital efficiency | 5 | Teaching budget, not plant capital |
| Defensibility | 1 | Anyone can copy it, which is the point of a public good |
| Public value | 2 | Builds product-development skills and buyer specifications; small, indirect effect on protein security |

Weighted scores: balanced 2.95, investors 2.95, policy 2.50, startups 3.35, manufacturers 3.05, research 2.50, international 2.75. With public value at 3 instead of 2, the balanced score would be 3.10. Under the balanced preset it ranks below T10 (3.05) and T9 (3.00) and above T7 (2.10). It beats every existing play on capital efficiency and fills a gap none covers. It is a low-priority public good and should merge with any L6 talent proposal (GFI-023, GFI-024, GFI-057).
