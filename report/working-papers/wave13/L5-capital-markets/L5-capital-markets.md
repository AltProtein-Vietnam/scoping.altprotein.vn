# Capital, markets and business services: GFI solutions screened for Vietnam

Line: L5 capital, markets and business services (wave 13). Source prefix: SLK. Date: 2026-10-15.

## Verdict

None of the 20 GFI entries assigned to this line becomes a new play or public good. Six strengthen existing items, six are already covered, six are not for Vietnam now and two are out of scope. The useful yield is on how to finance T1, the domestic textured-protein line. Vietnamese law and named providers already offer finance leasing (Circular 26/2024/TT-NHNN), factoring including factoring for the purchaser (Circular 20/2024/TT-NHNN), bank supply-chain finance anchored on a large buyer (for example BIDV's SCFast 2026), an SME credit guarantee fund in Ho Chi Minh City whose per-customer cap covers most T1 line sizes, and Decree 57/2018 support for processing plants; the package names none of them. These turn the package's "bank debt and trade finance" advice into named instruments, add a financing test (the anchor buyer's bank must finance the supplier) and a legal constraint (pooled offtake must not become a price agreement among competing chay makers), and narrow RM-12 to T4 and T5 scale. Yield class: **added a route** (financing for T1) **and a constraint**; no conclusion is reversed, and corporate venture arms, investor platforms and food-protein accelerators were a clean negative for Vietnam.

## Headline findings

1. **Finance leasing is a legal, existing route for T1 equipment, not for its working capital.** Circular 26/2024/TT-NHNN (in force 1 July 2024) lets licensed lessors lease machinery and production lines and do sale and leaseback, in VND unless the lessee earns foreign currency [VN-direct, High, SLK-01]. Production-line leases stood at over VND 6,900 billion (about USD 265 million, our calculation) on 30 June 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-04]. BIDV-SuMi Trust Leasing says over 40% of its book is production machinery, mainly for SMEs (company statement) [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-24].
2. **Factoring has a current rule and a small market.** Circular 20/2024/TT-NHNN replaced Circular 02/2017 and allows factoring with and without recourse and factoring for the purchaser, for under one year [VN-direct, High, SLK-05]. Balances were about VND 16,000 billion in August 2025, about 0.1% of credit (our calculation) [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-06]. The SBV circulated a draft amendment in March 2026; we found no issued text [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-07].
3. **Buyer-anchored supply-chain finance is the concrete form of "trade finance" for T1.** BIDV offers supplier finance, buyer-side and seller-side factoring and distributor overdrafts across nearly 100 supply chains, with up to 85% of order value for SMEs referred by an anchor company (bank release) [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-19]. IFC and SECO fund a supply-chain finance programme to 2029, and IFC says suppliers are typically paid 30 to 60 days after delivery [VN-direct, High, SLK-18]. It only works with a large anchor buyer, which reinforces the package's advice on T1 ownership.
4. **A local guarantee fund can cover most of a T1 line.** The Ho Chi Minh City SME Credit Guarantee Fund's new charter (29 May 2026) allows guarantees of up to 100% of a loan, capped per customer at about VND 34 billion (USD 1.3 million, our calculation) for investment loans, with 20% owner's equity [VN-direct, High, SLK-20]. That covers almost all of the package's range for a T1 line's installed cost (USD 0.28 to 1.38 million), and the 20% equity rule means no loan is fully debt-funded anyway. So T1 needs no new concessional window; RM-12 matters at T4 and T5 scale.
5. **Decree 57/2018 support for processing plants is in force but uncertain.** Article 11 offers 60% of investment up to VND 15 billion (about USD 0.58 million) per project if processed value is at least 1.5 times raw value [VN-direct, High, SLK-21]. A T1 line meets that test only at low flour prices (1.37 to 1.97 times, our calculation). A replacement draft discussed on 31 March 2026 would cap support at 30% of investment, and officials call the current decree's effect "limited" [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-23].
6. **Pooled offtake needs a legal form.** The Law on Competition 2018 bans outright agreements fixing prices of goods among firms in the same relevant market (Art. 11(1), 12(1)) [VN-direct, High, SLK-08]. Parallel bilateral contracts with the line owner, or a cooperative of at least five members under the Law on Cooperatives 2023 (with tax relief on internal transactions and preferential credit), avoid a horizontal price agreement (our reading, not legal advice) [VN-direct, High, SLK-09].
7. **We found no venture arm of a Vietnamese food group.** None turned up for Masan, Vinamilk, TH, Nutifood, KIDO or PAN Group; VinVentures (Vingroup, USD 150 million) targets AI, semiconductors and cloud [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-12]. Domestic strategics would act through joint ventures, supply contracts or acquisitions.
8. **We found no accelerator in Vietnam with a food-protein track.** Two 2026 Ho Chi Minh City programmes cover sustainable agriculture and agricultural processing generally [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-14; SLK-15].
9. **The state already pays for export trade-fair booths.** MOIT's 2026 National Trade Promotion Programme paid 100% of booth rent and standard build for a 16-booth Vietnam pavilion at the Private Label Show (15 to 17 November 2026) [VN-direct, High, SLK-16]. This is a ready venue for the export quote test (DMV-11).
10. **Investor platforms, conferences, IP pools and marketplaces have nothing to work on in Vietnam yet.** With no disclosed institutional alternative-protein round in Vietnam (S11.1), these GFI ideas are not for Vietnam now; further desk research on them would be noise.

### Findings by instrument

#### 1. Equipment and finance leasing (GFI-053)

**The rule.** Finance leasing is a licensed credit activity. Circular 26/2024/TT-NHNN of the State Bank of Vietnam (SBV), in force from 1 July 2024, governs leasing by general finance companies and finance-leasing companies [VN-direct, High, SLK-01].

- Leasable assets are "machinery, equipment, tools, means of transport" not on the banned trade or import lists (Art. 3(2)). A textured-protein extruder, dryer and packing line fit this definition [VN-direct, High, SLK-01].
- The lessee needs a feasible plan for using the asset and the financial capacity to pay (Art. 5(1)(c) and (d)). Leases up to VND 100 million (about USD 3,800 at 26,000 VND per USD, our calculation) are "small-value" and skip the plan test (Art. 3(12), Art. 5(2)) [VN-direct, High, SLK-01].
- Sale and leaseback is allowed (Chapter III, Art. 26 to 29): a firm sells equipment it owns to the lessor and leases it back. The asset must be owned, undisputed, not pledged elsewhere and in normal operation (Art. 27) [VN-direct, High, SLK-01].
- Leases are in VND. Foreign currency is allowed only for imported assets paid to the supplier in foreign currency, and only if the lessee earns enough foreign currency to repay (Art. 9(2)) [VN-direct, High, SLK-01]. So a domestic textured-protein maker selling in VND would lease an imported Chinese line in VND.
- The visible text sets no minimum term, no deposit rule and no purchase-option price; the lessee chooses at the end whether to buy or keep leasing (Art. 16(2)) [VN-direct, High, SLK-01].
- A January 2026 SBV draft would raise the small-value threshold to VND 400 million and add intangible assets. We found no confirmation that it has been issued [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-02].

**The market.** The sector is small. Finance-leasing balances were about VND 40,700 billion at the end of March 2025 (about USD 1.57 billion, our calculation) [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-03] and VND 41,700 billion at 30 June 2025 for six of nine companies, about 0.25% of total credit [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-04]. **Production lines** are a real asset class: over VND 6,900 billion outstanding at 30 June 2025 (about USD 265 million, our calculation) [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-04]. The Vietnam Leasing Association's count of companies is internally inconsistent (nine companies, of which eight active, one suspended and two bankrupt) [VN-direct, Low, SLK-04]. BIDV-SuMi Trust Leasing is named in the sources we read [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-04; SLK-24]; Sacombank Leasing, Agribank Leasing and Chailease International Leasing appear only in search snippets we did not read [VN-direct, Low, snippets only, not registered]. Leasing firms cannot take public deposits and face bank-like safety ratios, which limits how cheaply they can fund themselves [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-04].

BIDV-SuMi Trust Leasing (BSL), a 2017 joint venture of BIDV and Japan's Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Bank, says over 40% of its outstanding leases are production machinery and equipment, mainly for SMEs, with about 12% of the market; its chief executive says eight leasing companies operate and the sector held just over VND 40 trillion at the end of 2024, about 0.26% of credit (company statements) [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-24]. So there is at least one named lessor whose main book is the kind of asset a T1 line needs.

**Gap.** We could not read any lessor's terms (financing ratio, term, rate): www.bsl.com.vn, www.chailease.com.vn and vcbleasing.com.vn were unreachable through the session proxy (connection reset, 503 or connect refused), and sbv.gov.vn returned 403. The question of terms for a USD 0.1 to 1.4 million food-processing line therefore needs a phone call.

**Reading for T1.** Leasing is a legal, existing route for the equipment part of a T1 line, including sale and leaseback for a chay maker that already owns equipment. But T1's equipment is cheap (USD 0.28 to 1.38 million installed, package figure) and its larger need is working capital (USD 0.6 to 3.5 million). Leasing does not fund working capital. So leasing strengthens the T1 financing advice at the margin; it does not change it.

#### 2. Factoring (GFI-060)

**The rule.** Circular 20/2024/TT-NHNN, in force from 1 July 2024, replaced Circular 02/2017/TT-NHNN (Art. 32) [VN-direct, High, SLK-05].

- Providers: commercial banks, general finance companies, factoring finance companies and foreign bank branches (Art. 2.1, 3.1) [VN-direct, High, SLK-05].
- Three forms: factoring with recourse to the seller (Art. 3.9), without recourse (Art. 3.10) and **factoring for the purchaser** (Art. 3.11), where the factor pays the seller on the buyer's behalf and the buyer repays. The last is the nearest Vietnamese form to reverse factoring (supply-chain finance arranged by a large buyer) [VN-direct, High, SLK-05].
- Eligible receivables come from contracts for goods or services; receivables with one year or more to run, already pledged, overdue or disputed are excluded (Art. 7). Factoring terms must be under one year (Art. 17.1) [VN-direct, High, SLK-05].
- Domestic factoring is in VND; foreign currency only for foreign-currency receivables from non-residents and a few other cases (Art. 9.2). International (export) factoring is defined (Art. 3.17) [VN-direct, High, SLK-05].

**The market.** Factoring balances were about VND 16,000 billion in August 2025 against nearly VND 17 million billion of total credit, about 0.1% (our calculation) [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-06]. Speakers at the Vietnam Banks Association (VNBA) and IFC roundtable came from Vietcombank, Techcombank and Citibank. They named obstacles: duplicate pledging of receivables, appraisal of buyers, accounting treatment, and four legal gaps (ownership of receivables in the seller's bankruptcy, anti-assignment clauses, the right to collect from the buyer, priority on enforcement). VNBA said it would send proposals to amend the rules to the SBV [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-06].

**Reading for T1.** Factoring fits the working-capital gap better than leasing: a textured-protein maker that sells on credit to chay makers, co-packers or a distributor could factor its invoices. It works best when the buyer is large and creditworthy (CJ Foods Vietnam, Vissan, a national distributor), because the factor prices the buyer's risk. It does not help with the long tail of small chay makers. It needs no new law; it needs a bank willing to price the buyer.

Rule changes since 2024. Both circulars are more than 12 months old, so we checked for amendments. On 13 March 2026 the SBV circulated a draft amending the factoring circular (Official Letter 1776/NHNN-TD), and VNBA collected comments until 23 March 2026 [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-07]. The draft's content was not on the page we could read, and we found no issued amendment by 15 October 2026. A search snippet mentions a "Circular 31/2026/TT-NHNN" on finance-lease interest rates in force from 15 August 2026; we could not find or read it, so it is unverified [VN-direct, Low, search snippet only, not registered]. A search snippet also gives a factoring balance of about VND 22,000 billion in 2026; we did not read the page (thitruongtaichinhtiente.vn returned 403), so we keep VND 16,000 billion (August 2025) as the figure read.

#### 3. Pooled procurement and offtake behind a T1 line (GFI-019, GFI-034)

**What the package already says.** Section 26.7 suggests "pooled offtake from two or three chay makers behind one line, a letter of intent or a joint trial". The T1 kill test is fewer than three buyers signing conditional orders totalling at least 2,000 t a year. Section S11.4 suggests an offtake from a named food manufacturer.

**A legal constraint the package does not mention.** The Law on Competition 2018 prohibits outright "agreements on directly or indirectly fixing prices of goods or services" among enterprises in the same relevant market (Art. 11(1) with Art. 12(1)). Exemption is possible for a limited period if the agreement benefits consumers and meets one of four conditions (Art. 14); the English text we read does not mention small firms in Article 14 [VN-direct, High, SLK-08]. Article 11(1) does not say whether it covers purchase prices. So a horizontal agreement in which competing chay makers fix the price they will all pay for soy flour, or for textured protein from one line, carries a legal risk that a lawyer should check (our reading; not legal advice) [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-08].

Two structures avoid a horizontal price agreement (our reading):

- **Parallel bilateral offtakes.** The T1 line owner signs a separate supply contract with each buyer. Each buyer agrees volume and price only with the seller. This is an ordinary sale-of-goods contract (we did not read the Commercial Law for this line).
- **A buyers' cooperative.** The Law on Cooperatives 2023 lets at least five official members, including Vietnamese legal persons, form a cooperative (Art. 4(7), Art. 30) [VN-direct, High, SLK-09]. A cooperative serves members through "internal transactions" under written agreements and may require members to register their demand in advance (Art. 4(1), Art. 9(4)) [VN-direct, High, SLK-09]. Income from internal transactions may be exempt from or reduced for corporate income tax (Art. 22(3)(a)), and cooperatives can get preferential loans and investment-credit guarantees from the Cooperative Development Support Fund (Art. 23(1)) [VN-direct, High, SLK-09]. A cooperative of five or more chay makers could buy soy flour jointly or own a textured-protein line and sell to members. Whether a cooperative's joint buying is treated as one buyer or as an agreement among competitors under the Law on Competition is not settled in what we read.

One Vietnamese search found no chay makers' association or joint-buying group (section 12); the Food and Foodstuff Association of Ho Chi Minh City describes no joint buying [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-25]. Absence in our log is not proof of absence.

#### 4. Loan funds and state credit (GFI-052, against RM-12)

GFI proposes infrastructure loan funds, loan guarantees and due-diligence support for first plants. In Vietnam the nearest existing instruments are state ones, and the package already covers most of them (NATIF interest subsidy, Decree 156/2025 credit treatment, the Investment Support Fund, Decree 263/2026 hi-tech agriculture zone subsidies; section S11.9).

One state lender is missing from the package: the **SME Development Fund (SMEDF)**. Decree 45/2024/ND-CP, in force 10 June 2024, amended its rules. It lends to innovative SMEs and to SMEs in industry-linkage clusters and value chains that have feasible plans (amended Art. 16), and lends indirectly through commercial banks, which appraise and bear the risk (amended Art. 22) [VN-direct, High, SLK-10]. A 2021 press report gave its rate as 80% of the lowest commercial lending rate (2.16% a year short term, 4.0% medium and long term at the time) through five partner banks (BIDV, MB, SHB, HDBank, Bac A Bank) [VN-direct, Low, SLK-11]. We could not read current rates, loan caps or terms. A textured-protein line owned by an SME that supplies a chay value chain could plausibly apply as a "value chain" borrower (our reading). This is a named, existing lender under Vietnamese law that fits T1 better than any venture instrument, but its scale and current terms are unknown.

Provincial SME credit guarantee funds are covered in section 10 below. We did not check the Vietnam Development Bank this wave.

**RM-12** (a concessional window with DFI co-finance and guarantees) remains an option, not an instrument with a provider. We found no Vietnamese or regional fund lending specifically to alternative-protein plants.

#### 5. Corporate venture arms of Vietnamese food groups (GFI-062)

We found **no corporate venture arm of a Vietnamese food or dairy group** (Masan, Vinamilk, TH, Nutifood, KIDO, PAN Group) in English or Vietnamese searches [VN-direct, Low, two searches]. The Vietnamese corporate venture funds we found are outside food:

- **VinVentures** (Vingroup, October 2024): USD 150 million, of which USD 50 million for new investments, seed and Series A, in AI, semiconductors and cloud; food, agriculture and biotechnology are not mentioned [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-12].
- **Vinh Hoan's** outward stakes through Vinh Technology Pte Ltd (2021) and **VinaCapital Ventures** (2024) are already in the package (S11.5).

A foreign food strategic has invested in a Vietnamese food business-to-business platform: DealStreetAsia tags CP Foods (CPF), Genesia Ventures and Quest Ventures on Kamereo's USD 4.6 million round in July 2021; the article is paywalled, so the investors' roles are unconfirmed [VN-direct, Low, SLK-13].

Reading: the package's view that a domestic strategic buyer is the likeliest lead (S11.6) stands, but that buyer would act through its balance sheet (a joint venture, a supply contract or an acquisition), not through a venture fund. Vietnamese food groups have no venture arm to approach.

#### 6. Accelerators (GFI-061)

The package lists ThinkZone, VIISA (dormant), New Energy Nexus Vietnam, SIHUB, the Saigon Hi-Tech Park incubator and regional alternative-protein accelerators (S11.11), and OQ-070 asks about 2026 programmes. Two 2026 programmes in Ho Chi Minh City are new to the package:

Its sustainable-agriculture track takes SMEs, agritech firms and cooperatives with revenue of at least USD 1 million; benefits total "nearly US$20,000"; food and protein are not mentioned [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-14].
- **High-tech agriculture startup contest 2026** run by the HCMC Agricultural Hi-Tech Park (AHTP) management board with the VNU-HCM University of Science. Agricultural processing (*chế biến nông sản*) is in scope; protein is not mentioned and no prize amounts are given [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-15].

#### 7. Import and export facilitation (GFI-077)

GFI proposes trade-promotion directories, compliance services and trade finance. Vietnam already has a state programme that pays for trade-fair presence. Under the **2026 National Trade Promotion Programme**, MOIT's Trade Promotion Department ran a Vietnam pavilion of about 16 booths at the **Private Label Show 2026** (Rosemont, Chicago, 15 to 17 November 2026) for agricultural, aquatic, food and beverage firms. The state pays 100% of booth rent and standard build; firms pay travel, a VND 10 million fee per booth and a refundable VND 30 million deposit. Applications closed on 30 July 2026 [VN-direct, High, SLK-16].

Reading: a private-label food fair is the right venue for the export route in ch17 (Vietnamese co-packers making wrapped or chay foods for foreign retailer brands; DMV-11, OQ-334). The instrument exists and is free at the booth level; what is missing is a Vietnamese exporter with a plant-protein or protein-upgraded chay product to show. So GFI-077 strengthens the export moves already in the package; it is not a new play. A search snippet says Decree 230/2026/ND-CP amended the trade-promotion Decree 28/2018/ND-CP in 2026; we did not read it [VN-direct, Low, snippet only].

#### 8. Buyer-anchored supply-chain finance: the working-capital route for T1 (GFI-060, GFI-026, GFI-052)

This is the most useful finding of the line. GFI-060 asks for factoring and reverse factoring; GFI-026 asks investors to promote "debt and inventory financing". Vietnam already has both as ordinary bank products, with a donor programme behind them.

- **The rule** is Circular 20/2024/TT-NHNN (section 2 above), which allows factoring for the seller and for the purchaser [VN-direct, High, SLK-05].
- **A named provider.** BIDV's supply-chain finance range includes supplier finance, buyer-side factoring (*bao thanh toán bên mua*), seller-side factoring and distributor overdrafts. BIDV says it serves nearly 100 supply chains, naming autos, animal feed, chemicals and fertilisers and building materials. Under its SCFast 2026 package (up to VND 15,000 billion, about USD 577 million, our calculation) it offers SMEs referred by an anchor company up to 85% of order value with inventory as collateral, collateral up to 10% lower than standard, and unsecured limits up to VND 10 billion (about USD 0.38 million, our calculation) for distributors in target sectors (bank's own release) [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-19].
- **A donor programme.** The IFC and SECO Viet Nam Supply Chain Finance Program entered a second phase in March 2025 with a CHF 5 million SECO grant to 2029, working on the legal framework and bank capacity with the SBV. IFC says Vietnamese suppliers are typically paid 30 to 60 days after delivery [VN-direct, High, SLK-18].

**What this means for T1** (our reading). The package says T1's main need is USD 0.6 to 3.5 million of working capital (VND 15.6 billion to 91 billion at 26,000 VND per USD, our calculation) and that it suits "bank debt and trade finance". Buyer-anchored supply-chain finance is the concrete form of that advice:

- A textured-protein maker that supplies a large anchor buyer (for example CJ Foods Vietnam or Vissan, both T1 first customers in the package) could finance its receivables through supplier finance or factoring priced on the anchor's credit.
- An ingredient distributor that buys the line's output could carry stock on a distributor overdraft.
- The constraint: these products need an anchor that the bank already finances. The long tail of small chay makers is not an anchor. So the financing route reinforces the package's advice that T1 should be owned by, or contracted to, a large chay maker, co-packer or distributor. It adds a test: before building, ask the anchor buyer's bank whether it will put the T1 supplier into the anchor's supply-chain programme.

We found no Vietnamese supply-chain finance programme naming food or chay makers as a sector; animal feed is the nearest named sector [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-19]. Masan and Vinamilk supplier programmes were not found in our searches (absence in our log only).

#### 9. Price-risk tools (GFI-002, marketplace exchanges)

GFI-002 asks for exchanges that match buyers and sellers of ingredients. Vietnam's commodity exchange, the Mercantile Exchange of Vietnam (MXV), works through 33 market members and links to CME Group and other foreign exchanges; Decree 302/2026/ND-CP of 1 August 2026 replaced the earlier commodity-exchange decrees [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-17]. A search snippet reports MXV quoting soybean and soybean meal futures in September 2026; we did not read that page (403), so it is unverified [VN-direct, Low, snippet only]. A T1 owner could in principle hedge the soybean element of its flour cost through an MXV member, but food-grade, low-temperature defatted soy flour is not an exchange-traded product, and we found no evidence on how its price tracks soybean or soybean meal futures. This is a component to test, not a finding that changes T1.

For ingredient matching, the package already records the trade fair Food Ingredients Vietnam (about 250 exhibitors, May 2026), distributors (Brenntag, VMC, TDGIMEX) and the HoReCa platform Kamereo (section 16.5, D4). A marketplace platform would add little in a market where buyers meet sellers this way.

#### 10. Loan guarantees: a local provider exists (GFI-052, against RM-12)

GFI-052 asks governments to "provide loan guarantees" for first plants. Vietnam has provincial SME credit guarantee funds under Decree 34/2018/ND-CP. The Ho Chi Minh City fund (the city now includes the former Ba Ria-Vung Tau and Binh Duong) received a new charter on 29 May 2026 (Decision 3204/QĐ-UBND) [VN-direct, High, SLK-20]:

- charter capital about VND 227.9 billion (about USD 8.8 million, our calculation) (Art. 8);
- a guarantee of up to 100% of a loan, for working capital or medium and long-term loans (Art. 30);
- a cap per customer of 15% of actual charter capital for investment loans, about VND 34 billion or USD 1.3 million (our calculation), and of the customer's owner's equity for working-capital loans (Art. 32.2 and 32.3);
- total guarantees up to three times charter capital, about VND 684 billion or USD 26 million (our calculation) (Art. 32.4);
- conditions: a feasible plan, owner's equity of at least 20% of the project, no bad debt, and collateral (Art. 29). Fees are set by the city and were not visible.

Reading: the per-customer cap (about USD 1.3 million) covers almost all of the package's range for a T1 line's installed cost (USD 0.28 to 1.38 million). So a guaranteed bank loan for a T1 line in Ho Chi Minh City is possible under existing rules, if the owner is an SME with 20% equity. It cannot carry a fermentation first plant (T4, T5) of tens of millions of USD. So RM-12 stays necessary for T4 and T5, while T1 needs no new window. We read only the Ho Chi Minh City fund; older figures say there were about 25 provincial funds in 2021 to 2022 (search snippet, not read) [VN-direct, Low, snippet only].

#### 11. State support for a processing plant: Decree 57/2018 (GFI-052)

The package does not mention Decree 57/2018/ND-CP on incentives for enterprises investing in agriculture. Its Article 11 supports processing facilities with 60% of investment, capped at VND 15 billion (about USD 0.58 million, our calculation) per project, covering waste treatment, roads, power, water, workshops and equipment. The condition is that "the value of processed agricultural products increases at least 1.5 times the value of raw agricultural products" [VN-direct, High, SLK-21]. In July 2026 MAE still listed Decree 57/2018 and Decree 98/2018 (linkage contracts) as current policy; it also reported more than 23,455 agricultural cooperatives, nearly 40% linked with enterprises [VN-direct, High, SLK-22]. A draft decree to replace Decree 57/2018 was discussed on 31 March 2026; it would cap support at 30% of investment costs with per-project caps, and the Deputy Finance Minister said Decree 57's effectiveness "remains limited" [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-23].

Reading for T1 (our calculation; `calc_l5.py`). Landed Chinese textured protein at USD 1.29 to 1.38 per kg over delivered flour at USD 0.70 to 0.94 per kg gives a ratio of 1.37 to 1.97, before yield losses and other inputs. So a T1 line meets the 1.5 times test only when flour is cheap, which is also the condition for its margin. We could not confirm whether processing imported soy flour counts as processing "agricultural products" under Article 11, nor whether support is limited to particular areas. Support is paid from provincial budgets and has a reputation for slow payment (SLK-23 says effectiveness is limited). Treat it as a possible bonus, not a financing plan.

For T10 (mung bean and rice protein), Decree 98/2018 linkage contracts with farmers are the legal form for buying domestic crops forward with state support; we did not read Decree 98/2018 this wave.

#### 12. Market services and investor platforms (GFI-013, 026, 038, 049, 050, 051, 056, 065)

- **Business-to-business toolkits, brokers and directories (GFI-013, GFI-038, GFI-049).** The package already maps buyers and channels (D4, D5 `buyer_map.csv`, section 16.5) and proposes a standing panel of 10 to 15 research and procurement buyers (DMV-08). Plant proteins already reach small makers through distributors (Brenntag, VMC, TDGIMEX) and kitchens through HoReCa platforms (Kamereo). We found no chay makers' association. The Food and Foodstuff Association of Ho Chi Minh City became a *hiệp hội* (business association) on 28 November 2025, but the report of its congress describes no joint buying, directory or credit service [VN-direct, Medium, SLK-25]. It is a possible convener for DMV-08, not evidence of a service.
- **Intellectual-property pools (GFI-050).** T1 has no intellectual property (ch26), and the fermentation plays would license strains and processes from abroad. With no Vietnamese patent holders in alternative protein to pool, this is not for Vietnam now (our reading; we did not search patent databases).
- **Investor coordination, platforms and events (GFI-026, GFI-051, GFI-056, GFI-065).** These assume deal flow. The package found no disclosed institutional round for a Vietnam-based alternative-protein company, and no disclosed Vietnamese deal above USD 5 million (S11.1). Vietnam already has an investor network (VPCA) and the package lists funding programmes (S11.10, `funding_programs.csv`). A Vietnamese investor platform or conference would have nothing to show yet. The "debt and inventory financing" part of GFI-026 is covered above (section 8).

#### 13. Scope checks (GFI-003, GFI-076, GFI-107)

- **Demand forecasts (GFI-003)** are out of scope: the study does not make market-size forecasts and sizes demand only as labelled scenarios ([[ch18-demand-sizing]], `demand_model.py`).
- **Category marketing (GFI-076)** is out of scope: consumer marketing plans are excluded.
- **Climate and biodiversity synergies (GFI-107)** propose advocacy coalitions with climate and conservation groups. The study frames protein around security, industrial value and trade, and the measurement part (life-cycle assessment) is covered by PO-032 (grid emission factors) and by line L4 (GFI-093). We found no Vietnam-specific reason to act: not for Vietnam now.
