# Plant-based solutions from GFI's database, screened for Vietnam

Line: L1 plant-based (wave 13). Source prefix: SLP. Date: 2026-10-15.

## Verdict

We screened the 15 plant-based entries of the Good Food Institute (GFI) Solutions Database assigned to this line [general, Medium, GSD-01]. None justifies a new play or public good: 5 are already covered by the study, 4 are global frontier work Vietnam would buy rather than make, 3 are not for Vietnam now, and 3 strengthen existing items. The useful yield is a constraint on play T10 (mung bean and rice protein for plant drinks). At 2023 to 2025 import prices, mung beans alone cost USD 3.0 to 4.9 per kg of isolate, above the USD 2 to 3 of Chinese pea protein. Rice protein from broken rice costs USD 5.1 to 6.6 per kg of product in raw material alone, so it works only as a co-product of a rice syrup or starch line, and we found none in Vietnam (our calculation). Two items are strengthened: a better-priced second job for a mung bean isolate (replacing egg white powder), and a functional-testing extension of public good P4 (protein-quality laboratory). Yield class: **added a constraint**, with two questions partly closed (OQ-386, OQ-094) and one new disagreement (mung bean import tonnage and price). Further desk work on these entries would be noise: what remains needs buyer samples, mass balances from equipment suppliers and laboratory price lists.

## Headline findings

1. **Screen result.** Of 15 entries: strengthens existing 3 (GFI-017 to P4; GFI-018 to T10 and T4; GFI-078 to T10 and target product profile TPP-05); already covered 5 (GFI-035, 036, 040, 041, 096); global frontier, monitor 4 (GFI-011, 025, 037, 059); not for Vietnam now 3 (GFI-039, 103, 106). No new candidate play or public good [VN-direct, Medium, GSD-01; table in `data_solution_screen.csv`].
2. **A mung bean isolate made from imported beans cannot match Chinese pea protein on raw material alone.** Partner-reported imports cost USD 0.67 to 0.81 per kg FOB in 2023 to 2025. At 23.4 g of protein per 100 g of bean and an assumed 60 to 80% recovery, beans cost USD 3.0 to 4.9 per kg of 85% isolate before processing, against USD 2 to 3 per kg for Chinese pea protein (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, SLP-14; DIE-12; BUY-09; estimate 2025]. The line needs a paid starch co-product or a higher-priced job.
3. **Rice protein pays only as a co-product, and we found no host in Vietnam.** Enzyme-assisted extraction of VD20 broken rice reached about 77% protein and 55% recovery after 36 h at laboratory scale (Can Tho University and Nguyen Tat Thanh University, 2026) [VN-direct, Medium, SLP-01]. At USD 327 to 372 per t and 7.9 g of protein per 100 g, raw material alone costs USD 5.1 to 6.6 per kg of product, with about 940 kg of carbohydrate left per tonne (our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, FS-26; DIE-12; SLP-01; estimate 2026]. Elsewhere rice protein is a by-product of rice syrup [general, Medium, SLP-08]. Three searches found no Vietnamese rice syrup, rice starch or rice protein maker [VN-direct, Low].
4. **Broken rice now costs less per tonne than cassava starch.** Fragrant 100% broken rice was quoted at USD 368 to 372 per t for export on 19 September 2026, against USD 520 to 540 per t FOB for cassava native starch in April 2026 [VN-direct, Medium, FS-26; FS-11]. A broken-rice saccharification line would give sugar for T4 and a protein co-product for T10. Not costed.
5. **Egg-white replacement is a better-priced job for a mung bean isolate than plant milk.** Vietnam imported 2,536 t of egg white and egg powders in 2025, with Indian egg white powder at USD 5.90 per kg [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02, revealed]. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) raised no questions on mung bean protein isolate (more than 80% protein, ordinary wet process) for use at 3 to 90% of foods [general, High, SLP-11]. The market is small: tens to a few hundred tonnes of isolate (our calculation).
6. **Mung bean import data disagree.** For 2023 Vietnam reports USD 138.3 million and 103,835 t (weight estimated by UN Comtrade, USD 1.33 per kg); partners report 129,380 t for USD 86.1 million FOB (USD 0.67 per kg). Partners report 185,470 t in 2024 and 167,108 t in 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, SLP-14; FORM-08]. Logged as a disagreement; we use the partner tonnage and unit values.
7. **Buyers specify function, but no laboratory sells functional testing.** Meat processors specify gel strength and emulsion and retort stability; plant-milk makers specify solubility and stability under ultra-high-temperature (UHT) treatment [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-05; BUY-16, revealed]. No laboratory in the study's register lists functional or texture testing, and one search found only a dealer selling texture analysers [VN-direct, Low, SLP-17]. An open standard method set exists (Embrapa, 2022) [general, Medium, SLP-10]. This fits P4 as an extension, not a new public good.
8. **Vietnam has almost no texturisation research; Thailand has some.** OpenAlex counts 3 Vietnam-affiliated papers on extrusion, high-moisture or shear-cell texturisation of meat analogues from 2015 to 2026, against 31 Thai papers, including high-moisture extrusion of mung bean protein at Thailand's National Metal and Materials Technology Center (MTEC) [VN-direct, Medium, SLP-07; SLP-06]. The 2026 IUH paper is all-IUH by affiliation, but its equipment is still unverified [VN-direct, Medium, TIC-21; SLP-07].
9. **Shear-cell texturisation is commercial, but small and European.** Rival Foods makes "several hundred tons a year" in the Netherlands and raised EUR 10 million in June 2025 to double output (company claims in press) [general, Low, SLP-12; signal 2027]. It does not change T1, whose economics are set by soy flour, not by texturising method.
10. **Fat and flavour are not Vietnamese gaps now.** Vietnamese chay fillings use palm, soybean or generic vegetable oil; coconut appears only in imported analogues and one small cashew-coconut brand [VN-direct, High, FORM-01]. In Vietnam 23% of respondents ranked "tasted more like meat" among their top three reasons to eat more plant-based meat, below the six-country average of 28% [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02, stated]. Meat flavourings circulate as imported, repacked products with a fraud reputation (a 2011 report) [VN-direct, Low, SLP-13].
11. **Vietnamese research on local plant proteins is thin and laboratory-scale.** On-topic work since 2015 covers enzyme-assisted extraction from broken rice (2026), rice bran (2016, 2023) and black sesame meal (2026), and hydrolysates of mung bean, hyacinth bean and lima bean (2025). It focuses on bioactivity more than ingredient function [VN-direct, Medium, SLP-01; SLP-02; SLP-03; SLP-04; RD-25; SLP-07].
12. **The "mung bean starch water" stream does not exist at scale in Vietnam.** Glass noodles are made mainly from canna starch, for example by more than 20 producers in Thai Nguyen (former Bac Kan) [VN-direct, Low, SLP-18]. The stream is studied in Thailand, whose glass-noodle industry uses mung bean starch. Vietnam's own mung bean output is about 92,000 t, about a third to two-fifths of supply (snippet; our calculation) [VN-direct, Low, SLP-15; SLP-14].

### 2. Texturisation beyond extrusion (GFI-025)

- **GFI's case.** Fibre spinning (wet, electro, jet and blow spinning) can make finer fibres than extrusion, but needs food-grade solvents and scale-up. GFI rates it early adoption [general, Medium, GSD-01].
- **Status in 2026.** Spinning remains research. Shear-cell texturisation (a Couette cell that shears heated protein between cylinders) is commercial at small scale: Rival Foods makes several hundred tonnes a year in Geldrop, Netherlands, sells to restaurants and caterers, and claims a cost structure competitive with extrusion [general, Low, SLP-12; signal 2027]. We found no shear-cell unit in Asia.
- **Vietnam.** We found no shear-cell or spinning work. Texturisation research of any kind is 3 Vietnam-affiliated papers since 2015, against 31 Thai ones [VN-direct, Medium, SLP-07]. The three are the 2026 IUH high-moisture meat analogue with jackfruit fibre (TIC-21), a 2026 single-author paper from HCMUTE on hot-melt extrusion for antioxidant delivery (SLP-05) and a Taiwan-based team's pineapple-pomace extrudates with a Vietnamese co-author.
- **IUH (OQ-386).** Crossref and OpenAlex list all six authors of TIC-21 at the Department of Nutrition and Food Science, Institute of Biotechnology and Food Technology, IUH [VN-direct, Medium, TIC-21; SLP-07]. The publisher withholds the abstract from all three metadata services, and the Springer page asks for an authorisation step we did not follow. Two searches found no IUH extruder listing. Whether IUH holds a food extruder is still unknown.
- **Effect on T1.** None. T1 is dry textured soy made by standard extrusion, and its margin is set by the delivered price of soy flour against Chinese landed product ([[ch26-plays]] T1). Thai public extrusion capacity (MTEC, Kasetsart University) is the nearest trial route for later high-moisture work, alongside the Thai pilot-plant access already in T5 [VN-adjacent, Medium, SLP-06].

### 3. Characterisation service and functionality database (GFI-017, GFI-011) against P4

- **Do buyers need function data?** Yes. Meat processors use added protein with starch, fibre, phosphates and carrageenan, and specify gel strength, water and fat binding, and emulsion stability through retort sterilisation [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-05, revealed]. Plant-milk makers specify solubility, low viscosity, UHT stability and neutral flavour [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-16, revealed]. Chapter 16 already asks research bodies to publish functional tests of local proteins in Vietnamese formats.
- **Is there a service?** We found none. None of the 32 laboratories in `pilot_labs.csv` lists functional or texture testing. HUST lists protein extraction and characterisation (SDS-PAGE, DSC, in vitro digestibility), and FIRI's NACEFA lists proximate analysis. One search found a Vietnamese dealer selling an Ametek TA1 texture analyser, price on request, but no fee-for-service offer [VN-direct, Low, SLP-17].
- **Are local data missing?** Largely. Vietnamese papers on mung bean, broken rice, rice bran, peanut, sesame and okara proteins since 2015 are few, laboratory-scale and mostly about extraction yield or bioactivity (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory). They are not about the functions buyers specify [VN-direct, Medium, SLP-07; SLP-01; SLP-02; SLP-03; SLP-04; RD-24; RD-25]. One older Vietnamese study tested peanut protein as a sausage binder [VN-direct, Medium, HXE-02].
- **Has the global gap closed since 2021?** Not as a database: one search found no open plant-protein functionality database launched in 2024 to 2026. A standard open method set exists: Embrapa's 2022 guide covers emulsifying activity and stability, foaming capacity and stability, water and oil holding, water solubility and gelling, and is licensed CC BY-NC-ND [general, Medium, SLP-10].
- **New public good or P4 extension?** An extension. The likely hosts (FIRI NACEFA, HUST, Can Tho University's Institute of Food and Biotechnology, Nguyen Tat Thanh University's hi-tech centre at Saigon Hi-Tech Park) and users (T1, T9, T10 makers and the TPP buyers) are the same as P4's. A separate public good would beat P4 on no criterion. The extension: a standard functional panel on Embrapa's methods, two buyer tests (gel strength in a sausage model, UHT stability in a 5 g per 100 ml protein drink), and an open dataset for local crops and side streams linked to the P5 by-product atlas. GFI-011's sequence and machine-learning database is global research Vietnam would use.

### 4. Biological protein isolation (GFI-018) and T10

**Rice arm.**

- Enzyme-assisted extraction has been done in Vietnam. Viscozyme (a cellulase blend) on VD20 broken rice flour gave about 76 to 77% protein and about 55% recovery at pH 5.5, about 53 degC, 29 to 30 U/g and 36 h [VN-direct, Medium, SLP-01]. A 2010 Chinese study recovered 46 to 75% of broken-rice protein with proteases and measured emulsifying capacity of 58 to 72% and water holding of 2.0 to 2.9 g/g [general, Medium, SLP-09]. Rice protein is lysine-limited: DIAAS 42 for brown rice concentrate in pigs [general, High, SCI-40].
- **Economics** (our calculation; `tools/broken_rice_protein_calc.py`). Broken rice at USD 327 to 372 per t (FS-26; the domestic price converted at 26,000 VND per USD), 7.9 g protein per 100 g (DIE-12, ordinary polished rice) and 55% recovery give about 43.5 kg of protein in 57 kg of product per tonne. Raw material alone is USD 7.5 to 8.6 per kg of protein, or USD 5.1 to 6.6 per kg of product. About 940 kg of carbohydrate-rich residue is left per tonne. Chinese pea protein costs USD 2 to 3 per kg [VN-direct, Low, BUY-09]. So the carbohydrate must be sold, as rice syrup or starch, for the protein to compete. That is how rice protein is made elsewhere: "a by-product from the processing of white rice for syrup production" [general, Medium, SLP-08].
- **Host.** Three searches in English and Vietnamese found no Vietnamese rice syrup, rice starch or rice protein maker [VN-direct, Low]. Vietnamese syrup and glucose are made from cassava (Vedan's glucose capacity, VCO-03).
- **Link to T4.** Broken rice (USD 368 to 372 per t, September 2026) now costs less per tonne than cassava native starch (USD 520 to 540 per t FOB in April 2026; USD 405 to 415 in January 2026) [VN-direct, Medium, FS-26; FS-11; FS-01]. While cassava stays high, a broken-rice saccharification line is a second sugar feedstock with a protein co-product. Broken rice is also a food, an export and a feed, and rice protein is poorly soluble and lysine-limited, which fits TPP-05's UHT drink specification badly. We record it as a note for T4 and a route for T10's rice arm, not a play.

**Mung bean arm.**

- **Supply and price.** Partner-reported imports of HS 0713.31 were 129,380 t at USD 0.67 per kg FOB (2023), 185,470 t at USD 0.70 (2024) and 167,108 t at USD 0.81 (2025), with Myanmar at 109,577 t in 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, SLP-14]. Vietnam's own report for 2023 is USD 138.3 million and 103,835 t, a weight UN Comtrade flags as estimated, or USD 1.33 per kg [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-08; SLP-14]. Domestic output is about 92,000 t on about 83,000 ha (search snippet of a 2021 WorldVeg-led Delphi study; year not seen) [VN-direct, Low, SLP-15].
- **Economics** (our calculation; `tools/mung_isolate_calc.py`). At 23.4 g protein per 100 g of bean (DIE-12, code 3010), 85% isolate purity and an assumed 60 to 80% recovery, 4.5 to 6.1 kg of beans make 1 kg of isolate. Beans alone cost USD 3.0 to 4.9 per kg of isolate at partner-reported prices, and USD 6.0 to 8.1 at Vietnam's 2023 reported value [VN-direct, Low, SLP-14; DIE-12; estimate 2025]. A search snippet suggests product yields of 8 to 19% of bean mass in one 2023 study, which would mean lower recovery and higher cost; we could not open the paper. The starch, about half the bean, must carry part of the cost. TPP-05 already names Acecook as a buyer of pea and mung bean starch.
- **Starch water.** The brief asks about protein from mung bean starch-processing water. In China and Thailand the glass-noodle industry uses mung bean starch, and Thai researchers have recovered protein from its wastewater. In Vietnam glass noodles (*miến*) are made mainly from canna starch [VN-direct, Low, SLP-18]. We found no Vietnamese mung bean starch stream at scale.
- **What this means for T10.** T10 already asks for "cost near imported pea protein". The calculation shows raw material alone breaks that for both arms unless a co-product is sold. That is a constraint a plan would otherwise hit late. We propose adding it to T10's "what has to be true" and kill tests, with no score change.

### 5. Products, formats and ingredients

- **Meat snacks (GFI-040).** Vietnam's equivalents are chay formats already made from imported soy protein and gluten: dried chay "beef, chicken, fish, squid", chay floss and dry soy-gluten "ribs". A Ho Chi Minh City dealer sells Chinese lines for these formats and names no customers (FORM-34, re-read 2026-10-15). About 40,700 kg of dry soy-and-gluten "ribs" sold on Lazada (MKT-03). Imported mushroom jerky appears only in premium stores [VN-direct, Medium, FORM-34; MKT-03; FORM-01]. Their makers are T1 buyers and diaspora exporters (TPP-09). Already covered.
- **Private label (GFI-036).** Saigon Co.op has more than 2,500 private-label SKUs from more than 100 makers. GN Foods makes all five Co.op Select frozen chay dishes, and Lotte Mart sells Choice L chay [VN-direct, Medium, AIB-28; CPC-15; ECO-35]. Already covered by TPP-02.
- **Hybrids (GFI-096).** GFI's 20 to 50% plant share describes US and European blends. Vietnamese evidence sets 10 to 20% in fine emulsions and up to 30% in coarse mince, and no study supports 50% in pork products [VN-direct, Medium, HXE-01; HXE-02; BUY-05]. Already covered by TPP-03.
- **Animal-ingredient substitution (GFI-078).** Vietnam imported 2,536 t of egg white and egg powders (USD 16.9 million) and 6,778 t of gelatin in 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, BUY-02, revealed]. Mung bean protein isolate is the base of the best-known plant egg and has US GRAS status for uses at 3 to 90% of foods [general, High, SLP-11]. Against Indian egg white powder at USD 5.90 per kg, a mung bean isolate's raw-material cost (USD 3.0 to 4.9) leaves room that plant milk priced against Chinese pea protein does not. The volume is small: replacing 10% of egg powder imports is about 250 t a year (our calculation). It competes with the fungal binder of TPP-06. Gelatin replacement is mostly hydrocolloids, not protein. Strengthens T10 and TPP-05.
- **Fats (GFI-059, GFI-103).** Vietnamese chay fillings list palm, soybean or generic vegetable oil. Coconut appears in two imported Italian frozen analogues, one imported Chinese roll, plant milks and one small Vietnamese brand of cashew and coconut cheese and yoghurt [VN-direct, High, FORM-01]. Frozen analogues carry a median 13 g of fat per 100 g (n = 8). Vietnam crushes about 31 kt of copra and exports little coconut oil [VN-direct, Medium, FS-24]. Fat encapsulation matters for high-moisture whole cuts, which Vietnam does not make, so it is frontier work to monitor. Fat-profile design is not for Vietnam now: the documented nutrition issue for analogues is sodium (median 597 mg per 100 g, a child's whole lunch allowance) [VN-direct, Medium, NQR-01; FORM-01].
- **Flavour catalogue (GFI-106).** Vietnamese respondents rank a meatier taste below variety, nutrition, availability and fewer additives [VN-direct, Medium, CON-02, stated]. Meat flavourings in Vietnam have been imported, repacked and used to pass pork off as beef (2011 report) [VN-direct, Low, SLP-13]. Chay makers get flavour from seasonings (ECO-37; BUY-26). The live issue is animal-free certification of flavourings for vegan chay claims (DMV-13), not flavour discovery. Not for Vietnam now.
- **Open formulation sheets (GFI-035) and partnerships (GFI-041).** The target product profiles are open specification sheets, and DMV-08 proposes a standing buyer panel. ACC and Angel Yeast already run application days at Vietnamese customer sites [VN-direct, Low, AIS-38]. Already covered.
- **Molecular farming (GFI-037).** US regulatory status reviews exist for animal proteins in soy and pea [general, Medium, FTB-29; FTB-31; FTB-33]. Vietnam would buy such an ingredient, not make it, and would face its GMO certificates ([[ch07-rules]]). Monitor.
- **Turkey (GFI-039).** No turkey appears in the study's diet, retail or buyer data. Not for Vietnam now.

## Limits

- **Blocked or failed:** Springer pages for TIC-21 and the WorldVeg mungbean article (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40066-020-00273-7) redirect to an authorisation step, which we did not follow; the mungbean area and output figures therefore come from a search snippet. MDPI (https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/15/7/1265) and DOAJ returned HTTP 403; we used abstracts from OpenAlex and PubMed. The public OpenAlex API returned HTTP 429 (shared daily budget), so we used the OpenAlex MCP connector. USDA FoodData Central returned a rate-limit error with the public demonstration key.
- **Not verified:** whether IUH holds a food extruder; Vietnam's mung bean output year and farm-gate price; industrial recovery rates for mung bean isolate (60 to 80% is our assumption); the price of mung bean starch in Vietnam; whether any Vietnamese firm hydrolyses broken rice; the full GFI APAC mung bean report (landing page only, SLP-16).
- **Old evidence:** the meat-flavouring report (SLP-13) is from 2011 and is context only.
