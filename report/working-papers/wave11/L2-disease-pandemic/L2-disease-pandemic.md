# Animal disease, zoonoses and pandemic risk in the protein people eat

Line: L2 Animal disease, zoonoses and pandemic risk (wave 11). Source prefix: PAN. Date: 2026-10-01.

## Verdict

The hypothesis holds in part. Animal disease makes the supply and price of pork volatile, and pork and poultry carry a real but modest human-health burden (*Streptococcus suis*, *Salmonella*, rare H5N1 cases). It does not make Vietnam's protein supply as a whole insecure: in the worst shock (2019, 23% of pigs culled) poultry output rose 16.5%, imports were ordered in, and household tofu use did not change. The most important new fact is official: in September 2025 MAE's animal health department wrote that 79% of typed ASF samples in the north were recombinant strains against which the three licensed vaccines "have no protective effect", so the ASF exposure is now structural, not a vaccine away from closing. The second leg of the hypothesis, that alternative protein for food reduces this risk, fails at the study's scale: the benchmark path spares about 142,000 pigs a year in 2035, against 1.2 million culled for ASF in 2025 alone (our calculation), and we found no study that measures a fall in zoonotic, pandemic or resistance risk from replacing meat; the claims come from advocacy and company authors. Yield class: **added a constraint** (the scale and framing limit on the disease leg of the case), with a **clean negative** on "alternative protein reduces disease risk" and an **update** to the ASF vaccine and recombinant facts.

## Headline findings

1. **Recombinant ASF now dominates the north and the licensed vaccines do not cover it, by MAE's own account.** In 2025, 158 of 200 typed ASF samples in the north (79%), 32 of 42 in the centre and Central Highlands (76%) and 6 of 10 in the south (60%) were recombinant genotype I/II; overall 196 of 252 (77.8%, our calculation). The three licensed vaccines (NAVET-ASFVAC, AVAC ASF LIVE, Dacovet-ASF2) are made from genotype II virus and "have no protective effect" against other genotypes. [VN-direct, High, PAN-33] This crosses the wave 3 signpost "recombinant share above 50%" on surveillance samples. [VN-direct, High, PAN-33, signal 2026 to 2030]
2. **The 2019 shock was large for pork, small for protein.** Over 5.9 million pigs were culled (about 23% of the herd); live-weight pig output fell 13.8% and pork supplied in Q1 2020 was 19.3% below a year earlier. In the same year poultry output rose 16.5% and pork's share of meat output fell from 71.5% to 65.6%. [VN-direct, High, PAN-03]
3. **Price effects run both ways.** In 2019 live pig prices first collapsed to VND 24,000 to 35,000 per kg (May 2019, panic selling and pork avoidance), then hit a record above VND 90,000 per kg in late December 2019. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-04; VN-direct, High, PAN-03] The 2025 wave, which culled about 1.2 million pigs (3.8% of the end-2025 herd, our calculation), lowered prices: culling sales caused local oversupply and live hogs fell to about VND 50,000 per kg in November 2025. [VN-direct, High, MAC-01]
4. **Consumers and the state switched to other animal protein, not plant protein.** The 2020 policy response was to import about 100,000 t of pork in a quarter (43,553 t arrived by 7 April 2020, up over 300%) and to promote "pork substitutes and frozen pork" (Notice 132/TB-VPCP). [VN-direct, High, PAN-03] In 2025 "consumers increasingly substituted chicken for pork". [VN-direct, High, MAC-01] Hanoi traders reported a short panic shift to tofu and vegetables in March 2019 [VN-direct, Low, PAN-34, stated], but annual household tofu use did not move (2018 to 2020). [VN-direct, High, DIE-05, revealed]
5. **The modelled economic cost is real.** A published model finds a 10% fall in pig output cuts GDP 0.36% (USD 0.88 billion, 2018 base) and costs nearly 247,000 jobs; the 2019 fall (13.8%) lies between its 10% and 25% scenarios. [VN-direct, Medium, GEO-14; PAN-03, projection (published model, 2018 base)]
6. **Human H5N1 in Vietnam is rare and recent cases are sporadic.** Vietnam has reported 130 human H5N1 cases and 65 deaths since 2003, of which 2 cases (1 death) in 2020 to 2024, 1 case in 2025 and none in 2026 to 6 September. The 2024 death followed wild-bird hunting. WHO judges sustained human-to-human spread "currently considered unlikely". [VN-direct, High, PAN-01; PAN-02]
7. **Poultry HPAI losses are small against the flock and vaccination is half done.** From 1 January to 24 July 2026: 80 outbreaks in 17 provinces and over 349,400 birds dead or culled, about 0.06% of the end-2025 flock (our calculation); the first 2026 vaccination round covered 51.44% of required birds. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-09; MAC-12]
8. **The steady human-health burden from pork is foodborne and occupational.** *S. suis* is the leading cause of adult bacterial meningitis in northern Vietnam (2018 to 2024 cohort) [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-13]; MOH counted 123 cases and 2 deaths in 2025 [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-15]; a 2011 to 2014 estimate gives 1,437 to 1,866 DALYs a year and USD 2.27 to 2.88 million a year in indirect costs. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-11] *Salmonella* was found in 58.1% of 671 retail pork samples in the north. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-17]
9. **Resistance risk tracks how animals are farmed.** In Mekong chicken farms veterinary advice cut antimicrobial use 66% and mortality 40% [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-21]; prophylactic use "never reduced the probability of disease" [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-23]. The 2023 national AMR strategy (Decision 1121/QD-TTg) left the animal action plan to MAE; we still found no numeric 2030 target for antimicrobial use in animals. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-24]
10. **Pandemic risk evidence for Vietnam points at wildlife trade, live-bird markets and backyard poultry, not at the volume of meat eaten.** Coronavirus RNA rose from 20.7% of field rats at traders to 55.6% in restaurants [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-25]; H6 viruses with dual receptor binding circulate in live-bird markets [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-26]. The costed global prevention package is surveillance, wildlife-trade control and less deforestation, not diet change. [general, Medium, PAN-28]
11. **We found no study that measures a fall in zoonotic, pandemic or resistance risk from replacing animal-source food with alternative protein.** Peer-reviewed claims that it does are reviews or opinion, several by advocacy or company authors. [general, Medium, PAN-30; PAN-31; PAN-27]
12. **Scale settles the question for this study.** D-BENCH spares about 142,271 pigs and 2.5 million chickens and ducks a year in 2035 (app-d8); 2025 ASF culls were 8.4 times and 2019 culls 41.5 times the pigs spared (our calculation). Read against the standing herd, the pigs spared are about 0.5% of it (a flow against a stock), about the size of one ordinary year's herd change (the herd grew 0.5% in 2025 despite ASF, MAC-01). We found no evidence that a change of that size would move herd-level disease risk, which the sources tie to biosecurity, trade and farm practice rather than to herd size. [VN-direct, Low, our calculation from app-d8, MAC-01, PAN-03, estimate 2035]

**Vaccines.** About 7.8 million doses had been produced and supplied by August 2025, but MAE said the vaccination rate was "still low". [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-06] The September 2025 MAE letter is the first official statement we found that the licensed vaccines do not protect against the recombinant strain, and it reverses the emphasis: biosecurity is "the foundation" and vaccination "an important supporting tool", chosen by local genotype surveillance. [VN-direct, High, PAN-33] A 2026 scoping review finds genotype II candidates give "limited cross-protective immunity" and that genotype II-specific PCR may under-detect recombinants. [general, Medium, PAN-08] A 2026 comparative review describes Vietnam's control as "reliance on partial live-attenuated vaccination", against Thailand's physical biosecurity. [VN-adjacent, Medium, PAN-37]

**Policy.** Decision 779/QD-TTg (2 May 2026) sets the 2026 to 2030 national plan for avian influenza, foot-and-mouth disease and ASF: at least 30% fewer ASF outbreaks and culls than the 2020 to 2025 average, and 30% fewer HPAI outbreaks than the 2019 to 2025 average. The summary we read gives no numeric vaccination target. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-10, projection 2030 (official target)]

**Economic losses.** The only national estimate remains the published model in GEO-14. Its full text gives scenario results we have not used before: GDP down 0.36%, 0.90% and 1.80% for 10%, 25% and 50% falls in pig output (USD 0.88, 2.20 and 4.40 billion on a 2018 base); about 247,000 jobs lost and household income down nearly USD 600 million in the 10% scenario; smallholders draw 20 to 30% of income from pigs, medium and large farms 50 to 100%. [VN-direct, Medium, GEO-14, projection (published model, 2018 base)] We found no peer-reviewed loss estimate for the 2025 wave.

**Reading.** ASF is a pork-supply and pork-price exposure with a strong distributional sting (smallholders lose most; integrators gain share, as F2.11 says). The new vaccine fact makes "a major wave every two to three years" (F2.2.1 position) more, not less, likely to 2030. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-33; GEO-15, trend 2030]

### 3. Foodborne and occupational zoonoses tied to animal protein

- ***S. suis*.** Leading cause of adult bacterial meningitis in northern Vietnam (26 of 62 identified pathogens, 2018 to 2024, one tertiary hospital). [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-13] Burden 2011 to 2014: incidence 0.249 to 0.324 per 100,000 a year (imputed), 1,437 to 1,866 DALYs a year, direct cost USD 370,000 to 500,000 and indirect cost USD 2.27 to 2.88 million a year. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-11] MOH reported 123 cases and 2 deaths in 2025 and named *tiết canh* (raw pig blood pudding) as a main route. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-15] The National Hospital for Tropical Diseases treats 50 to 100 patients a year. [VN-direct, Low, PAN-16] Globally, about 37% of cases had eaten high-risk food and 38% had pig-related jobs; case fatality 12.8%. [general, Medium, PAN-12] Southeast Asian infection is "strongly linked to consumption of raw or undercooked pork and occupational exposure". [VN-adjacent, Medium, PAN-14]
- ***Salmonella* in pork.** 58.1% of 671 retail pork samples in three northern provinces (traditional 60.5%, modern retail 50.9%, food services 80.5%). [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-17] A risk model for boiled pork in urban Hung Yen gives an annual salmonellosis incidence of 17.7% with a 90% interval of 0.89 to 45.96%; one province, wide interval. [VN-direct, Low, PAN-18] 59.4% of 69 pork *Salmonella* isolates were multidrug resistant, including in "boutique" traceable pork. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-19] How much contaminated food adds to Vietnam's foodborne disease burden is "unclear". [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-20]
- **Food fraud linked to disease.** About 300 t of ASF pork reached Hanoi school meals in early 2026 (already in the package: CHN-36, TRU-20, BUY-20).

**Reading.** The steady human-health cost of animal protein in Vietnam is foodborne and occupational, concentrated in pork handling and raw-blood dishes. It is a food-safety problem with food-safety fixes (slaughter hygiene, cold chain, cooking, behaviour). A plant protein product avoids these specific pathogens but brings its own food-safety record (HSC-34 is a plant-based outbreak).

### 4. Antimicrobial use and resistance

- The package already has: the 1 January 2026 ban on prophylactic use (GEO-24), 71.7% of 2015 national use going to animals (MAC-36), and the global projection (GEO-25).
- New: AMU in chickens about 19 times that of humans on the same Mekong farms; MDR *E. coli* 63.3% in chickens and 55.1% in humans; isolates more similar within a farm than between farms. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-22] Prophylactic use did not reduce disease. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-23] Veterinary advice cut AMU 66% and mortality 40%, but only 35 of 102 recruited farms stayed in; most of the rest stopped raising chickens. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-21] In Europe, resistance genes were highest in farmers and tracked AMU and biosecurity. [general, Medium, PAN-29]
- Decision 1121/QD-TTg (25 Sep 2023) is the national AMR strategy to 2030; the veterinary action plan 2024 to 2030 sits with MAE. We found no numeric animal AMU target (primary text blocked). [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-24]
- A September 2025 trade-press summary cites resistance of 70% or more in *Salmonella*, *E. coli* and *S. aureus* from Vietnamese animal products (one researcher's work). [VN-direct, Low, PAN-40]

**Reading.** Resistance risk depends on how much antibiotic each animal gets, and Vietnam's 2026 ban and farm-advice evidence show that lever works. Fewer animals would cut total use roughly in proportion, but at 0.2% displacement the effect is far smaller than the ban's.

### 5. Pandemic risk from animal production and wildlife

- **Wildlife.** Directive 29/CT-TTg (23 Jul 2020) names "transmission of epidemics from wild animals to humans, cattle and poultry", stops wildlife imports (with exceptions for listed aquatic species), orders illegal wildlife markets closed and asks for databases and veterinary supervision of wildlife farms. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-32] Field evidence from southern Vietnam shows coronaviruses amplifying along the field-rat food chain and present on 17 of 28 wildlife farms. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-25] We did not find an evaluation of the directive's results.
- **Livestock.** Backyard and bushmeat systems raise transmission from wild animals; intensive systems "amplify the impact" through density and live transport. [general, Medium, PAN-27] Vietnamese poultry is "usually reared outside" and exposed to wild waterbird viruses. [VN-direct, Medium, PAN-39] The 1940 to 2004 record already in the package: 60.3% of emerging diseases zoonotic, 71.8% of those from wildlife (PRB-12).
- **Advocacy against peer review.** The UNEP and ILRI release lists rising demand for animal protein among seven drivers (PRB-13, intergovernmental). Peer-reviewed costed prevention (PAN-28) targets wildlife trade, deforestation and surveillance. Papers that name plant-based or cultivated meat as pandemic prevention are opinion or reviews: PAN-30 (author from an advocacy organisation), PAN-31 (authors from a cultivated-meat company), and a 2020 opinion piece and a 2021 review we did not register.

**Reading.** For Vietnam the evidence points to wildlife trade, live-bird markets and backyard poultry as the spillover interfaces, and to farm biosecurity as the amplifier. None of these is changed by a small fall in meat demand.

### 6. Does alternative protein for food reduce these risks?

| Exposure | Mechanism by which alternative protein for food could help | Evidence it does | Effect at study scale (D-BENCH 2035) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ASF supply and price shocks | A non-animal protein whose supply does not fall when pigs die | Revealed: none. 2019 and 2025 substitution went to chicken, fish, eggs and imported pork; annual tofu use flat (DIE-05, MAC-01, PAN-03). Hybrids trim about a sixth of a recipe's pork exposure at 20% extension (ch11) | 142,271 pigs a year spared, against 1.2 M culled in 2025 (8.4 times) and 5.9 M in 2019 (41.5 times) (our calculation). No measurable effect |
| HPAI in poultry | Fewer birds, fewer contacts | None found | 2.5 M birds a year spared (slaughter flow) against a flock of 585 M (stock); risk sits in backyard and live-bird-market contact, not in volume. No measurable effect |
| *S. suis*, *Salmonella* | Fewer pork meals, fewer exposures | None found; risk is concentrated in raw blood, slaughter and handling, not in meat volume | Negligible. A plant product cannot replace *tiết canh* |
| AMR | Fewer animals, less total AMU | None measured. Use per animal is the lever (PAN-21, PAN-23) | About 0.2% of meat protein; far smaller than the 2026 ban's effect |
| Pandemic (wildlife, live-bird markets) | Less demand for animal products | None found; costed prevention does not include diet change (PAN-28) | None |

**Counter-arguments supported by evidence.** Risk depends on how animals are farmed and traded (PAN-27, PAN-29, PAN-21, PAN-33). A small displacement does not change herd sizes: D-BENCH 2035 pigs spared equal about 0.5% of the end-2025 herd if read as slaughter flow against stock (142,271 against 31.4 M), and the 2025 herd itself grew 0.5% despite ASF (MAC-01). Plant-protein supply chains have their own exposures: imported soy, pea and gluten (P.4, L3 of this wave) and plant-food outbreaks (HSC-34).

**What the study can say.** Alternative protein supply chains are not exposed to animal disease, so a larger plant or fermentation sector would diversify the sources of protein (a portfolio argument). The study should not say alternative protein reduces zoonotic, pandemic or antimicrobial-resistance risk in Vietnam: no study measures that, and the modelled scale is two orders of magnitude too small to move herd-level risk. [VN-direct, Medium, synthesis]

### 7. Resilience: what people switched to

- 2019: chicken. Poultry output up 16.5%, flock to a record of nearly 500 million, pork share of meat output down 6 points (PAN-03); USDA consumption data in ch11 (DIE-10) agree.
- 2019 short run: Hanoi consumers and traders reported a month of pork avoidance with vegetable prices doubling, tofu at VND 3,000 to 4,000 a piece and fish and beef up 1.5 to 2 times (traders' claims). [VN-direct, Low, PAN-34, stated] This is a food-safety panic, not a lasting shift: annual tofu use was 0.44 kg per person a month in both 2018 and 2020 (DIE-05). Follow the revealed figure.
- 2020: government ordered pork imports and promoted frozen and imported pork in processing; COVID-19 moved shoppers online and to frozen prepared products; avian influenza plus COVID-19 cut poultry demand. [VN-direct, High, PAN-03]
- 2025: chicken again (MAC-01). An August 2025 opinion piece says chicken, beef and seafood restaurants gained and "some people" went vegetarian; no data. [VN-direct, Low, PAN-35]

**Reading.** The food system bridged both shocks with other animal protein and imports. We found no evidence that plant protein bridged the gap beyond a few weeks of panic.

## Next-wave candidates

1. **Official ASF and HPAI series for 2025 and 2026** (outbreaks, culls, genotype shares, vaccine doses by farm type). A deeper version of this line. Desk research could not close it: the 2026 totals appear only in snippets. A written request to the MAE Department of Livestock Production and Animal Health would. It would change F2.2.1 and the shock register, not a conclusion.
2. **Did plant protein sell more during ASF waves?** A different kind of question (revealed demand). Needs retail scan or retailer data for 2019 and mid-2025 (tofu, soy milk, chay products). A positive result would change ch11.5 and the resilience argument; a null would close it. Desk research would be noise.
3. **Do processed-meat makers use soy extension as an ASF hedge?** Deeper version of ch11.5; needs interviews with three to five processors (Vissan, Ha Long Canfoco and others). Would change the hybrid route's case in ch16 and ch18.
4. **Pork-borne disease burden in DALYs for Vietnam.** Deeper; ILRI Hanoi and Hanoi University of Public Health may hold unpublished estimates. Would firm the food-safety leg of ch11.6, not the security case.
5. Further desk work on "alternative protein reduces pandemic risk" would be noise: the literature has no measured effect, and the study's scale is too small to matter.
